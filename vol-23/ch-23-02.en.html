1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

The Pit Supervisor has a request on the desk: Survey wants R05 closed for an hour and forty minutes on Thursday to rework the windrow and the edge of the main ramp. The AHS Controller opens the simulator, closes R05 in a copy of the network and reads the answer within a second. All 37 producing trucks change route. Ore trucks now leave the pit over the west ramp — R09, R24, R25, R12 — and their cycle grows by 236.0 s. Waste trucks, oddly, get 39.4 s faster. The fleet loses 311.7 t per hour. The AHS map check says ONE_EXIT: the pit has one usable way out.

Then the supervisor asks the question the screen did not answer. "Road maintenance also wants R09 regraded this week. Can they do it on the same day?" The controller closes R09 as well. EX01 and EX02 lose every route to C01; 8415.0 t per hour is gone. Neither closure alone loses a single circuit. Together they cut the pit floor off.

The scene raises the questions of this chapter. Where does the simulator's road come from, and how do we know it is the same road as the one in mine.yaml? Which closures leave no alternative, and which pairs of harmless closures become harmful together? Why can a longer route be faster? And how does a node on a graph become something the AMS can reserve, one truck at a time?

This chapter covers:

  • building the simulator's road graph from the single source of geometry and checking it with invariants;
  • the structure of the reference mine's network: one loop, eighteen bridges, four area boundaries;
  • intersections and single-lane sections as exclusive road resources with demand and capacity;
  • closure what-if analysis over every segment and every pair of segments, and a ranking of critical closures;
  • M-20481 via R05 and via R24 — distance, grade, speed and time;
  • SCN-MAP-R05 as the shared scenario for the engineering and operations views.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Build the simulator's road graph from mine.yaml and road-network.geojson, deriving segment length, signed grade, speed limit, area membership and comms/GNSS attributes, and verify the M-20481 loaded distance against the reference test value of the simulator.
  2. Model intersections and single-lane segments as exclusive road resources (conflict cells, single-lane blocks) with capacity, and show how the model feeds AMS reservations.
  3. Analyse connectivity and route cost under segment closures: compute the reroute of pit traffic via the west ramp (R09–R24–R25–R12) when R05 is closed, and identify the closure sets that isolate the pit (R05 and R24) or D01 (R03 or R04).
  4. Compare loaded travel time for M-20481 via the main ramp R05 and via the west ramp R24 with the steady-state truck model, and explain why the shorter route is not necessarily the faster one under different grades and speed limits.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Use an R05 closure what-if to explain to a Pit Supervisor how pit traffic reroutes via the west ramp and what the longer route costs in cycle time and tonnes per shift.
  2. Read a simulated network view to locate DT17, M-20481's route, the autonomous areas AA-North, AA-Central and AA-South and the access points AP-01 to AP-04.
  3. Identify the closures that leave no alternative route (e.g. R03, or R05 and R24 together) and plan road-maintenance windows so that each such closure has an agreed production plan (e.g. no D01 tipping during R03 work; R05 and R24 never closed together while the pit is producing).
  4. Explain how the access point AP-04 at P3 changes where light vehicles may enter AA-North and which segments a permission must then cover.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

Every volume of this handbook has used the same road. ch-01-01 drove M-20481 over it and found a 1564.1 s cycle. ch-07-03 planned routes on it. ch-10-04 cut it into blocks and cells for reservations. ch-12-05 described its geometry, ch-12-08 put it into the AHS map, ch-12-09 versioned it, and ch-14-07 closed parts of it and handed them to road crews. Each chapter used the part of the road it needed, with its own lab code. The integrated simulator of this volume needs one road model that all of them can share — FMS dispatch, AMS reservations, ADS travel, map versions, network coverage and the scenario runner — without anyone typing a length into a configuration file.

A length typed by hand is the number most likely to survive a map change unnoticed. Version 0.2.0 of mine.yaml added the west ramp R24, the crest P3 and the access point AP-04 without changing any published cycle number, because R05 stayed the shortest route from every shovel. A simulator that had hard-coded "the pit has one exit" would have been wrong from that day, and nobody would notice until a closure what-if gave the wrong answer.

Operations meets the same problem as combinations. A single closure is usually checked: the AMS map check runs, the FMS recomputes cycle times, and ch-14-07 gives the handover procedure. Two closures planned by different people on the same day may each be harmless and fail together. The simulator checks all pairs in well under a second; a person with a map cannot.

This chapter builds the road graph once from the single source and checks it, turns it into what the other models consume (routes and travel times, exclusive resources for the AMS), and runs closure what-ifs over every segment and every pair.

3. Terminology and foundations

Road graph. The simulator's road network (glossary road-network) is an undirected graph: 25 nodes and 25 two-way road segments (road-segment), each with one lane per direction. Nodes have a kind (intersection, crest, ramp toe, load, queue, dump, crusher, park-up, fuel bay, water fill, workshop, gate) and coordinates on the local grid REFMINE-GRID with a reduced level (RL). A route (route) is an ordered list of segments; the planner sees each segment as two directed arcs, because time costs differ by direction (ch-07-03).

Single source of geometry [H]. Only mine.yaml holds node coordinates. Segment lengths and grades are derived from them; road-network.geojson is generated from mine.yaml by tools/build_road_network.py and is never edited by hand. A chapter, a configuration file or a lab may compute a length; none may type one in.

Signed grade. The grade of a segment is rise over horizontal run, signed in the direction of travel. R05 is stored from P1 to P2 and falls 120 m, so its grade from P1 is −8.99 %; a loaded truck climbing from P2 sees +8.99 %. Tables in this chapter give the grade from the segment's first node in mine.yaml unless they say "loaded".

Area membership. Every segment belongs to one area: AA-North (pit, 12 segments), AA-Central (haul roads, X, C01, V, 10 segments), AA-South (dump, 2 segments) or the manned MIA (1 segment). An access point (access-point) is a node where a manned vehicle or person requests entry to an autonomous area (autonomous-area): AP-01 at G01, AP-02 at P1, AP-03 at Y and AP-04 at P3.

Origin–destination pairs. The closure analysis uses three kinds of pairs [H]: the loaded leg of each circuit (circuit) from loading area to destination, its empty return, and access from the workshop MIA-WS to each loading unit's queue point (light vehicles, the Emergency Response Team). A lost pair has no route on the closed network.

Bridge and loop. In graph terms a bridge is a segment whose removal disconnects the graph; it is a single point of failure (single-point-of-failure) for whatever lies beyond it. A loop is a closed path. For a connected graph with N nodes and E segments the number of independent loops is

L=E−N+1

Critical segment [H]. A segment, or a set of segments closed together, is critical when its closure loses an origin–destination pair that the network otherwise serves, or raises circuit travel time more than any alternative. A critical pair is a pair of segments that loses a circuit although neither segment does on its own.

Road-graph invariant [H]. A condition the road graph must satisfy every time it is built; the simulator refuses to run when one fails. Section 4.2 lists them.

Exclusive road resources. Where two movements may need the same road at the same time, the AMS reserves that road for one truck. At an intersection (intersection) the resource is a conflict cell (conflict-cell); each movement (intersection-movement) needs a set of cells, and two movements conflict when their sets overlap — the conflict zone (conflict-zone) of the pair (ch-10-04). On a single-lane section (single-lane-section) the whole span is one block for both directions (ch-14-03). This chapter treats both as resources with a demand (passes per hour) and a capacity (passes per hour when one truck at a time occupies the resource for a known time).

What-if analysis (what-if-analysis). Running a closure, restriction or outage on a copy of the network before it is applied. A road closure (road-closure) removes a segment in both directions; the planner then finds a reroute (reroute) or reports that none exists.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 Building the graph from the single source

ahs_sim.mine.load_mine() reads mine.yaml and derives each segment's horizontal length from its end nodes' grid coordinates, its rise from their RLs, its slope length as h2+ΔRL2 and its grade as ΔRL/h. ahs_sim.engine.road_graph() wraps the result as the simulator's road model: per segment it returns length, signed grade from the first node, speed limit, area, comms class, GNSS multipath risk and rolling resistance. Nothing else in the simulator holds geometry. The FMS model computes cycle times on it, the AMS model splits its routes into blocks, the ADS travel model takes speed limits and grades from it, mapping.build_map turns it into AHS-MAP-0001, and network attaches coverage to its segments.

FIG-23-02-01 shows the graph as the simulator holds it: site speed limit as colour, signed grade on every segment that is not flat, the four areas, the access points and M-20481's loaded route. The orange marker is DT17 half-way up R05 on its first loaded climb after 10:00 in the seed-0 baseline shift (10:18) — a synthetic position, used here only to practise reading the view.

Seven segments are not flat:

4.2 Invariants: what must hold every time the graph is built

LAB-23-02-01 checks ten invariants [H]. The first seven are those of engine.road_graph; the last three are added in the lab on the public API:

  1. Positive lengths — every segment is longer than zero.
  2. Geometry consistent — each segment's horizontal length equals the distance between its nodes.
  3. Plausible grades — no grade steeper than 12 % [A]; a typing error in an RL shows up here first.
  4. Connected — every node reaches every other.
  5. Every circuit reachable — each loading unit reaches every destination that accepts its material.
  6. M-20481 route — the shortest loaded route from EX02-L to C01 is R18, R08, R06, R05, R01, R02, R21.
  7. M-20481 distance — that route measures 4500.5 m, the reference value of the simulator tests, within 0.5 m.
  8. Independent geometry — lengths and grades recomputed from the raw YAML coordinates equal the model's, and the generated GeoJSON agrees within its rounding (0.1 m, 0.01 %); both files carry mine_version 0.3.0.
  9. Area boundaries at access points — the nodes where segments of two areas meet are exactly the access-point nodes G01, P1, Y and P3, each between the areas its access point names.
  10. Movements defined — every movement through Intersection X that any circuit route uses, on the open network or with any single segment closed, has a cell set in the reservation model (section 4.4).

All ten hold on the reference mine. The largest difference between the raw recomputation and the GeoJSON is 0.048 m in length and 0.0046 % in grade — rounding, as expected. The model and the raw recomputation agree exactly, because they run the same arithmetic on the same coordinates; the check is still worth having, because it fails the moment someone edits the GeoJSON by hand or loads a stale copy.

Invariant 9 says that a vehicle can cross between areas only at a node with an access point; a new short cut from the MIA to V would fail at V and name the missing access point. Invariant 10 links the road graph to the reservation model: a closure that sends traffic through a movement without cells must be found by the model, not in the field.

The invariants run on load and stop the run [R]. A what-if on a broken graph is worse than no what-if, because it looks like an answer.

4.3 Structure of the network: one loop, eighteen bridges

The reference mine has N=25 nodes and E=25 segments and is connected, so it has L=25−25+1=1 independent loop. LAB-23-02-01 finds it: X–P1–P2–Z–W–P3–V–X, made of R01, R05, R06, R09, R24, R25 and R12. Every one of the other eighteen segments is a bridge.

This one number explains most of what the closure analysis will find:

  • A bridge is a single point of failure for everything beyond it. R02 and R21 lead to C01; closing either loses both ore circuits. R03, R04 and R22 lead to D01; closing any of them loses both waste circuits. R07, R08, R10 and R11 and the loading-area links R17 to R20 each serve one loading unit. R13 and R16 connect the workshop; R14, R15 and R23 serve the park-up PK1, the fuel bay FB1 and the water fill point WF1.
  • A loop segment alone never loses anything. Closing one of the seven segments of the loop leaves the other way round the loop open. Traffic reroutes; nothing is cut off.
  • Two loop segments always split the loop. The loop breaks into two arcs. Whether that loses a circuit depends on which nodes end up on the side away from X: the pit floor Z (EX01, EX02), the bench W (EX03, EX04), or only a crest.

The second ramp of mine.yaml 0.2.0 created the loop; before it the network was a tree and every segment a bridge. The ramp changed no published cycle number, but it changed the failure structure of the whole mine.

4.4 Intersections and single-lane sections as exclusive road resources

In the road graph Intersection X is a node with four arms: R01 to P1 and the pit, R02 to C01, R03 to Y and D01, R12 to V and the west ramp. A node cannot be reserved. The AMS needs to know which part of the intersection a truck occupies and which other trucks it then excludes. The handbook's model of X (design D2 of ch-10-04, which this chapter uses unchanged) draws four conflict cells, X1 to X4, and gives each movement the cells it needs, in the order the truck reaches them. FIG-23-02-02 shows the step from graph node to reservation model, with the movements the circuit routes actually use.

Of the twelve possible movements between four arms, D2 defines ten. The two it leaves out, R02 → R03 and R03 → R02, would take a truck from the crusher straight to the dump; LAB-23-02-01 confirms that no circuit route uses them on the open network or with any single segment closed (invariant 10). Of the 45 pairs among the ten movements, 37 conflict, as ch-10-04 found.

The road model supplies the demand. LAB-23-02-01 counts, for every intersection node, the movements that the four circuit routes use and weights each by its circuit's free-cycle rate (trucks of the baseline allocation divided by the steady-state cycle). With R05 open, X carries 155.5 passes per hour in four movements; with R05 closed it carries 145.5 passes per hour in four different ones, all to or from R12. Intersection V, which carries no circuit traffic at all with R05 open, suddenly carries 145.5 as well:

Intersection R05 open (passes/h) R05 closed (passes/h)
X 155.5 145.5
V 0.0 145.5
W 68.2 145.5
Z 155.5 75.8
Y 68.2 69.7

These are free-cycle passes with no queueing, as in ch-10-04; they are an upper estimate of demand.

The resource supplies the capacity. In D2 every pair of the movements used with R05 open shares a cell, and so does every pair used with R05 closed. X then behaves as one resource: one truck at a time. If each pass holds the cells for thold, the capacity is

C=3600thold

passes per hour. With the hold time of 15.13 s that ch-10-04 derived for the best reservation horizon [A], C = 237.9 passes per hour, and X is 65 % utilised with R05 open and 61 % with R05 closed. ch-10-04 obtained the same capacity, 238.0, by simulating 240 waiting trucks.

A single-lane section is the same idea on a segment. ch-14-03 closed one lane of R03 over 300 m for road work; the open lane must carry both directions, and the AMS reserves the span as one block. The occupancy per pass is the crossing time at the harmonic mean of the loaded (13.0 m/s) and empty (13.9 m/s) speeds plus a 10 s clearance [A]: 32.3 s. With mixed_fleet.single_lane_capacity_per_h the capacity is 111.4 passes per hour against a demand of 68.2 — the figures of ch-14-03, reproduced from the road model's speeds and demands.

How this feeds the AMS model: the road model hands over two things per route. First, the segments, which ams.route_blocks cuts into blocks — M-20481's loaded route becomes 26 blocks at 200 m [A], seven of them on R05. Second, at each intersection node on the route, the movement (arriving segment, leaving segment), which the cell table turns into cells. ams.ReservationTable then grants all or nothing. In the lab DT17 requests X1, X2 and the first block of R02 for R01 → R02 and gets them; DT24 then requests X3 and X1 for R03 → R01 and is refused while DT17 holds X1; it is granted once DT17 releases. If cells were granted one at a time instead, three trucks could each hold one cell and wait for the next — the circular wait of SCN-DEADLOCK-X on X1 to X3. Its detection and resolution belong to the AMS model (ch-23-05); the road model's part is to make sure every cell set exists and is consistent with the routes.

4.5 Closure what-if over every segment

engine.closure_whatif closes each segment in turn and reports, for every origin–destination pair, the new route, the segments added and removed, the extra length and the extra steady-state travel time (truck model of ch-01-01, loaded at the rated 220 t on loaded legs), and the pairs with no route. LAB-23-02-02 adds two things on the public API: the process nodes cut off from X (park-up, fuel bay, water fill, workshop and the access-point nodes), and the haulage lost per hour from the fleet model (planning_ops.closure_loss_t_per_h, one deterministic shift with circuits without a route held, as in ch-14-07).

The 25 single closures fall into four groups:

Group Segments Effect
Loop, used by circuits R01, R05, R06 All 37 trucks reroute over the west ramp; 311.7 t/h lost
Loop, unused by circuits R09, R12, R24, R25 No circuit changes route, except R09: waste trucks move to R24 and the fleet gains 183.3 t/h; R24 and R25 reroute only the workshop access
Bridges to destinations R02, R21 (C01); R03, R04, R22 (D01) Two circuits lost: 8598.3 t/h (ore) or 7333.3 t/h (waste)
Bridges to loading areas R07, R17 (EX01); R08, R18 (EX02); R10, R19 (EX03); R11, R20 (EX04) One circuit lost: 3630.0 to 4143.3 t/h
Bridges to process nodes R13, R16 (workshop); R14 (PK1); R15 (FB1); R23 (WF1) No circuit lost; access or service node cut off

The R05 closure is the case of SCN-MAP-R05. On the loaded legs, both ore circuits replace R06, R05, R01 with R09, R24, R25, R12: +1364.6 m and +104.6 s. Their empty returns take the same detour in reverse: +1364.6 m and +131.4 s. Both waste circuits replace R09, R06, R05, R01 with R24, R25, R12: +440.7 m loaded, yet 59.9 s faster, and +20.5 s on the empty return. Per circuit, the ore cycles grow by 236.0 s (EX01 1572.4 → 1808.4 s, EX02 1564.1 → 1800.1 s) and the waste cycles shrink by 39.4 s (EX03 1914.7 → 1875.3 s, EX04 1883.4 → 1844.0 s). The fleet model turns that into 311.7 t lost per hour, or 3740 t over a 12 h shift if R05 stayed closed all shift. These are the numbers of ch-07-03 and ch-14-07, now produced by the simulator's own road model.

Two single closures need the process-node column to be understood. Closing R03 loses EX03 and EX04, but it also cuts off Y and the water fill point WF1: a water truck bound for WF1 has no route either. Closing R15 loses nothing in the production matrix and still cuts off the fuel bay FB1 — over a long closure, every truck that needs fuel is affected. An analysis that looks only at circuits reports both as less severe than they are.

The R09 row is a modelling effect. The simulator routes by shortest distance by default, to stay consistent with earlier volumes; with R09 closed the waste trucks are pushed onto R24, which is faster for them. A closure that improves production signals that the routing rule is not minimising time (section 4.7).

4.6 Pairs of closures and the critical ranking

LAB-23-02-02 then closes all 300 pairs of segments. Fifteen pairs lose a circuit that neither of their segments loses alone. All fifteen are pairs of loop segments — as section 4.3 predicts — out of the 21 such pairs:

Pair type Pairs Circuits lost Haulage lost
One of R01, R05, R06 with one of R12, R24, R25 9 (e.g. R05+R24, R06+R24, R01+R12) EX01, EX02, EX03, EX04 15,931.7 t/h — the whole fleet
R09 with one of R01, R05, R06 3 (e.g. R05+R09) EX01, EX02 8415.0 t/h
R09 with one of R12, R24, R25 3 (e.g. R09+R24) EX03, EX04 7333.3 t/h

The remaining six loop pairs cut off only a crest or the MIA side: R01+R05 or R01+R06 strand P1 (AP-02); R24+R25 strands P3 (AP-04); R12+R24 and R12+R25 cut the workshop, park-up and fuel bay off from X. None of these loses a circuit, but each removes an access point or a service from the network for as long as it lasts.

The AHS map check agrees with the reachability results and adds the exit view of ch-12-08. With loaded exits (check_map(…, loaded_exits=True)) R05 alone gives ONE_EXIT; so do R24 alone and R25 alone, because a loaded truck at P3 can no longer reach X without going back into the pit. R05+R24, R06+R24 and R05+R25 give PIT_ISOLATED with UNREACHABLE errors; R03 gives UNREACHABLE for the waste circuits. "An exit is a path, not a segment" (ch-12-08): R06+R24 isolates the pit although the ramp R05 itself is open.

FIG-23-02-03 ranks the closure sets: first by circuits lost, then by haulage lost per hour, then by circuit travel-time increase. Pairs are drawn only where the pair loses more than its parts.

Three readings for planning:

  • The top of the ranking is pairs, not single roads. The most damaging single closures (R02, R21) lose half the production; nine pairs of individually harmless closures lose all of it.
  • R09 is quietly critical. Alone it costs nothing (or gains, under distance routing). Combined with any closure of the main route it cuts off EX01 and EX02; combined with any closure of the west route it cuts off EX03 and EX04. It is the only segment that appears in six of the fifteen critical pairs.
  • R03 and R04 need no partner. Each alone cuts off D01. The waste plan must assume no D01 tipping for the whole window of any work on either road.

4.7 M-20481 via R05 and via R24: shorter is not always faster

engine.m20481_route_comparison runs M-20481 on the open network and with R05 closed, using haul_cycle.compute_cycle and the steady-state truck model of ch-01-01. FIG-23-02-04 plots the loaded haul against distance from EX02-L: elevation, speed and elapsed time.

M-20481 Via R05 Via R24 (R05 closed) Change
Loaded route R18 R08 R06 R05 R01 R02 R21 R18 R08 R09 R24 R25 R12 R02 R21 —
Loaded distance 4500.5 m 5865.1 m +1364.6 m
Loaded travel 804.0 s 908.6 s +104.6 s
Empty travel 503.1 s 634.5 s +131.4 s
Cycle 1564.1 s 1800.1 s +236.0 s

The loaded climb is where the routes differ. On a grade the truck is power-limited, and its steady speed is

v=ηPmg(G+RR)

with drivetrain efficiency η, net power P, gross mass m, signed grade G and rolling resistance RR (ch-01-01). On R05, G+RR = 0.0899 + 0.03 and the loaded truck climbs at 11.44 km/h: 421.4 s for 1339.6 m. On R24, G+RR = 0.0633 + 0.03 and it climbs at 14.71 km/h: 348.6 s for 1425.0 m. The longer ramp is 72.8 s faster.

M-20481 still takes the R05 route when it can, because R24 is not where the extra distance is. The detour adds R09 (109.1 s), R25 (1303.8 m at 40 km/h, 117.3 s) and R12 instead of R06 and R01. FIG-23-02-04 shows the two time curves crossing: the west route reaches the crest earlier and loses the lead on the long flat road back to X.

For the waste circuits the balance tips the other way. EX03's shortest loaded route goes down to Z and up R05: 6550.7 m and 999.5 s. Time routing (compute_cycle(…, route="time")) chooses R24 instead: 6991.4 m and 939.5 s, 440.7 m longer and 59.9 s faster; the cycle falls from 1914.7 s to 1854.8 s. EX04 is the same: 6452.2 m and 983.8 s against 6892.9 m and 923.9 s. The ore circuits do not change, because for them the shortest route is also the fastest.

Two design consequences follow [R]. First, the routing rule must be stated with every what-if, because the same closure looks different under distance and time routing (the R09 row of section 4.5). Second, the simulator's default of shortest distance is a teaching choice that reproduces earlier chapters; a site whose AMS routes by time must run the what-if with time routing, or it will plan around a gain or a loss that does not exist. ch-07-03 also checked that the time-stepped dynamic model of ch-07-01 does not change the ranking of these routes; it adds 2 to 17 s per route.

4.8 Attributes along a route: comms, GNSS and access

The road model carries attributes as well as geometry, and a reroute moves trucks onto different attributes. LAB-23-02-01 adds up M-20481's loaded route by attribute:

M-20481 loaded Via R05 Via R24
Marginal comms 1339.6 m (R05) 1886.9 m (R09, R24)
High GNSS multipath risk 1339.6 m (R05) 0.0 m
Medium GNSS multipath risk 653.4 m (R08, R18) 2078.4 m (R08, R18, R24)

The detour trades the high-multipath ramp for a longer marginal-coverage climb. Whether that causes more communication stops is a question for the network model (ch-23-08); the road model's job is to make the exposure visible before the closure is applied.

The same model answers where light vehicles enter AA-North. Since mine.yaml 0.2.0 there are two access points into the pit: AP-02 at P1 (top of R05) and AP-04 at P3 (top of R24). The shortest route from the workshop to every loading unit now runs through V and R25 and enters at AP-04 — for EX02, R16, R13, R25, R24, R09, R08 (4998.2 m). The AA-North segments a light vehicle drives from each access point are:

To From AP-02 (P1) From AP-04 (P3)
EX01 queue point R05, R06, R07 R24, R09, R07
EX02 queue point R05, R06, R08 R24, R09, R08
EX03 queue point R05, R06, R09, R10 R24, R10
EX04 queue point R05, R06, R09, R11 R24, R11

A permission requested at AP-04 for work at EX02 must therefore cover R24, R09 and R08, not R05 and R06 — the opposite ramp from the one most people picture as "the way into the pit". During an R05 closure AP-04 is also where every autonomous truck leaves the pit, so entries there are granted only between trucks (REQ-04-08-007).

4.9 Trade-offs and model limits

  • Steady-state travel. No acceleration, braking, corners or truck interaction; route rankings are unaffected (ch-07-03), cycle times are underestimated (ch-23-03).
  • Distance routing by default. It reproduces earlier chapters, not a time-routing AMS (section 4.7).
  • Segment resolution. The smallest map unit is the segment. A 300 m lane closure on R03 can only be represented as a block over the whole 1403.6 m segment in the map (ch-14-03); the single-lane calculation of section 4.4 assumes the span is represented as it is on the ground.
  • Free-cycle demand. Intersection demand ignores queues and the C01 limit: an upper estimate (ch-23-04 gives realised demand).
  • Static closure analysis. closure_whatif and the fleet-model loss assume the closure lasts long enough for a new steady state. The transient — trucks draining, reservations being released, map versions being distributed — is in the scenario timeline (section 6.2), the AMS model (ch-23-05) and map rollout (ch-23-08).
  • What the scenario run contains. In scenario_runner, SCN-MAP-R05 runs as a partial recipe: the registry key events are scripted and the fleet event log is the baseline shift. The closure's production effect is a fleet-rate blend [A], not part of the event log. Its timeline "pass" is consistent with the registry by construction, not evidence that the closure was modelled event by event.

5. Responsibilities and interface boundaries

Element Responsibility for the road network and closures
FMS Uses route lengths and travel times from the road model for cycle times and allocation; holds circuits without a route and codes the holds (DLY-OD-04); reports haulage lost per closure
AMS Holds closures as overlays on the map version in force; replans routes; runs the map check with loaded exits before a planned closure is applied; turns routes into blocks and intersection movements into cells; grants all or nothing
ADS Drives the granted path with the speed limits and grades of the map version it has confirmed; does not use roads outside its granted path
VCS Not involved in the road model; executes speed and braking commands
Independent safety functions Act regardless of the road model; a remote stop on R24 during an R05 closure stops the only open exit, and the isolation is reported, not prevented
Onboard safety-related systems Not involved; a truck stopped by one becomes an obstruction the AMS treats like a temporary closure of its block
People Pit Supervisor approves closures and their production plan; AHS Controller applies and lifts closures and reads the what-if; Survey and the road crew own the work and the as-built; the planner keeps the critical-pair list in the maintenance schedule
Simulator (this chapter) Builds the road graph from mine.yaml, checks the invariants, computes what-ifs; never controls a truck and never applies a closure

Two boundaries matter most. Geometry has one owner: mine.yaml, through Survey and the change process of ch-12-09. The simulator, the AHS map and the FMS all derive from it; none corrects it. The what-if advises, people decide: the simulator ranks closures and reports their cost; the Pit Supervisor decides whether a closure goes ahead, and the AHS Controller applies it in the AMS.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation: the open network and M-20481

On the open network all ten invariants hold, every origin–destination pair has a route, and both pit exits are usable. M-20481 runs EX02-L → C01 over R18, R08, R06, R05, R01, R02, R21 (4500.5 m) in a 1564.1 s cycle. X carries 155.5 free-cycle passes per hour; V carries no circuit traffic. The workshop's shortest route into the pit enters at AP-04. The map check reports no issue.

Normal operation is when the weekly maintenance plan is checked against the bridges and the fifteen critical pairs (section 8.3).

6.2 Abnormal operation: SCN-MAP-R05 (shared timeline for sections 7 and 8)

SCN-MAP-R05 is the reference closure: R05 closed for the windrow and edge rework, pit traffic over the west ramp, a new map version, and the return to R05. Its times come from the registry: T−3600 s, T+0 s, T+1800 s, T+2400 s and T+2700 s, with T+0 s the submission of the as-built survey. The drain at T−3389.4 s is the reconstruction of ch-10-03 and ch-12-09; the 421.4 s bound is computed here. In the scenario runner the key events are triggered at 10:00 (T+0 s), so the closure starts at 09:00 [A].

6.3 Degraded operation: a second loss while R05 is closed

During the SCN-MAP-R05 window the network has no loop left: every segment is a bridge. Any further loss on the west route is now an isolation, whether it is a planned closure, a truck stopped on R24 or a remote stop of AA-North [A] (this combination is a what-if built from the closure sets of section 4.6, not a registered scenario):

  • R24 lost (e.g. a truck stopped on the ramp): R05+R24 — every circuit lost, PIT_ISOLATED, 15,931.7 t/h, all trucks in the pit held.
  • R09 lost (e.g. a planned regrade, as in section 1.1): R05+R09 — EX01 and EX02 cut off; EX03 and EX04 still leave over R24; 8415.0 t/h.
  • R25 or R12 lost: R05+R25 or R05+R12 — every circuit lost; with R12 the workshop side is cut off from X as well.

The road model cannot prevent these. It shows one exit and no loop, and the what-if for any further closure shows the isolation before it is applied; the isolation procedures are those of ch-14-07 and ch-16-04.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

Decision Choice Alternative Why
Source of geometry mine.yaml only; lengths and grades derived Lengths in the simulator's configuration One edit, one truth; typed lengths survive map changes unnoticed
Graph form Undirected segments, directed time costs Directed lanes Every segment is two-way with one lane per direction; lane-level detail lives in the AHS map
Invariants Ten checks on load, run stops on failure Checks in tests only A what-if on a broken graph looks like an answer
Closure analysis Exhaustive over singles and pairs Selected cases 325 closure sets in well under a second; pairs are where the surprises are
Cost of a closure Lost circuits, then fleet-model haulage per hour, then travel time Route length only Length misranks R09 and the waste circuits (section 4.7)
Routing rule Distance by default, time as an option Time by default Reproduces earlier chapters; the rule is stated with every result
Intersection model Cell table per intersection (D2 at X) Whole intersection as one resource Same capacity for this mine's traffic; cells allow partial holds and must therefore be complete

The value of the exhaustive scan is completeness: a list assembled by experience would include R05+R24 and probably miss R06+R25 or R09+R12.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

Along the timeline of section 6.2:

  • Before T−3600 s — road model → AMS and FMS. The what-if for R05 runs on a copy of the network: reroutes, cycle times, haulage per hour, map check, cut-off nodes, intersection demand. The AMS and FMS receive the same closure set so that their routes and cycle times agree (ch-07-03: route costs and FMS cycle times use the same model).
  • T−3600 s — AMS. The closure becomes an overlay on AHS-MAP-0001; R05 leaves the planning network. Missions replan from their next decision node. Intersection movements at X change from R01 arms to R12 arms; the cells of the new movements already exist (invariant 10).
  • T−3600 to T−3389.4 s — AMS ↔ ADS. Trucks granted on R05 complete their traverse; no new block on R05 is granted. The 421.4 s loaded-climb time is the upper bound on the drain from the road model.
  • T+1800 s — candidate map version. Before activation, the candidate version is built into a road graph and must pass the same invariants. A changed R05 that came back 0.2 m shorter is fine; one whose RL moved enough to change the grade by a percentage point is not a windrow rework and goes back to Survey.
  • T+2400 to T+2700 s — AMS. Trucks that have confirmed the new version are routed over R05 again; reservations on R24 and at V are released as the last trucks pass.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

The network view of FIG-23-02-01 is the controller's map of the road model: areas, access points, speed limits, grades, the route of a selected mission and the trucks on it. During a what-if it shows, side by side:

  • the closure set and the map-check result (none, ONE_EXIT, PIT_ISOLATED, UNREACHABLE);
  • per circuit: rerouted or held, extra cycle time, trucks affected;
  • haulage lost per hour, per shift and for the planned window;
  • process nodes and access points cut off;
  • intersections whose demand changes, with demand against capacity;
  • the routing rule and the mine_version used.

In the simulator none of this is an alarm. In the AMS the corresponding conditions — ONE_EXIT acknowledged, PIT_ISOLATED requiring a decision — follow ch-12-08 and ch-14-07.

8.3 Procedure excerpt — planning and running the R05 closure of SCN-MAP-R05

  1. Planner (week before): list the week's closures and windows. Check each against the bridges and the fifteen critical pairs. R05 on Thursday 09:00–10:40 [A]: loop segment, no circuit lost alone. R09 regrade on the same day: R05+R09 is a critical pair — move the R09 work to another day or agree a production plan for EX01 and EX02. Any work on R03, R04 or R22 is planned with no D01 tipping for its whole window.
  2. AHS Controller (day before): run the R05 what-if. Record: 37 trucks rerouted, ore cycles +236.0 s, 311.7 t/h, 519.4 t over the 6000 s window, ONE_EXIT, V from 0.0 to 145.5 passes per hour, AP-04 the only exit.
  3. Pit Supervisor: approve the closure with the production plan (ch-14-07: extra ore trucks or accept the ore loss). State that no second closure on R09, R24, R25 or R12 may be applied during the window without a new approval as an isolation.
  4. AHS Controller (T−3600 s): apply the closure; read ONE_EXIT; check permissions and work zones on R24, R25, P3 and V before the reroute takes effect (REQ-04-08-007); watch the drain.
  5. AHS Controller (window): treat any stop on R24 as a possible isolation; if a truck stops on the ramp, follow the isolation procedure (ch-16-04) rather than waiting for it to clear.
  6. AHS Controller (T+2400 to T+2700 s): after activation of the validated version, confirm that routes return to R05 and that V's demand falls back; record the closure as ended.

8.5 Training and competency implications

  • AHS Controllers practise naming, for any closure, whether it reroutes, isolates a circuit or cuts off a service node.
  • Pit Supervisors and planners learn the loop structure (one loop of seven segments, all else bridges), so that critical pairs can be reasoned out, not memorised.
  • Everyone who requests permissions learns that AP-04 is an entry into the pit with its own route, and that the permission covers R24, not R05.
  • Simulator rehearsal supplements, never replaces, site training and authorisation (ch-16-09).

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-23-02-001 [H] The simulator shall derive every segment length and grade from the node coordinates of mine.yaml, and no configuration, lab or scenario input shall contain a typed route or segment length. Rationale: typed lengths survive map changes unnoticed; the second ramp of mine.yaml 0.2.0 changed the failure structure without changing a single published cycle number (sections 2 and 4.3). Verification: test (LAB-23-02-01 recomputes lengths and grades from the raw coordinates and the GeoJSON); inspection.

REQ-23-02-002 [H] The simulator shall check the road-graph invariants of section 4.2 each time it builds the road graph, and shall refuse to run a scenario or a what-if when any invariant fails, naming the failed invariant. Rationale: a what-if on a broken graph looks like an answer (section 4.2). Verification: test (LAB-23-02-01; a test with a perturbed node coordinate shows the named failure).

REQ-23-02-003 [H] A closure what-if shall report, for the closure set: the rerouted and lost origin–destination pairs including workshop access, the process nodes and access points cut off, the AHS map check with loaded exits, the haulage lost per hour from the fleet model and the change in demand at each intersection. Rationale: an analysis of circuits only misses the cut-off fuel bay, water fill or access point (section 4.5); route length alone misranks closures (section 4.7). Verification: test (LAB-23-02-02); demonstration with FIG-23-02-03.

REQ-23-02-004 [H] The site shall keep a list of the bridges and critical pairs of its road network, computed by an exhaustive scan of single and paired closures and recomputed on every map version that changes connectivity, and a planned closure that completes a critical pair with another closure in force shall be approved by the Pit Supervisor as an isolation before it is applied. Rationale: fifteen pairs of individually harmless closures lose one or more circuits, nine of them all production (section 4.6). Verification: test (LAB-23-02-02 all-pairs scan); inspection of the maintenance schedule and approvals.

REQ-23-02-005 [H] Every movement through an intersection that any route can use, on the open network or with any single segment closed, shall have a defined set of conflict cells, and the reservation model shall grant a movement's cells all or nothing. Rationale: a closure moves traffic onto different movements (section 4.4); a movement without cells cannot be reserved consistently, and partial grants can form the circular wait of SCN-DEADLOCK-X. Verification: test (LAB-23-02-01 invariant 10 and reservation demonstration).

REQ-23-02-006 [H] Every what-if result shall record the mine_version, the ahs_sim version, the routing rule (distance or time) and the truck model used. Rationale: the same closure costs or gains production depending on the routing rule (sections 4.5 and 4.7). Verification: inspection of the what-if output.

REQ-23-02-007 [H] The network view shall show, for each access point into AA-North, the segments between the access point and each loading area, so that a permission request can be checked against the route the requester will drive. Rationale: from AP-04 the route to EX02 runs over R24 and R09, not R05 and R06 (section 4.8). Verification: demonstration (LAB-23-02-01 access table); inspection.

Acceptance criteria for the road model of the integrated simulator [H]:

  • all ten invariants pass on mine.yaml 0.3.0 and the M-20481 loaded distance is within 0.5 m of the reference test value;
  • the R05 closure reproduces the reroute R09–R24–R25–R12, +1364.6 m and +236.0 s per ore cycle, and 311.7 t/h;
  • R03 or R04 alone loses EX03 and EX04; R05+R24 isolates the pit; the all-pairs scan finds 15 critical pairs;
  • intersection demand at X reproduces ch-10-04 (155.5 passes per hour) and the single-lane case reproduces ch-14-03 (111.4 passes per hour capacity).

Validation. The SCN-MAP-R05 timeline passes in the scenario runner, but trivially: the key events are scripted from the registry and the fleet log is the baseline shift. Nothing here is validated against a real mine; the registry times and every rate are illustrative [A]. Passing these tests demonstrates model logic only. It is not safety evidence for any real system.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Copying lengths into the simulator's configuration "to make it faster". It saves nothing measurable and makes the next map version wrong.
  • Treating the GeoJSON as the source. It is generated; edits to it are lost or, worse, kept in one copy.
  • Testing closures one at a time. On this network every single closure of a loop segment is harmless; every danger is in pairs.
  • Checking only the production matrix. The water fill point, the fuel bay and the access points are nodes too.
  • Ranking closures by extra metres. R24 adds metres and saves time; R09 closed looks like a gain.
  • Drawing cells only for today's movements. A closure brings new movements into use; their cells must already exist.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Approving two closures separately. Each approval is correct; the combination isolates the pit.
  • Calling R24 "unused". It carries no circuit traffic with R05 open, but it is the only exit while R05 is closed and the shortest light-vehicle route into the pit at all times.
  • Writing a permission for "the pit ramp" when the requester enters at AP-04.
  • Reading a waste-cycle gain during an R05 closure as an improvement to keep. It is an artefact of distance routing.
  • Treating a stopped truck on R24 during an R05 closure as a delay. It is an isolation.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — the loaded climb. Truck class HT220 (equipment.yaml, [A]): empty 165 t, payload 220 t, net power 1800 kW, drivetrain efficiency 0.8. On R05 loaded, G+RR = 0.0899 + 0.03 = 0.1199 and v=0.8×1800000/(385000×9.81×0.1199) = 3.18 m/s = 11.44 km/h. Time on 1339.6 m: 421.4 s. On R24, G+RR = 0.0933, v = 14.71 km/h, 348.6 s on 1425.0 m. The longer ramp saves 72.8 s.

Example 2 — loops and bridges. N = 25, E = 25, connected: L = 1. Closing any two of the seven loop segments splits the loop into two arcs: 21 pairs. A pair loses EX01 and EX02 if Z ends up on the side away from X, and EX03 and EX04 if W does. Z is cut off by one of R01, R05, R06 with one of R09, R24, R25, R12 (12 pairs); W by one of R01, R05, R06, R09 with one of R24, R25, R12 (12 pairs); both by one of R01, R05, R06 with one of R24, R25, R12 (9 pairs). Pairs losing anything: 12 + 12 − 9 = 15.

Example 3 — demand and capacity at V. With R05 closed, ore circuits (10 trucks at EX01, cycle 1808.4 s; 9 at EX02, 1800.1 s) and waste circuits (9 at EX03, 1875.3 s; 9 at EX04, 1844.0 s) all pass V twice per cycle: 2×3600×(10/1808.4+9/1800.1+9/1875.3+9/1844.0) = 145.5 passes per hour. If V's movements all conflict and a pass holds the cells 15.13 s [A], utilisation is 145.5 / 237.9 = 0.61.

15. Key takeaways

  • The simulator's road comes only from mine.yaml; lengths and grades are computed, never typed, and ten invariants check the graph every time it is built.
  • The reference mine has one loop (R01, R05, R06, R09, R24, R25, R12) and eighteen bridges; that structure predicts every critical closure.
  • Closing R05 reroutes all 37 trucks over R09–R24–R25–R12: +236.0 s per ore cycle, −39.4 s per waste cycle, 311.7 t/h.
  • R03 or R04 alone cuts off D01; fifteen pairs of loop segments lose circuits, nine of them all production (e.g. R05+R24, R06+R24).
  • Check the process nodes and access points too: R03 cuts off WF1, R15 the fuel bay, R24+R25 the access point AP-04.
  • A longer route can be faster: R24 climbs at 14.71 km/h against 11.44 km/h on R05; time routing sends the waste trucks over R24 by choice.
  • Intersections and single-lane sections become exclusive resources with demand and capacity; every movement a route can use needs its cells.
  • From AP-04 the way into the pit is R24, not R05; permissions follow the route actually driven.
