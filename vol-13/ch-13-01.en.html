1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

08:40 on the DAY shift, EX02. The Shovel Operator drops the fourth bucket into DT17 and watches it pull away. The next truck, DT18, has been standing at the Queue point for two minutes; it now drives round behind the shovel to its Cusp point, stops, reverses in alongside the shovel and creeps the last metres. The operator counts while the bucket hangs over the empty spot: forty-five seconds between the last pass on DT17 and the first on DT18. "I dig for 132 seconds and wait 45 for every truck. A quarter of my shift is truck changes."

At 09:10 the floor at the spot is rutted and the operator calls DZ03. Fifteen minutes later loading resumes; four loads have gone. At the shift meeting three people read the same numbers differently. The Pit Supervisor's hourly target for EX02 was 4743 t, the shovel limit of Volume 01; the face model of this chapter gives 4209 t per loading hour with nine trucks. The AHS Controller points out that two trucks are almost always waiting at EX02-Q and asks why the shovel still waits for trucks. The Shovel Operator asks for double-sided loading, which would cut the exchange to 16.5 s. Three questions follow:

  • What limits EX02 — its swing, its truck exchange, its trucks, or the crusher behind them?
  • Would double-sided loading, a tenth truck or a faster swing add the most tonnes, and by how much?
  • How should a clean-up, a break or a payload correction be planned, coded and reported so that the shift report points to the real cause?

This chapter covers:

  • the loading cycle of a face shovel split into passes, truck exchange and shovel idle (hang) time, and productivity per operating hour, single- and double-sided (FIG-13-01-01);
  • why truck exchange, not swing, is the lever that autonomous loading changes, and what the 35 s spot time of the reference mine means;
  • the match factor of EX01–EX04 and a seeded face model of EX02 against the number of trucks (FIG-13-01-02);
  • operating delays, standby and downtime at the face, the loss tree from digging rate to shift tonnes (FIG-13-01-03), and the loading-unit status the FMS needs;
  • the SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02 clean-up as the shared walkthrough, and SCN-PAYLOAD-OVER-EX02 as a cycle-time feedback.

The chapter stays at one loading unit. How the spot is designated and how the truck manoeuvres into it are in ch-13-02 and ch-13-08; fleet-level truck numbers are in ch-21-01 and ch-21-02. Every cycle and pass value comes from equipment.yaml and is an assumption [A]; the shovels EX01–EX04 are generic manned hydraulic face shovels [H].

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Decompose the EX02 loading cycle into truck spot, bucket passes, truck exchange and shovel wait components and calculate shovel productivity in tonnes per operating hour from the equipment.yaml parameters [A].
  2. Analyse how truck exchange time, rather than swing cycle time, bounds shovel productivity when autonomous trucks spot one at a time, and quantify the gain from double-sided loading at EX02 [A].
  3. Derive the truck–shovel match factor for the EX02 → C01 route and predict whether EX02 or its trucks are the constraint as the number of assigned trucks varies.
  4. Calculate the effect of shovel operating delays (teeth change, face clean-up, operator break) on effective loading productivity at EX02 using the time usage model.
  5. Specify the loading-unit status data (operating, delay, down, relocating) that the FMS needs from EX01–EX04 to dispatch trucks correctly.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Interpret shovel hang time and truck queue time at EX02-Q on the control room display and decide whether to request more trucks, fewer trucks or a spotting change from the FMS.
  2. Allocate lost loading time at EX02 to the correct delay codes (shovel, truck, spotting, clean-up) so that daily loading productivity reports point to the real cause.
  3. Explain to a Shovel Operator moving from manned haulage which operator habits (early bucket staging, signalling trucks in) still matter with autonomous trucks and which are replaced by system calls.
  4. Set an hourly loading target for EX02 from the productivity model and track actual against target with the Pit Supervisor during the shift.
  5. Plan Shovel Operator breaks and crib changes at EX01–EX04 so that loading gaps are covered by FMS reassignment rather than by trucks idling at the Queue point.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

Volume 01 treated EX02 as one server with a service time of 35 + 132 = 167 s per truck — spotting plus loading — and found the shovel limit at 3600 × 220 / 167 = 4743 t/h (ch-01-05). ch-10-02 put EX02 into the closed network of the mine and showed that C01, not the shovels, is the ore bottleneck with R05 open. ch-14-04 priced DZ03's clean-ups at EX02 over a shift. All three kept the shovel's 167 s as a black box.

That box hides the part of the cycle that autonomous haulage changes most. The four passes of 33 s are the Shovel Operator's work and do not depend on who drives the truck. What happens between two loads does: the loaded truck must leave the spot, the next truck must reach its Cusp point — behind the shovel, facing away from the face (ch-13-02) — reverse in and creep to the spot, and only then can the first bucket be dumped. In this handbook's model the next autonomous truck may start reversing only when the loaded truck has cleared the spot, ten seconds after the last pass [A] (ahs_sim.loading, depart_clear_s). Those ten seconds are not in Volume 01's 167 s. With them the shovel needs 177 s per truck and the shovel limit falls to 4475 t/h — the first reason why the Pit Supervisor's target in section 1.1 was out of reach.

The second reason is variability. Trucks do not arrive at even intervals, and a shovel cannot bank idle time to spend later when three trucks arrive together (ch-01-05). The third is time without loading: shift change, breaks, clean-ups and maintenance. The fourth sits outside the face: when C01 is saturated, a faster EX02 adds trucks to the crusher queue, not ore to the stockpile (ch-10-02).

A loading productivity number is therefore only useful when it says which of these it includes. The Shovel Operator, the AHS Controller and the Pit Supervisor in section 1.1 were each looking at a different layer of the same loss tree.

3. Terminology and foundations

Bucket pass and swing cycle. One dig–swing–dump–return of the bucket; HS-FACE needs four passes of 55 t each, 33 s per pass, 132 s per truck, 220 t per truck (equipment.yaml) [A].

Shovel cycle time [H]. The shovel's time per truck when a truck is waiting: load time plus truck exchange time. It excludes time in which no truck is available.

Truck exchange time [H]. The shovel's time between the end of one load and the start of the next while a truck is waiting. In single-sided loading (one spot) it is the loaded truck's clearance of the spot plus the next truck's Spotting into it. In double-sided loading (two spots on either side of the shovel) the next truck spots on the other side while the current one is loaded, and the exchange shrinks to the shovel's swing to the other side, or to the part of the Spotting that the load did not cover.

Spotting is the truck's manoeuvre from the Queue point through the Cusp point, where it stops travelling forward and starts reversing, into the loading spot. Designation and manoeuvres are treated in ch-13-02 and ch-13-08; here only their duration matters. A longer Spotting manoeuvre is normal in-cycle time, not a delay (delay-codes coding_rules, ch-07-05).

Shovel idle time (glossary shovel-idle-time; the operator's "hang time"). Time in which a working shovel has no truck available to load, beyond the exchange. Coded DLY-NP-03 for the shovel. Queue time is the truck's wait at the Queue point for a working shovel, DLY-NP-01.

Productivity of a loading unit, in this chapter, is tonnes per operating hour while it is loading or able to load (Working Time in the GMG model, SRC-PUB-GMG-TUM-2020); effective productivity spreads the tonnes over a whole shift, after standby, downtime and operating delays.

Match factor (MF). ch-01-05 defined MF=nts/(nLtc) with the shovel's time per truck ts and the free truck cycle tc [H]. ahs_sim.loading.match_factor uses the same form; this chapter puts load plus exchange into ts (177 s single-sided), so EX02's nine trucks give MF = 1.02 where ch-01-05, with 167 s, gave 0.96. Both are right for their definitions; compare match factors only when ts is the same.

Excavator [A]. A backhoe-type loading unit that digs below its tracks. The reference mine has none; where this chapter mentions one, it is an [A] variant with its own pass count and swing, not a baseline machine.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The loading cycle

For one truck, with np passes of swing time tsw, payload m, exchange tx and shovel idle time th per truck:

tL=nptsw,P=3600mtL+tx+th  (t per operating hour)

The exchange depends on the loading mode (ahs_sim.loading.exchange_time_s):

txsingle=tclear+tspot,txdouble=max(tside, tspot+tclear−tL)

with tclear = 10 s for the loaded truck to clear the spot [A], tspot = 35 s (truck_spot_time_s, section 4.2) and tside = 16.5 s, half a swing cycle, for the shovel to turn to the other spot [A]. The double-sided formula assumes that the next truck is already waiting at its Cusp point when loading starts on the other side, and starts reversing once that side has been cleared [A]. LAB-13-01-01 gives for EX02:

Quantity Single-sided Double-sided
Load time, 4 × 33 s 132 s 132 s
Truck exchange 45 s (10 + 35) 16.5 s (side change)
Shovel time per truck 177 s 148.5 s
Exchange share of the shovel cycle 25.4 % 11.1 %
Productivity, no idle time 4475 t/h 5333 t/h

FIG-13-01-01 draws both cycles on one time axis.

Two limits frame the table. With no exchange at all — a truck that appears under the bucket the moment the last one vanishes — the shovel would dig 220 t every 132 s, 6000 t/h; that rate is never reached. Double-sided loading removes the Spotting from the shovel's critical path but leaves the side change; it gains 19.2 % over single-sided loading, provided that a truck is always waiting. Whether one is, is the subject of section 4.4.

4.2 What the 35 s spot time means

equipment.yaml gives HS-FACE a truck_spot_time_s of 35 s [A]. The handbook's models can also compute the Spotting manoeuvre itself. Since the revision of ch-13-02 and ch-13-08 (WP-FIX-13SPOT) the truck stands side-on beside the shovel and reaches the spot by a staged reverse-in: it turns round behind the shovel, stops at its Cusp point facing away from the face, and reverses in alongside (ahs_sim.spotting). For the side-on spot S1 at EX02-L spotting_time_s gives 55.5 s — 25.6 s forward from the entry of the turn round to the Cusp point (approach_to_cusp), 4.0 s stopped at the Cusp point, 20.4 s reversing and 5.4 s creeping the last 3 m [A]. The entry is not the Queue point: EX02-Q lies 206.2 m from EX02-L along R18, and from there the model gives 90.2 s to spotted (ch-13-08). The first edition's radial reverse-in, kept as a comparison, gave 52.4 s from a 30 m approach leg and 32.9 s from its Cusp point. The numbers describe different things, and the handbook reads them as follows [A] (a handbook convention, also stated in the comment of truck_spot_time_s in equipment.yaml): the 35 s class value is the part from the Cusp point to spotted — reverse plus creep, 25.8 s in the side-on model — and the approach to the Cusp point is not on the shovel's critical path. In double-sided loading the approach overlaps the other truck's load by construction. In single-sided loading the reading assumes that the next truck waits at its Cusp point — the staging position — while the loaded truck is loaded, which holds only when the Cusp point lies clear of the departing truck's exit path; ch-13-02 makes that a condition of building the Cusp point, and ch-13-08 section 4.5 shows what it is worth.

If the approach cannot overlap — the waiting truck may start its approach to the Cusp point only once the spot is clear — the whole modelled 55.5 s falls into the exchange:

Reading [A] Single-sided exchange Productivity, no idle time
Class value = Cusp point → spotted (handbook reading) 10 + 35 = 45 s 4475 t/h
Whole modelled manoeuvre, entry and turn round included (sensitivity) 10 + 55.5 = 65.5 s 4010 t/h

The difference, 464 t/h, is larger than the effect of a tenth truck on EX02 single-sided. The rest of this chapter uses the handbook reading, and every number that depends on it says so. A site that measures its own exchange should measure it as defined in section 3 — from the end of one load to the start of the next with a truck waiting — and not as a spot time from a specification sheet.

4.3 Exchange time versus swing time

Per second, swing matters more: one second off the swing saves four seconds per truck. LAB-13-01-01 gives 103.5 t/h per second of swing and 25.4 t/h per second of exchange at EX02's single-sided operating point. But the seconds that are available to remove are not the same. The swing is the Shovel Operator's skill, the dig and the material; a 10 % faster swing (3.3 s) is a large improvement in practice [A]. The exchange is set by the loading arrangement and the truck system: double-sided loading removes 28.5 s of it at once. To gain the same with the swing, EX02 would need a swing of 25.9 s instead of 33 s, 21 % faster.

Exchange (s) 15 30 45 60 75 90
Shovel limit (t/h) 5388 4889 4475 4125 3826 3568

This is the sense in which the exchange, not the swing, bounds productivity with autonomous trucks [A]: the truck system spots one truck at a time, each exchange carries a fixed clearance, and no operator skill shortens the reversing of a truck the operator does not drive. In manned haulage the shovel operator and the truck driver can shorten the exchange between themselves; with autonomous trucks the same shortening has to be designed into the loading arrangement — double-sided spots, Cusp points close to the spot, short clearance distances — and into the interface rules of ch-13-03.

4.4 Single- versus double-sided loading at EX02

The closed form of section 4.1 assumes that a truck is always waiting. With nine trucks on EX02 that is barely true. EX02's free truck cycle is 1564.1 s: 1397.1 s of travel, dumping and spotting at C01 (from haul_cycle.compute_cycle) plus 35 s Spotting and 132 s loading at EX02 — the same free cycle as ch-01-05. With the shovel time per truck of section 4.1:

MFsingle=9×1771564.1=1.02,MFdouble=9×148.51564.1=0.85

Single-sided, the trucks are just sufficient: MF = 1 at 8.84 trucks. Double-sided, EX02 would need 10.53 trucks to stay busy. The face simulation of LAB-13-01-02 (simulate_face, one shovel in a closed loop, C01 folded into the travel time) confirms it: deterministically EX02 loads 4473 t/h single-sided and 4583 t/h double-sided — 2.5 % more, not 19.2 %. The trucks, not the side change, limit EX02 with nine trucks.

With variability (gamma-distributed spot, pass and travel times, CVs 0.2, 0.1 and 0.1 [A]; seeds 1–10; one-hour warm-up) both modes lose output, and the gap stays small at nine trucks:

FIG-13-01-02 shows the whole sweep from five to fourteen trucks.

Three readings matter for decisions:

  1. Double-sided loading pays when the shovel is the constraint. At nine trucks it adds 127 t/h; at eleven, 642 t/h. Its value depends on the trucks, and the trucks depend on the plan.
  2. Single-sided loading saturates at ten trucks. Above that every added truck only lengthens the queue at EX02-Q — from 215 s to 381 s per truck when the eleventh is added — for 24 t/h.
  3. Double-sided loading moves waiting from the trucks to the shovel. At nine trucks the trucks wait 18.5 s instead of 127.3 s, and the shovel waits 34.3 s instead of 11.1 s per truck. The same tonnes cost less truck time.

Double-sided loading is not free. It needs a second spot with its own Cusp point, a floor kept clean on both sides, reversing paths that stay out of the swing envelope and a Shovel Operator who loads alternately to left and right (ch-13-02, ch-13-08). The model knows none of this; it prices only the time.

The ore shovels are shovel-limited single-sided; the waste shovels are truck-limited, and double-sided loading adds nothing to them deterministically. That is the face view. The fleet view adds the destinations: EX01 and EX02 share C01, which accepts 40 tips per hour with its single tipping bay (ch-10-02), 8800 t/h of ore. The two ore shovels together already exceed that single-sided (2 × 4473 t/h in the face model). A faster EX02 at the baseline allocation therefore lengthens the queue at C01-Q rather than adding ore — the reason ch-14-04 found that lost loading time at EX02 is not lost production one for one. Double-sided loading at the ore faces becomes valuable when C01 is not the limit (a second tipping bay, ore to a stockpile [A]) or when trucks are moved from ore to waste and the ore shovels must hold their output with fewer trucks.

4.6 Idle and queue time together

The deterministic face model never shows idle time and queue time at once: with nine trucks single-sided the shovel is never idle and each truck waits 28.9 s. The seeded model shows both: 11.1 s of shovel idle time and 127.3 s of queue per truck. ch-01-05 explained why: idle time cannot be stored. For the control room this means that a shovel reporting idle time while trucks queue is not a contradiction and not necessarily a dispatch fault — it is bunching. The useful question is how much of each there is, and which way the balance moves when a truck is added or removed (section 8.1).

4.7 Time without loading: delays, standby and downtime

Teeth change, face clean-up and operator breaks are often lumped together as "operating delays". In the GMG time usage model they fall into three different categories, and the delay codes follow (delay-codes.yml):

Event at EX02 Code GMG category
Shift change DLY-SO-01 Operating Standby
Shovel Operator's break DLY-SO-02 Operating Standby
Teeth (ground-engaging tool) change, planned DLY-DN-02 [A] (no specific code exists; the handbook uses this one) Downtime
Face clean-up by DZ03, EX02 left without trucks DLY-OD-04 (coding rule) Operating Delay
EX02 relocating DLY-OD-07 Operating Delay

ahs_sim.loading.effective_productivity applies them as intervals in which loading stops completely and that do not overlap. LAB-13-01-01's illustrative shift [A]: 12 h, loading rate 4209 t/h from the seeded face model, shift change 0.5 h, break 0.5 h, teeth change 0.5 h and six clean-ups of 900 s (1.5 h, the on-demand count of ch-14-04):

Qshift=P(T−∑idi)=4209×(12−3.0)=37884 t,3788412=3157 t per shift hour

The clean-ups alone take 12.5 % of the shift. Two cautions apply. First, effective_productivity stops loading for the whole interval. In a real shift a break that falls in time the shovel would have waited for trucks costs less, and ch-14-04's lost loading time (loads delivered fewer × time of one load) is the better measure for clean-ups. Second, the tonnes above are EX02's. Whether the mine loses them depends on C01 (section 4.5).

4.8 Payload corrections feed back into the cycle

A payload report above the site limit (SCN-PAYLOAD-OVER-EX02) or a load visibly short changes the next loads: the Shovel Operator adds a pass, or takes material back. Either costs about one swing cycle, 33 s [A]. On a shovel-limited EX02 one corrected load per hour costs 41 t/h (20.34 loads/h fall to 20.15).

A systematic rule costs more. LAB-13-01-01 uses payload_distribution with the "top_up" rule [A] — add a pass while the shortfall to 220 t is at least half an expected pass, at most five passes — and lets the mean bucket fill vary:

Mean bucket fill [A] Loads with a fifth pass Mean passes Mean payload (t) Shovel time per truck (s) Productivity (t/h)
1.00 0.8 % 4.005 220.3 177.2 4477
0.95 6.6 % 4.066 212.5 179.2 4269
0.90 39.4 % 4.394 217.5 190.0 4121

At a fill of 0.90 two loads in five take a fifth pass, and productivity falls by 356 t/h against full buckets — more than double-sided loading adds at nine trucks. Payload estimation, limits and the overload response belong to ch-13-04; the point here is that the dig, not only the truck exchange, sets the shovel cycle.

4.10 Model limits

The face model has one shovel and a private destination; it ignores the shared crusher, traffic on the haul road, interaction between a departing and an arriving truck beyond the 10 s clearance, payload variation and Spotting failures. Spotting time is one random variable, not a manoeuvre. Delays stop loading completely. All rates are illustrative; none is measured at a real shovel.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation

EX02 loads single-sided with nine trucks. In the seeded face model it loads 4209 t per loading hour, about 19.1 loads. Each truck waits 127.3 s at EX02-Q on average, and the shovel is idle 11.1 s per truck beyond the 45 s exchange. Two trucks at EX02-Q are a normal sight; so is a shovel that occasionally waits.

6.2 Abnormal operation — SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02 (shared timeline for sections 7 and 8)

The scenario registry fixes T+0 s (Shovel Operator requests DZ03), T+120 s (work zone reserved, trucks held) and T+900 s (DZ03 leaves, zone released, loading resumes). From T+60 s to T+900 s EX02 cannot start a Spotting manoeuvre: 840 s, the time of 4.75 loads at 177 s. Because a load in progress is always finished and the queue refills the shovel at once after T+900 s, the loss is between four and five loads depending on where in the shovel cycle the request falls — 4.49 loads on average over every second of the cycle, 794 s of lost loading time, 987 t at EX02. In the seeded runs (request 4 h into the shift) the loss is 4.0 loads on average, between three and five. ch-14-04's on-demand zone from T+120 s to T+900 s held EX02 for 780 s; the two measures agree within one load.

6.3 Degraded operation — double-sided loading falls back to single-sided

EX02 runs double-sided when spillage on the left spot makes it unusable [A]. The Shovel Operator withdraws the left spot; the AMS stops calling trucks to it; EX02 continues single-sided. With nine trucks the seeded face model falls from 4336 t/h to 4209 t/h, 127 t/h, and the queue at EX02-Q rises from 18.5 s to 127.3 s per truck. The degradation is small because EX02 is truck-limited (section 4.4); with eleven trucks the same fallback would cost 642 t/h and the clean-up of the left side would be urgent.

6.4 Abnormal operation — SCN-PAYLOAD-OVER-EX02 as a cycle-time event

At T+0 s payload monitoring on a truck loaded at EX02 reports a payload above the site limit; at T+5 s the FMS records the event and notifies the Shovel Operator (registry). Whatever the response — ch-13-04 treats it — the Shovel Operator adjusts the next loads. If the next three loads are each one pass lighter at the start and topped up at the end [A], each costs one extra swing cycle: 3 × 33 = 99 s of shovel time, more than half a load at 177 s.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations along SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02

  • T+0 s — loading-unit status: EX02 "clean-up requested", cause DLY-OD-04 pending, planned end unknown [A]. The FMS stops sending new trucks only if the planned end is long enough that they would arrive during the zone; otherwise they join the queue. Without a planned end the FMS cannot decide and keeps its assignments (REQ-10-01-004).
  • T+60 s — AMS event spot cleared for the last truck; Spotting not granted for the next one, reason "work zone pending". From here the trucks' waiting is a hold, not a queue.
  • T+120 s — AMS event work zone reserved with the zone ID; the FMS codes trucks held at EX02-Q DLY-OD-04 and EX02's time without trucks DLY-OD-04 (ch-14-04).
  • T+900 s — AMS work zone released; loading-unit status back to loading. The first Spotting grant goes to the first held truck. The FMS must close the codes on this event, not on the planned end.
  • T+935 s — ADS spotted; the exchange of this truck is measured from the release, not from the last load before the zone, otherwise one exchange of more than 800 s pollutes the exchange statistics.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

For each loading unit the AHS Controller sees the state and cause, loads and tonnes in the current hour against the target, the trucks at the Queue point, the shovel's idle time and the trucks' queue time over the last hour, and the loading mode. The pair of hang and queue tells the story:

Over the last hour Likely meaning First question / request
Hang high, queue low Too few trucks for the shovel Can the FMS add a truck without starving another unit?
Queue high, hang low Shovel-limited Is double-sided loading possible? Is a truck better used elsewhere?
Both high Bunching or uneven arrivals Is something upstream (C01, R05, an intersection) releasing trucks in groups?
Exchange longer than usual Spotting problem: floor, spot designation, manoeuvre Ask the Shovel Operator; consider clean-up (ch-13-08)
Load time longer than usual Dig, payload corrections Ask the Shovel Operator; check payload reports (ch-13-04)

8.3 Procedure excerpt — clean-up at EX02 and the hourly target

A. Clean-up (SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02; work-zone steps are ch-14-04 and ch-16-04 and not repeated):

  1. T+0 s — Shovel Operator: request DZ03 by radio and terminal; state the reason (rut, spillage, boulder) and the expected duration.
  2. T+0 s — AHS Controller: enter EX02 "clean-up" with the expected end in the loading-unit status; check that the FMS stops dispatching trucks that would arrive during the zone.
  3. T+60 s — AHS Controller: confirm on the HMI that no Spotting is granted and that the trucks at EX02-Q are shown as held, not queued.
  4. T+120 s to T+900 s: work-zone procedure (ch-14-04).
  5. T+900 s — AHS Controller: release; confirm EX02 back to loading; watch the first exchange.
  6. After the event — AHS Controller: check that the trucks' hold and EX02's time without trucks are coded DLY-OD-04, and that the exchange statistics exclude the zone.

B. Hourly target:

  1. Start of shift — AHS Controller with the Pit Supervisor: take the loading rate per loading hour from the seeded face model for the planned trucks and loading mode (4209 t for EX02 single-sided with nine trucks); scale each clock hour by its planned loading minutes (a break of 30 min halves it: 2105 t).
  2. Each hour: compare actual tonnes; if the shortfall exceeds about one load (220 t) [A], read the hang–queue table of section 8.1 before asking for trucks.
  3. At a change of trucks or loading mode: re-derive the target; do not keep a target computed for another arrangement.

C. Breaks:

  1. Shift plan — Pit Supervisor: stagger breaks of units that can take each other's trucks (EX03 and EX04; EX01 and EX02), and enter each break as planned unavailability with its planned end.
  2. During the break — FMS and AHS Controller: apply the dynamic policy (or an approved plan); do not let trucks park at the Queue point of a unit on break unless no other unit can take them.

8.5 Training and competency implications

For Shovel Operators moving from manned haulage [H]:

  • Still matters: having the first bucket full and staged over the spot when the truck is spotted (the first pass starts with the spot, not after it); keeping the floor clean and calling clean-up early (ch-14-04); keeping the face in a shape that allows short swings; consistent bucket fills (section 4.8).
  • Replaced by system calls: signalling a truck in by horn or lights, waving it back, or telling a driver to stop short. The truck moves only on a Spotting grant for the current spot version and stops on its own sensing (ch-13-08); the operator designates, cancels and confirms on the terminal (ch-13-03).
  • New: reading the HMI's state of each truck; knowing that a cancelled spot sends the truck back through its Cusp point, which costs more time than a slightly imperfect spot (ch-13-02); reporting the shovel's state with a planned end.

For AHS Controllers: the hang–queue table; the difference between held and queued; the coding rules. For Pit Supervisors: targets from a model with stated assumptions, and breaks as part of dispatch.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-13-01-001 [H] The FMS shall derive each loading unit's hourly loading target from a productivity model that states the load time, the truck exchange time and its reading of the spot time, the loading mode, the number of assigned trucks and whether arrival variability is included, and shall scale each hour by its planned loading time. Rationale: a target from the shovel limit alone (4743 t/h) is unreachable for EX02 (4209 t per loading hour, sections 1.1, 4.4). Verification: inspection of the target record; analysis (LAB-13-01-01, LAB-13-01-02).

REQ-13-01-002 [H] The loading-unit status reported to the FMS shall include the loading mode (single- or double-sided) and the number of active loading spots, with the time of each change, in addition to the state, cause, time of entry and planned end of REQ-10-01-003. Rationale: the shovel time per truck changes from 177 s to 148.5 s with the mode and so do the trucks needed (section 4.4). Verification: inspection of the status message; test (a mode change appears in the FMS record).

REQ-13-01-003 [H] For every load the FMS shall record the load start, load complete and spotted events with time stamps, and shall report load time, exchange time and shovel idle time per truck separately for each loading unit and hour; an exchange that contains a work zone, a break or another unavailability shall be excluded from exchange statistics and reported under the unavailability's code. Rationale: exchange and swing are different levers (section 4.3); one exchange of more than 800 s across a clean-up would distort the statistics (section 7.3). Verification: test (synthetic event log with a clean-up); inspection of the shift report.

REQ-13-01-004 [H] Spotting time, including a longer or repeated Spotting manoeuvre, shall be recorded as in-cycle time and not as a delay; the number of Spotting attempts per load shall be recorded separately. Rationale: coding rule of delay-codes.yml (ch-07-05); re-spots are evidence for ch-13-02 / ch-13-08, not a delay. Verification: inspection of coded events.

REQ-13-01-005 [H] Planned loading-unit unavailability (operator break, planned clean-up window, teeth change) shall be entered in the FMS before trucks are dispatched into it, with start and planned end, so that trucks are reassigned or timed to arrive at its end. Rationale: with a planned end the dynamic policy halves the parked truck time of a 30 min break at EX02 (2.49 to 1.27 truck-hours, section 12). Verification: test (LAB-13-01-02 breaks); inspection of the shift plan.

REQ-13-01-006 [H] A change of a loading unit's loading mode shall be approved by the Pit Supervisor and shall take effect in the FMS only together with the AMS's spot set for that mode (ch-13-02). Rationale: the FMS would otherwise size trucks for spots that do not exist. Verification: inspection; test (mode change without spot set is refused).

REQ-13-01-007 [H] Every loading productivity figure in a report shall state whether it is per operating hour or per shift hour, which time usage categories were excluded, and whether it is the loading unit's output or the mine's production. Rationale: EX02's 4475, 4209 and 3157 t/h describe different things (FIG-13-01-03); lost loading time at EX02 is not lost ore while C01 is the bottleneck (section 4.5). Verification: inspection of the report template.

10. Verification and validation

  • Model verification. LAB-13-01-02 checks that the deterministic single-sided face model equals queueing.deterministic_loop with the same service time (4473.3 against 4474.6 t/h, within one load per shift); sim/tests/test_loading.py checks the closed forms. Twelve chapter tests assert every value quoted here.
  • Validation against a site. A site validates the model by measuring load, exchange and idle time per truck from events (REQ-13-01-003) for each loading mode and comparing with the seeded model at the actual truck numbers. A persistent difference in exchange time is a Spotting question (ch-13-08); in load time, a dig or payload question.
  • Acceptance. A loading-productivity report is accepted when its numbers reconcile: tonnes = loads × payload; loading hours × productivity = tonnes; the time usage categories add up to the shift (REQ-01-05-002).

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Treating the shovel as 167 s of service. It hides the clearance and the exchange, the part autonomous loading changes.
  • Comparing match factors with different shovel times. MF 0.96 (ch-01-05) and 1.02 (this chapter) are the same EX02.
  • Deciding double-sided loading from the closed form. +19.2 % assumes a truck is always waiting; at nine trucks the gain is 2.5 % deterministic, 3.0 % seeded.
  • Ignoring the destination. A faster ore shovel behind a saturated C01 adds queue, not ore.
  • No code for a common event. Teeth change has no specific code in delay-codes.yml; this chapter codes a planned change DLY-DN-02 [A] and a broken tooth DLY-DN-01 [A]. A site without such a rule codes the same event differently on every shift.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Adding a truck because the shovel shows idle time while trucks also queue: bunching, not shortage.
  • Coding re-spots as delays, or a clean-up as shovel downtime.
  • Leaving a break out of the FMS, so that trucks park at the Queue point for half an hour.
  • Judging the Shovel Operator against the shovel limit instead of a target from the model.
  • Waving a truck in from the shovel cab; the truck does not see the signal and the habit undermines the spot designation.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — EX02's cycle. tL = 4 × 33 = 132 s; tx = 10 + 35 = 45 s; 3600 × 220 / 177 = 4475 t/h. Double-sided: tx = max(16.5, 35 + 10 − 132) = 16.5 s; 3600 × 220 / 148.5 = 5333 t/h. All [A].

Example 2 — trucks needed. MF = 1 at 1564.1 / 177 = 8.84 trucks single-sided and 1564.1 / 148.5 = 10.53 double-sided. Nine trucks keep a single-sided EX02 busy; double-sided needs one or two more.

Example 3 — the clean-up. Spotting stopped from T+60 s to T+900 s: 840 / 177 = 4.75 loads. Lost: 4 or 5 loads, 4.49 on average, 987 t at EX02; seeded 4.0. With C01 saturated, part of it is recovered by EX01 (ch-14-04).

Example 4 — hourly target. Seeded rate 4209 t per loading hour; an hour with a 30 min break: 4209 × 30 / 60 = 2105 t; an hour with a 15 min clean-up [A]: 4209 × 45 / 60 = 3157 t.

Example 5 — payload correction. One extra pass per hour: (3600 − 33) / 177 = 20.15 loads instead of 20.34; 41 t/h.

Example 6 — breaks at EX03 and EX04 (LAB-13-01-02, fleet model, 30 min each at 10:00). Together: waste lost 2640 t (fixed) or 3960 t (dynamic). Staggered (EX03 at 10:00, EX04 at 10:30): 2640 t (fixed) or 2420 t (dynamic). Staggering pays only when the dispatch policy moves trucks to the working unit: 1540 t, seven loads, against breaks taken together under the same policy. For EX02 alone, 1760 t of ore with both policies — C01 decides — but parked truck time falls from 2.49 to 1.27 truck-hours with the dynamic policy. Losses are against each policy's own undisturbed shift.

15. Key takeaways

  • A shovel's productivity is load time plus exchange plus idle time; with autonomous trucks the exchange — 45 s of 177 s at EX02 single-sided — is the part the loading arrangement can change.
  • The 35 s spot time is read as Cusp point → spotted [A] (section 4.2); the whole modelled manoeuvre, turn round included, would make the exchange 65.5 s. State the reading.
  • Double-sided loading gains 19.2 % only when a truck is always waiting; with nine trucks EX02 is truck-limited and gains 127 t/h, with eleven 642 t/h.
  • Idle time and queue time coexist in a real shift; read them together.
  • Breaks, clean-ups and teeth changes are standby, operating delay and downtime, not one "delay" category.
  • A loading-unit's output is not the mine's production while C01 is the bottleneck.
