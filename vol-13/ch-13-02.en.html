1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

Mid-morning at EX02. DT17, on mission M-20481, has waited at its Cusp point behind and to the right of the shovel, facing away from the face, while DT16 was loaded. When DT16 drove off, the Shovel Operator called DT17 in, and it reversed alongside the shovel towards the loading spot S1. It stops, and the shovel operator terminal shows a line the operator has already seen once for this truck: DT17 not spotted — attempt 2 — S1 v4 — lateral +0.61 m, heading +3.4°. From the cab the truck looks close enough. The floor is covered in spillage from the last loads, and a rut runs across the spot. Two more trucks wait at EX02-Q. Load DT17 where it stands, let it try again, or move the spot? The AHS Controller, who sees the same line on the HMI, asks something else: if the third attempt fails too, is this the truck, localisation on R18, or a floor that a dozer must fix?

The scene raises the questions this chapter answers:

  • What exactly is a loading spot inside the AHS — and its Cusp point, its staging position and its paths — and who may create, move or remove them?
  • Where does a truck stand beside a face shovel, a backhoe or a wheel loader, and why?
  • How is the Cusp point built, so that the next truck can wait there while the loaded truck leaves?
  • Why does a truck refuse to report "spotted" 0.6 m from the spot, and where does that tolerance come from?
  • What happens to a spot when the shovel propels, the map changes or a dozer must work on the floor?

This chapter covers:

  • the loading spot as an object in the AHS — pose, tolerance, Cusp point, staging position, final path, exit path, version — and its relation to the map version of ch-12-08 and ch-12-09;
  • loading spots by loading unit: the side-on spot beside a face shovel, lower-level loading from a backhoe, and a wheel loader working a V; single-sided, double-sided and drive-by loading (FIG-13-02-04);
  • the spot geometry at EX02-L and its valid region (LAB-13-02-01, FIG-13-02-01, FIG-13-02-03);
  • how the Cusp point and the staging position are built: six methods described in vendor patents, the conditions a Cusp point must meet, and the handbook's procedure — hold the relative offset, check, search, refuse (LAB-13-02-02, FIG-13-02-05);
  • the spot tolerance and the acceptance check, the effect of the floor and of the GNSS multipath on R18;
  • the spot life cycle (FIG-13-02-02), the failure modes and the AMS and ADS reactions;
  • SCN-SPOT-FAIL-EX02 through the engineering and operations views, with SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE and SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02 on either side of it.

How a spot is designated physically, the step-by-step Spotting procedure, path planning, the manoeuvres, truck exchange time and what the truck senses belong to ch-13-08 (summary in section 4.10); the truck–shovel messages are ch-13-03. Every tolerance, clearance, range, speed and threshold here is an illustrative assumption [A] computed with ahs_sim 0.27.0; none is a recommended value. Where a vendor document or patent is cited, it is an example [V] of how a supplier has described the problem, not a description of what any product does today.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Specify how a loading spot is represented in the AHS map — pose, tolerance, Cusp point, staging position, final and exit paths, version — and who may create, move or retire it at EX02-L.
  2. Describe the spot geometry for a face shovel (side-on), a backhoe loading from the upper bench and a wheel loader, and the space each of single-sided, double-sided and drive-by loading needs.
  3. Specify how the AMS builds a Cusp point and a staging position from the spot — the conditions, the relative offset, the search and the refusal — and compare the methods published in vendor patents [V].
  4. Analyse spotting failure modes (spot outside the free space, inside the swing envelope, no Cusp point, staging in the exit path, stale spot after shovel movement) and specify the AMS and ADS reactions to each.
  5. Calculate how the medium GNSS multipath risk on R18 (mine.yaml) affects the acceptance check at EX02-L and derive the spot tolerance from the swing envelope and the floor [A].

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Demonstrate how a Shovel Operator places, adjusts and cancels loading spots at EX02, and biases the Cusp point to one side, from the operator interface; and when to cancel a spot rather than accept a poor truck position.
  2. Apply the control room procedure for a truck that repeatedly fails to spot at EX02-L, including when to involve a dozer clean-up or Survey.
  3. Explain why the next truck should wait at the Cusp point, and what on the floor prevents it.
  4. Relate spotting time, re-spot counts and the staging position to EX02 loading productivity in a shift report.
  5. Explain the Shovel Operator's spot-placement trade-offs at EX02 (swing angle, floor condition, face advance, exit path) and their effect on cycle time and spillage.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

EX02 is a manned hydraulic face shovel of the generic HS-FACE class (equipment.yaml): four passes of 55 t, a 33 s swing cycle, 132 s of loading per truck and a class value of 35 s for the truck to reverse into position — read in this handbook as Cusp point → spotted — all [A]. It loads ore for C01 and is served by nine trucks, DT11 to DT19, among them DT17 on M-20481 (ch-01-03, ch-10-01). The trucks arrive along R18, the 206 m link from EX02-Q to EX02-L, limited to 15 km/h and marked in mine.yaml with a medium GNSS multipath risk.

With single-sided loading the shovel waits while the loaded truck leaves and the next one reverses in — the truck exchange (ch-13-01). A published loading-tool guide states it plainly: in single-sided loading "the loading tool generally will need to wait during truck exchange, thus limiting production levels" (SRC-PUB-OEM-LOADING-TOOL-GUIDE-2022 [V]). How long it waits depends first on where the next truck is when the spot becomes free. In the lab model a truck that waits at its Cusp point beside the shovel reverses in 25.8 s; the same truck called from EX02-Q only once the spot is clear adds 60.3 s of travel and a gear change (ch-13-08 section 4.5). And the spot it reverses into is not fixed: the Shovel Operator places it as the face advances, the floor changes and the shovel moves, several times an hour. Four problems depend on getting the spot right.

  1. A shared object between a manned machine and autonomous trucks. The spot is created by a person, checked by the AMS, driven to by the ADS and loaded over by the person again. If they hold different versions of it — a spot moved on the terminal while a truck still reverses to the old one — the truck ends up where nobody expects it. ch-10-07 fixed the rule that a spot change withdraws the path of a truck not yet spotted; this chapter defines the object the rule acts on.
  2. The Cusp point must be built, not assumed. Where the truck turns, where it waits and where the loaded truck leaves are all consequences of the spot. A Cusp point in the exit path forces the next truck to wait further back; one too close to the shovel puts the turn round inside the tail swing.
  3. The floor decides whether a spot is reachable. In the lab model a spilled and rutted floor more than doubles the attempts per load, each re-spot costing about 52 s of shovel time (section 4.6). SCN-SPOT-FAIL-EX02 is the worst form: three failed attempts and a dozer clean-up.
  4. Spots go stale. A spot is valid only for the shovel pose, floor and map it was checked against. When EX02 propels, when a new loading-area definition is activated (SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE) or when DZ03 gets a work zone on the floor (SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02), the old spots — and their Cusp points — must stop being used at once and for every truck.

3. Terminology and foundations

Loading spot (glossary loading-spot). The pose at which a truck stands to be loaded: the target pose of the truck's rear-axle centre, position and heading. Beside a face shovel the truck stands side-on: alongside the shovel, its body tangential to the swing circle at the point where the bucket dumps, its front pointing away from the face [H]. EX02's spots are named S1, S2, … as in ch-10-07.

Radial spot. A spot whose body points straight away from the shovel's centre of rotation. The first edition of this chapter used it as the baseline; it is kept here only as a comparison (section 4.3).

Loading area (glossary loading-area). The area around a loading unit where trucks spot and are loaded; in the AHS map the zone Z-EX02-L of the load node EX02-L (ch-12-08).

Queue point (glossary queue-point). Where a truck waits to enter the loading area; EX02-Q for EX02, 206 m from EX02-L along R18.

Staging position (glossary staging-position) [H]. Where the next truck waits inside the loading area to be called in. It may be the Cusp point itself; when the Cusp point cannot be used for waiting, it lies earlier on the approach.

Cusp point (glossary cusp-point). Where the truck stops travelling forward and starts reversing towards the spot (the handbook's definition, forward → reverse only). The path-planning literature calls every change between forward and reverse a cusp ("the path has cusps if the car shifts into or out of reverse gear", SRC-PUB-REEDS-SHEPP-1990 [F]); the handbook keeps the narrower meaning.

Final path (glossary final-approach) and exit path (glossary exit-path) [H]. The final path is the reversing path from the Cusp point into the spot; the exit path is the loaded truck's forward path from the spot out of the loading area.

Relative offset [H]. The pose of the Cusp point in the spot's own frame — in the model the parameters of the reverse (turn, radius, clothoids, final straight). Holding it means moving the Cusp point with the spot.

Swing angle (glossary spot-offset-angle). The angle between the shovel's dig direction and the line from its centre of rotation to the body centre of a truck at the spot, where the bucket dumps. 90° is beside the shovel.

Spot version, spot life cycle, valid region, free space, swing envelope, spot tolerance and re-spot keep their meanings of the first edition (glossary): a version per issue of a spot; the states proposed, active, occupied, invalidated, retired; the spot poses that pass every check; the usable floor of the map version less the zones on it; the space of the shovel's moving parts — in the model the counterweight's tail-swing circle of 8 m, plus a swing margin of 1.0 m, and a bucket reach of 16 m within which the body centre must lie [A]; the deviations at which a truck reports itself spotted; a further attempt after a failed one.

Lower-level loading (glossary lower-level-loading). A backhoe on the upper bench loading trucks that stand on the floor below. Wheel loader (glossary wheel-loader) and backhoe (glossary excavator) are the other two kinds of loading unit treated here; the reference mine has none of either (section 4.2).

Shovel operator terminal [A]. The device in the shovel cab for designating, moving and cancelling spots, biasing the Cusp point and signalling load complete. equipment.yaml lists it under shovel_devices as SOT, together with the shovel position source SPS, both fitted to EX01–EX04 [A].

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The spot as an object in the AHS map

ch-12-08 records the loading area as a zone of the map version — Z-EX02-L, attached to the load node — and notes that a real map holds spot positions and approach paths inside it, which change as the face advances. Two layers follow from that, with different owners and different rates of change [H]:

  1. The loading-area definition belongs to the map version: the boundary of the usable floor, the Queue point, the entry from R18 and the exit, and the speed limit inside. It changes a few times a week, through the change control of ch-12-09, with Survey's evidence. SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE ends with a new definition being activated.
  2. The spots and everything built from them — Cusp points, staging positions, final and exit paths — are an overlay on that version (glossary map-overlay). They change several times an hour, at the Shovel Operator's request, and the AMS builds and validates them against the active map version and the shovel's current pose. A spot never outlives the map version it was checked against, and a Cusp point never outlives its spot.

The paths are part of the record, not a reference. The AMS builds the Cusp point, the final path and the exit path from the spot and distributes them with the spot version; the ADS follows them with its own local stop and avoidance (human decision on the role split; section 5). Patents of AHS suppliers describe the same arrangement — a management apparatus that sets the switchback (Cusp) point and generates the target travel path (SRC-PUB-PATENT-US10108196, SRC-PUB-PATENT-JP2012113429 [V]) — but they are descriptions of inventions, not of products in service.

4.2 Loading spots by loading unit

The first edition treated only the face shovel, and placed the truck the wrong way round: reversing radially towards the shovel from a Cusp point on the face side. The practice described in the literature is the opposite. At a face shovel "the haul truck turns and stops facing backwards to the back of the shovel, then spots to its loading location beside the shovel" (SRC-PUB-OWENS-2013-SPOTTING [F], a study of manned trucks at rope shovels). This section sets out the three kinds of loading unit and the three ways of feeding them. FIG-13-02-04 draws the illustrative layouts of ahs_sim.spotting.example_layout [A].

Face shovel, truck at the same level (EX01–EX04). The truck stands alongside the shovel, side-on. A supplier's patent defines the loading position as lying "outside of the arc defined by the tail and/or corner of the shovel, but within the reach of the bucket" (SRC-PUB-PATENT-US9157754 [V]); the handbook's valid region is the same idea with assumed radii (section 4.4). For a front shovel loading at its own level a loading-tool guide gives a swing of 60°–90° as a reference value (SRC-PUB-OEM-LOADING-TOOL-GUIDE-2022 [V]). In a manned study the truck bed centre stood on average 21.57 m from the centre of a large rope shovel (variance 3.01 m², 180 GPS tracks at one mine; SRC-PUB-OWENS-2013-SPOTTING [F]) — a measure of manned scatter, not a value for HS-FACE.

Backhoe on the upper bench, truck below (lower-level loading). The backhoe stands on the bench and loads trucks that stand on the floor below its toe. The same guide lists a swing of 30°–60° for this arrangement and states that a "backhoe can be more productive because of reduced cycle times, if lower level loading" ( SRC-PUB-OEM-LOADING-TOOL-GUIDE-2022 [V], reference values). A study of coal backhoes found the excavation cycle shortest "when the dump truck is positioned at an angle of 35-60° to the axis of the excavator", and recommends reversing trucks "to the left and to the right" to shorten the exchange (SRC-PUB-LITVIN-2018-BACKHOE [F], small machines and manned trucks). For the AHS the free space changes shape [A]: the truck's limit is the toe of the bench (a clearance to it), not the tail swing, which turns on the bench above. The illustrative layout puts the backhoe's centre of rotation 4.0 m behind the crest, a 4.5 m bench, the toe 1.6 m beyond the crest, and the truck parallel to the toe with 2.0 m between its side and the toe, its body centre at 40° from the dig direction and 14.9 m from the centre of rotation [A]. A backhoe loading trucks at its own level swings 90°–180° in the same guide [V]; it is not modelled.

Wheel loader. The loader does not swing; it drives a short V between the face and the truck. Trade advice for manned operation is to put the trucks "as close as possible to the spot where the loader is working, at a 30- to 45-degree angle to the loading face", the loader marking the spot by hoisting a full bucket "over the spot where you want the dump body" (SRC-PUB-CE-2020-WHEEL-LOADER [F], construction-scale loaders; transfer to mining-class loaders and autonomous trucks is [A]). The loading-tool guide asks for the truck to be placed "at recommended angle to minimize loader maneuver and cycle time as well as wear" (SRC-PUB-OEM-LOADING-TOOL-GUIDE-2022 [V]). For the AHS two things differ [A]: the spot moves with every few loads as the dig point moves, so designation happens far more often; and the loader's own travel is a moving obstacle next to the truck's final path, so the AMS keeps the loader's V out of the truck's free space. The layout of FIG-13-02-04 (d) places the truck at 40° to the face line and removes two corridors 2.5 m either side of the loader's centre path (back out, drive to the truck's side) [A]. No published source on spotting autonomous trucks at wheel loaders was found (SRC-PUB-WHEEL-LOADER-AHS-SPOTTING [TBV]); a 1994 patent on autonomous haulage already used a wheel loader as the loading resource with a queue and ready / done signals (SRC-PUB-PATENT-US5586030 [V]), and suppliers list both shovels and front-end loaders as the loading tools of their autonomous trucks (SRC-PUB-OEM-AHS-OVERVIEW-WEB [V]).

Single-sided, double-sided and drive-by. Mines load trucks "single side, double side or standard/modified drive-by" (SRC-PUB-EMJ-2015-LOADING-PRODUCTIVITY [F]). They differ in what falls into the exchange and in the space they need:

Mode In the exchange Needs Sources

With double-sided loading "the following truck arrives and starts its maneuver without having to wait for the preceding truck to finish loading", and whether it can be used "is governed by a number of factors; including accessible space, site layout and equipment configurations" (SRC-PUB-MUSTAFFA-2020-LOADING-PRACTICE [F], a construction case study). A supplier patent pursues the drive-by direction for autonomous trucks: "if switchback operation of a work vehicle can be omitted, the cycle time of the work vehicle is shortened" (SRC-PUB-PATENT-US11650589 [V]). ch-13-08 section 4.4 compares the three at EX02-L.

Manned trucks in the same loading area. Rules for manned trucks shape the side and orientation too. A United States federal rule for surface coal mines, for example, requires that dippers, buckets and loading booms "shall not be swung over the cabs of haulage vehicles until the drivers are out of the cabs and in safe locations" (SRC-REG-US-30CFR-77-1607 [F]). It is quoted here only as an example of a regulatory constraint on mixed manned operation; it is not the reference mine's jurisdiction and not a requirement of this handbook.

4.3 Spot geometry at EX02-L

ahs_sim.loading places EX02 at the load node EX02-L, facing along the direction from EX02-Q to EX02-L — 104.04° from east — because mine.yaml gives node positions but no face direction [A]. In EX02's own frame (x towards the face, y to the left) LAB-13-02-01 adds an assumed loading floor: usable from 95 m behind the centre of rotation to 10 m ahead of it, 1 m short of the toe of the face at 11 m, and 45 m to either side [A].

spotting.side_on_spot places S1 on the right at a swing angle of 90° with the body centre 14.4 m from the centre of rotation, the middle of the valid band of section 4.4; the truck lies parallel to the shovel's axis, its front pointing away from the face. The radial comparison. The first edition's spot — radial, rear-axle centre 12.25 m from the centre of rotation, reached by a 30 m straight approach along the R18 direction and a 90° reverse turn — needs 52.4 s from the start of the approach, 32.9 s from its Cusp point, and puts that Cusp point 25.1 m ahead of the shovel, on the face side; the body reaches 35.1 m towards the face. On this chapter's floor it leaves the free space by 25.1 m (LAB-13-02-01). It survives as a comparison: it is what a planner produces if it ignores where the face is.

Double-sided. The mirror spot on the left takes the same 55.5 s. The two approaches turn round behind the shovel over the same floor, so their swept bodies overlap (by 11.3 m in the model); two trucks can stage at the two Cusp points (37.9 m apart) but not turn round at the same time. The AMS sequences them through its reservations (section 4.5).

4.4 The valid region of a spot

A spot is valid when the AMS can serve it [H]; the thresholds are [A]:

  1. Tail swing. No part of the body comes closer to the tail-swing circle than the 1.0 m swing margin — at the spot and over the whole manoeuvre — the margin the truck's reversing monitor uses (SwingEnvelope.intersects; REQ-13-08-001).
  2. Reach. The body centre lies within the bucket's reach, so that the bucket can place material over the middle of the body without the shovel propelling.
  3. Free space. The body, swept over the whole manoeuvre and the exit, stays inside the usable floor of the active map version and outside any zone overlaid on it.
  4. A Cusp point exists. The AMS can build a Cusp point and a final path for it that meet the conditions of section 4.5 (drivable radius, free space, tail swing); otherwise the spot cannot be served however good its own pose is.

For a side-on truck the first two checks act across the truck, towards and away from the shovel, and they do not depend on the swing angle: the inner side lies at the radius less half the body width, so the tail-swing check with its margin needs a radius of at least 8 + 1.0 + 3.8 = 12.8 m, and the reach allows at most 16 m. Below 80° the truck's rear reaches the toe of the face; from 115° on the held Cusp point would send the turn round off the floor, and the AMS's search finds another one; at 140° no candidate fits and the spot is refused (section 4.9). The valid region at EX02-L in this model is thus a band of 12.8 to 16.0 m between 80° and 130° on either side (green in FIG-13-02-01). It depends on the shovel's pose, the floor, the map and the Cusp-point conditions at once, so it cannot be computed once and stored: every proposal is checked against the region of the moment.

Where to put the spot. The lab's illustrative swing model [A] takes the 33 s class cycle at 90° and scales half of it — the loaded and empty swing — with the angle. Each 10° then costs about 1.8 s per pass, 7.3 s per four-pass load. A loading-tool guide gives a similar rule of thumb, "1 second for each 10° increase or decrease in average swing angle", as an estimated rate for reference (SRC-PUB-OEM-LOADING-TOOL-GUIDE-2022 [V]). With a side-on spot and a held Cusp point, the Spotting time does not change with the angle at all: the whole manoeuvre turns with the spot. The conclusion [R]: place the spot at the smallest swing angle the face allows, with a free-space margin for the spillage the next loads will make, and move it as the face advances. Other trade-offs are not in the model [H]: a spot close to the face puts the truck where spillage from the dig falls; a rutted patch costs re-spots (section 4.6); a spot on the side the shovel will advance into is soon stale.

4.5 How the Cusp point, the staging position and the paths are built

This is the heart of the chapter. A spot alone does not tell a truck where to turn, where to wait or where the loaded truck will leave. The AMS builds those from the spot (human decision on the role split: the AMS builds the Cusp point and the final path and distributes them; the ADS follows and keeps its local stop and avoidance) [H].

The conditions a Cusp point must meet. The handbook's conditions [H] (thresholds [A]); FIG-13-08-07 shows them on S1's candidates:

  1. Reachable in reverse. From the Cusp point a reverse path into the spot exists with a radius not below the HT220 minimum of 15 m [A], curvature continuous, ending in a straight (ch-13-08 section 4.3).
  2. Reachable forward. From the Queue point or the staging position a forward path reaches the Cusp point.
  3. Swept body inside the free space. Both paths and the exit stay inside the usable floor, outside the tail swing plus its margin, and outside the loading unit's travel area — for a wheel loader its V, for a shovel the area it will propel into (the "travel area" of SRC-PUB-PATENT-JP2012113429 [V]).
  4. Staging (the lever on exchange time). A truck waiting at the Cusp point must be clear of the loaded truck's exit path — in the model by at least 2.0 m [A]. If not, the staging position moves back along the approach, and the travel from there falls into the exchange. A supplier's patent puts the aim plainly: it is desirable that the next vehicle has already travelled to near the switchback point while the current one is loaded (SRC-PUB-PATENT-JP2012113429 [V]).
  5. Both sides. The left and right Cusp points, final paths and exits do not overlap, or the AMS separates them in time through its reservations.
  6. Floor wear. A truck that stops and steers at the same Cusp point every time cuts a rut there; several Cusp points used in turn (method D) or a slowly moving Cusp point spread it.
  7. People and light vehicles. The Cusp point and the final path are reserved; patrol vehicles and people are kept out of the reserved areas (ch-13-08 section 4.6; the exclusive area of SRC-PUB-PATENT-WO2016051526 [V]).

The handbook's procedure: hold → check → search → refuse [H]. spotting.place_cusp implements it:

  1. Hold the relative offset of the previous spot (method B) — cheap, and it keeps what the operator chose.
  2. Check it against conditions 1–4 (and 5 where double-sided). If it passes, done.
  3. Search (method A): try the candidates of a grid of reverse turns, final straights and radii on the requested side, and take the one with the shortest Cusp-point → spotted time that passes, the smaller swept area on a tie.
  4. Refuse: if nothing passes, there is no Cusp point; the spot is rejected with the reasons, and the Shovel Operator is told (REQ-13-02-012).

4.6 Spot tolerance and the acceptance check

When the reversing leg ends, the ADS compares its estimate of the truck's pose with the spot: the truck is spotted when the deviation along the truck is within ±0.50 m, across it within ±0.50 m and the heading within ±3° [A]. Otherwise it reports not spotted with the three deviations.

Which axis is which. For a side-on spot the axis across the truck points towards and away from the shovel: an error across moves the body towards the tail swing or away from the bucket. An error along the truck moves the body centre along the swing circle: the bucket drops its load nearer the front or the back of the body, which shifts the load distribution (ch-13-04). The first edition, with a radial spot, had it the other way round; its ±0.20 m "along" bound belongs to the radial geometry.

Upper bound from the swing envelope. REQ-13-02-007 requires the tolerance towards the shovel to be smaller than the spot's tail-swing clearance less the 1.0 m swing margin [H]. At S1 that is 2.60 − 1.0 = 1.60 m across, and the reach allows 16.0 − 14.4 = 1.60 m away from the shovel. ±0.50 m across meets the requirement with room: an accepted truck keeps at least 2.10 m to the tail-swing circle with the across error alone, and 1.87 m in the worst corner of the box (along, across and heading together), its body centre within 15.09 m (LAB-13-02-01). At the inner edge of the band, 12.8 m, nothing is left: ±0.50 m would take the body to 0.27 m of the circle in the worst corner, and the requirement is not met — a site places spots inside the band, never on its edge, and records the derivation with the tolerance.

What then sets the tolerance? With the envelope no longer binding at S1, the tolerance is a loading-quality choice [A]: across, the load centred over the body; along, the front-to-rear distribution of ch-13-04. A looser across tolerance buys little on a spilled floor once along and heading dominate (table below).

Lower bound from the truck. A tolerance tighter than the truck's placement scatter produces re-spots on a perfect floor. The lab models the final pose error as Gaussian, with assumed standard deviations per floor [A]: 0.12 m and 0.6° (clean), 0.30 m and 1.5° (spilled), 0.45 m and 2.5° (spilled and rutted). Multipath doubles σ, which matters little against a ±0.50 m tolerance: the protection level of 0.076 m is less than a sixth of it. Two other effects are not irrelevant.

  1. Outliers. One fix in a hundred carries a 1–5 m error. At one fix per second [A], the 25.8 s from the Cusp point to the spot contain 26 fixes, and the probability of at least one outlier is 1−0.9926=0.230. An acceptance check on the single fix at the stop would declare a well-placed truck "not spotted". On the clean floor the lab finds exactly that: with one raw fix 1.0 % of the attempts are false rejects (acceptance 0.990), with the component-wise median of five fixes taken at rest none (acceptance 1.000). On the spilled floor the median lowers the false accepts from 0.0076 to 0.0060 and the false rejects from 0.0163 to 0.0056.
  2. Loss of the fixed solution. In FLOAT the protection level of 1.089 m exceeds twice the tolerance. The truck cannot tell whether it is within ±0.50 m; it must hold (localisation_degraded, ch-13-08) instead of reporting.

The tolerance is therefore not chosen from σ. It is bounded by the swing envelope above and the truck's placement on the floor below, and usable only while the estimate is robust to outliers and its protection level is well inside the tolerance. The handbook rule [A]: acceptance only while the protection level is at most half the position tolerance — 0.25 m at ±0.50 m — which FIXED with medium multipath (0.076 m) meets and FLOAT (1.089 m) does not. Integrity concepts are ch-05-06's; the alert limit for Spotting is a site design decision, not a value this handbook asserts.

4.8 The spot life cycle

Every spot version passes through the same states, held by the AMS [H]; its Cusp point, staging position and paths share them:

  • PROPOSED. Designated by the Shovel Operator (ch-13-08 describes how) or set from the loading plan. Nothing may be driven to it. The AMS runs the checks of section 4.4 and builds the Cusp point by the procedure of section 4.5.
  • ACTIVE. The checks passed and a Cusp point exists; the operator can signal it ready for the next truck (spot signal, ch-10-07).
  • OCCUPIED. One truck is assigned. It stays occupied while that truck approaches, waits at the staging position, is loaded and departs, and returns to active when the truck has cleared the exit.
  • INVALIDATED. Failed a check, no Cusp point, cancelled or stale. Never used again; a truck working to it aborts. A correction is a new version.
  • RETIRED. No longer needed, or invalidated with no truck still referring to it; kept in the log.

The ADS never creates or moves a spot or a Cusp point; it reports spotted, not spotted with deviations, or spot infeasible when its own sensing finds the path blocked. The FMS decides which loading unit a truck serves, never a physical spot.

Cancellation has one limit. A truck already spotted, being loaded or departing is under the bucket: a cancel of its spot is refused, as REQ-10-07-002 requires — the operator loads it where it is, or the controller cancels the truck's leg. A spot whose truck is still approaching, staging or reversing can be cancelled at any time.

Versions only grow at a loading unit, so "newer" is a comparison of two integers. The truck's Spotting state machine (ahs_sim.loading.SpottingMachine) accepts an assignment only with a version newer than any it has worked to: a truck aborted from S1 v4 can be given S2 v5, never S1 v4 again. The lab shows the refusal ("guard failed: spot_version_newer").

4.10 Designation, procedure, paths and sensing (summary of ch-13-08)

The Shovel Operator designates a spot (bucket over it, terminal map, loading plan or an automatic proposal); the AMS builds the Cusp point and the paths; the next truck travels from the Queue point, turns round and waits at the staging position; on the call-in it reverses along a curvature-continuous final path into the spot, ending with a creep, and stops on its own sensing in every phase; on go it leaves along the exit path. ch-13-08 treats all of this, including path planning and the truck exchange; this chapter uses only the outcome — a final pose, compared with the assigned spot version.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation

The Shovel Operator designates S1; the AMS validates it, builds its Cusp point from the held offset and shows it active within seconds. The model needs less: the AMS's new path from where DT17 stopped to S2's Cusp point is 46.7 m forward (19.5 s, after 4.0 s to stop and change to forward), then 4.0 s dwell, 20.5 s reverse and 5.4 s creep — DT17 stops at T+68.5 s in the model, 53.5 s after the new assignment (ch-13-08 section 6.2). The registry keeps T+75 s; the 6.5 s between them is within the precision of an illustrative timeline and is read as slack (holds, a slower creep on the rut). DT17 is never loaded at EX02 in this scenario; it stands empty, inside the loading area, from T+75 s until it gets its path out at T+240 s. The ch-14-04 condition that no truck is at a spot when the zone becomes active is met because the zone has nobody inside only once DT17 has left R18 [A].

6.3 Abnormal operation — SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE

The registry fixes two times: at T+0 s the FMS sets EX02 to relocating; at T+3600 s the AMS activates the new loading-area definition. For the spots [H]: at T+0 s the AMS invalidates every active and occupied spot of EX02 (loading unit relocating) with their Cusp points, withdraws the paths of trucks still approaching or staging (ch-10-07) and retires the spots once no truck refers to them. Until T+3600 s no proposal from EX02 is accepted, because there is no loading-area definition to check it against. Afterwards a proposal still carrying the old map version is rejected (map_version_mismatch in the lab), and the first spot checked against the new version becomes active, its Cusp point built by search because nothing is held for the new face.

A smaller case happens many times a shift: the shovel propels towards the face. The lab's threshold [A] lets a 0.6 m move pass and invalidates both spots after a 6.0 m move; a truck waiting at its Cusp point (DT11 in the lab) goes to ABORTED and waits until the operator designates from the new position. The new spot's Cusp point is the old one moved 6.0 m with it — the held offset passes the checks again, because the face advanced with the shovel.

6.4 Degraded operation

Localisation degraded during the creep. If the RTK solution at EX02-L drops to FLOAT, the protection level (1.089 m) exceeds the acceptance limit of section 4.7. The truck holds with localisation_degraded and reports neither spotted nor not spotted; the controller sees a localisation hold, coded DLY-OD-06 while the truck is held.

Shovel pose or terminal silent. Without fresh shovel-pose reports the AMS cannot detect a stale spot. After a time limit [A] it assigns no truck to EX02's spots and calls no staged truck in; a truck under the bucket follows the link-loss rules of ch-13-03 (SCN-SHVLINK-EX03). Silence alone does not invalidate the spots; it only stops new trucks being driven to them.

Floor degrading slowly. Between clean-ups the acceptance rate falls and the attempts per load rise. Nothing fails; the exchange time grows (section 8.4).

7.3 Interface and timing considerations along SCN-SPOT-FAIL-EX02

  • T−78 s to T+0 s. DT17 works to S1 v4 throughout. The first failure triggers the single automatic re-spot to the same version; the AMS counts it and changes nothing.
  • T+0 s to T+5 s. The not spotted report carries the version and the three deviations; a report without a version would be useless after T+10 s.
  • T+10 s to T+15 s. Cancellation and new designation arrive together. The AMS invalidates S1 v4 (DT17 goes to ABORTED), validates S2 v5, finds its held offset off the floor, searches a Cusp point, plans DT17's path to it and assigns it. The registry's 5 s covers this; DT17 is at rest, and were it moving, the invalidation alone would stop it.
  • T+15 s. DT17 accepts S2 v5, newer than any version it has worked to, although the AMS announced v5 as current with the invalidation.
  • T+75 s to T+90 s. The third failure within 127 s raises the second-level alarm to the AHS Controller; no fourth attempt is sent. The hold at EX02-Q is an AMS action requested by the controller: no truck receives a spot assignment.
  • T+240 s. The work-zone reservation becomes effective only once DT17 has left R18.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

The HMI shows, per loading unit, the spots with version, side, swing angle, status (occupied with the truck's ID, invalidated with the reason) and map version, each with its Cusp point (held or searched), the staging position and whether a truck is waiting there. Each failed attempt appears with truck, version and deviations; attempts per load, Spotting time and exchange time per truck for the last hour are one click away. Re-spot alarm in two levels: not spotted twice to the Shovel Operator (two consecutive reports), repeated failed spot to the AHS Controller (three in 300 s [A]). Further alarms: spot proposal rejected with its reason (including no Cusp point), staging moved back (the Cusp point cannot be used for waiting), spots invalidated — shovel moved, shovel pose not reported. The shovel operator terminal shows the same spot list, the incoming truck and its state, and every validation result in words: "too close to the face", "inside the tail swing", "out of reach", "no Cusp point on this floor".

Cancel or accept? A decision tree for the Shovel Operator [H]:

  1. Truck already spotted or loading? Not a spot question; load it.
  2. Stopped outside the tolerance because of the floor — spillage, a rut, a soft patch where earlier trucks struggled? The next truck will fail too: cancel and move the spot away from the cause; if that fails, ask for a clean-up.
  3. A one-off deflection on a good floor, and the truck's actual pose clear of the tail swing and within reach? Designate a new spot at the truck's pose and load it.
  4. A large heading deviation, the body angled towards the counterweight or away from the bucket? Cancel: a re-spot costs about 52 s, a badly loaded truck costs spillage and an uneven load.

8.3 Procedure excerpts

A. Truck repeatedly fails to spot at EX02-L (SCN-SPOT-FAIL-EX02).

  1. Shovel Operator (T+5 s): on the first-level alert (two consecutive not spotted), read the deviations; the AHS Controller sees the same line. Sideways and in heading twice suggests the floor; stopped short suggests a rut or an obstacle the truck sensed.
  2. Shovel Operator (T+10 s): cancel the spot and designate a new one away from the cause — here one truck width further round (S2 at 120°). Do not accept the truck where it stands when the floor is the cause.
  3. AHS Controller (T+15 s): confirm that S1 v4 is invalidated, that S2 v5 has a Cusp point, and that DT17 holds S2 v5.
  4. AHS Controller (T+80 s to T+90 s): on the second-level alarm, read the three attempts together: a failure at a new spot after two at the old one means the floor. Stop call-ins from EX02-Q, request DZ03 with the Pit Supervisor, tell the Shovel Operator.
  5. Shovel Operator (T+100 s, T+120 s): cancel the remaining spot; request the clean-up (SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02).
  6. AHS Controller (T+240 s): send DT17 back to EX02-Q; the zone becomes effective once it has left R18 (ch-14-04).
  7. Shovel Operator (after release): designate a fresh spot on the cleaned floor; the first truck is the check. If it fails again on a clean floor, ask the controller to involve ADS support [A] or Survey.
  8. Survey (on request): when trucks stop short or offset in the same direction on a clean floor, check the floor and the loading-area definition against the map version (ch-05-01 for datum symptoms).

B. After the shovel moves. The Shovel Operator designates again after every propel, without waiting for a truck to reach an old spot; the AHS Controller checks that the old versions show shovel moved, that no truck is still assigned to them and that the new spots carry a Cusp point; for a relocation, Survey provides the new loading-area definition, and until it is active EX02 has no spots.

C. Placing a good spot (Shovel Operator). The smallest swing angle the terminal accepts, with room for the spillage the next loads will make; not where the last trucks were deflected; not on the side the shovel will advance into; a Cusp point on the side where the floor is clean, biased if needed; in double-sided loading, approaches that do not cross the other truck's exit.

8.5 Training and competency implications

Shovel Operators learn their shovel's valid region, the effect of the swing angle on their swing, why trucks stand side-on and wait at the Cusp point, how to bias the Cusp point and what no Cusp point means, how to read a not spotted line, the cancel-or-accept tree, and that every propel needs new spots. AHS Controllers learn to read three attempts as a pattern, to hold call-ins before the floor gets worse, to tell a localisation hold from a failed spot, and to notice when trucks no longer stage at the Cusp point. Pit Supervisors learn the attempts-per-load and staging indicators; Survey, that stopping short on a clean floor can mean an outdated loading-area definition, and that a loading-area definition must leave room for the turn round, the staging position and the exit.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-13-02-001 [H] The AMS shall hold each loading spot as a versioned record with at least: spot ID, version, loading unit, side, target pose, swing angle and radius, tolerance and its derivation, the Cusp point(s) with their relative offset and the method that produced them, the staging position, the final and exit paths, the map version and the shovel pose it was validated against, the role and time of proposal, its status and its occupant. Rationale: every party must refer to the same issue of the spot and of everything built from it (section 4.1). Verification: inspection of the AMS data model; test (LAB-13-02-01 SpotRecord, LAB-13-02-02)

REQ-13-02-002 [H] Any change of a spot's pose shall create a new spot version; spot versions shall increase monotonically per loading unit, and an invalidated version shall never become active again. Rationale: an edit in place lets a truck reverse to a pose nobody sees any more. Verification: test (LAB-13-02-01 life-cycle tests: new version on move; old version refused)

REQ-13-02-003 [H] Before a proposed spot becomes active, the AMS shall check against the active map version, the zones overlaid on it and the current shovel pose (a) that the truck's body, at the spot and swept over the manoeuvre built for it, stays inside the free space and outside the shovel's tail-swing envelope plus the swing margin, (b) that the body centre lies within the bucket's reach and (c) that the paths respect the minimum turning radius, and shall reject the proposal with the failed checks otherwise. Rationale: a spot outside the valid region is unsafe or unreachable (sections 4.4, 4.9). Verification: test (LAB-13-02-01 failure cases: 75°, 11.3 m, 12.3 m, 16.5 m rejected; 90° at 12.8 m and 14.4 m accepted)

REQ-13-02-004 [H] The AMS shall invalidate every active or occupied spot of a loading unit, with its Cusp points and paths, when the shovel's reported pose has moved or turned beyond configured thresholds [A] since validation, when the map version changes, when a zone is overlaid on the loading area, or when the loading unit is set to relocating; and shall assign no truck to a spot while the shovel's pose reports are older than a configured limit [A]. Rationale: a spot is valid only for the shovel, floor and map it was checked against. Verification: test (LAB-13-02-01 SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE: 0.6 m kept, 6.0 m invalidated; map mismatch rejected)

REQ-13-02-005 [H] Only the Shovel Operator (or the loading plan, as a proposal) shall propose spots; only the AMS shall validate, assign and invalidate them and build their Cusp points and paths; the Shovel Operator and the AHS Controller may cancel them. A cancellation of a spot whose truck is spotted, loading or departing shall be refused. Rationale: one owner per action; a truck under the bucket moves only by a decision of people (ch-10-07). Verification: test (LAB-13-02-01 permission and life-cycle tests); inspection of the terminal and HMI functions

REQ-13-02-006 [H] The ADS shall report a truck spotted only when its estimated pose is within the spot tolerance, the estimate is robust to single-fix outliers (for example a median of several fixes at rest [A]) and the localisation protection level is at most a configured fraction [A] of the position tolerance; otherwise it shall report not spotted with the deviations, or hold when the protection level is too large. Rationale: medium multipath on R18 makes single-fix acceptance reject about one well-placed truck in a hundred (section 4.7). Verification: test (LAB-13-02-01 acceptance Monte Carlo, single versus median); SIL with injected outliers (ch-13-07)

REQ-13-02-007 [H] The spot tolerance in the direction towards the loading unit's centre of rotation — across the truck for a side-on spot, along it for a radial spot — shall be smaller than the tail-swing clearance of the spot's nominal pose less the configured swing margin [A], taking the heading tolerance into account, and the derivation shall be recorded with the tolerance. Rationale: a tolerance larger than the clearance lets an accepted truck stand inside the swing envelope (section 4.6). Verification: analysis (section 4.6 tables, LAB-13-02-01 tolerance_box); inspection of the configuration record

REQ-13-02-008 [H] A truck working to a spot version that is cancelled or invalidated shall abort its approach, come to rest and shall not continue to that version; it shall move again only on a newer assignment. No AMS action on a spot shall override a stop decided by the truck's own sensing. Rationale: fail closed (a principle of the handbook's reference architecture). Verification: test (SpottingMachine transitions; LAB-13-02-01 walkthrough, old version refused)

REQ-13-02-009 [H] The AMS shall alert the Shovel Operator after a configured number of consecutive not-spotted reports [A] (two here), and shall raise a repeated-failed-spot alarm to the AHS Controller when a configured number of failed attempts [A] (three here) occur at one loading unit within a configured window [A] (300 s), showing each attempt's truck, version and deviations. Rationale: two failures call for a look at the spot; three attempts read together reveal the floor (section 8.3 A; the two-level alarm is a handbook convention). Verification: test (LAB-13-02-01 walkthrough: alert at T+5 s, alarm at T+80 s); inspection of the HMI and the terminal

REQ-13-02-010 [H] The AMS shall log every spot transition with time, role, version and reason, and the FMS shift report shall show Spotting time, attempts per load and the share of exchanges with the next truck waiting at the Cusp point per loading unit. Rationale: re-spots and staging travel are in-cycle time and invisible in delay codes (section 8.4). Verification: inspection of logs and the shift report

REQ-13-02-011 [H] After a failed attempt the ADS may re-spot automatically at most once to the same spot version [A]; further attempts shall follow a new decision by the Shovel Operator. Rationale: repeated automatic attempts rut the floor further and keep the shovel idle. Verification: test (SIL, ch-13-07); inspection of the ADS configuration

REQ-13-02-012 [H] For every spot the AMS shall build the Cusp point and the final path by first applying the relative offset held from the previous spot of the loading unit (or set by the Shovel Operator), checking it against the free space, the swing envelope with its margin, the loading unit's travel area and the minimum turning radius, then searching alternatives when the held offset fails; when no Cusp point passes, the AMS shall reject the spot with the reasons and tell the Shovel Operator. The method used shall be recorded with the spot version. Rationale: the Cusp point follows from the spot, the floor and the unit, and a spot no path reaches must not become active (section 4.5). Verification: test (LAB-13-02-02: held at S1 and after a 6 m propel; searched at 120°; refused at 140°)

REQ-13-02-013 [H] The AMS shall let the next truck wait at the Cusp point only when its body there is at least a configured gap [A] (2.0 m here) from the body swept along the loaded truck's exit path; otherwise it shall place the staging position earlier on the approach and show the staging position on the HMI. Rationale: waiting at the Cusp point is the largest lever on single-sided exchange time, and a waiting truck in the exit path is a collision course (sections 4.5, 8.4). Verification: test (LAB-13-02-02: straight exit refused at 0.75 m, 15° and 30° S-curves accepted); inspection of the HMI

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Spotting a face-shovel truck radially, with its Cusp point on the face side: it needs floor where the face is.
  • Storing spots as map-version features, so that every adjustment needs a change set — or outside any map version, so that nothing checks them against the floor.
  • Editing a spot in place instead of issuing a new version.
  • Treating the Cusp point as given rather than built — and so never checking it against the exit path.
  • Checking the tail-swing envelope at the spot only, not over the whole manoeuvre including the turn round; or with a smaller margin than the truck's monitor uses.
  • Setting the tolerance from the GNSS σ instead of from the envelope and the floor; or reusing a radial bound for a side-on spot.
  • Accepting on one fix; accepting in FLOAT.
  • Letting the AMS "accept" a truck centrally instead of issuing a new spot at the truck's pose.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Accepting a poor truck position when the floor is the cause, so the next truck fails in the same place.
  • Loosening the tolerance to stop re-spots instead of cleaning the floor.
  • Leaving a spot at a large swing angle as the face advances, and paying for it in every swing.
  • Letting spillage build up on the exit, so that trucks can no longer wait at the Cusp point.
  • Propelling the shovel and waiting for the trucks to find out.
  • Treating re-spots and staging travel as invisible because they carry no delay code.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — The two Spotting times. 55.5 s from the entry: 25.6 s forward round to the Cusp point, 4.0 s dwell, 20.4 s reverse, 5.4 s creep. From the Cusp point 20.4+5.4=25.8 s, the part the handbook compares with the 35 s class value [A]. Single-sided exchange with the truck waiting at the Cusp point: 10+25.8=35.8 s → 4,719 t/h; with the class value, 10+35=45 s → 4,475 t/h (productivity_tph).

Example 2 — The valid radius band and the tolerance. A side-on body's inner side lies at r−3.8 m from the centre of rotation, clear of the 8 m circle when r≥11.8 m and of the circle plus the 1.0 m margin when r≥12.8 m; the body centre lies within the 16 m reach when r≤16.0 m. At r = 14.4 m the clearance is 2.60 m; REQ-13-02-007 needs the across tolerance towards the shovel below 2.60−1.0=1.60 m, and the reach allows at most 16.0−14.4=1.60 m away from it. ±0.50 m leaves 2.10 m to the circle with the across error alone and 1.87 m in the worst corner with ±0.50 m along and ±3°.

Example 3 — One truck width further round. At the body-centre radius of 14.4 m, a 7.6 m shift along the arc is 7.6/14.4=0.528 rad = 30.2°, so S2 lies at about 120° [A]. The held offset would take the turn round 4.66 m off the floor; the search finds a 60° reverse turn with a 10 m final straight: 25.9 s from the Cusp point, free-space margin 1.81 m. Under the swing model [A] the move costs 4×33×0.5×30/90=22.0 s of swing per truck — acceptable for a few loads, not as the permanent spot.

Example 4 — What staging is worth. If the exit at S1 ran straight back, the waiting truck would stand 0.75 m from it; the staging position moves to the start of the turn round, and every exchange gains the 25.6 s forward and the 4.0 s gear change: 35.8+29.6=65.4 s instead of 35.8 s with a clean floor and no designation time — the 30° S-curve exit is worth about 29.6 s per truck.

15. Key takeaways

  • Beside a face shovel the truck stands side-on: it turns round behind the shovel, stops at the Cusp point facing away from the face, and reverses alongside. The radial spot of the first edition is a comparison only.
  • A loading spot is a versioned overlay on the map version: a pose, its tolerance, its Cusp point, staging position and paths, the map version and shovel pose it was checked against, and its status.
  • The AMS builds the Cusp point: hold the relative offset, check, search, refuse. The conditions are reverse reachability, free space, tail swing with its margin, the unit's travel area and — the lever on exchange time — a staging position clear of the exit path.
  • In the model the valid region at EX02-L is 12.8–16.0 m and 80°–130°: tail swing with its margin and reach set the radius, the face and the Cusp point the angle. S1 sits at 90° and 14.4 m.
  • The side-on band is 3.2 m wide; the tolerance across the truck is bounded by the swing envelope (1.60 m at S1) and in practice set by loading quality; ±0.50 m in the model. The floor is the lever.
  • Medium multipath on R18 matters through outliers and FIXED loss, not through σ: accept on a robust estimate with a protection-level gate.
  • Two consecutive failures alert the Shovel Operator, three in 300 s the AHS Controller; then the floor is the suspect: hold, clean up, re-spot.
