1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

A rain shower crosses the pit in the middle of a DAY shift. Five minutes later DT17, loaded with 220 t on mission M-20481, pulls away from a hold at the ramp toe P2 and starts up R05. On the AHS Controller's screen an event appears: DT17 — wheel slip — R05. The truck does not stop. It accelerates more slowly than usual, needs about 28 s instead of 12 s to reach its climbing speed, and then climbs at the same 11.4 km/h as on a dry day. Twenty seconds later the controller has applied a speed restriction on R05 for descending trucks. A quarter of an hour after the first drops the Pit Supervisor is on the radio: "Is R05 still good, or do we send everyone round by R24?"

Nothing has failed and nobody is hurt, yet the shift is now full of questions that only physics can answer:

  • Why did a loaded truck slip on a ramp it then climbed without trouble?
  • Why restrict the empty trucks going down, when it was a loaded truck going up that slipped?
  • How much longer does an empty truck need to stop on a wet 9 % ramp, and how fast may it go?
  • If DT17 had to stop on the ramp now — for a fire, a remote stop or a person — would it stay where it stopped?
  • Who decides that the ramp is wet, who checks it, and who says it is dry again?

This chapter covers:

  • the traction limit: how adhesion and the load on the driven axle cap the force a truck can put on the road, and where a climb stops being power-limited and becomes traction-limited (wheel slip);
  • the braking chain — electric retarding, service brakes, secondary brake, parking brake — and who commands each;
  • stopping distance on grade in the time-stepped model of ahs_sim.dynamics, compared with the model of ch-04-05, and the direction-dependent speed limits that follow from it;
  • holding on grade, including where DT17 stops and whether it stays there in SCN-FIRE-DT17;
  • a wet-ramp decision tree for SCN-TRACTION-R05: who restricts, who inspects, who declares the surface and who reopens.

Every adhesion coefficient, brake force, retarding value, delay and verified-clear distance in this chapter is an illustrative assumption [A] (module defaults of ahs_sim.dynamics). None is braking-performance data of any machine, none is a measured friction value of any road and none is a recommended setting.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Calculate the traction-limited tractive effort of DT17 on R05 from drive-axle load and an assumed adhesion coefficient [A], and identify when wheel slip rather than engine power limits the climb.
  2. Model the braking chain — electric retarding, service brakes, secondary and parking brakes — and compute stopping distance on the R05 descent as a function of speed, mass, grade, adhesion and reaction delay.
  3. Specify direction-dependent ADS speed limits for R05 such that the modelled stopping distance stays within the distance the ADS can verify as clear [A], with grade and surface condition as explicit inputs.
  4. Compare retarding demand and braking energy for an empty descent of R05 and of R24, and identify on which ramp and in which load state the descent speed is retarding-limited [A].

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Explain to AHS Controllers why empty trucks descend R05 at 40 km/h (the site speed limit and the equipment class's downhill cap, not the retarding capacity), and why a wet ramp (SCN-TRACTION-R05) can lead to lower speed limits or a temporary ramp closure.
  2. Apply a reduced-traction decision tree for R05: who requests a speed restriction, who inspects the surface, what evidence is needed and who authorises reopening.
  3. Brief the Emergency Response Team on why DT17 stopped where it did in SCN-FIRE-DT17, relating the stop location to grade, braking and parking-brake holding on the ramp.
  4. Decide, from the traction and braking comparison, when to divert traffic from a wet R05 to the gentler R24, and which speed restrictions must still be applied on R24.
  5. Record wheel-slip reports and overspeed interventions with consistent delay codes and segment IDs so that recurring road-condition problems can be traced to R05, R24 or R03.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

ch-07-01 explained why a loaded HT220 climbs R05 at 11.4 km/h: the climb is power-limited. Wheel power, 80 % of 1800 kW [A], balances grade and rolling resistance at that speed. That explanation silently assumes that the tyres can put the whole force on the road. On a dry ramp they can, with a wide margin. On a wet one the margin shrinks, and on a slippery patch it disappears.

Three facts about the reference mine make traction and braking a daily operational question rather than a design-office one.

Every loaded haul climbs. The pit floor is at RL 300, C01 at RL 424 and D01 at RL 445 (mine.yaml). Loaded trucks climb R05 (8.99 %, 1339.6 m) or the west ramp R24 (6.33 %, 1425.0 m) and empty trucks come down them. The braking demand on the ramps falls on empty trucks descending at up to 40 km/h; the traction demand falls on loaded trucks climbing and, above all, on loaded trucks starting from rest on the ramp.

The ODD already draws a line. The illustrative operational design domain of ch-04-02 allows 10 % on a dry road and 8 % on a wet one [A]. Wet R05 is outside; wet R24 and R09 are inside. That line is a rule; this chapter supplies the mechanics behind it — and what happens in the minutes between rain starting and someone declaring the road wet.

Stopping on a ramp is not the end of the problem. ch-04-07 stopped DT17 at SP1 on mid-ramp R05 when it caught fire. ch-04-05 stopped fourteen trucks in AA-North with one remote stop, two of them on R05. Neither chapter asked whether a truck that has stopped on a 9 % grade, loaded, on a wet surface, will stay there once the retarder has faded and only the parking brake is left.

Watering raises the same question: ch-14-05 left the traction side of watering to Volume 07; water lowers adhesion [F] (SRC-PUB-NSW-SB18-09).

3. Terminology and foundations

Adhesion and adhesion coefficient. Adhesion is the friction available between tyre and road as a fraction of the weight on the wheels (glossary, ch-04-05). The adhesion coefficient μ is that fraction. This chapter uses the teaching table of ahs_sim.dynamics.ADHESION [A]: dry 0.45, damp 0.30, wet 0.20, wet and slippery 0.12. ch-04-05 used its own lab-local table (dry 0.5, wet 0.3, spillage 0.2 [A]). The two tables are both illustrative; they are not two measurements that disagree, and section 4.5 shows how much the difference matters. For the illustrative ODD of ch-04-02 (dry / wet only), damp counts as wet.

Tractive effort and Rimpull. Rimpull is the propulsion force available at the rim of the driven wheels (glossary); ch-07-01 models it as min(Fmax,ηP/v) with a low-speed cap of 900 kN [A]. Tractive effort is the force actually transmitted to the road; it is the smaller of the Rimpull demanded and the traction limit.

Traction limit. The largest force the driven axle can transmit before the tyres slip: adhesion times the load on the driven axle. On HT220 only the rear axle is driven; the module assumes it carries 67 % of the weight loaded and 50 % empty [A].

Wheel slip and traction control. Wheel slip is the state in which the driven wheels turn faster than the truck moves because the demanded force exceeds the traction limit. Traction control is the VCS function that limits drive force to what the road will take and reports the slip event. In the model, traction control is perfect: drive force is clipped to the traction limit and the step is flagged as slip.

Braking chain. The ordered set of devices that slow and hold the truck (FIG-07-02-01): electric retarding (the drive motors act as generators; continuous, speed-dependent, no holding at standstill), service brakes (friction brakes on all wheels), the secondary brake (a fallback when the service circuit fails) and the parking brake (holds the stationary truck). Retarding is the glossary's continuous braking on downhill grades.

Reaction, build-up and braking. The V07 model splits a stop into reaction (demand to brake command; drive cut, nothing brakes yet), build-up (forces rise linearly) and braking (full available force until rest). ch-04-05's latency and actuation delay correspond to the first two but are travelled at constant speed.

Verified-clear distance. The distance ahead the ADS can confirm to be free (perception, Volume 06; granted path, ch-10-04). This chapter uses 50 m [A] only to show how a speed limit follows from it.

Grade sign. Grades are signed in the direction of travel: R05 is +8.99 % for a truck climbing from P2 to P1 and −8.99 % for a truck descending. Rolling resistance always opposes motion.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 Forces on a truck on grade

For a truck of mass m on a grade G with rolling resistance RR, ch-07-01 wrote the resistance to motion as

Fres=mg(G+RR)

Climbing loaded on R05 that is 385 000 kg × 9.81 m/s² × (0.0899 + 0.03) = 453.0 kN. Descending, the same expression is negative: gravity pushes the truck down the ramp and the braking chain must hold it.

The driven axle can transmit at most

Ftrac=μϕmg

where ϕ is the share of the weight on the driven axle (0.67 loaded, 0.50 empty [A]). For the loaded truck the traction limit is 1138.7 kN dry, 759.1 kN damp, 506.1 kN wet and 303.7 kN on a wet, slippery surface (LAB-07-02-02, experiment B1). A steady climb is possible only while Ftrac≥Fres, which gives the critical adhesion

μcrit=G+RRϕ

Mass cancels: a heavier truck needs more force but also presses harder on the road. What matters is the grade, the rolling resistance and how the weight is shared between axles. That is why the loaded truck is better placed than the empty one: 67 % of a loaded truck's weight sits on the driven axle, 50 % of an empty one's.

Ramp, load G+RR ϕ [A] μcrit
R05, loaded 220 t 0.1199 0.67 0.179
R05, empty 0.1199 0.50 0.240
R24, loaded 220 t 0.0933 0.67 0.139
R24, empty 0.0933 0.50 0.187

A loaded truck climbs R05 on dry, damp and wet surfaces but not on a slippery one; the same holds on R24. An empty truck could not climb a wet R05, which production never asks of it.

4.2 The braking chain and who commands it

FIG-07-02-01 shows the chain and the elements that command it.

Electric retarding. The drive motors act as generators and the energy is dissipated in resistor grids [A]. The module models the available retarding force as

Fret(v)=min(Fret,max,Pretv)

with Pret = 2700 kW and Fret,max = 900 kN [A], faded to zero below 0.5 m/s: a motor that does not turn cannot hold a stationary truck.

Service brakes. Friction brakes on all wheels, 1200 kN in total at the wheels, building up over 0.6 s [A]. They add to retarding in a stop and take over as the truck slows below the speed at which retarding fades.

Secondary brake. 600 kN [A], used when the service circuit fails; LAB-07-02-01 can switch it in.

Parking brake. 1100 kN holding force at standstill [A], assumed to act on the driven axle only, so it is limited by adhesion on that axle (section 4.6).

Who commands each. In normal operation the ADS plans speed and stops and sends deceleration demands to the VCS, which blends retarding and service braking. The independent safety function (remote stop SF-01, emergency stop at the machine SF-02; ch-04-04) and onboard systems (the fire safe-state request of ch-04-07) send their demands to the VCS directly, without the ADS. The VCS arbitrates: the strongest active demand wins and no central command can release a stop the vehicle side has decided (ch-01-04 section 4.6, principle 1). Traction control limits drive force to adhesion; the model simply caps braking force at μmg.

Which device takes which share of a stop, how fast each builds up, and how the VCS blends them are design values of the vehicle supplier. Brake systems of earth-moving machinery are the subject of ISO 3450:2011, edition 4, Earth-moving machinery — Wheeled or high-speed rubber-tracked machines — Performance requirements and test procedures for brake systems, whose published scope covers service, secondary and parking brakes [F] (SRC-STD-ISO3450, catalogue level). What that standard requires, and whether and how its requirements apply to an autonomous haul truck and to brake demands from an ADS, is not verified here [TBV] (SRC-PUB-BRAKE-AUTONOMOUS-APPLICABILITY). No performance value in this chapter comes from it.

4.3 Stopping on grade: the time-stepped model

ahs_sim.dynamics.stop_on_grade integrates

meffdvdt=−Fb(t)−mg(G+RR),Fb(t)≤μmg

with meff = 1.05 m (rotating parts, ch-07-01) in steps of 0.01 s. During the reaction delay (0.5 s [A]) Fb = 0 and the drive is cut, so gravity already acts: a descending truck speeds up, a climbing one slows. The retarder then builds up over 0.3 s and the service brakes over 0.6 s [A]; their sum is capped by adhesion on all wheels. The model is a full-braking stop — it uses all the braking the chain and the road allow. In the handbook's stop classes (ch-04-05) that is closest to class I; a class C stop deliberately brakes at a planned deceleration.

LAB-07-02-01, experiment A1, gives for the empty truck descending R05 at 40 km/h:

Surface (μ [A]) Reaction Build-up Braking Total Time Peak deceleration
Dry (0.45) 5.6 m 6.5 m 13.1 m 25.2 m 3.79 s 3.64 m/s²
Damp (0.30) 5.6 m 6.6 m 23.7 m 35.9 m 5.70 s 2.24 m/s²
Wet (0.20) 5.6 m 6.7 m 43.9 m 56.2 m 9.30 s 1.31 m/s²
Wet, slippery (0.12) 5.6 m 6.8 m 109.8 m 122.2 m 20.89 s 0.56 m/s²

Reaction and build-up, 12 m together, hardly change with the surface. The braking phase does: adhesion falls by a factor of 3.75 from dry to slippery, braking distance grows by more than 8. The road decelerates the truck by gμ and gravity takes g(|G|−RR) back: 4.41 − 0.59 m/s² dry, 1.18 − 0.59 m/s² slippery, which with the effective mass leaves the 0.56 m/s² of the table. Grade amplifies poor adhesion — ch-12-05's conclusion, sharpened.

FIG-07-02-02 shows the same effect over speed for level road, R24 (−6.3 %) and R05 (−9.0 %). On a dry road the three grades lie close together; on a slippery one they fan out, and at 40 km/h R05 needs 122.2 m against 84.6 m on R24 and 48.8 m on level road.

The loaded climb is the opposite case. DT17 at 11.44 km/h on +8.99 % stops in 2.4 m dry and 3.0 m slippery: gravity does most of the work, and the problem on the way up is not stopping but staying stopped (section 4.6).

4.4 Direction-dependent speed limits

An ADS speed limit for a segment and direction must keep the stopping distance within what the ADS can verify as clear [H] (ch-10-04 makes the granted path cover it as well). With the verified-clear distance D = 50 m [A], LAB-07-02-01 experiment A4 finds, by bisection on the V07 model, the highest speed that stops within D:

Descending empty Dry Damp Wet Wet, slippery
R05 (−8.99 %), site limit 40 km/h 40.0 40.0 37.5 24.8
R24 (−6.33 %), site limit 40 km/h 40.0 40.0 40.0 30.2

(km/h; the site limit caps the result.) In the climbing direction the loaded truck is power-limited at 11.4 km/h on R05 and 14.7 km/h on R24 and stops within 3 m on any surface in the table, so no stopping-based limit binds. The limits are therefore direction-dependent: a ramp restriction for wet weather concerns the descending lane.

This is why section 1.1 restricted the descending lane: the climbing truck's slip reveals low adhesion, but the trucks going down carry the risk. A single "wet-weather speed" for the whole mine would be too strict on level roads and possibly not strict enough on R05.

The speed limits move the cycle. Restricting R05 to 37.5 km/h in both directions adds 8.0 s to M-20481; restricting it to 20 km/h adds 120.6 s, the value ch-04-02 gave for the same cap under SCN-GNSS-R05 (LAB-07-02-02, experiment B5). Only the empty descent slows; the loaded climb is already below either cap.

4.5 Comparison with the model of ch-04-05

ch-04-05 computed stopping distance with ahs_sim.safety.stopping_distance: latency and actuation delay are travelled at constant speed, followed by braking at a constant deceleration. Its lab-local inputs [A] were 1000 kN of braking force capped by adhesion, latency 1 s, actuation 1 s and a class C target of 1.0 m/s². LAB-07-02-01 experiment A2 calls the same function with the same inputs and reproduces ch-04-05's numbers exactly:

Case ch-04-05 class C ch-04-05 class I (dry / wet / spillage) V07 full braking (dry / damp / wet)
R05 empty, down, 40 km/h 84.0 m 36.5 / 48.4 / 67.1 m 25.2 / 35.9 / 56.2 m
R24 empty, down, 40 km/h 84.0 m 35.7 / 45.8 / 60.0 m 23.5 / 32.2 / 47.2 m
R05 loaded, up, 11.44 km/h 10.7 m 7.7 m (dry) 2.4 / 2.5 / 2.7 m

Both stand. ch-04-05's class C distance is the stop the handbook chooses for a remote stop — controlled, steerable, load kept in the body — and the input for exclusion distances and reservation horizons (ch-10-04). The V07 value is the shortest stop the modelled chain and road allow.

Why is V07's full-braking stop shorter than ch-04-05's class I? Experiment A3 walks the V07 stop towards ch-04-05's inputs one change at a time for R05 empty at 40 km/h, dry:

Step Stopping distance
V07 default: reaction 0.5 s, build-up 0.3 / 0.6 s, retarder + 1200 kN, μ 0.45 25.2 m
Reaction 1.0 s 31.9 m
Build-up 1.0 s 33.5 m
Service brakes only, 1000 kN 35.0 m
Adhesion 0.5 33.3 m
ch-04-05 class I (constant speed through latency and actuation) 36.5 m

Most of the difference is in the delays; the last 3.2 m come from ch-04-05 travelling its actuation at the initial speed. Adhesion works the other way (0.5 against 0.45). Delays dominate on dry roads, adhesion on wet ones, as ch-04-05's sensitivity analysis also found. The two adhesion tables are both teaching values; this chapter labels which one each number uses.

4.6 Holding on grade

Once the truck is at rest the retarder no longer helps (it fades to zero below 0.5 m/s) and the holding must come from friction brakes. With the parking brake on the driven axle only, the holding margin is

H=min(Fpark,μϕmg)−mg|G|

(rolling resistance is not counted at standstill). LAB-07-02-01 experiment A5 and LAB-07-02-02 experiment B4 give:

Truck on ramp Downhill force Parking brake margin dry / damp / wet / slippery Service brakes (all wheels), slippery
R05, loaded 339.7 kN 760.3 / 419.4 / 166.4 / −36.0 kN 113.5 kN
R05, empty 145.6 kN 218.6 / 97.2 / 16.3 / −48.5 kN 48.7 kN
R24, loaded 239.0 kN 861.0 / 520.1 / 267.1 / 64.6 kN 214.2 kN
R24, empty 102.4 kN 261.8 / 140.4 / 59.4 / −5.3 kN 91.8 kN

As adhesion falls the limit moves from the brake to the road. On a slippery R05 the parking brake alone holds neither a loaded nor an empty truck; the service brakes on all wheels still do (lab-local addition). On a wet R05 the empty truck's margin is only 16.3 kN. Hence the rule: a truck stopped on a ramp keeps its service brakes applied until the parking brake is confirmed to hold (REQ-07-02-004), and stopped must be followed by holding on the HMI.

4.7 Traction-limited climbing and wheel slip

FIG-07-02-03 places R05 and R24 on the grade–adhesion plane. Below each line the truck cannot climb at steady speed; above it power sets the speed (ch-07-01). The Rimpull cap of 900 kN would bind only at 20.8 % (loaded).

The steady climb is half the story. Starting from rest at the comfort acceleration of 0.6 m/s² [A] needs 695.6 kN on R05 and 594.9 kN on R24 (experiment B3). The wet traction limit, 506.1 kN, lies between the steady resistance (453.0 kN) and the start-up demand: a loaded truck climbs a wet R05 at normal speed yet slips at every start. Climb of R05 from rest (LAB-07-02-02):

Climb of R05 from rest, loaded Time on R05 Wheel slip 99 % of climbing speed reached
Dry or damp 424.6 s 0.0 s after 12.4 s, 29.6 m
Wet (μ 0.20) 433.7 s 21.7 s after 28.0 s, 50.2 m
Wet, slippery (μ 0.12) cannot climb — —

On R24, wet, a start costs 7.5 s of slip and 1.7 s on the ramp (354.9 s against 353.2 s). The time is small; the information is not: a slip report from a loaded truck on R05 means adhesion is only 53 kN of traction away from making the climb impossible. A truck that stalls on a slippery patch is exactly the case the parking brake cannot hold.

In the adhesion sweep (experiment B2) R05 turns traction-limited between μ 0.18 and 0.16, R24 between 0.14 and 0.12. In the band between — 0.14 to 0.16 in the sweep — R24 still climbs and R05 does not: the physical case for diverting.

4.8 Retarding demand on the descent

On a descent the retarder must absorb the net downhill force mg(|G|−RR). Experiment A6:

Descent Speed Net downhill force Retarding available at that speed Speed at which retarding alone balances Power Energy per descent
R05, empty 40.0 km/h 97.0 kN 243.0 kN 100.2 km/h 1078.1 kW 130.0 MJ
R24, empty 40.0 km/h 53.9 kN 243.0 kN 180.4 km/h 598.6 kW 76.8 MJ
R05, loaded (bounding) 30.0 km/h 226.4 kN 324.0 kN 42.9 km/h 1886.7 kW 303.3 MJ
R24, loaded (bounding) 30.0 km/h 125.7 kN 324.0 kN 77.3 km/h 1047.6 kW 179.1 MJ

With the module's retarding curve [A] the empty descent is not retarding-limited on either ramp: an empty truck on R05 could hold up to about 100 km/h on retarding alone. Its 40 km/h comes from the site speed limit and from the equipment class's downhill cap (equipment.yaml, 40 km/h empty, 30 km/h loaded [A]), which the steady-state model of ch-01-01 labels as the retarding-limited speed, although it is not derived from the retarding capacity. The only case that comes close to a retarding limit is the loaded descent of R05 — 42.9 km/h — which does not occur in production at the reference mine and is a bounding case (as in ch-04-05). The descent speed of empty trucks is therefore set by the speed limit and, on a wet surface, by stopping distance. The retarding curve becomes the binding limit only if retarding capacity is lost (a fault in the grids or a derate) or on a grade much steeper than R05 [A].

The power column is a thermal load on the grids: 1078.1 kW for two minutes on every empty descent of R05; R24 needs 598.6 kW for 128.2 s. What the energy per descent costs or could return is ch-09-01's subject.

4.9 Trade-offs and model limits

The models ignore load transfer between axles when braking and climbing [A], imperfect anti-lock and traction control, adhesion varying along the road, with speed and with tyres [TBV], and the thermal rating of the retarder. They show which limit binds, how limits move with grade and surface, and in which direction a decision must err.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation

On dry R05 loaded trucks climb at 11.4 km/h with more than 680 kN of traction margin and start without slip; empty trucks descend at 40 km/h with a 25.2 m full-braking stop against the 50 m verified-clear distance [A].

6.2 Degraded operation — SCN-TRACTION-R05 (shared timeline for sections 7 and 8)

Initial state [A]. Normal production; loaded trucks climbing and empty trucks descending R05. DT17, loaded on M-20481, is held at P2 waiting for the next R05 block (ch-10-04), so it will start from rest.

Timeline. The registered key events are at T+0, 300, 320, 900 and 3600 s. Intermediate events marked [A] are added by this chapter; physical values come from LAB-07-02-01 and LAB-07-02-02.

Time Event Element What crosses a boundary
T+0 s Rain starts; R05 surface becomes wet People (weather) Nothing yet: the surface state in the AMS is still dry
T+300 s DT17 starts from P2; demanded 695.6 kN, traction control limits drive force to 506.1 kN; wheel slip reported DT17 (VCS, ADS) Slip event with segment R05 and position to the AMS
T+302 s [A] Slip event on the HMI, grouped by segment AMS Event to the AHS Controller
T+320 s Interim restriction on R05 for descending trucks on the slip report, 20 km/h [A], pending the wet declaration; Pit Supervisor informed AHS Controller, AMS Restriction to every truck on and approaching R05
T+328 s [A] DT17 reaches climbing speed after 21.7 s of slip; continues at 11.4 km/h DT17 —
T+900 s Pit Supervisor declares R05 wet (sets the surface wet): R05 leaves the illustrative ODD and traffic reroutes over R24; inspection arranged Pit Supervisor Surface state to the AMS
T+905 s [A] ODD check: wet R05 (9.0 %) outside; R05 closed to new routes; EX01, EX02 rerouted over R09, R24 and R25 AMS, FMS New routes; cycle times to the FMS
T+3600 s Surface inspected; restriction lifted or kept on the evidence Pit Supervisor Surface state; reopening recorded

T+0 s to T+300 s — the gap. The road is wet and the AMS still says dry. At 40 km/h on wet R05 the empty full-braking stop is 56.2 m, longer than the 50 m verified-clear distance [A]. Hence REQ-07-02-002 (the ADS acts on its own evidence), and a site may set the surface wet on rainfall alone rather than wait for a slip.

T+300 s — the slip. DT17 needs more force than the wet road can take because it starts, not because it climbs. Traction control clips drive force to 506.1 kN; DT17 accelerates at 0.13 m/s² instead of 0.6 m/s². This is the first hard evidence of low adhesion.

T+320 s — the interim restriction. 20 km/h [A] for descending trucks, pre-computed to keep the stop within 50 m even on a slippery surface (24.8 km/h, section 4.4); it costs 120.6 s per M-20481 cycle. It is a reaction to evidence, not the declaration that the road is wet.

T+900 s — the declaration and the diversion. The Pit Supervisor declares R05 wet. ch-04-02's illustrative ODD (wet grade limit 8 %) then takes R05 out: the choice is R24, hold or stop, never "run R05 carefully" (ch-04-02 section 4.1). The AMS closes R05 to new routes and the FMS recomputes: M-20481 via R24 takes 1800.1 s instead of 1564.1 s (+236.0 s, ch-01-03). If, as in ch-04-02's rain condition, reduced visibility also caps eight roads at 30 km/h, the cycle is 2048.7 s (+484.6 s). R24 itself stays inside the wet ODD; mechanically, loaded trucks still climb it at 14.7 km/h with μ 0.20 above its critical 0.139, and starts cost 7.5 s of slip each.

T+3600 s — inspection. The Pit Supervisor inspects the ramp and decides on evidence (section 8.3): restriction lifted and surface set dry, or kept.

6.3 Abnormal operation — SCN-FIRE-DT17: where DT17 stops and whether it stays

In ch-04-07: alarm at mid-ramp, 669.8 m below P1, DT17 climbing loaded at 3.18 m/s; controlled stop from T+5 s; at rest at SP1, 21.4 m beyond the alarm point (648.4 m below P1), at T+7.4 s, with 400 kN and 1 s actuation [A]. LAB-07-02-01 reproduces both numbers, and they stay the reference for SP1, the zone and the ERT approach (ch-16-06).

The V07 model adds detail without moving SP1 materially: 15.9 m under power until T+5 s, then, with the drive cut, gravity alone decelerates the truck at about 1.1 m/s² and it is at rest 1.4 m later, at T+5.8 s (17.3 m instead of 21.4 m). On a loaded climb stopping at once costs almost no distance — one reason ch-04-07 ranked SP1 first.

The fire chapters did not compute the holding. At SP1, 339.7 kN pull DT17 downhill. The parking-brake margin is 760.3 kN dry, 166.4 kN wet and −36.0 kN on a slippery patch, where only the service brakes on all wheels (113.5 kN) would hold. For the ERT briefing: DT17 stopped where it did because stopping at once on a loaded climb is short; and before anyone approaches, the HMI must show holding confirmed as well as stopped and safe state latched. ch-04-07's isolation condition (safe state latched, park brake applied) is not changed here; this chapter explains why "applied" must mean "holding".

6.4 Abnormal operation — SCN-RSTOP-AANORTH on the ramp

ch-04-05 stopped fourteen trucks with a class C remote stop. Two of them were on R05:

Truck Situation ch-04-05 class C (after delivery) V07 full braking, dry / wet
DT14 Loaded, climbing at 11.44 km/h 7.5 m 2.4 / 2.7 m
DT16 Empty, descending at 40 km/h 72.8 m 25.2 / 56.2 m
(bounding) Loaded, descending at 30 km/h 43.1 m 17.7 / 34.2 m

Uphill and loaded the grade stops the truck; downhill and empty the brakes and the road must. Class C is longer by design (ch-04-05 section 4.5); the V07 column shows the margin it gives up, and that on a wet ramp even the full-braking stop of DT16 reaches 56.2 m. After the stop DT16 has a wet parking margin of only 16.3 kN.

6.5 Degraded operation — SCN-WATER-WT02 on R03

R03 is almost level (+0.43 % from X to Y), so after WT02 has watered it traction is no issue, but stopping is: the empty truck at 50 km/h needs 30.4 m dry, 53.9 m wet and 79.8 m slippery; the loaded truck at 46.9 km/h 30.3, 46.9 and 66.9 m (experiment A1). Watering a level road stretches stops; watering a ramp also threatens climbs — the mechanics behind ch-14-05's rule that ramp watering is agreed with the owner of the wet-road restrictions.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

Walk the SCN-TRACTION-R05 timeline and ask what the design had to decide in advance.

T+0 s — what the vehicle knows first. Rain is not a vehicle input; slip and braking response are. Adapting only to the declared surface leaves the T+0 to T+900 s gap open, so the ADS takes the more restrictive of the declared surface and its own evidence (REQ-07-02-002): slip during acceleration, achieved against commanded deceleration, traction-control activity. How well such estimates work on haul roads is [TBV] (SRC-PUB-WET-ROAD-TRACTION).

T+300 s — clip, do not stop. Traction control limits force to adhesion and reports. It does not stop the truck: a truck that stops on a wet ramp to "be safe" creates a holding problem and a restart that will slip again. Stopping is right only when the truck cannot progress (traction below steady resistance), and then the VCS must hold on the service brakes (REQ-07-02-004).

T+320 s — a pre-computed restriction. Computed for the worst surface the site plans for and stored, so the controller applies a number rather than invents one; it trades 120.6 s per cycle against not knowing the adhesion.

T+900 s — one ODD rule, applied by the AMS. When the surface is declared wet, the AMS applies the ODD automatically: R05 closes to new routes, R24 and R09 stay open. The design decision is that the Pit Supervisor declares the surface and the AMS draws the consequences, so that the ODD cannot be argued away under production pressure.

Direction-dependent limits and checked holding. Limits are stored per segment and direction with grade, load and surface as inputs. "Parking brake applied" is a command, "holding" a result; the design reports the second.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

  • Slip events (VCS → ADS → AMS). Truck, segment, position, direction, load state, demanded and achieved force, duration (REQ-07-02-003). DT17's event reaches the HMI at T+302 s [A]; grouping by segment makes two slips on R05 one road problem, not two truck problems.
  • Restriction propagation. At T+320 s the restriction must reach every truck before it starts down R05; a truck already descending slows at 0.8 m/s² [A], about 7 s from 40 to 20 km/h.
  • Surface state as map data. Versioned like other overlays (ch-12-09) with author, time and review time; a state past its review time is shown as unknown and treated as wet [H].
  • Reaction delay budget. 0.5 s [A] in the V07 model, 1 s for ch-04-05's remote-stop path; a speed limit must use the slowest path that can demand a stop there.
  • Granted-path length. ch-10-04 sizes the reservation horizon from ch-04-05's class C distance (84.0 m at 40 km/h). On a slippery descent the full-braking stop alone is 122.2 m: if such surfaces are within the ODD, either the horizon or the speed must change with the surface state.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

During SCN-TRACTION-R05 the HMI [H] shows:

  • Slip events on the map at their position, with truck, direction and load state, grouped per segment. At T+302 s: DT17 — wheel slip — R05, at P2, climbing, loaded; the duration (21.7 s) is added when the slip ends.
  • Surface state per segment (dry, wet, unknown) with who set it and when (ch-04-02's conditions panel).
  • Restriction list: R05, descending, 20 km/h, reason slip report, applied by the AHS Controller at T+320 s, review time.
  • ODD layer: after T+900 s R05 shown outside with reason wet, 9.0 % > 8 %.
  • Truck state for any truck stopped on a ramp: stopped, then holding confirmed or an alarm holding not confirmed — service brakes applied.
  • Overspeed interventions: when the VCS brakes a truck that exceeds its cap, an event with segment and speed.

Decision tree — reduced traction on R05 [H]:

  1. Is there evidence of low adhesion on R05 (slip event, rain observed, watering error, overspeed interventions on the descent)? No → normal operation. Yes → step 2.
  2. AHS Controller applies the pre-computed interim restriction for descending trucks on R05 and informs the Pit Supervisor. → step 3.
  3. Has a truck stalled or failed to hold on R05? Yes → stop traffic onto R05 (closure), keep the stalled truck on its service brakes, call the Pit Supervisor to the ramp; any approach under positive communication. No → step 4.
  4. Pit Supervisor decides the surface state. Wet, including damp → the AMS takes R05 out of the ODD; step 5. Not wet (local patch) → restriction stays until inspection; step 6.
  5. Pit Supervisor chooses: run the pit over R24 (check R24 and R09 are inside the wet ODD and apply any visibility caps), hold the pit, or stop the site under a site-wide weather outage. → step 6.
  6. Inspection by the Pit Supervisor when the rain has stopped. Evidence: surface condition along the whole ramp, slip and overspeed events on R24 or on the restricted R05 since the rain, weather. Dry → surface set dry, restriction lifted, R05 reopened (recorded). Not dry → keep, set a review time, repeat step 6.

8.3 Procedure excerpt — slip report on R05

The steps follow the shared timeline of section 6.2.

  1. AHS Controller (T+302 s): acknowledge the slip event; check the truck's state (moving, stalled, holding). If stalled or not holding, go to step 7.
  2. AHS Controller (T+320 s): apply the interim restriction R05 descending 20 km/h [A], reason slip report, review time 30 min [A]; tell the Pit Supervisor by radio: "Wheel slip, DT17, R05, loaded, at P2. Interim restriction on R05 down, 20."
  3. AHS Controller: watch for further slip events on R05, R24 and R09 and for overspeed interventions; report any to the Pit Supervisor.
  4. Pit Supervisor (by T+900 s): decide the surface state of R05 (and R24, R09). If wet, set it; confirm with the AHS Controller that the AMS shows R05 outside the ODD and that EX01 and EX02 trucks are rerouted over R24.
  5. AHS Controller: confirm on the HMI that no truck is routed onto R05; trucks already on R05 clear it under the restriction; inform the Shovel Operators at EX01 and EX02 of the longer cycle.
  6. Pit Supervisor (T+3600 s): inspect R05 under a digital access permission obtained by positive communication. Record the evidence; set the surface state; if dry, ask the AHS Controller to lift the restriction and reopen R05. The AHS Controller confirms on the HMI that the first trucks back on R05 report no slip.
  7. Stalled or not holding: close R05 to new routes; keep the truck's service brakes applied (do not command a restart); Pit Supervisor to the ramp; Maintenance Planner informed if the truck's own traction or brakes are in doubt. Code the stalled truck DLY-OD-15 from the first slip report (section 8.4).

Interval Truck(s) Code GMG category
Restricted descent of R05 (T+320 s until lifted) descending trucks none: recorded as restriction interval with cause — (longer cycle)
Slower start of DT17 (slip 21.7 s) DT17 none: recorded as slip event, segment R05 —
Rerouted cycles over R24 EX01, EX02 trucks none: recorded as reroute interval with cause — (longer cycle)

Derated and restricted running and longer cycles carry no delay code under the GMG model (ch-04-06, REQ-16-07-009). They must therefore be recorded as intervals with a cause and a segment ID, or the cost of a wet ramp disappears into "slow cycles". The same applies to slip events: they are events, not delays, and their value lies in being counted by segment — R05, R24 and R03 — so that a road that slips every time it rains shows up as a road problem. Productivity per truck falls by the cycle ratio while the reroute lasts: 1564.1 / 1800.1 = 0.869 for M-20481.

8.5 Training and competency implications

  • AHS Controllers: recognise a slip event as road evidence; apply the pre-computed restriction without negotiating the number; tell stopped from holding; record restricted and rerouted intervals with cause and segment.
  • Pit Supervisors: own the surface declaration; know that wet R05 is outside the ODD and that the choice is R24, hold or stop; inspect and record evidence before reopening.
  • Emergency Response Team: for a truck stopped on a ramp, ask for holding confirmed before approaching (SCN-FIRE-DT17).
  • Maintenance Planner and Field Technicians: read a truck's slip and braking reports against the fleet's on the same segment to tell a truck problem from a road problem.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-07-02-001 [H] The ADS shall apply speed limits per segment and direction that keep the modelled stopping distance, for the truck's load state and the effective surface state, within the distance it can verify as clear; grade, load state and surface state shall be explicit inputs. Rationale: stopping distance on R05 depends strongly on grade and adhesion; one limit for all roads is wrong in both directions (section 4.4). Verification: test (LAB-07-02-01 experiment A4: limits for R05 and R24 by surface); inspection of the limit tables.

REQ-07-02-002 [H] The ADS shall use the more restrictive of the surface state declared in the AMS and the state indicated by its own slip and braking evidence; a declared dry state shall never raise a limit that the vehicle's evidence has lowered. Rationale: between rain starting and the declaration (T+0 to T+900 s) the vehicle may be the only source of evidence (section 6.2). Verification: test (SIL case: declared dry, slip injected; ch-07-08); inspection.

REQ-07-02-003 [H] The VCS shall limit drive force to the available adhesion and report every wheel-slip event with truck ID, segment ID, position, direction, load state, demanded and achieved force and duration. Rationale: slip events are the first evidence of low adhesion and must be traceable to a road (sections 4.7, 8.4). Verification: test (LAB-07-02-02 experiment B3: slip time and drive force on wet R05); inspection of event records.

REQ-07-02-004 [H] A truck coming to rest on a grade shall keep its service brakes applied until the VCS has confirmed that the parking brake holds; if holding cannot be confirmed the truck shall remain on its service brakes and raise an alarm. Rationale: on a slippery ramp the parking brake on the driven axle alone cannot hold a loaded or empty truck on R05 (section 4.6). Verification: test (LAB-07-02-01 experiment A5; HIL holding test, ch-18-02); inspection.

REQ-07-02-005 [H] The VCS shall report the availability of each stage of the braking chain; on loss of the service circuit or reduced retarding capacity the truck shall stop at a controlled deceleration or lower its speed to the value computed for the remaining chain, and report. Rationale: stopping distance and descent speed depend on which stages are available (sections 4.2, 4.8). Verification: test (LAB-07-02-01 with use_service=False, use_secondary=True); fault-injection test.

REQ-07-02-006 [H] The AMS shall offer pre-computed interim restrictions per segment and direction for a reduced-traction report, and shall record who applied each restriction, when, why and its review time. Rationale: the controller applies a number computed in advance, not an improvised one (section 7.1). Verification: inspection of the restriction table and records; demonstration with SCN-TRACTION-R05.

REQ-07-02-007 [H] A segment closed or restricted for reduced traction shall be reopened or relieved only after the Pit Supervisor has recorded an inspection and set the surface state; a timeout shall not reopen it. Rationale: reopening on the clock returns trucks to a ramp that may still be wet (sections 6.2, 8.3). Verification: inspection of the reopening records; test of the AMS (no reopening without a recorded surface state).

REQ-07-02-008 [H] Restricted and rerouted running caused by reduced traction shall be recorded as intervals with cause and segment ID and shall not be coded as delay; trucks stopped or held shall be coded under the rules of delay-codes.yml. Rationale: the cost of wet ramps must be traceable to R05, R24 or R03 (section 8.4). Verification: inspection of the shift records after SCN-TRACTION-R05.

10. Verification and validation

Verification of a real design follows ch-18-01, ch-18-02 and ch-07-08:

  • Model level (SIL). The labs are the oracle for the logic (limits by surface, slip clipping, holding check); their tests also check that the reproduced values of ch-04-05 and ch-04-07 stay unchanged.
  • HIL. Brake arbitration, blending and the holding check run on the real VCS controller with a simulated truck; fault injection of service-circuit and retarder faults (REQ-07-02-005).
  • Closed-site tests. Stopping and holding tests on a dry and a watered test grade, with measured adhesion and brake forces replacing every [A] value of this chapter. What brake tests a standard prescribes, and under which conditions, is [TBV] (SRC-STD-ISO3450 catalogue level; SRC-PUB-BRAKE-AUTONOMOUS-APPLICABILITY).
  • Production validation. Slip events, overspeed interventions and achieved decelerations collected by segment and surface state over seasons; a ramp whose slip rate rises in rain feeds back into the interim restriction table.

Validation of the ODD line itself — whether 8 % wet is right for this truck — is the supplier's and the mine's (ch-04-02).

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Equating "power-limited" with "no traction problem". The steady climb can be power-limited while every start is traction-limited (section 4.7).
  • Modelling braking without grade. On level road the slippery stop at 40 km/h is 48.8 m; on R05 it is 122.2 m.
  • Using the parking-brake rating as the holding force. On a wet ramp the road, not the brake, is the limit.
  • One reaction delay for all stop paths. The remote-stop path, the ADS path and the onboard path differ.
  • Mixing adhesion tables. Quoting a ch-04-05 number next to a V07 number without saying which table and model.
  • Treating the declared surface as the truth. It lags the weather by minutes.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Restricting the climbing lane after a climbing truck slipped and leaving the descending lane at 40 km/h.
  • Reopening when the rain stops rather than after an inspection.
  • Coding restricted or rerouted cycles as delay, or not recording them at all.
  • Approaching a truck stopped on a wet ramp on the strength of stopped alone.
  • "We'll run R05 wet, carefully." Wet R05 is outside the ODD (ch-04-02); the choice is R24, hold or stop.

12. Worked examples

All values [A], computed by LAB-07-02-01 and LAB-07-02-02 with ahs_sim 0.13.0 on mine version 0.3.0.

Example 1 — critical adhesion on R05. G+RR = 0.0899 + 0.03 = 0.1199. Loaded, ϕ = 0.67: μcrit = 0.1199 / 0.67 = 0.179. Empty, ϕ = 0.50: 0.240. Wet (0.20) lies between: the loaded climb works, the empty one would not.

Example 2 — the start on wet R05. Demand 404.25 t × 0.6 m/s² + 453.0 kN = 695.6 kN; wet traction 0.20 × 0.67 × 385 × 9.81 = 506.1 kN; acceleration (506.1 − 453.0) / 404.25 = 0.13 m/s²; 21.7 s of slip (lab).

Example 3 — the wet descent limit. Empty at 40 km/h on −8.99 %, μ 0.20: 56.2 m > 50 m. Bisection gives 37.5 km/h. On μ 0.12: 24.8 km/h. The interim 20 km/h [A] is below both, so it stops within 50 m on either surface.

Example 4 — holding DT17 at SP1. Downhill force 385 × 9.81 × 0.0899 = 339.7 kN. Parking brake on the driven axle, slippery: min(1100, 0.12 × 0.67 × 385 × 9.81 = 303.7) − 339.7 = −36.0 kN. Service brakes on all wheels: min(1200, 0.12 × 385 × 9.81 = 453.2) − 339.7 = 113.5 kN.

Example 5 — the cost of the wet afternoon for M-20481. Interim restriction on R05 for 580 s (T+320 to T+900 s): a restricted cycle takes 1684.6 s, so an EX02 truck spends 580 / 1684.6 = 0.34 of a cycle under it and loses about 0.34 × 120.6 = 41 s. Then the reroute at +236.0 s per cycle (1800.1 s) until reopening. Over a 45 min reroute [A] an EX02 truck completes 2700 / 1800.1 = 1.5 cycles instead of 2700 / 1564.1 = 1.73.

15. Key takeaways

  • The traction limit is adhesion times the load on the driven axle; the critical adhesion (G+RR)/ϕ does not depend on mass. Loaded R05 needs 0.179, R24 0.139 [A].
  • A steady climb can be power-limited while every start is traction-limited: on wet R05 a loaded start slips for about 20 s and then climbs normally.
  • Grade amplifies poor adhesion: on R05 the empty full-braking stop at 40 km/h grows from 25.2 m dry to 122.2 m slippery.
  • Speed limits are direction-dependent: on the ramps the descending lane carries the stopping risk.
  • Delays dominate stopping distance on dry roads, adhesion on wet ones; ch-04-05's class C values remain the reference for the stop the handbook chooses.
  • With the module's retarding curve the empty descent is limited by the speed limit and stopping distance, not by retarding [A].
  • Stopped is not holding: on a slippery R05 the parking brake alone cannot hold a loaded truck.
  • Wet R05 is outside the illustrative ODD: the choice is R24, hold or stop. The Pit Supervisor declares the surface; the AMS applies the consequence; reopening needs an inspection record.
