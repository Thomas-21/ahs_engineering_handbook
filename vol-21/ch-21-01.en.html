1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

It is 05:30, half an hour before the DAY shift. The Pit Supervisor and the mine planner read the shift plan in the control room: 85,000 t of ore from EX01 and EX02 to crusher C01, 75,000 t of waste from EX03 and EX04 to dump D01 [A]. The fleet list shows 40 autonomous trucks; the Maintenance Planner has already marked which are expected in the workshop. Two notes are attached to the plan. Survey will close R05, the main pit ramp, about an hour into the shift to rebuild its windrow (SCN-MAP-R05); while it is closed, every truck leaving the pit climbs the west ramp R24–R25. And EX02 will move to a new face in the afternoon (SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE).

The planner has three numbers on the screen: the plan needs 40 trucks; with R05 closed, 42; and the fleet model's production curve says 40 trucks move about 160,000 t per shift with R05 open and about 151,600 t with it closed. The Pit Supervisor asks what everyone in the room is thinking: "So do we need more trucks, or not? And if the crusher trips again, do I park the ore trucks or send them to waste?"

The questions this chapter addresses are:

  • How many trucks does the plan need, and which inputs decide the answer?
  • Why does the fortieth truck add almost as much as the twentieth, while the fiftieth adds much less?
  • Why does an autonomous fleet need fewer trucks than a manned one for the same plan in this model, and which assumptions carry that difference?
  • How many trucks does a closure of R05 cost, and why does it cost the ore circuits but not the waste circuits?
  • When C01 limits ore, how are the trucks split between the circuits, and when are some better parked at PK1?

This chapter covers:

  • the deterministic fleet-sizing calculation from mine plan to truck count, with the match factor and the bottleneck check (FIG-21-01-02, LAB-21-01-01);
  • the time-usage inputs that separate a manned from an autonomous requirement;
  • the seeded production curve with queueing, the point of diminishing returns and why it falls at a different truck count for manned and autonomous fleets (FIG-21-01-01, LAB-21-01-02);
  • the effect of an R05 closure, a face change at EX02, planned maintenance and a C01 limit on the requirement;
  • the split of the fleet between the ore and waste circuits when C01 limits ore, and park-up at PK1.

All targets, time-usage values and stoppage durations in this chapter are illustrative assumptions [A]. The results are educational and must not be used for investment or operational decisions.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Calculate the number of trucks required for the reference mine's ore (EX01, EX02 → C01) and waste (EX03, EX04 → D01) circuits from cycle time, payload, production target and time usage, and check the result with the match factor.
  2. Model how queueing at shovels and at C01-Q reduces the marginal productivity of each added truck, and locate the point of diminishing returns.
  3. Compare manned and autonomous truck requirements for the same production target and identify which time-usage assumptions [A] drive the difference.
  4. Calculate the additional trucks needed to hold production while R05 is closed and pit traffic is rerouted via the west ramp R24–R25 (M-20481 loaded haul 5865.1 m instead of 4500.5 m).

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Interpret a trucks-versus-production curve to decide how many trucks to run per shift and when to park surplus trucks at PK1.
  2. Explain how a shovel relocation that changes haul distance changes the number of trucks needed, and which planning inputs operations must keep current.
  3. Explain how planned maintenance and shovel availability enter the fleet plan, and how the Maintenance Planner and Pit Supervisor keep those inputs current.
  4. Allocate trucks between the ore and waste circuits for a shift from the fleet-sizing results when C01 throughput limits ore haulage.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

Fleet size is decided twice: rarely, as capital (how many trucks to buy or convert, ch-19-01, ch-21-04), and every shift, as operations (how many of the trucks that exist should run, on which circuit, and which should wait). Both use the same arithmetic, and the same mistakes spoil both.

The first mistake is to divide the tonnes by what one truck carries. A 220 t truck that cycled every 29 minutes (the average of the four free cycles) for all 12 hours would move about 5,500 t, so 160,000 t would need 30 trucks. The reference mine runs 40. The gap is not waste; it is downtime, shift change, breaks, delays and queueing, and each of these has an owner who can change it.

The second mistake is to stop at the deterministic answer, which assumes that every truck arrives at the shovel exactly when the previous one leaves. Real trucks bunch; a shovel stands idle while two trucks wait behind it a minute later (ch-01-05, ch-10-02).

The third mistake is to forget the bottleneck. The ore circuit is limited not by its shovels but by C01's single tipping bay; ore trucks beyond what C01 can take only lengthen the queue at C01-Q (ch-10-02).

And the inputs change. A closure of R05 lengthens the ore haul by 1365 m; a face change moves EX02's loading point; a shovel in planned maintenance cuts its circuit's capacity for hours. A fleet plan is only as current as its cycle times and availability figures, and keeping them current is work done by named roles.

3. Terminology and foundations

Fleet sizing (glossary fleet-sizing). Determining the number of trucks required to meet a production target. In this chapter, the trucks counted are trucks in the fleet, including those expected to be down; physical availability enters the calculation, not the count.

Time usage. The split of a truck's shift into the categories of the GMG Time Usage Model (SRC-PUB-GMG-TUM-2020) [F]: Downtime, Standby, Operating Delay and Working Time (ch-01-05). The fleet-sizing calculation uses the working hours per truck and shift: the shift minus Downtime, Standby and Operating Delay. The item-by-item values are assumptions [A]; the categories are the GMG ones.

Cycle time (glossary haul-cycle-time). Spotting and loading at the shovel, loaded travel, Spotting and dumping at the destination, empty travel, from ahs_sim.haul_cycle.compute_cycle on the reference mine's road network [H]. Queue time is not part of the free cycle; it enters through the queueing model.

Match factor (glossary match-factor). For n trucks shared by nL loading units with service time ts and cycle time tc, MF=nts/(nLtc) [H], the definition of ch-01-05. MF below 1 means spare shovel capacity, above 1 spare truck capacity. The balance truck count is the n that gives MF = 1.

Bottleneck (glossary bottleneck). The resource that limits a circuit's throughput. At the reference mine, C01's single tipping bay limits the ore circuit; the waste circuit, with three tip points at D01, is limited by its shovels.

Equivalent trucks. A fleet's working truck-hours divided by the shift length: 40 trucks that each work 9.300 h of a 12 h shift are 31.0 equivalent trucks. The fleet model in ahs_sim.economics turns time usage into tonnes this way and interpolates linearly between the two whole fleets around the equivalent number [H].

Marginal tonnes. The tonnes per shift added by one more truck in the fleet. Point of diminishing returns [H]: the first truck count at which the marginal tonnes fall below half the average tonnes per truck [A]. The fraction is a convention for locating the knee of the curve, not a law.

Per-unit and pooled count. Rounding up the trucks for each loading unit separately gives the per-unit count, the number a fixed assignment needs. Rounding up the circuit's fractional total gives the pooled count, the number a dispatcher that shares trucks between units could manage with. The difference is at most one truck per loading unit.

Park-up (glossary park-up). PK1, the autonomous park-up in AA-Central reached by R14 from Intersection V. A truck parked there is available but not operating.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 From mine plan to truck count

FIG-21-01-02 shows the calculation. It starts from the plan, not from the fleet, and ends with checks that can overrule it.

For a loading unit u with target Tu tonnes per shift, payload P, free cycle time tc,u and working hours per truck W, the trucks required are

Nu=Tu3600Wtc,uP

and the circuit's requirement is the sum over its loading units, rounded up per unit or pooled. The target is split equally between a circuit's loading units unless the plan says otherwise [A]. Every term has a source:

Term Source Who keeps it current
Tu Shift production plan (glossary production-plan) Pit Supervisor, mine planner
tc,u Routes of the AMS on the current road network; compute_cycle in the lab FMS from AMS routes; Survey for road geometry
W Time usage: availability, standby, delays Maintenance Planner (Downtime), Pit Supervisor (standby, delays)
P 220 t rated payload (equipment.yaml) [A] Site engineering

Example — EX02 by hand. With the autonomous time-usage preset (section 4.2), W = 9.300 h. EX02's cycle to C01 is 1564.1 s, so one truck makes 9.300 × 3600 / 1564.1 = 21.41 cycles and hauls 21.41 × 220 = 4,709 t per shift. Half the ore target is 42,500 t, so EX02 needs 42,500 / 4,709 = 9.02 trucks: 10 whole trucks for a fixed assignment. LAB-21-01-01 reproduces the module's value from these four inputs.

Three checks follow the count: the match factor (who will wait, trucks or shovels), the capacity check (a target above the bottleneck capacity cannot be met with more trucks) and the queueing check (section 4.5).

4.2 Time usage: where manned and autonomous differ

The two time-usage presets of ahs_sim.economics describe one truck in one 12 h shift [A]:

Item (hours per truck and shift) Manned preset Autonomous preset Differs because
Downtime (physical availability 0.88) 1.44 1.44 — same assumption
Shift change (DLY-SO-01) 0.50 0.10 operator change against control-room handover and staged restarts
Crib and operator breaks (DLY-SO-02) 0.75 0.00 manned trucks only
Other standby (e.g. parked at PK1) 0.30 0.30 — same assumption
Operating Delay (6 % of Operating Time, plus nuisance stops) 0.541 0.860 3 nuisance stops of 5 min (DLY-OD-03), autonomous only
Working hours 8.469 9.300
Use of Availability 0.853 0.962
Operating Efficiency 0.940 0.915

The presets differ only in shift change, breaks and nuisance stops. Physical availability, other standby and the share of other operating delays are the same in both: the model assumes no availability or productivity gain from autonomy, because none is established here. A site that claims one must measure it and state it.

Under these assumptions an autonomous truck works 0.831 h more per shift than a manned one, 9.8 % more. Two items carry the difference: the 1.25 h of shift change and breaks that a manned truck loses and an autonomous one mostly keeps, and the 0.25 h of nuisance stops that only the autonomous truck has. The second item is the fragile one. LAB-21-01-01 solves for the nuisance-stop rate at which the two presets work the same hours: about 13 stops of 5 minutes per truck and shift. Long before that, the autonomous fleet's advantage in this model shrinks with every stop; ch-21-03 prices it.

Operating Efficiency is lower for the autonomous preset and Use of Availability higher: less Standby, slightly more Operating Delay. One KPI alone would mislead in either direction.

4.4 Match factor and the bottleneck check

With 20 autonomous trucks on the ore circuit, the match factor is 1.065: slightly more truck capacity than shovel capacity. The balance truck count is 18.78. On the waste circuit, 20 autonomous trucks give 0.879 against a balance of 22.74; the manned preset's 22 trucks give 0.967. Read alone, these say: the ore shovels are fully served, the waste shovels will wait for trucks.

The match factor is about shovels. The capacity check asks about the whole circuit:

  • Ore loading capacity: two shovels, 167 s per load, 12 h: 113,820 t per shift.
  • C01 capacity: one tipping bay, 90 s per truck: 105,600 t per shift.
  • D01 capacity: three tip points, 85 s per truck: 335,435 t per shift.

C01 is the ore bottleneck. Taking the match factor at C01 instead of at the shovels, the ore trucks that keep C01's bay busy, working the whole time, number 17.42 — fewer than the 18.78 that keep the shovels busy. The ore circuit therefore reaches C01's limit before it reaches its shovels', and every ore truck beyond that point queues at C01-Q. The planned 85,000 t is 80 % of C01's capacity; the target is feasible, but there is little room above it.

The capacity check is also where planned maintenance can overrule the count. If C01 is shut for 4 of the 12 hours [A], its capacity is 70,400 t and the module reports the ore target as limited by the destination: no number of trucks meets 85,000 t (section 4.8).

4.5 Queueing and the point of diminishing returns

The deterministic count assumes no queue. ahs_sim.economics.production_curve runs the fleet model of ahs_sim.fms for every truck count from 20 to 60: fixed assignment, the baseline shares of the 37-truck allocation scaled to the fleet, queueing at the shovels and at C01-Q and D01-Q, gamma-distributed service and travel times. Time usage enters as equivalent trucks. Each point is the mean of five seeded runs (seeds 20481 to 20485) [A]. FIG-21-01-01 shows the curves for both presets, with the deterministic curve as a dashed line.

Trucks Autonomous, seeded mean (t) Manned, seeded mean (t)
20 83,493 76,822
40 160,034 147,345
48 184,979 172,777
53 195,918 185,339
60 204,007 196,482

The early part of the curve is nearly straight. Up to about 40 autonomous trucks each truck adds roughly as much as the last, close to the average (3,439 to 4,331 t between 21 and 40 trucks). Queues exist — at 40 trucks the fleet spends 13.7 truck-hours per shift waiting at shovels and 7.2 at the destinations — but they grow slowly.

Then the slope falls. With the convention of section 3 (marginal tonnes below half the average), the autonomous curve's point of diminishing returns is at 48 trucks: the 48th truck adds 1,869 t against an average of 3,854 t. The manned curve reaches it at 53 trucks (1,506 t against 3,497 t). Beyond those points the ore circuit is saturated at C01, ore stays between 100,000 and 103,300 t whatever the truck count, and added trucks only lengthen queues: at 60 autonomous trucks the fleet waits 39.0 truck-hours at shovels and 65.0 at destinations per shift.

Why the point differs, manned against autonomous. Diminishing returns are a property of the loading units and of C01, not of the trucks. What saturates them is working truck-hours. At the point of diminishing returns the autonomous fleet provides 37.2 equivalent trucks and the manned fleet 37.4 — almost the same. Because each autonomous truck works 9.300 h against 8.469 h, fewer of them reach that saturation: 48 against 53, a ratio of 1.10 that is the ratio of the working hours. The difference is the time-usage assumption of section 4.2 and nothing else. If nuisance stops rose to 13 per truck and shift, the two curves would coincide.

The seeded curve against the deterministic one. The deterministic curve (no seed) is never below the seeded mean: variability costs about 4 to 5 % of production between 35 and 46 trucks (168,086 t against 160,034 t at 40). The deterministic curve reaches its point of diminishing returns one truck earlier (47 and 52) and has flat steps: from 47 to 50 autonomous trucks it stays at 191,180 t and rises again from 51. The steps come from the fixed allocation. The model scales the baseline shares to the fleet by largest remainder; between 36 and 39 equivalent trucks the added trucks go to EX01 and EX02, which C01 can no longer serve, and only the 40th goes to a waste shovel that can use it. The seeded curve smooths the steps because its variability spreads the queues.

The small dip at saturation. The seeded autonomous curve falls once, by 332 t, from 58 to 59 trucks. That is not a physical effect. Near saturation the true marginal tonnes are close to zero, while the mean of five seeded shifts still varies by a few hundred tonnes (one shift's standard deviation is roughly 500 to 800 t at 30 to 45 trucks). The deterministic curve never falls. Read the seeded curve as non-decreasing within about 1,000 t, and never base a decision on a single step.

What the queues do to the count. The deterministic calculation gives 37.43 autonomous trucks as a fraction, 40 when rounded per unit. The seeded curve reaches 160,000 t at 40 trucks (160,034 t) and 152,950 t at 38. Rounding up per unit happened to cover the queueing loss; it will not always. For the manned preset the deterministic 42 trucks give 153,793 t on the seeded curve, and 44 are needed to reach the plan. The deterministic count is a lower bound on the trucks a plan needs.

4.6 R05 closed: the west ramp and the ore circuits

While R05 is closed the AMS reroutes pit traffic over R09, R24, R25 and R12 (SCN-MAP-R05). For M-20481 the loaded haul grows from 4500.5 m to 5865.1 m, and EX02's cycle from 1564.1 s to 1800.1 s, +15 %. The ore requirement rises from 18.10 to 20.82 fractional trucks for the autonomous preset (20 to 22 per unit), and the total from 40 to 42.

The waste requirement falls, from 19.34 to 18.93 fractional trucks. That is not an error, and the explanation matters for every planner who uses the closure numbers. For EX03 and EX04 the west ramp is the faster way out of the pit even though it is the longer one:

EX03 loaded haul to D01 Via R05 (R05 open) Via R24 (R05 closed)
Route out of the pit down R09 (462 m, −6.51 %), R06, up R05 (1340 m, 8.99 %) up R24 (1425 m, 6.33 %), R25, R12
Lift on the ramp from RL 300 to RL 420 (after descending 30 m from the RL 330 bench) from RL 330 to RL 420
Loaded climbing speed 11.4 km/h on R05 14.7 km/h on R24
Time on the ramp 421 s 349 s
Loaded distance / cycle 6550.7 m / 1914.7 s 6991.4 m / 1875.3 s

The waste shovels work on the RL 330 bench. Their shortest route goes down to the pit floor first and then climbs 120 m up the steeper main ramp, where a loaded truck is slowed by the grade. The west ramp starts at their bench, climbs only 90 m and at a gentler grade. The loaded leg is 60.0 s shorter (999.5 s against 939.5 s); the empty leg is 20.5 s longer (663.2 s against 683.7 s); the cycle is 39.4 s shorter after rounding. The ore shovels on the RL 300 floor have no such advantage: for them the west ramp is simply 1365 m more.

The waste trucks use R05 when it is open because the AMS, like the fleet model, routes by shortest distance (ch-16-07); the same 39.4 s appear in ch-07-03, ch-12-09 and ch-14-06. Routed by time, with R05 open, the waste trucks would climb R24 loaded and come back down R05 empty, a cycle of 1854.8 s for EX03, and the waste requirement would be 18.73 trucks — lower than with R05 closed. The closure does not help the waste circuit; it removes a routing choice that was costing it. Whether the FMS and AMS should route by time per circuit is a design decision for the dispatch policy (ch-21-02), not a conclusion of this chapter.

In the seeded fleet model, 40 autonomous trucks move 160,034 t per shift with R05 open and 151,630 t with it closed: 8,404 t less, about 700 t per hour of closure. Ore falls from 88,528 t to 78,320 t; waste rises from 71,507 t to 73,310 t. To hold 160,000 t with R05 closed the seeded curve needs 43 trucks: three more than the fleet has, and one more than the deterministic 42. The deterministic model gives a larger loss, 898 t per hour, and the fleet model's 37-truck full shift without losses gives 311.7 t per hour (ch-07-03). The numbers differ because the cases differ: in the full-shift case the ore circuit is saturated at C01 with R05 open, so part of the closure's loss falls on capacity that was not being used.

4.7 Face change at EX02

A face change moves EX02's loading point (SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE). ch-13-05 lays out two ways to reach a face 200 m north of the old one [A]: layout A lengthens the loading-area link R18 to 406.2 m; layout B runs R08 on to 653.4 m and keeps R18 short. LAB-21-01-01 builds both in memory (never in mine.yaml), adds two faces farther along R08 [A], and sizes the ore circuit for each:

EX02 face EX02 cycle (s) EX02 trucks (fractional → whole) Ore circuit (per unit / fractional)
Today 1564.1 9.02 → 10 20 / 18.10
Layout A (R18 406.2 m) 1660.1 9.58 → 10 20 / 18.65
Layout B (R08 653.4 m) 1610.6 9.29 → 10 20 / 18.37
Layout B + 400 m on R08 1706.6 9.85 → 10 20 / 18.92
Layout B + 800 m on R08 1802.6 10.40 → 11 21 / 19.47

Two points follow. First, where the extra distance lies matters more than how long it is. Layout A adds 200 m on a 15 km/h loading-area link with high rolling resistance and costs 96.0 s per cycle; layout B adds 206 m on a 30 km/h approach and 6 m less on the link, and costs 46.5 s. Second, the whole-truck count hides creep. Faces up to 400 m beyond layout B leave EX02 at 10 trucks while its fractional requirement rises from 9.02 to 9.85; another 400 m tips it to 11. A plan that tracks only whole trucks will be surprised by a step that has been building for months. The fractional requirement, the match factor and the cycle time are the inputs to watch; they are refreshed when the new loading area is activated, at T+3600 s in SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE.

4.8 Planned maintenance and shovel availability

Planned truck maintenance enters through physical availability. If more trucks than usual are in the workshop, the fleet's average physical availability falls and so do the working hours per truck. For the autonomous preset [A]:

Physical availability Working hours per truck Trucks required (per unit / fractional)
0.88 (preset) 9.300 40 / 37.43
0.85 8.962 41 / 38.85
0.80 8.398 43 / 41.46

Every 0.01 of physical availability is worth about half a truck in this plan. The Maintenance Planner's forecast of trucks in the workshop therefore belongs in the shift plan, not in a surprise at the pre-start meeting.

Planned maintenance on a shovel or on C01 acts differently: it reduces capacity, not working hours, and more trucks cannot replace it.

  • C01 shut for 4 h [A]. C01's capacity falls to 70,400 t; the ore target of 85,000 t is limited by the destination and the requirement for ore trucks is no longer the question. The question becomes the circuit allocation of section 4.9.
  • EX01 in planned maintenance for 4 h [A]. The circuit's loading capacity is still above the target, but an equal split would ask EX01 for 42,500 t while it can load at most 37,940 t in its 8 hours. The plan must re-split the target in proportion to loading hours (EX01 40 %, EX02 60 % [A]); then each shovel's capacity holds (37,940 t and 56,910 t) and the ore circuit needs 19 trucks. The model assumes that EX01's trucks are reassigned to EX02 while EX01 is down.

Shovel availability is a constraint to check; truck availability is an input to the count.

4.9 Splitting the fleet when C01 limits ore

When C01 is down for part of the shift, the ore tonnes it can take are capped, and the question is how to split the fleet between the circuits and whether any truck should be parked. ahs_sim.economics.allocate_circuits runs each circuit alone in the fleet model with k ore trucks and m−k waste trucks, caps the useful ore at C01's remaining capacity, chooses the split with the most useful tonnes for every fleet size m, and adds trucks while the best total grows by at least 1,000 t per truck [A]. The rest are parked at PK1. Results for the 40 autonomous trucks (deterministic, R05 open):

C01 down [A] C01 cap (t per shift) Ore trucks Waste trucks Parked Useful ore (t) Waste (t)
none — 21 19 0 95,874 72,152
4 h 70,400 15 25 0 69,055 94,659
6 h 52,800 11 29 0 50,641 108,407

With the module's rule, no truck is parked: every truck taken off the ore circuit is worth more than 1,000 t on the waste circuit, which saturates only at 31 trucks (110,880 t). The rule counts every tonne of waste as useful. That is a planning assumption, and often a wrong one: waste moved far ahead of the mining sequence may have little value, and the dump may have its own limits. LAB-21-01-02 adds a waste limit of the plan plus 10 % (82,500 t [A]), with the same search and rule:

C01 down [A] Ore trucks Waste trucks Parked at PK1 Useful ore + waste (t)
4 h 16 22 2 70,400 + 82,500
6 h 12 22 6 52,800 + 82,500

Now trucks are parked: two for a 4 h stoppage, six for 6 h. Each would add less than 1,000 t of useful material and more queue time. Parked at PK1 they are available the moment C01 restarts, and use no fuel, tyres or traffic capacity meanwhile.

The optimum is flat. With R05 open and no cap, splits from 20 to 23 ore trucks are within 886 t of the best; with R05 closed, splits from 15 to 26 are within 963 t. The seeded model picks a different split (20 ore and 20 waste in both cases) because a few hundred tonnes of noise decide among nearly equal options. The model can say which splits are reasonable; it cannot say which one is best by a margin worth arguing about. Within the flat range the shift's priority — ore to C01 first, or the waste stripping schedule — should decide, and that is the Pit Supervisor's call with the mine planner.

The cap is applied after the run, so the model assumes that the FMS sends ore trucks to C01 only at the rate the plant can take (ore dispatch throttling, ch-15-04). Reassignment at the moment C01 stops is dispatch (ch-21-02).

4.10 Model assumptions and limits

  • Time usage as equivalent trucks. Downtime, standby and delays are spread evenly over the fleet as fewer trucks working the whole shift [H]. A real shift loses whole trucks at particular times; the average is right, the timing is not.
  • Fixed assignment. Neither the curve nor the allocation model is an optimised dispatch (ch-21-02).
  • No road congestion. The fleet model has no traffic interaction on roads or at Intersection X. With R05 closed, the whole pit uses R24, R25 and V; real congestion there would add to the closure's cost.
  • Shortest-distance routing unless told otherwise (section 4.6).
  • Seeded noise. Differences below about 1,000 t between neighbouring points carry no information.
  • One shift type with constant targets; pit deepening and multi-year change are in ch-21-07.
  • Illustrative inputs. Targets, time-usage items, stoppage durations, the waste limit and the 1,000 t rule are [A].

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

The walkthrough uses SCN-MAP-R05 on a DAY shift. Registry key events: T−3600 s R05 closed to autonomous traffic for the rework, the AMS replans missions over the west ramp and the FMS recomputes cycle times and allocation; T+0 s as-built survey submitted; T+1800 s map change validated against survey; T+2400 s new map version deployed and road reopened; T+2700 s routes return from the west ramp to R05, reservations on R24 and V released. The closure lasts 1.75 h. Times not in the registry are marked [A]. The abnormal case adds the registry timeline of SCN-CRUSHER-C01-DOWN (T+0, T+5, T+300 s of that scenario), placed at T+600 s [A] of this one.

With R05 closed and C01 capped at 70,400 t for the shift, the allocation model gives 17 ore and 23 waste trucks with no truck parked; with the waste limit of 82,500 t, 18 ore, 21 waste and one parked. When R05 reopens at T+2400 s the waste trucks become slower and the ore trucks faster; with R05 open and the waste limit, the same cap gives 16 ore, 22 waste and two parked. The split is revisited at each change of a constraint, not once per shift.

Trucks held at C01-Q and ore trucks parked because C01 is down are coded DLY-OD-10, the destination-down code. The parked trucks are not Downtime; they remain available, and Physical Availability is not reduced by the plant's stoppage.

6.3 Degraded: the map change fails validation

At T+1800 s the validation finds that the as-built windrow does not match the change set [A]. The new version is not deployed, and R05 stays closed until the change set is corrected: for the rest of the DAY shift [A], about 11 hours from T−3600 s.

The fleet plan for the rest of the shift is the closed case. The closure now costs about 700 t per hour for the whole period, about 7,700 t, and the plan of 160,000 t would need 43 trucks. The site has 40. Three responses are acceptable:

  • accept the shortfall and report it with its cause (the closure), split between the circuits by the shift's priority;
  • move trucks from waste to ore within the flat range to protect C01's feed, and record the waste shortfall;
  • re-plan the next shift with R05 closed from the start, so that the NIGHT shift's targets match its fleet.

Reopening R05 on the old map, or raising speed limits on R24, to recover tonnes is not acceptable: closures and speed limits are inputs to the fleet plan, never its variables.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

Deterministic first, seeded second. The deterministic calculation can be checked by hand (section 4.1) but has no queues. The seeded curve adds them at the cost of noise and of a model that cannot be checked by hand. The first is for explaining, the second for committing.

Per-unit or pooled rounding. A fixed assignment needs the per-unit count; a dispatcher that shares trucks could do with the pooled count. The difference at the reference mine is one or two trucks (40 or 39 autonomous; 42 or 40 with R05 closed). The choice should match the dispatch policy that will actually run (ch-10-01, ch-21-02).

Five replications. Enough to locate the knee within one or two trucks while keeping the lab fast; the dip at 58 to 59 trucks is the visible price.

Equivalent trucks. Treating lost hours as fewer trucks for the whole shift is correct on average but misprices a loss whose timing matters; ch-21-03 values such losses with a bottleneck model.

Routing by distance. The model follows the AMS's routing so that the closure numbers match what the trucks will do. A time-routing option exists (route="time"); using it in the fleet plan while the AMS routes by distance would promise a waste cycle that no truck drives.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

For SCN-MAP-R05 the data flow behind the fleet plan is:

  • T−3600 s — AMS → FMS. The new routes after the closure; the FMS recomputes the free cycle per loading unit from the routes the AMS will actually use, not from a separate estimate.
  • T−3540 s [A] — FMS → Pit Supervisor. The new requirement per circuit and the proposed split, each with the time of its inputs. A requirement computed from cycle times older than the last closure or reopening is marked stale [R].
  • T+2400 s — AMS. Reopening for confirmed trucks. Trucks still pending the new map version keep the detour (ch-12-09), so the network the fleet sees is mixed until T+2700 s.
  • T+2700 s — AMS → FMS. Routes return to R05; the FMS recomputes cycle times. The waste cycles grow by 39.4 s; the allocation goes back to the plan's split.
  • Per shift — Maintenance Planner → FMS. Trucks and shovels expected in planned maintenance, with their windows, before the shift plan is fixed.
  • Per face change — Survey and FMS. The new loading area's geometry at activation (T+3600 s in SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE) and the resulting cycle time.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

During SCN-MAP-R05 the AHS Controller sees the closure on the map panel and the trucks rerouting over R24. The Pit Supervisor's planning view shows, per circuit: trucks assigned and required (fractional and whole), cycle time before and after the closure, the match factor, and expected tonnes against the plan, with the production curve for the current network. When C01 trips, it adds C01's remaining capacity, the proposed split and the trucks proposed for PK1 with the tonnes forgone. Parked trucks appear at PK1 with their delay code.

8.3 Procedure excerpt — shift fleet plan and park-up decision

Procedure [H], at the start of each shift and after each change of a constraint:

  1. Maintenance Planner: confirm the trucks and shovels in planned maintenance and their windows.
  2. Mine planner: confirm the targets per circuit and the waste allowance.
  3. Mine planner: check that the cycle times in the FMS are from the current routes (closures, face changes) and note their time.
  4. Mine planner: run the deterministic requirement and the capacity check; flag any target above a bottleneck.
  5. Mine planner: read the production curve at the available fleet; note the tonnes and the distance to the point of diminishing returns.
  6. Pit Supervisor: decide the split between circuits within the flat range, by the shift's priority.
  7. Pit Supervisor: decide park-up when the model shows trucks adding less than the threshold [A]; record the cause.
  8. AHS Controller: apply the allocation; send parked trucks to PK1 as they become free, never by cancelling a loaded haul; code the parked time.
  9. Pit Supervisor: when the constraint changes (C01 restarts, R05 reopens), return parked trucks first and review the split.

8.5 Training and competency implications

  • Pit Supervisors: reading a production curve; "more trucks" against "more capacity"; the flat optimum; park-up as a decision with a cause.
  • Mine planners: the formula by hand; the capacity check; the seeded curve and its noise; the west-ramp effect.
  • AHS Controllers: applying an allocation; sending trucks to and from PK1; coding parked time.
  • Maintenance Planners: how the forecast of trucks in the workshop enters the plan (about half a truck per 0.01 of Physical Availability here).

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-21-01-001 [H] A fleet requirement shall be stated with its inputs: the target per circuit and its split between loading units, the cycle time per loading unit with the routes and closures it assumes, each time-usage item with its source, the payload, and the rounding rule (per unit or pooled). Rationale: the same plan gives 37.43, 39, 40 or 42 trucks depending on rounding and closures (section 4.3). Verification: inspection of the fleet-plan record; test (LAB-21-01-01 sizing table).

REQ-21-01-002 [H] The fleet-sizing result shall be checked against the capacity of every loading unit and destination of the circuit for the shift, including planned maintenance windows; a target above a bottleneck's capacity shall be reported as capacity-limited and shall not be converted into additional trucks. Rationale: C01 limits the ore circuit; trucks beyond its capacity only queue (sections 4.4, 4.8). Verification: test (LAB-21-01-01 planned C01 stop and EX01 stop).

REQ-21-01-003 [H] A truck count used for a commitment, a purchase or a conversion shall be checked with a queueing model whose number of replications and seeds are stated, and shall be quoted with the deterministic count beside it. Rationale: the deterministic count is a lower bound; the seeded curve needs 40 autonomous trucks where the deterministic fraction is 37.43, and 44 manned trucks where the per-unit count is 42 (section 4.5). Verification: inspection; test (LAB-21-01-02 seeded curves).

REQ-21-01-004 [H] The FMS shall recompute the cycle time of every loading unit from the routes the AMS uses whenever a closure is applied or lifted or a new loading area is activated, and shall show the Pit Supervisor the resulting requirement per circuit with the time of its inputs. Rationale: R05's closure changes ore cycles by +236 s and waste cycles by −39.4 s; a face change at EX02 by up to 238.5 s (sections 4.6, 4.7). Verification: test (LAB-21-01-01 closure and face-change cases); demonstration with SCN-MAP-R05 at T−3600 s and T+2700 s.

REQ-21-01-005 [H] A comparison of manned and autonomous truck requirements shall use the same physical availability, operating-delay share and other standby for both, unless a measured difference is stated with its source, and shall list each time-usage item that differs. Rationale: the difference in this model comes from shift change, breaks and nuisance stops only (section 4.2). Verification: inspection of the comparison; test (LAB-21-01-01 time-usage rows).

REQ-21-01-006 [H] Trucks shall be parked at PK1 for a constraint only on the Pit Supervisor's decision, recorded with its cause, the expected tonnes forgone and the condition for their return; the decision shall be reviewed when that constraint changes. Rationale: park-up is right when a truck adds less than it costs in queue time, and wrong once C01 or R05 is back (sections 4.9, 6.2). Verification: inspection of the shift log; demonstration with SCN-CRUSHER-C01-DOWN.

REQ-21-01-007 [H] Fleet sizing and allocation shall take road closures, speed limits, reservations, traffic rules and safety functions as fixed inputs; no truck count, split or tonnes target shall be met by changing any of them. Rationale: the FMS assigns and the AMS executes and enforces; a fleet plan that changes a safety input moves risk, not tonnes (sections 5, 6.3). Verification: inspection of the model inputs and of the plan's change record.

REQ-21-01-008 [H] The shift fleet plan shall track the fractional requirement and the cycle time per loading unit, not only the whole-truck count, and shall refresh truck and shovel availability from the Maintenance Planner's forecast before each shift. Rationale: whole-truck counts hide creep until a step (section 4.7); every 0.01 of physical availability is about half a truck here (section 4.8). Verification: inspection of the plan records over a month of shifts.

10. Verification and validation

The arithmetic is verified by hand. Section 4.1 reproduces the module's EX02 requirement; the test checks that the lab's hand calculation equals the module's value.

The models are verified against each other. The production curve at 37 trucks with no losses reproduces the fleet model's baseline DAY shift (191,180 t); the deterministic curve is non-decreasing; the seeded curve is non-decreasing within 1,000 t. The lab's tests pin the values quoted in this chapter so that a later change to ahs_sim that moves them is noticed.

The closure numbers are verified against other chapters. EX02's cycles (1564.1 s and 1800.1 s), the waste circuit's 39.4 s, the layouts A and B of the face change (1660.1 s and 1610.6 s) and the full-shift closure loss (311.7 t per hour) match ch-12-09, ch-13-05 and ch-07-03.

Validation at a site would compare predicted and recorded tonnes per shift against fleet size over a window of shifts, using measured cycle times and time usage, and state the error. The reference mine is fictional; nothing here is validated against a real operation.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Sizing from payload and calendar hours: 30 trucks for a plan that needs 40.
  • Stopping at the deterministic count: the manned plan needs 44 trucks on the seeded curve, not 42.
  • Ignoring the bottleneck: ore trucks added beyond C01's capacity queue at C01-Q.
  • Giving autonomy an availability gain by default: a difference not measured is not an input.
  • Using one cycle time for a whole circuit: the closure moves the ore and waste cycles in opposite directions.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Planning with yesterday's cycle times after a closure or a face change.
  • Reading one step of the seeded curve as a trend, such as the 332 t dip at 58 to 59 trucks.
  • Sending every truck somewhere when C01 is down, so that the queues absorb the trucks the plan does not need.
  • Forgetting parked trucks when the constraint is lifted.
  • Adding trucks to a circuit whose shovel is in maintenance.

12. Worked examples

All values from LAB-21-01-01 and LAB-21-01-02 (ahs_sim 0.23.0) unless stated. Targets and time usage [A].

Example 1 — Working hours. Autonomous preset: 12 × 0.88 = 10.56 available hours; minus 0.1 + 0.0 + 0.3 = 0.4 h of Standby gives 10.16 operating hours; Operating Delay 0.06 × 10.16 + 3 × 5 / 60 = 0.860 h; working 9.300 h. Manned: 10.56 − 1.55 = 9.01; delay 0.06 × 9.01 = 0.541; working 8.469 h.

Example 2 — The ore circuit. EX01: 9.300 × 3600 / 1572.4 = 21.29 cycles, 4,684 t per truck, 42,500 / 4,684 = 9.07 trucks. EX02: 9.02 (section 4.1). Total 18.10, per unit 10 + 10 = 20. Match factor (10 × 167 / 1572.4 + 10 × 167 / 1564.1) / 2 = 1.065.

Example 3 — Break-even nuisance stops. Autonomous working hours without stops: 10.16 × 0.94 = 9.550 h. Equal to the manned 8.469 h when the stops take 1.081 h: 1.081 × 60 / 5 = 13.0 stops per truck and shift.

Example 4 — The closure. EX02: 9.300 × 3600 / 1800.1 = 18.60 cycles, 4,092 t per truck, 10.39 trucks; EX01 10.43; ore 20.82 (22 per unit). Waste 18.93 (20 per unit). Total 42. Seeded curve: 43 trucks to reach 160,000 t; at 40 trucks 151,630 t, a loss of 700 t per hour, about 1,230 t over the 1.75 h of SCN-MAP-R05.

Example 5 — A C01 stoppage of 6 h. C01's cap is 105,600 × 6 / 12 = 52,800 t. Without a waste limit: 11 ore trucks (50,641 t) and 29 waste trucks (108,407 t). With the waste limit of 82,500 t: 12 ore trucks reach the cap, 22 waste trucks reach the limit (83,406 t hauled), and the remaining 6 trucks are parked at PK1.

Example 6 — Planned maintenance. Physical availability 0.85 instead of 0.88: working hours 12 × 0.85 = 10.2, minus 0.4 = 9.8; delay 0.06 × 9.8 + 0.25 = 0.838; working 8.962 h. Requirement 38.85 fractional, 41 per unit: one truck more than the preset.

14.1 Engineering assignments

  1. Run production_curve with ten replications instead of five. Does the dip at 58 to 59 trucks disappear? Does the point of diminishing returns move?
  2. Change the convention for diminishing returns from 50 % to 70 % of the average. Where are the two points now, and what does that say about using one number for the knee?
  3. Compute the requirement for the waste circuit with route="time" and R05 open, and explain the result to a Pit Supervisor in three sentences.

15. Key takeaways

  • Size the fleet from the plan: target, cycle time, working hours and payload. The deterministic count for the reference mine is 40 autonomous trucks (37.43 fractional) or 42 manned.
  • Check the count against the bottleneck. C01's single bay limits ore at 105,600 t per shift; trucks beyond it queue.
  • Queueing makes the deterministic count a lower bound. The seeded curve needs 40 autonomous and 44 manned trucks.
  • Diminishing returns set in at 48 autonomous or 53 manned trucks in this model: the same 37 equivalent trucks, reached with fewer trucks that each work longer. The difference is time usage, not the trucks.
  • The seeded curve is non-decreasing within about 1,000 t; its 332 t dip at saturation is noise.
  • Closing R05 costs the ore circuits, not the waste circuits: the west ramp is already faster for EX03 and EX04, and the distance routing was costing them 39.4 s per cycle.
  • When C01 limits ore, split the fleet within the flat optimum by priority, and park trucks at PK1 only with a cause and a condition for their return.
  • Closures, speed limits and safety functions are inputs to the fleet plan, never variables.
