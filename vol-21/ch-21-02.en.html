1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

It is 09:45 on a DAY shift at the reference mine. The plant control room phones the AHS Controller: the crusher crew will stop C01 at 10:00 for about two hours to clear a build-up in the chute. The FMS dispatch board shows the morning's targets from the optimiser — EX01 18.4 trucks per hour, EX02 21.6, EX03 15.6, EX04 21.6 — and 203,712 t for the shift. The fleet model that the site uses to check plans expects 191,180 t for an undisturbed shift. The Pit Supervisor asks three questions. Why does the optimiser promise 12,532 t more than the fleet model? What should the 19 ore trucks do for the next two hours: hold at C01-Q, park at PK1 or go to waste? And does the dispatch policy the site is trialling make any difference?

Twenty minutes later DZ03 is called to clean up the floor at EX02, and the board keeps sending trucks to EX02.

The scene raises the questions of this chapter:

  • How is the allocation of trucks to shovels and destinations written as a linear programme, and what do its solution and its shadow prices mean on a shift?
  • Why is the optimum above the fleet model, and which number should be used for what?
  • What happens when R05 is closed, and when a closure cuts D01 off?
  • Do dispatch policies differ during an unplanned C01 stop, and how does notice change that?
  • Where does optimisation stop, and how is an override recorded so that the model can be improved?

This chapter covers:

  • the truck-flow linear programme of the reference mine, by hand for a 2 × 2 case and with SciPy for the mine (LAB-21-02-01, FIG-21-02-01), its shadow prices in shift terms, and the gap to the fleet model;
  • the optimum with R05 closed and the infeasibility when R03 is closed;
  • four dispatch policies during SCN-CRUSHER-C01-DOWN, without and with notice, and the re-plan options (LAB-21-02-02, FIG-21-02-02);
  • the responsibility split — the FMS optimises assignments, the AMS executes missions and enforces reservations and traffic rules — including SCN-DEADLOCK-X, treated qualitatively;
  • target rates in shift terms and the rules for overriding the optimiser.

Dispatch mechanics are in ch-10-01, the C01 interface and response in ch-10-07 and ch-15-06; this chapter refers to them without change. All values are illustrative [A] from the handbook's educational models; money is in placeholder units "cu" [A]. The results must not be used for operational or investment decisions.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Formulate truck-flow allocation between shovels EX01–EX04 and destinations C01 and D01 as a linear programme and solve it for the reference mine.
  2. Compare dispatch policies (fixed assignment, minimise shovel idle time, minimise truck wait, target-rate tracking) in seeded simulation using productivity and queue metrics.
  3. Explain the responsibility split in which the FMS optimises assignments while the AMS executes missions and enforces reservations, and why optimisation must never bypass AMS traffic rules.
  4. Analyse how a closure of R05 changes the optimal allocation when the west ramp R24–R25 becomes the pit exit, using the network-flow model with segment closures.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Evaluate a dispatch re-plan when C01 stops — hold ore trucks, divert ore shovels to waste or park trucks — and choose with the Pit Supervisor using the model output.
  2. Describe which dispatch decisions the AHS Controller and Pit Supervisor may override and how each override is recorded for later analysis.
  3. Explain the optimiser's target rates to Shovel Operators and the Pit Supervisor in shift terms (trucks per hour, expected queue).
  4. Judge when to override the optimiser, for example during dozer DZ03 clean-up at EX02, and record the reason so the model can be improved.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

The reference mine runs 37 producing trucks: ten on EX01 and nine each on EX02, EX03 and EX04 (equipment.yaml). Ore goes to C01, which has one tipping position and takes 40 loads per hour; waste goes to D01, which has three tip points and spare capacity. ch-10-01 showed the consequence: with R05 open the ore circuit is crusher-limited and every ore truck waits about 146 s per cycle, while the waste circuit is truck-limited. A rule that only chooses each truck's next loading unit within its material moves waiting between C01-Q and the ore shovels; it changes neither fact.

An optimiser asks how the fleet's working time should be spread over all loading units and destinations at once. Its answer is a set of flow rates — loads per hour on each loaded leg, empty trips per hour on each return — which become the targets of a target-tracking policy and an input to the shift plan. Three things make this harder than it looks:

  • The optimum is not a forecast. The linear programme has no queues, no variability and no start or end of shift. If the board shows it as the plan, every shift looks short.
  • Plant stops are where dispatch matters, and where it can do least. In ahs_sim.fms a truck gets a new loading unit only when it has dumped (ch-10-01, ch-10-07). An unplanned C01 stop leaves the loaded ore trucks committed to C01.
  • The optimiser does not drive the trucks. Routes, speeds, reservations and the order of trucks at Intersection X are AMS decisions. An allocation that looks better on paper by sending more trucks through X at once can make a traffic problem such as SCN-DEADLOCK-X more likely, and the optimiser does not see it.

3. Terminology and foundations

Linear programme (LP) (proposed glossary linear-programming) [H]. An optimisation problem with a linear objective and linear constraints on continuous variables.

Truck flow (proposed glossary truck-flow) [H]. A rate in loads per hour on an arc: a loaded flow x(u,d) from loading unit u to destination d, or an empty flow y(d,u) back.

Binding constraint (glossary binding-constraint). A constraint used to its limit at the optimum; the others have slack.

Shadow price (proposed glossary shadow-price) [H]. The change of the optimum per unit increase of a constraint's right-hand side — here tonnes per hour per extra working truck, or per extra load per hour at a shovel or at C01. It is zero for a constraint with slack. Also called the dual value.

Dispatch policy (glossary dispatch-policy). The four compared here [H]:

  • fixed assignment — every truck returns to its home loading unit;
  • minimise shovel idle time — the allowed loading unit expected to have waited longest for a truck;
  • minimise truck wait — the allowed loading unit where the truck can start loading earliest (ch-10-01's dynamic rule);
  • target-rate tracking (proposed glossary target-rate-tracking) — the allowed loading unit furthest behind its target rate, by default the LP optimum.

"Allowed" means the home unit's material while its destination is not known to be down, and otherwise any material whose destination is up: during a known C01 stop an ore truck may be sent to waste (ahs_sim.economics).

Dispatch decision epoch (glossary dispatch-decision-epoch). In ahs_sim.fms the end of tipping; a loaded truck keeps its destination. Stopping notice (glossary stopping-notice): the time between C01's STOPPING and the stop time it announces (ch-10-07). Hold, park, divert, reassign (glossary truck-hold, park-up, divert-to-waste, reassignment): loaded ore trucks wait at C01-Q; they wait at PK1; EX01 and EX02 move to a waste block; empty ore trucks go to the waste shovels (ch-10-07, ch-15-06).

Delay codes follow delay-codes.yml: DLY-NP-01 (wait at a loading unit), DLY-NP-02 (wait at a working destination), DLY-OD-10 (destination down), DLY-OD-04 (held for a work zone), DLY-OD-17 (held in a traffic deadlock).

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The truck-flow network

FIG-21-02-01 shows two views of one problem. On the left is the road network: EX01 and EX02 on the pit floor at Intersection Z, EX03 and EX04 on the bench at W, the main ramp R05, the west ramp R24–R25, Intersection X where every haul road meets, C01 behind C01-Q and D01 behind R03 and R04. On the right is the network the LP sees: no roads, only a loaded arc from each shovel to its destination and an empty arc from each destination to every shovel a truck can reach.

The arcs carry times. fms.fleet_times computes every leg with ahs_sim.haul_cycle on the network as it is, open or with segments closed. A load from EX01 to C01 costs 0.2959 truck-hours: loading service (167 s), loaded travel and the service at C01 (90 s). An empty arc costs its travel. Each shovel can load 3600 / 167 = 21.56 loads per hour; C01 takes 40.0 on its one position; D01 takes 127.1 on three. The fleet supplies 37 truck-hours of working time per hour (time usage is treated in ch-21-01 and ch-21-03).

The empty arcs let a truck that has tipped at C01 go to EX03, as long as every node balances. The LP pools the fleet across circuits; the baseline allocation of the fleet model ties each truck to its home shovel.

4.2 The formulation

With payload P = 220 t, loaded-arc times t(u,d) and empty-arc times e(d,u) in truck-hours, shovel rates Su, destination rates Dd and N working trucks, the LP is [H]:

max &P∑u,dx(u,d)s.t. &∑dx(u,d)≤Su  ∀u,∑ux(u,d)≤Dd  ∀d,&∑u,dt(u,d)x(u,d)+∑d,ue(d,u)y(d,u)≤N,&∑dy(d,u)=∑dx(u,d)  ∀u,∑ux(u,d)=∑uy(d,u)  ∀d,x,y≥0.

The truck-hour row is Little's law in its simplest form: trucks = rate × time per trip. Optional rows add a minimum rate per material or a waste-to-ore ratio. ahs_sim.economics.build_lp writes the matrices for scipy.optimize.linprog (HiGHS); solve_flow reports the binding rows, the slack and the duals, and picks among equal optima the one that uses the fewest truck-hours, so that the empty flows are not arbitrary [H]. An infeasible problem is reported with the solver's message, never patched.

4.3 The 2 × 2 case by hand

tiny_flow_problem has two shovels S1 (ore) and S2 (waste), 24 loads per hour each; destinations D1 and D2, 30 each; payload 100 t; 20 working trucks. A load from S1 takes 0.30 truck-hours and the empty return 0.20; a load from S2 0.50 and 0.25. The balance rows give y1=x1 and y2=x2, and the LP reduces to

max 100(x1+x2)s.t.x1≤24,  x2≤24,  0.5x1+0.75x2≤20,  x1,x2≥0.

The destination limits cannot bind. The shared resource is truck time: a truck-hour on S1 carries 100 / 0.5 = 200 t, on S2 100 / 0.75 = 133.3 t. Fill S1 first: x1 = 24 uses 12 truck-hours; the other 8 give x2 = 8 / 0.75 = 10.667. The optimum is 100 × (24 + 10.667) = 3,466.7 t/h, with S1's rate and the truck-hours binding.

The shadow prices follow from the same reasoning. One more truck-hour goes to S2: +133.3 t/h. One more S1 load per hour adds 100 t but takes 0.5 truck-hours from S2, worth 66.7 t: +33.3 t/h. Everything else has slack: zero.

LAB-21-02-01 solves the case with solve_flow and gets the same flows, binding rows and shadow prices. Re-solving with one more truck-hour and one more S1 load raises the optimum by 133.33 and 33.33 t/h. The reader now knows what the solver's numbers mean.

4.4 The reference mine with R05 open

LAB-21-02-01 solves the mine for 37 working trucks:

Arc Loads per hour Truck-hours per load
EX01 → C01 18.44 0.2959
EX02 → C01 21.56 0.2947
EX03 → D01 15.61 0.3476
EX04 → D01 21.56 0.3433
Empty C01 → EX01 / EX03 / EX04 18.44 / 15.61 / 5.95 0.1409 / 0.1549 / 0.1505
Empty D01 → EX02 / EX04 21.56 / 15.61 0.1691 / 0.1799

The optimum is 16,976 t/h — 8,800 t/h of ore and 8,176 t/h of waste — or 203,712 t per 12-hour shift. EX02's rate, EX04's rate, C01's rate and the truck-hours bind; EX01 has 3.11 loads per hour of slack, EX03 5.95, D01 a great deal.

  • C01 is still the ore bottleneck. 40.0 loads per hour reach it, split between EX02 (at its limit) and EX01, whose cycle is a few seconds longer. No allocation gives more ore (ch-10-01, section 4.3).
  • The empty flows cross circuits. Trucks tipping at C01 go on to EX03 and EX04, trucks tipping at D01 to EX02. The truck-hours split 18.05 to ore and 18.95 to waste, where the baseline puts 19 trucks on ore and 18 on waste: the ore circuit needs about one truck fewer than it has.
  • The waste shovels are not equal. EX04's cycle is shorter (0.5232 against 0.5319 truck-hours per load with the return), so it runs at its limit and EX03 takes the remaining truck time — the 2 × 2 logic again.

4.5 Shadow prices in shift terms

Constraint Shadow price (t/h per unit) Per 12-hour shift Reading
Truck-hours (one more working truck) 413.6 4,964 t the marginal truck works EX03
C01 rate (one more load per hour) 39.3 472 t needs truck time taken from EX03
EX04 rate (one more load per hour) 3.6 43 t almost nothing
EX02 rate (one more load per hour) 1.0 12 t almost nothing
EX01, EX03, D01 rates 0 0 slack

The marginal truck-hour goes to EX03, the waste shovel with spare rate; one more C01 load takes an EX01 cycle of truck time away from it:

λtruck=PcEX03=220 t0.5319 h=413.6 t/h,λC01=P−cEX01λtruck=220−0.4368×413.6=39.3 t/h.

cEX03 = 0.5319 h is EX03's own free cycle, 1,914.7 s, as in ch-10-01's route table. In shift terms for the Pit Supervisor [A]:

  • One more truck is worth about 4,960 t per shift — if it goes to EX03 or EX04. The fleet model agrees on direction: with DT40 (not scheduled in the baseline) added to EX03 or EX04 under fixed assignment the shift gives 196,020 t, 4,840 t more; added to EX01 or EX02, 191,180 t, nothing more. A shadow price is the value of a unit where the LP would put it.
  • One more load per hour at C01 is worth about 470 t per shift, not the 2,640 t of 12 extra ore loads. Valuing a C01 upgrade at "220 t per extra load" overstates it more than five times in this model.
  • Shovel rates are almost worthless at the margin. The fleet is short of truck time, not of shovel capacity.

Shadow prices hold for small changes. A large change moves the optimum to another set of binding constraints, and the LP must be solved again (section 4.9).

4.6 Why the optimum (203,712 t) is above the fleet model (191,180 t)

The deterministic fleet model of ch-10-01, with fixed assignment, delivers 191,180 t on the undisturbed DAY shift (ore 103,180 t, waste 88,000 t), 94 % of the bound. LAB-21-02-01 splits the gap of 12,532 t with a lab-local LP on the same FlowProblem in which the trucks are split as in the baseline (19 ore, 18 waste) and cannot change circuit:

Model Tonnes per shift Difference
LP, trucks pooled across circuits 203,712 —
LP per circuit (19 ore, 18 waste trucks) 195,876 pooling: 7,836 t
Fleet model, fixed assignment 191,180 dynamics: 4,696 t

Pooling (7,836 t). The per-circuit LP still feeds C01 its 40 loads per hour, but 18 waste trucks bring only 7,523 t/h of waste against 8,176 t/h when pooled. The pooled LP hauls waste with the truck time that the 19th ore truck spends waiting at C01-Q — in the fleet model 16.55 truck-hours of DLY-NP-02 per shift.

Dynamics (4,696 t). The fleet model starts with every truck at its shovel and C01 empty; the first load reaches C01 after 1,061 s. At 40 loads per hour those 17.7 minutes are about 11 loads, and the fleet model indeed delivers 469 ore loads against the LP's 480. Waste loses about the same at the start, and loads in progress at the end are not counted. Variability, absent from the deterministic model, adds bunching and widens the gap (ch-10-01, section 4.5).

The lesson for the board [R]: show the optimum as a bound, not as the plan. A shift that delivers 191,180 t has not lost 12,532 t; at most the pooling term is recoverable, and only if trucks may change circuits.

4.7 R05 closed: the west ramp as the pit exit

With R05 closed (SCN-MAP-R05) the AMS routes the trucks from Z down R09 to W, up R24 to P3, along R25 to V and R12 to X. M-20481's loaded haul from EX02 to C01 grows from 4,500.5 m to 5,865.1 m, and EX02's free cycle from 1,564.1 s to 1,800.1 s (0.4345 to 0.5000 h). The waste cycles shrink slightly (EX03 0.5319 to 0.5209 h), because bench traffic no longer descends R09 to climb R05 (ch-10-01, section 4.8). LAB-21-02-01 re-solves with closed_segments=("R05",):

The LP keeps C01 full by moving 2.63 truck-hours per hour from waste to the longer ore route, and lets waste fall by 936 t/h. The binding set stays; the prices move. A truck is worth slightly more (EX03's cycle is shorter); extra crusher capacity is worth almost nothing, because every extra ore load costs a long ore cycle of truck time.

Under fixed assignment the fleet model gives 187,440 t with R05 closed (ch-10-01): the 19 ore trucks become the limit and 5,940 t of ore are lost. The per-circuit LP gives 192,290 t, only 195 t below the pooled LP; what matters is the direction of the re-plan. The LP puts about 2.6 trucks' worth of working time more on ore [A]; ch-10-01 found that moving one waste truck to ore recovers 5,060 t of ore for 4,840 t of waste. Whether the ore is worth the waste that day is the Pit Supervisor's decision.

4.8 When the network is cut: R03 closed

R03 and R04 are the only road from X to D01. With R03 closed fms.fleet_times finds no route from EX03 or EX04 to D01. flow_allocation does not invent one: it lists EX03 and EX04 as unreachable and solves without them.

  • Without a waste requirement the LP is feasible: ore only, 8,800 t/h (105,600 t per shift), C01 and EX02 binding. It uses 17.4 truck-hours per hour; 19.6 trucks have no useful work, and the truck shadow price is zero.
  • With a minimum waste rate of 1,000 t/h [A] the LP is infeasible: HiGHS returns status 2, "The problem is infeasible". No allocation can meet the plan; the plan or the closure has to change.

An infeasible result is information. An optimiser that silently dropped the waste requirement, or kept sending trucks towards a closed road, would mislead the controller. The decision belongs to people — park the spare trucks at PK1, re-plan the waste shovels or reopen R03 earlier — and the AMS will not route a truck over R03 while it is closed (REQ-10-01-005).

4.9 Two re-plans on the LP

C01 down. With C01's capacity set to zero (destination_availability) the LP runs both waste shovels at 21.56 loads per hour, 9,485 t/h, using 22.7 truck-hours per hour: 14.3 trucks have no useful work. The re-solved targets say what the normal ones cannot — ore targets zero, waste shovels full, the rest of the fleet parked rather than queued.

DZ03 at EX02. In SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02 the AMS reserves a work zone in EX02's loading area from T+120 s to T+900 s and holds the trucks at EX02-Q; EX02 cannot load. Re-solving with EX02 at zero gives 14,228 t/h: EX01 rises from 18.44 to 21.56 loads per hour, EX03 from 15.61 to 21.56, and about five trucks have no useful work for those 13 minutes. The board still shows the normal targets unless someone tells the FMS that EX02 is not loading (section 8.3).

4.11 Dispatch policies during SCN-CRUSHER-C01-DOWN

The registry gives the key events — C01 not ready at T+0 s, tipping bay withheld and trucks held at C01-Q at T+5 s, FMS reassignment proposal and controller approval at T+300 s — but no duration. LAB-21-02-02 stops C01 from 10:00 to 12:00 [A]; the policies react from T+300 s.

Without notice (a trip):

Policy Tonnes Ore Lost vs undisturbed DLY-OD-10 (truck-h) C01-Q queue per C01 load (s) Shovel idle (h)
Fixed 173,800 85,800 17,380 34.0 474 10.28
Minimise shovel idle 173,800 85,800 17,380 34.1 469 10.22
Minimise truck wait 173,140 85,360 18,040 33.9 471 10.43
Target-rate tracking 173,140 85,800 18,040 33.7 451 10.44

The policies barely differ: three loads between best and worst. Fixed assignment loses 17,380 t, ch-10-07's value for a 120-minute stop under hold. The reason is structural. When C01 trips, every ore truck is either loaded and committed to C01 or driving empty to an ore shovel. A truck is offered a new loading unit only when it has dumped, and no ore truck dumps while C01 is down. From T+300 s the policies may send ore trucks to waste, but have none to send; they differ only after the restart. Over ten paired seeds (20481 to 20490) [A] minimise shovel idle gives 792 t more than fixed on average (−220 to +1,540 t per seed), minimise truck wait 264 t less (−1,100 to +1,100) and target-rate tracking 132 t more (−440 to +1,100): a few loads, changing sign between seeds. Seed 20481 alone gives 167,860, 168,960, 166,980 and 168,080 t.

With 900 s of notice. If the plant sends STOPPING at 09:45 for a stop at 10:00 [A], the FMS acts from 09:50 (notice less the 300 s decision delay), and ore trucks that tip in the last ten minutes are dispatch points:

Minimise truck wait 175,780 83,600 92,180 20.7 327

Now the policies differ. Minimise shovel idle sends the trucks tipping during the notice to the waste shovels that have waited longest, gains 3,080 t over fixed and cuts DLY-OD-10 by 13 truck-hours; over ten seeds its gain averages 4,048 t and is positive on every seed (2,860 to 4,840 t). Fixed assignment ignores notice by definition. Target-rate tracking does worst of the adaptive policies: its targets assume C01 running, so it spreads the moved trucks by how far each unit is behind its normal target rather than by where they can load soonest, and its loading queues are the longest (128 s per load). A target-tracking policy is only as good as its targets.

4.13 Trade-offs and model limits

  • The LP is a steady-state bound: no queues, variability, start or end of shift, or road interaction. Use it for direction, shadow prices and feasibility; compare shifts with the fleet model.
  • Pooling is an assumption. If a site restricts circuit changes, the per-circuit LP is the right bound.
  • Dispatch points. Trucks are re-dispatched only after dumping; en-route reassignment (ch-10-07, ch-10-08) needs the AMS to cancel and replan missions.
  • No road congestion in fms: SCN-DEADLOCK-X is not modelled (section 7.2).
  • Assumed numbers: stop window, notice, seeds, plan margin and minimum waste rate are [A]; differences of one or two 220 t loads are within the model's resolution.

5. Responsibilities and interface boundaries

Element Responsibility in dispatch optimisation
FMS Solves the flow LP and shows it as a bound with its binding constraints; turns targets into assignments through the dispatch policy; proposes re-plans (hold, park, divert, reassign) with expected tonnes; records decisions, target changes and overrides
AMS Turns each assignment into a mission; routes it on the network as it is (closures, work zones); grants and withholds reservations; holds trucks at queue points and PK1; enforces traffic rules at Intersection X; detects and resolves deadlocks; may reject an assignment. No FMS target or policy changes any of this
ADS Drives the mission within its granted path; takes its own safety decisions, which no assignment overrides
VCS Actuation; reports faults that remove a truck from dispatch
Independent safety functions Stop trucks regardless of FMS and AMS state; a stopped truck is not dispatchable until it may move again
Onboard safety-related systems Payload, fire and tyre alarms can end a cycle and remove a truck from the optimiser's fleet
Plant (C01 control system) Sole source of C01's state, STOPPING notice and restart
People Pit Supervisor: owns the shift plan, approves policy and target changes and moves across materials; AHS Controller: approves proposals, overrides with a recorded reason, keeps inputs current; Shovel Operator: reports loading-unit state and rate limits; planner and FMS engineering: maintain the LP's inputs

The boundary is ch-10-01's: the FMS says what is wanted, the AMS decides what is safe and possible now. The optimiser sits entirely on the FMS side. Its outputs are rates and assignments (truck, loading unit, destination, material). It reads what the AMS reports — routes, closures, held trucks — but has no means to change a route, a speed, a reservation or the order of trucks at an intersection, and must not be given one (REQ-21-02-003).

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

Sections 6, 7 and 8 follow SCN-CRUSHER-C01-DOWN on one timeline; T+0 s is 10:00:00 on the DAY shift. Registry events: T+0 s, T+5 s and T+300 s. The other times are [A]: the alarm at T+30 s (ch-02-06), the restart at T+7,200 s (the lab's window) and, for the planned variant, STOPPING at T−900 s.

6.1 Normal operation: targets as a bound

Before 10:00 the FMS shows the LP optimum (203,712 t; C01 and truck-hours binding) as a bound with the fleet model's expectation alongside. The policy follows the targets, and each assignment goes to the AMS, which routes it and grants reservations as on any shift.

6.2 Abnormal operation: an unplanned C01 stop

At T+0 s C01 trips. Ore trucks on R02 and in C01-Q stop under the AMS's hold; empty trucks heading for EX01 and EX02 will be loaded. At T+300 s the proposal can reach almost no one: in the fleet model only DT19, tipping at T+0 s, reaches a dispatch point before the restart (ch-10-07). The policies lose 17,380 to 18,040 t; the re-plan options lie within 880 t. The decision is made on traffic grounds: hold at C01-Q while the held count stays below the hold limit, otherwise park at PK1. The AMS holds until READY and releases the queue one bay grant at a time.

6.3 Degraded operation: the optimiser's inputs are wrong

  • C01 down, targets unchanged. Until the targets are re-solved, target-rate tracking spreads trucks by targets that assume C01 running; with notice it delivers 3,300 t less than minimise shovel idle (173,580 against 176,880 t).
  • DZ03 at EX02, EX02 still at 21.56. If nobody reports the clean-up, the board keeps sending trucks to EX02-Q, where the AMS holds them. In the fleet model a DZ03 work zone from T+120 s to T+900 s after 10:00 [A] costs 660 t under fixed assignment (190,520 t) and 1,760 t under target-rate tracking (189,420 t), whose loading queues over the shift grow from 14.7 to 20.0 truck-hours.

The remedy is an input, not another algorithm: the Shovel Operator reports the clean-up, the plant state reaches the FMS, the LP is re-solved. Until then the controller overrides and records why.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

  • Show the LP as a bound with its binding constraints. Its value is in what binds, what an extra truck is worth and whether a plan is feasible — not in its tonnage.
  • Solve on the network as the AMS sees it. Arc times come from current routes, including closures (R05) and cut-offs (R03), read from the same source as the AMS.
  • Report infeasibility; never relax silently. "Infeasible: D01 unreachable; EX03, EX04 cut off" is the right output with R03 closed and a waste requirement.
  • Prefer simple, explainable policies. The four policies differ by three loads undisturbed and during a trip, by up to 15 loads with notice. Minimise shovel idle and minimise truck wait can be explained in one line on the HMI.
  • Reachability is an AMS capability. Making trucks reachable during a stop (en-route reassignment) needs mission cancellation and replanning (ch-10-08); a STOPPING notice from the plant achieves much of it at far less cost.
  • Count moves. About 380 to 450 reassignments a shift are 32 to 37 an hour, each a mission and a set of reservations.

7.2 Failure modes and mitigations

Failure mode Effect Mitigation
LP shown as the plan Every shift appears about 12,500 t short Bound and fleet-model expectation side by side (REQ-21-02-002)
LP on the open network while R05 is closed Targets the routes cannot meet Solve on the AMS's current network; re-solve on every change (REQ-21-02-004)
Infeasibility hidden (R03 closed) Trucks sent towards a closed road; waste plan silently dropped Report unreachable units and infeasibility (REQ-21-02-005)
Targets not re-solved when C01 stops Target tracking spreads trucks by wrong targets Capacity zero, re-solve, pause targets (REQ-21-02-006)

Dispatch adds conflicting movements at X Held C01 queue spills back; SCN-DEADLOCK-X more likely AMS hold limit and staged release (ch-15-06); deadlock handling by the AMS (ch-10-05)
Override not recorded Model never learns of clean-ups or plant behaviour Structured override record (REQ-21-02-007)

SCN-DEADLOCK-X, treated qualitatively. After a stop and restart, trucks arriving at X from R01, R02 and R03 each hold one conflict cell inside X and wait for a cell held by another; the wait-for graph closes into a cycle. The AMS detects no progress at T+0 s, finds the cycle at T+30 s and applies its resolution policy at T+35 s (ch-10-05); the trucks are coded DLY-OD-17 until it is resolved.

ahs_sim.fms, on which every policy here runs, has no road interaction — no conflict cells, reservations or passing at X. The policy comparison therefore cannot show whether a policy makes SCN-DEADLOCK-X more or less likely, and this chapter does not claim that it can. From the mechanisms [H]:

  • Every haul cycle passes X twice. Heavy reassignment does not change the number of passages, but it changes their mix and timing — more movements between R02 and R03, and bunched arrivals when trucks held for C01 are released towards several shovels at once after a restart.
  • A held C01 queue that spills back along R02 puts loaded ore trucks beside the conflict area that waste trucks use every cycle (ch-15-06, section 4.5). A rule that keeps loading ore trucks during a C01 stop feeds that queue.
  • The defences are AMS functions: the hold limit, routing further held trucks to PK1, staged release, conflict-cell reservations at X, deadlock detection and resolution. The optimiser's part is to stop proposing ore loads while C01 is down and to show the held count against the limit.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

  • Inputs, not solve time, set the pace. The mine's LP solves in milliseconds. It should be re-solved on every change of closures (AMS), loading-unit states and rate limits (Shovel Operators) and C01 state (plant gateway), not on a timer alone [R].
  • Decision delay. Without notice no ore truck is reachable at T+300 s; with notice the delay is subtracted from it. A decision delay longer than the notice wastes the notice.
  • ICD boundary. An FMS assignment carries only the assignment ID, truck, loading unit, destination and material (REQ-10-01-005). Targets, flows and shadow prices stay in the FMS and on the HMI.
  • Fail closed on inputs. A stale or missing C01 state is treated as not ready (ch-02-06, ch-15-03); the optimiser takes the same view.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

On the dispatch board [H], beside the panels of ch-10-01:

  • Targets per loading unit in shift terms: trucks per hour, one truck every n minutes, planned utilisation, trucks on the circuit, expected queue (the derated targets of section 4.12).
  • Bound and expectation: the LP optimum labelled as a bound, with its binding constraints in words, and the fleet model's expectation.
  • Re-plan proposals during a destination stop: hold, park, divert, reassign, each with reachable trucks and expected tonnes, and the plant's estimate.
  • Input status (closures, rate limits such as "EX02: DZ03 work zone, not loading", C01 state and age) and open overrides with person, time and reason.

8.3 Procedure excerpt — dispatch during a C01 stop

  1. AHS Controller (T−900 s, planned variant): on STOPPING, acknowledge, inform the Pit Supervisor, check the FMS shows the stop time; ask the plant for the expected duration and record it with its source.
  2. AHS Controller (T+0 s to T+30 s): acknowledge the C01 alarm; phone the plant for cause and estimate; note the number of trucks held.
  3. FMS (T+0 s): set C01's capacity to zero, re-solve the LP, pause ore targets.
  4. AHS Controller and Pit Supervisor (T+300 s): read the proposal. Without notice expect almost no reachable trucks and decide on traffic grounds (hold below the hold limit, otherwise park at PK1). With notice and an estimate above about an hour [A], consider divert. Record the decision and the estimate used.
  5. AHS Controller (during the stop): keep the held count below the hold limit; ask the plant again every 30 min [A]; raise a changed estimate with the Pit Supervisor if it changes the best option.
  6. AHS Controller (T+7,200 s): confirm READY with the plant by voice; let the AMS release the queue one bay grant at a time; check that ore targets are restored and moved trucks and shovels are returning.
  7. AHS Controller (end of shift): confirm DLY-OD-10 for held time and DLY-NP-02 after the restart; check that every override has a reason; record the actual stop duration.

For DZ03 at EX02 the roles are the same: the Shovel Operator requests DZ03 and reports "EX02 not loading — clean-up"; the controller sets EX02's target to zero for the work zone (T+120 s to T+900 s in the registry), records the override and restores it when the zone is released. Trucks held at EX02-Q for the clean-up are coded DLY-OD-04 (coding rules); the model's DLY-OD-11 for them is recoded.

8.5 Training and competency implications

Controllers and Pit Supervisors need to read the LP as a bound with reasons and a shadow price in trucks and tonnes per shift; to know that without notice almost nobody is reachable during a C01 stop, while with notice timing matters; and to ask the plant for STOPPING with a time. Shovel Operators need targets in their terms and the habit of reporting a rate limit or clean-up at once. Everyone needs to know which decisions the optimiser never makes. LAB-21-02-02's C01 and DZ03 runs suit a joint controller and supervisor session.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-21-02-001 [H] The FMS flow optimisation shall compute truck-flow targets from the arc times of the AMS's routes on the current road network, the currently reported rates of loading units and destinations and the working trucks, and shall report with every solution its binding constraints and the shadow prices of the truck-hours and of each destination. Rationale: the binding constraints and shadow prices, not the tonnage, say what limits the shift and where an extra truck is worth something (sections 4.4, 4.5). Verification: test (LAB-21-02-01: 2 × 2 hand solution and perturbation; R05 open and closed); inspection.

REQ-21-02-002 [H] The FMS shall present the LP optimum as an upper bound, labelled as such, together with an expectation for the same inputs from a model that includes queueing and the start and end of the shift. Rationale: the optimum is 12,532 t above the fleet model on the undisturbed shift; shown as a plan it makes every shift look short (section 4.6). Verification: inspection of the HMI; analysis (LAB-21-02-01 gap decomposition).

REQ-21-02-003 [H] Dispatch optimisation shall act only through assignments of truck, loading unit, destination and material. No target, policy or optimisation output shall set or change a route, a speed, a reservation, a hold, a work zone, the order of trucks at an intersection or a traffic rule, and the AMS shall enforce its traffic rules and reservations on every assignment regardless of its origin. Rationale: the FMS optimises assignments; the AMS executes missions and enforces reservations and traffic rules (sections 5, 7.1, 7.3). Verification: inspection of ICD-FMS-AMS and the optimiser's outputs; test (an assignment needing a closed route is rejected by the AMS).

REQ-21-02-004 [H] The FMS shall re-solve the flow optimisation when a road segment is closed or reopened, when a loading unit or destination changes state or reported rate, and when the working fleet changes; target-rate tracking shall not use targets computed for the previous state. Rationale: targets for another network or a running C01 misdirect trucks (sections 4.7, 4.9, 6.3). Verification: test (LAB-21-02-01 R05 and C01-down re-solves; LAB-21-02-02 target-rate tracking with unchanged targets).

REQ-21-02-005 [H] When a closure leaves a loading unit or destination unreachable, or the optimisation is infeasible, the FMS shall report the unreachable units and the requirement that cannot be met, and shall not relax a production requirement or assign trucks to unreachable units without a recorded decision of the Pit Supervisor. Rationale: with R03 closed D01 is unreachable and a waste requirement cannot be met (section 4.8). Verification: test (LAB-21-02-01 R03 case: EX03 and EX04 unreachable; infeasible with a minimum waste rate).

REQ-21-02-006 [H] When a destination is reported not ready or announces a stop, the FMS shall set its capacity to zero from the time the stop takes effect, shall propose a re-plan that lists only the trucks it can reach with the expected tonnes of hold, park, divert and reassignment over the estimated stop, and shall not propose loading further trucks for that destination while it is down. Rationale: without notice the options differ by a few loads, with notice by thousands of tonnes; loading more ore trucks during a stop feeds the held queue near Intersection X (sections 4.11, 7.2). Verification: test (LAB-21-02-02 C01 runs without and with notice); inspection of the proposal.

REQ-21-02-007 [H] Every override of a dispatch recommendation, a target or a re-plan proposal shall be recorded with the person, the time, the affected trucks or loading units, the reason as a category and free text, and the delay code of the resulting waiting. Rationale: overrides carry what the model does not know (clean-ups, digging conditions, plant behaviour) (sections 6.3, 8.3). Verification: inspection of the override record; demonstration with the DZ03 case.

REQ-21-02-008 [H] A change of dispatch policy or plan margin shall be evaluated before use on a fleet model with a C01 stop without and with notice and on at least ten paired seeds, reporting tonnes by material, waiting by place and delay code, shovel idle time and reassignments per shift. Rationale: policy differences of a few loads change sign between seeds; their value appears around plant stops and depends on notice (sections 4.10, 4.11). Verification: analysis (LAB-21-02-02).

10. Verification and validation

Verification — make test CH=ch-21-02 checks: the 2 × 2 LP against the hand solution and by perturbation; the mine with R05 open and closed (tonnes, flows, binding constraints, shadow prices; the truck price equals payload over EX03's cycle); R03 closed (unreachable, infeasible, status reported); the gap decomposition and the marginal-truck runs; the four policies undisturbed and with the C01 stop without and with notice, the notice sweep, the seed study, the response options, the DZ03 run, the targets in shift terms and the EX04 loop; the figures in both languages, with the SVG drawing its own arrowheads.

Validation — not possible with these models: whether pooled flows are acceptable at a site; the real distribution of C01 stop durations and the notice the plant can give (the registry gives no duration); the effect of policies at Intersection X (needs reservations, ch-10-04 and ch-10-05, or field data); the reachability of trucks with a site's AMS.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Solving the LP on the open network while R05 is closed.
  • Treating the optimum as achievable; the shortfall against a steady-state bound is mostly start-up and queueing.
  • Planning shovels at 100 % of their rate, which with any variability means an unbounded queue.
  • Letting the optimiser specify routes or priorities at X (REQ-21-02-003).
  • Hiding infeasibility by relaxing a plan requirement in code.
  • Refining the policy when the dispatch point, not the policy, limits what can be done during a stop.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Telling a crew "you were 12,500 t short" against the bound.
  • Valuing C01 capacity at 220 t per extra load (about 39 t/h with R05 open, 8 with R05 closed).
  • Adding a truck to the ore circuit while C01 limits ore; the fleet model gains nothing.
  • Not asking the plant for notice — or acting on it half an hour early.
  • Not reporting DZ03; the optimiser cannot see the work zone.
  • Overriding without a reason: the override fixes the shift, the reason fixes the model.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — The 2 × 2 LP by hand. Cycles 0.30 + 0.20 = 0.5 and 0.50 + 0.25 = 0.75 truck-h per load; 200 and 133.3 t per truck-hour. S1 first: 24 × 0.5 = 12 truck-h; 20 − 12 = 8 truck-h → x2 = 10.667. Tonnes 100 × 34.667 = 3,466.7 t/h. Shadow prices: 133.3 t per truck-hour; 100 − 0.5 × 133.3 = 33.3 t per S1 load.

Example 2 — An extra truck in shift terms. 413.6 t/h × 12 h = 4,964 t per shift, if the truck goes to EX03. Fleet model, DT40 at EX03: 196,020 − 191,180 = 4,840 t (22 loads); at EX01: 0 t. At 2 cu per tonne [A] the same truck is worth about 9,700 cu per shift or nothing, depending on where it is dispatched.

Example 3 — A derated target for EX04. At 90 % of 21.56 [A]: 19.4 loads/h, one truck every 60 / 19.4 = 3.1 min; trucks needed 19.4 × 0.5232 = 10.15. Seeded loop with 10 trucks: 18.80 loads/h, utilisation 0.87, mean queue 93 s. LP cost of the margin on all shovels: 203,712 − 203,029 = 683 t per shift.

15. Key takeaways

  1. The truck-flow LP spreads the fleet's working time over shovels and destinations; its truck-hour row is "trucks = rate × time per trip". Solve a small case by hand first.
  2. With R05 open, C01, the truck-hours and two shovel rates bind; an extra truck is worth about 4,960 t per shift — at a waste shovel only — and an extra C01 load per hour about 470 t.
  3. The optimum (203,712 t) is a bound: about 7,800 t of the gap to the fleet model (191,180 t) is pooling of trucks across circuits, about 4,700 t start-up and shift end.
  4. With R05 closed the optimum moves truck time to ore (192,485 t); with R03 closed D01 is unreachable and a waste requirement is infeasible — reported, not hidden.
  5. During an unplanned C01 stop the policies barely differ, because loaded trucks keep their destination; with notice they differ by thousands of tonnes, and notice used too early gives the gain back.
  6. Re-solve targets when the mine changes, and show them in shift terms below 100 % of shovel rate.
  7. The FMS assigns; the AMS executes and enforces reservations and traffic rules. Optimisation never bypasses them, and effects at X such as SCN-DEADLOCK-X are outside the fleet model.
  8. Overrides with reasons are how the model learns what it cannot see.
