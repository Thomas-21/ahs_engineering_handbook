1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

Thursday, DAY shift. The Survey lead has three requests on the whiteboard in CR1. The R05 windrow and edge rework is booked for Tuesday, and the Pit Supervisor wants the as-built "the moment the dozer is off the ramp". EX02 moves to a new face next week, and the AHS map needs its new loading area before the shovel can load. And yesterday afternoon the AHS Controller watched trucks derate one after another on lower R05 (SCN-GNSS-R05) and asked Survey to "check GB1". The workshop adds a fourth item: GB1's antenna has to be moved next month for building works at the MIA.

The map team asks the question that sits under all four: "When you hand us coordinates, what are they — which grid, which control, which epoch, how good, and who checked them?" Nobody answers at once. The AHS Controller asks a second one: "To survey R24 before Tuesday, does your crew enter at AP-04 or AP-02, and what does the permission hold?"

The questions this meeting raises are the subject of the chapter:

  • What chain connects a survey mark on the ground to a lane in the AHS map, and where can it break?
  • What must a survey deliver to the AHS, and which accuracy attributes must each deliverable carry?
  • How far does an error in survey control move a map feature, and does the geometry of the control matter?
  • What must be checked when the base station GB1 is moved or replaced, and why does it matter for every truck?
  • Which survey request goes first, and how does a Survey crew work inside an autonomous area?

This chapter covers:

  • the survey control chain from survey marks through the local mine grid REFMINE-GRID to AHS map coordinates, and the role of GB1 (FIG-12-01-01);
  • the site transformation and error propagation from control to map features, computed and checked by Monte Carlo in LAB-12-01-01;
  • survey deliverables for the AHS and the survey hand-off package the map team accepts (FIG-12-01-02);
  • GB1 checks after a move, a replacement or repeated degradation alarms;
  • surveying inside autonomous areas under digital access permissions, scheduling the west-ramp survey before an R05 closure, and prioritising competing survey requests.

Photogrammetry and drones are ch-12-02; mobile LiDAR ch-12-03; terrain models ch-12-04; as-built validation ch-12-07; the AHS map data model ch-12-08; map change management ch-12-09. Every accuracy, tolerance and error value in this chapter is an illustrative assumption [A]; survey accuracy requirements and geodetic standards are topics only [TBV].

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Explain the survey control chain from survey marks through the local mine grid (REFMINE-GRID) to AHS map coordinates, and the role of RTK base station GB1.
  2. Specify the survey deliverables the AHS needs (road edges, windrows, loading areas, dump edges, intersections) and the accuracy attributes each must carry, leaving values to the site [A].
  3. Calculate how a survey control error propagates into AHS map feature positions in a worked example [A].
  4. Specify the survey checks required when GB1 is moved or replaced, and their effect on map and localisation consistency.
  5. Design the survey hand-off package (coordinate system, epoch, method, accuracy statement, author) that the map team accepts.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Plan a survey task inside AA-North with digital access permission, including entry at AP-02 and coordination with the AHS Controller.
  2. Prioritise competing survey requests (R05 rework, EX02 face move, GB1 check after GNSS degradation) by production and safety impact.
  3. Schedule a survey of the west ramp R24, crest P3 and Intersection V before a planned R05 closure so that the detour is mapped from current data.
  4. Brief the Survey crew on the choice between AP-02 and AP-04 for pit entry and on what their permission area covers.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

An autonomous truck drives where the AMS grants it a path, and the AMS plans on the AHS map. The truck knows where it is from GNSS positioning corrected by the RTK base station GB1, refined by its own sensing (Volume 05). Both sides therefore depend on survey: the map's lanes, edges and zones come from surveyed geometry, and GB1's own position was surveyed. If the map and the truck's position do not rest on the same control, the truck can be exactly where its receiver says and still not be where the map says. The vehicle's own sensing stops it if the road ahead differs from what it expects (Volume 06), but a site that relies on that has already lost the map as a planning tool.

The reference mine sets the frame. All positions are easting, northing and reduced level in REFMINE-GRID, a local engineering grid in metres (mine.yaml). It is deliberately not a geodetic coordinate reference system, so that the fictional mine cannot be placed on the Earth (ch-01-03). GB1 stands at the MIA at (−1450, 250, RL 425), about 4.1 km from the EX02 loading spot (ch-01-03). R05 carries high GNSS multipath risk and R24, R07, R08, R17 and R18 medium (mine.yaml); six segments, 4.09 km of road, are medium or high (ch-01-03).

Four pieces of work at the reference mine need Survey in the coming weeks:

  • SCN-MAP-R05. R05 is closed for windrow and edge rework; pit traffic climbs the west ramp R24 and the loaded leg of M-20481 grows from 4.50 km to 5.87 km (LAB-12-01-01 recomputes both with ahs_sim.mine). The as-built of R05 is the input to the map change; the detour's map must be current before the closure.
  • SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE. The AMS activates EX02's new loading area at T+3600 s (registry). The area is only as good as the survey of the new floor and face toe.
  • SCN-GNSS-R05. At T+300 s the AHS Controller calls Survey to check GB1 and satellite availability (registry). What "check GB1" means has not yet been defined; ch-04-02 and ch-16-07 record only the outcome.
  • GB1 antenna move. A planned move or replacement of the base station changes the anchor of every RTK position on site.

The production stakes differ by more than an order of magnitude. LAB-12-01-01 runs the deterministic shift of ahs_sim.fms: each hour that R05 stays closed costs 311.7 t, because the detour carries the traffic (the same figure ch-16-04 found for a permission on R05 and R06); each hour that EX02 stands without a valid loading area costs 3960 t, under fixed and under dynamic dispatch alike. Neither figure says anything about GB1, which touches every truck at once. Section 4.10 turns these numbers into a priority rule.

3. Terminology and foundations

Survey, the activity and the role. Survey as an activity (glossary survey) is the measurement of positions and shapes on site. Survey as a role (glossary survey-role) is the reference-mine role that owns road and map data on site and checks the GNSS infrastructure (mine.yaml).

Survey control and the control register. Survey control is the set of survey marks on stable ground whose coordinates are known and published, from which all other survey is derived. The control register [H] lists each mark's published easting, northing and RL in REFMINE-GRID, its status (in use, suspect, disturbed, destroyed), the date of its last check and the register version. In the lab the marks are labelled CM1 to CM9; these are lab labels [A], not reference-mine IDs.

Local mine grid and reduced level. REFMINE-GRID is a plane grid in metres (glossary local-mine-grid); RL is the height above the site's fictional datum (glossary reduced-level). A real site relates its grid to a geodetic datum and a map projection (glossary geodetic-datum); how that is done, and which datum and projection apply, are topics only here [TBV] (SRC-PUB-GEODETIC-DATUM-EPOCH).

Working frame and site transformation. Survey instruments and GNSS rovers deliver coordinates in a frame of their own — in the lab a teaching working frame [A]. The site transformation [H] converts working-frame coordinates to REFMINE-GRID. It is fitted on control marks observed in both frames and is itself a controlled item with a version. Survey software often calls this step a site calibration [TBV]; the handbook avoids the word "localisation" for it because that word means the truck's own position estimate (glossary localisation).

Similarity (Helmert) transformation. The 2D transform used by ahs_sim.mapping: one scale, one rotation, two shifts (section 4.3). Heights are handled separately by an offset.

Residual and check point. A residual is the difference between a mark's published coordinate and its fitted position. A check point is a point of known coordinates that is not used in the fit and is observed to test it; the same idea serves ground control in photogrammetry (ch-12-02).

RTK and the base station. RTK positioning corrects a rover with carrier-phase corrections from a base station at a known position (glossary real-time-kinematic, base-station). At the reference mine GB1 provides the corrections for trucks and survey rovers alike, carried over the site network (ch-11-01).

Epoch. In this handbook the survey epoch [H] is the date and time that a set of observations represents. Open pits change by the hour, so a coordinate without an epoch cannot be compared with anything. Geodetic epochs — the reference date of a datum on a moving continent — are a related but different topic [TBV].

Accuracy attribute and accuracy statement. An accuracy attribute is the stated uncertainty attached to a surveyed feature, horizontally and vertically, with the confidence level it refers to. An accuracy statement [H] says how that figure was obtained: from check points, from a model, or from the instrument's specification. The handbook gives the format; the site gives the values [A].

Error propagation. How uncertainty in inputs (control marks, observations) becomes uncertainty in outputs (map feature positions). The lab propagates it twice: with a closed-form first-order expression and by Monte Carlo — 2000 simulated surveys with seeded noise [A].

Survey hand-off package. The bundle that travels from Survey to the map team with every delivery [H] (section 4.8).

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The control chain

FIG-12-01-01 shows the chain from the ground to the truck.

Read it from the top. Survey marks on stable ground carry published coordinates in the control register. Two things are derived from those marks: the site transformation, which turns instrument coordinates into REFMINE-GRID, and the position of GB1, which every RTK rover on site — survey rovers and trucks — implicitly uses. Survey observes road edges, windrows, loading areas, dump edges and intersections, transforms them into the grid and hands them over as a package. The map team edits and validates a new map version (ch-12-08, ch-12-09); the AMS plans and grants on the deployed version; the truck's ADS localises with GB1's corrections.

Every link can break, and each break has a different signature [H]:

Link How it breaks What it looks like downstream
Marks A mark is disturbed by bench work, a grader or a blast; its published coordinate is now wrong Residuals at that mark grow; features near it move (section 4.5)
Register Two versions in use; a mark re-coordinated but not re-issued Two crews deliver the same road at different positions
Site transformation Fitted on marks clustered on one side; an old version reused Errors grow with distance from the control (section 4.4)
GB1 position Base moved or replaced and given a wrong coordinate Every RTK position on site shifts by the same vector (section 4.6)
Observations Multipath near highwalls; poor satellite geometry Large scatter on lower R05; the feature's own error dominates
Hand-off Frame, epoch or control version missing The map team cannot compare the delivery with the map (section 4.8)

The last row of FIG-12-01-01 is the point of the chapter: the map and the truck's position agree only while both rest on the same control and the same GB1 coordinate.

4.2 The local mine grid and what it is not

REFMINE-GRID has three properties that matter for survey. It is plane: distances and grades are read directly from coordinates, which is why mine plans, survey and machine guidance use a site grid [H]. It is local: its origin is the crusher C01 at (0, 0, RL 424), and it has no defined relation to latitude and longitude. And it is fictional by design: road-network.geojson declares it as "LOCAL:REFMINE-GRID (metres; NOT WGS 84)" (ch-01-03).

For this chapter the consequence is simple. Every coordinate in the reference mine, every survey delivery and every map version is in REFMINE-GRID and RL. Where a real site would carry a documented link from its grid to a national datum, the reference mine carries none, and the handbook does not invent one. The lab's working frame is a teaching device: a plane frame related to the grid by a scale of 1.000150, a rotation of 0.40° and shifts of 1000 m and −500 m [A], plus a height offset of −12.0 m [A]. It shows what a transformation does; it describes no real projection.

4.3 The site transformation

The 2D similarity transform of ahs_sim.mapping.Similarity2D maps working-frame coordinates (e,n) to grid coordinates (e′,n′):

e′=s(cosθe−sinθn)+te,n′=s(sinθe+cosθn)+tn

fit_similarity estimates the four parameters by least squares from at least two control marks observed in both frames, and returns the residuals at the marks. With more marks than the minimum, the residuals show how well the marks agree with each other and with the transform.

LAB-12-01-01 places six control marks CM1–CM6 around the area the AHS map covers (network A, [A]): near the MIA and GB1, on the west rim beyond P3, on the south rim near P1, on the east rim above P2, on the north rim beyond EX02, and near D01. Each mark is observed in the working frame with a per-axis error of 0.02 m [A]. One seeded survey gives:

Quantity True [A] Fitted
Scale 150 ppm 151.7 ppm
Rotation 0.40° 0.4000°
RMS residual at the six marks — 0.0259 m
Largest residual — 0.0419 m at CM3

Heights are transformed by an offset fitted as the mean of the six differences between published RL and observed height. The fitted offset is −12.007 m against the true −12.0 m; over 2000 simulated surveys its standard deviation is 0.0122 m, equal to σ/n=0.03/6 with a height error of 0.03 m per observation [A]. Real sites may model height with more than an offset (a tilted plane, a geoid model); that is a topic here [TBV].

4.4 Error propagation from control to map features

Once fitted, the transform is applied to every surveyed feature, and its errors go with it. For a 2D similarity fitted on n marks with independent per-axis noise σ, a first-order expression gives the per-axis standard deviation of a transformed point at distance r from the centroid of the marks:

σT(r)=σ1n+r2∑iri2

where ri are the distances of the marks from their centroid. The first term is the uncertainty of the shift, the second that of rotation and scale, which grows with distance. LAB-12-01-01 checks the expression against a Monte Carlo run of 2000 seeded surveys [A]: at every feature the two agree within 5 %. It also compares network A with network B, four marks clustered within a few hundred metres of the MIA — a control network that was never extended to the pit [A]. Results in millimetres:

The "95th percentile" columns are of the horizontal error. The column with the feature's own observation error adds a per-axis error of 0.02 m on low-risk segments, 0.03 m on medium and 0.05 m on high multipath risk [A], assigned from the mine.yaml class of the segment each feature lies on.

Three readings matter.

  1. With control that surrounds the work, control contributes about a centimetre. Network A's σ stays between 8.2 and 14.9 mm everywhere the AHS map reaches, and it is smallest near the centroid, which lies 194 m from the middle of R05.
  2. On R05 the observation, not the control, dominates. With the assumed 0.05 m error near the highwall, the 95th percentile at P1, P2 and the R05 midpoint is 124–127 mm, against 20–22 mm from control alone. Improving the control network would not help there; a method less sensitive to multipath on lower R05 would [A].
  3. Clustered control extrapolates badly. Network B is better than network A only near the MIA (GB1: 11.4 mm against 14.9 mm). At EX02-L its σ is 105.0 mm, 10.0 times network A's, and its 95th percentile reaches 261.8 mm; at D01 298.2 mm. The marks did not get worse; the work moved away from them. A rotation error that is invisible across a few hundred metres of MIA becomes a displacement of decimetres four kilometres away.

The design rule that follows is old survey practice [TBV] and the handbook adopts it [H]: control must surround the area it serves, including in height, and a delivery must say which marks were used. Worked example 1 (section 12) repeats the EX02-L figures by hand.

4.5 A disturbed mark

Marks on a mine's rim do not stay put. Bench work, a grader or a blast can move a mark without anyone noticing. The lab moves CM5, on the north rim beyond EX02, by 0.15 m [A] while its published coordinate stays unchanged, and repeats the survey:

Check Clean survey CM5 disturbed by 0.15 m
RMS residual at the six marks 0.0259 m 0.0611 m
Largest residual 0.0419 m at CM3 0.1138 m at CM5
Leave-one-out discrepancy at CM5 0.0402 m 0.1710 m
Largest leave-one-out discrepancy at any other mark 0.0506 m (CM3) 0.0779 m

Least squares spreads a blunder over all residuals — every residual grows — but the disturbed mark keeps the largest. The leave-one-out check is sharper: fit on five marks, predict the sixth, compare with its published coordinate. CM5's discrepancy of 0.171 m stands well clear of the others. The procedure that follows is part of every control fit [H]: report residuals per mark, run leave-one-out, and if one mark stands out, set it "suspect" in the control register, refit without it, and have Survey re-establish it. A delivery fitted on a suspect mark is not accepted (REQ-12-01-003).

4.6 GB1: the anchor of every RTK position

GB1's corrections let a rover compute its position relative to GB1's antenna. The rover then adds the coordinate GB1 was given. If GB1 is moved or replaced and given a coordinate that is wrong by a vector Δ, every RTK position on site — trucks, survey rovers, machine guidance on dozers and shovels (ch-12-06) — shifts by approximately Δ [F] (SRC-PUB-NGS-RT-GUIDELINES-2014). Nothing on a truck reports this. The rover's quality indicators describe the solution relative to the base, not the correctness of the base's coordinate [TBV] (SRC-PUB-SURVEY-CONTROL-PRACTICE); a truck positioned 0.10 m east of where it is reports a healthy fix.

The consequences for the AHS are specific.

  • Map and trucks diverge. A map surveyed before the change and trucks localised after it disagree by Δ everywhere at once. A lane boundary drawn 0.10 m off is not a hazard by itself [A], but Δ adds to every other error and is the same everywhere, so nothing averages it out.
  • New survey disagrees with old map. A survey made after the change shows every road "moved" by Δ. Change detection (ch-12-04, ch-12-07) then reports a uniform shift — the tell-tale of a base problem, not of road works.
  • 3DMG digs to the wrong place. A dozer restoring EX02's loading floor to design with machine guidance (ch-12-06) works Δ off the design.

LAB-12-01-01 gives GB1 a coordinate error of (0.06, −0.08) m, 0.10 m [A], and re-observes four check points (CM1, CM2, CM3, CM6) with a rover on GB1's corrections. The individual differences from their published coordinates are 0.0684 to 0.1247 m; their mean is 0.0621 m east and −0.0844 m north, 0.1047 m in all. With 0.02 m per observation and four marks, the lab flags a mean above 3σ/n=0.03 m [A] — a teaching rule, not a tolerance. The error is flagged. Without an error the same check gives a mean of 0.0048 m and is not flagged.

The GB1 check [H] that this chapter specifies has three parts:

  1. Status. GB1 transmitting, corrections current at the trucks (correction age), no alarms at the base (ch-11-01, ch-16-02 shift-start checks).
  2. Position. Re-observe at least three check points distributed around the mine with a rover on GB1's corrections; compare with their published coordinates; flag a common offset above the site's limit [A]. A common offset points to GB1; scattered differences point to the marks or the observations.
  3. Record. GB1's coordinate, its source (which marks, which register version), the check result and the date go into the control register and the map frame record (section 4.8).

When GB1 is moved or replaced the order matters [H]: (a) before the change, record a check of the old base; (b) establish the new antenna position from the control marks, not from the old base alone; (c) before trucks use the new base, run the position check above; (d) record the new coordinate as a new version of the GB1 entry, linked to the map frame record; (e) if the check is flagged, the trucks must not use the new base until it is resolved — the AHS Controller treats it like a site-wide loss of GNSS (ch-11-01) rather than letting trucks run on a base whose coordinate is in doubt. Whether a real site's GNSS equipment allows a seamless switch, and what the receivers do during it, is a Volume 05 topic [TBV].

4.9 Surveying inside autonomous areas

Survey crews work inside autonomous areas under the same digital access permissions as any manned entry (ch-10-06, ch-16-04): they stop at an access point, pass the access checks, request by radio and terminal, wait for the area to be confirmed clear, read back the permission and enter. A Survey crew is not an exception, however short the task.

Two choices decide what a survey costs: the access point and the area. ch-10-06 and ch-16-04 give the rule — AP-02 for work on R05, R06 and the pit floor; AP-04 for the RL 330 bench — and the costs of permissions on R06. LAB-12-01-01 adds the cases this chapter needs: surveys of the pit-exit corridor, with R05 open and with R05 closed. A loading unit is "held" when its loaded route has no path with the area locked, and "rerouted" when the path changes:

Survey area (segments locked) R05 open R05 closed (SCN-MAP-R05)
R24 from AP-04, stopping short of W (R24) none held, none rerouted all four units held
R24 including Intersection W (R24, R09, R10, R11) EX03, EX04 held all four units held
Crest P3 (R24 and R25) none held, none rerouted all four units held
R25 and Intersection V (R25, R12, R13, R14, R15) none held, none rerouted all four units held
R05 from AP-02 (R05) all four rerouted over R24 none: R05 is already closed

The table carries the operational lesson of the chapter. Before an R05 closure, the west ramp, P3 and V can be surveyed at no production cost — R24 carries no baseline route — as long as the crew stops short of W, whose lock would hold the bench circuits of EX03 and EX04. During the closure, the same surveys isolate AA-North: R24, P3 and V are the only way out, and a lock on any of them holds every loading unit. A detour that has to be surveyed after the closure has started cannot be surveyed without stopping the pit. Hence the scheduling rule [R]: survey the detour — R24, P3, R25 and V — before the closure, deliver it, and have the map team confirm that the detour's map elements are current (ch-12-08) before R05 is closed. The crew's route from AP-01 to AP-04 crosses V (ch-16-04); before the closure that crossing costs nothing either.

Conversely, the R05 survey itself is cheapest during the closure: the area is already empty of traffic, and the permission on R05 adds nothing to the closure's cost.

What the permission covers [H]: the segments in the area and nothing else. A crew permitted on R24 short of W does not walk to the junction to "get the last string"; it asks for an extension or a new permission. The crew briefing says where the area ends on the ground (in this case: the last marker before W), what happens if the permission expires (leave, or stop and call — a stale permission is not a permission, ch-10-06), and which access point is the exit.

4.10 Prioritising survey requests

Survey's time is finite, and the three requests of section 1.1 compete for it. The handbook ranks them by what waiting costs [H]:

  1. Anything that may affect every truck's position comes first. A GB1 check after repeated degradation alarms (SCN-GNSS-R05) is first even though no tonnage figure can be put on it: if GB1 is wrong, every map comparison and every other survey made with it is wrong too. ch-04-02 and ch-16-07 have the controller keep the R05 restriction until Survey reports; the check also unblocks every later survey.
  2. Then whatever stops a loading unit. EX02 without a valid loading area costs 3960 t per hour in the lab's shift (fixed and dynamic dispatch alike).
  3. Then whatever lengthens cycles. An R05 closure costs 311.7 t per hour while R24 carries the traffic.
  4. Except when the detour is at risk. If anything threatens the only open exit — a GB1 problem that also degrades R24 (medium multipath), a discrepancy on the detour's map — reopening R05 becomes the priority, because the next step is an isolated AA-North.

The numbers are from one deterministic model with assumed parameters [A]; the order they produce — loading unit before cycle time, detour risk above both — is the part that carries over. The Pit Supervisor owns the decision; Survey proposes it with these figures.

4.11 Trade-offs and model limits

  • The lab's working frame, marks, error inputs and thresholds are assumptions [A]; real GNSS errors are not Gaussian or independent, and multipath near highwalls is correlated along the road [TBV].
  • Only a 2D similarity plus a height offset is modelled; real site transformations may include more parameters [TBV].
  • The control marks are perfectly stable unless the lab disturbs one; real marks settle and move slowly.
  • The lab does not simulate the truck side of FIG-12-01-01 (localisation); it is described only. The localisation model ahs_sim.localisation is covered in Volume 05.
  • Access costs use loaded routes and reachability only; clear times and lost tonnage per area are in ch-16-04.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation

The control register is current; marks are checked on a schedule and after nearby work [A]; GB1's status is in the shift-start check (ch-16-02) and its position check is recorded on its own schedule [A]. Survey works under permissions, delivers packages, and the map team accepts or rejects them on completeness before editing.

6.3 Degraded operation — SCN-GNSS-R05: "check GB1"

The registry: T+0 s DT17's integrity degrades and it derates; T+40 s a second truck reports degradation at the same place and one truck stops; T+60 s the controller applies a ramp speed restriction; T+300 s Survey is called to check the base station and satellite availability. ch-04-02 and ch-16-07 continue with Survey finding GB1 and the corrections working, the degradation attributed to satellite geometry, and the restriction kept with a review time. This chapter defines what Survey does between T+300 s and that finding [A]:

  1. GB1 status and correction age at the trucks (from the network-health and GNSS views; ch-11-01).
  2. A position check: three or more check points re-observed with a rover on GB1's corrections — at least one near R05, such as CM3 near P1, and others around the mine. A common offset above the limit points to GB1; a clean result points away from it.
  3. A report to the AHS Controller and Pit Supervisor: "GB1 working; corrections current; check points within the limit; degradation local to lower R05" — or the opposite, which turns a local restriction into a site-wide problem.

The pattern matters: degradation at one place on several trucks points to the place (geometry, multipath); degradation everywhere points to GB1 (ch-11-01). A wrong GB1 coordinate produces neither pattern, which is why the position check exists.

6.4 Abnormal operation — SCN-POSCOM-LV07 applied to a Survey vehicle

The access process for a Survey vehicle is the process of SCN-POSCOM-LV07: radio request at T+0 s, area selected and submitted at T+20 s, lock at T+25 s, confirmed clear and granted at T+90 s, permission by radio with read-back at T+95 s, entry at T+100 s (registry). ch-16-04 found that with baseline traffic on R05 and R06 clearing takes 346–492 s, not 65 s; a Survey crew planning a ramp survey with R05 open should expect minutes at AP-02, not seconds. With R05 already closed (section 6.2) the area is empty and confirmation is quick.

6.5 Abnormal operation — GB1 moved or replaced

A planned move follows the order of section 4.6 [H]: old-base check recorded; new position established from the marks; position check passed before any truck uses the new base; new GB1 entry in the control register and map frame record. The change is scheduled like any infrastructure change with an impact on every truck — with the Pit Supervisor, at a time when a failed check can be absorbed [R].

7.3 Interface and timing considerations along SCN-MAP-R05

  • T−5 d. Survey permission on R24 is an AMS permission like any other; the AMS returns the units held (none) before submission (REQ-10-06-011). A request that would include W shows EX03 and EX04 held, and the crew stops short.
  • T−4 d. The detour package carries the control and transformation versions. The map team compares it with the current map; any change to R24, R25, P3 or V is a map change of its own, deployed before T−3600 s (ch-12-09).
  • T−3600 s to T−300 s. The R05 permission sits inside a closure; the AMS shows both. The permission ends with a release confirmation, not with the closure.
  • T+0 s. The hand-off package arrives with its epoch (T−1200 s to T−300 s) and with "map version compared". The version check is a data interface, not a phone call.
  • T+1800–2400 s. Validation (ch-12-07) uses the package's accuracy statement to tell deviations from noise. At deployment the AMS grants nothing on R05 to a truck that has not confirmed the new version (SCN-MAP-MISMATCH-DT17, ch-12-09).

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

The AHS Controller sees survey crews as permissions: area, holder, access point, validity window, state (requested, locked, granted, released). Next to them: GB1 status and correction age; pending map versions and the delivery they come from; closures and their reasons. Survey's control register is not on the HMI, but its result is: the map frame record names the GB1 coordinate version in force, and a GB1 check in progress is shown as such.

8.3 Procedure excerpts

A. A survey task inside AA-North (Survey crew and AHS Controller).

  1. Survey: state the task, the segments, the time needed and the preferred access point, at least a shift ahead for planned work [A].
  2. AHS Controller: choose the access point and the smallest area that contains the route and the task; read the units held and rerouted before submitting; if a circuit is held, get the Pit Supervisor's approval or change the area (ch-16-04).
  3. Survey crew: at the access point, run the access checks; request by radio and terminal.
  4. AHS Controller: submit; wait for confirmed clear; give the permission by radio; the crew reads back the area, its limit on the ground and the validity window.
  5. Survey crew: work only inside the area; at the limit, stop. Ask for an extension before the window ends.
  6. Survey crew: leave by the agreed access point; report out; the controller confirms the release.

B. Before an R05 closure (Survey, AHS Controller, map team).

  1. Survey (T−7 d): plan the detour survey — R24 from AP-04 short of W, then P3, R25 and V — while R05 is open.
  2. AHS Controller: confirm that no unit is held by these areas (section 4.9).
  3. Survey: deliver the detour package; map team: confirm the detour's map elements or deploy a change before the closure.
  4. Survey (T−1 d): check control near the work and record a GB1 position check.
  5. Pit Supervisor: release the closure plan only when steps 3 and 4 are recorded.

C. Checking GB1 (after a move or replacement, or on the controller's call during GNSS alarms).

  1. AHS Controller: call Survey with the pattern seen (one place or everywhere) and the restriction in force.
  2. Survey: check GB1 status and correction age.
  3. Survey: re-observe at least three check points around the mine with a rover on GB1's corrections; compute the mean difference.
  4. Survey → AHS Controller and Pit Supervisor: report "within limit" or "common offset of … m".
  5. AHS Controller: if flagged, treat it as a site-wide GNSS problem — no truck on the new or suspect base — and inform the Pit Supervisor; if not flagged, continue with the local restriction and its review condition.

8.4 KPIs and delay codes affected

A closure for map work lengthens cycles — 236.0 s more per EX02 cycle on the detour (ch-10-03) — and shows as fewer loads, not as a delay code. Trucks or loading units held because a survey permission or work zone holds their circuit are coded DLY-OD-04 [H] (coding_rules). Speed restrictions while Survey checks GB1 also show as cycle time. A useful non-GMG indicator is the age of the map frame record: days since GB1's position was last checked and since the control marks near the active pit were last re-observed.

8.5 Training and competency implications

Survey crews learn the access procedure as manned-entry workers, not as visitors, and practise reading the area limit on the ground. Surveyors learn the control register, leave-one-out checks and the GB1 position check, and the hand-off package. AHS Controllers learn to read a GNSS pattern (one place, everywhere, nowhere visible) and what "check GB1" involves. Pit Supervisors learn the priority rule and why the detour is surveyed before a closure. Map-team members learn to reject an incomplete package.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-12-01-001 [H] Every survey deliverable to the AHS map shall carry horizontal and vertical accuracy attributes with their confidence level and an accuracy statement naming their basis (check points, model or specification); the values shall be set by the site. Rationale: an attribute without a basis cannot separate a deviation from noise during validation (section 4.7). Verification: inspection of hand-off packages

REQ-12-01-002 [H] Control used for AHS map deliveries shall surround the area delivered, in plan and in height, and the delivery shall name the marks used. Rationale: clustered control extrapolates badly — 105.0 mm σ at EX02-L from marks near the MIA against 10.5 mm from marks around the mine (section 4.4). Verification: analysis (LAB-12-01-01), inspection of the control register

REQ-12-01-003 [H] Each site transformation fit shall report residuals per mark and a leave-one-out check; a mark that stands out shall be set "suspect" in the control register and excluded until re-established. Rationale: a 0.15 m disturbance raises one mark's leave-one-out discrepancy to 0.171 m while the others stay below 0.078 m (section 4.5). Verification: test (LAB-12-01-01), inspection

REQ-12-01-004 [H] The map team shall accept a survey delivery for map editing only with a complete hand-off package (section 4.8) in REFMINE-GRID and RL, and shall reject, not complete, a package with a missing field. Rationale: frame, epoch and control version cannot be reconstructed after the fact. Verification: inspection; test of the map change workflow (ch-12-09)

REQ-12-01-005 [H] After GB1 is moved or replaced, no truck shall use its corrections until a position check on at least three check points around the mine has been recorded within the site's limit, and GB1's coordinate shall be recorded as a new version in the control register and the map frame record. Rationale: a base coordinate error shifts every RTK position on site and is not reported by the rovers (section 4.6). Verification: inspection of the GB1 change record; drill

REQ-12-01-006 [H] Before R05 is closed for planned work, R24, P3, R25 and Intersection V shall have been surveyed or confirmed against the current map, with the result delivered before the closure. Rationale: during the closure any survey permission on the detour holds every loading unit (section 4.9). Verification: inspection of the closure plan (SCN-MAP-R05)

REQ-12-01-007 [H] A Survey crew inside an autonomous area shall work only within the area of its digital access permission and shall request an extension or a new permission for any work outside it. Rationale: the permission protects only its area; the AMS grants paths everywhere else. Verification: inspection of permission records; observation in drills

REQ-12-01-008 [H] The AHS map frame record shall name the control-register version, site transformation version and GB1 coordinate version on which the current map version rests, and the AMS shall display it with the map version. Rationale: map and truck positions agree only while both rest on the same control and base (FIG-12-01-01). Verification: inspection; test (HMI against the map frame record)

10. Verification and validation

  • The lab verifies its arithmetic. Eleven tests in LAB-12-01-01 check the working-frame round trip and an exact fit, the single fit, the agreement of the closed-form σ with Monte Carlo within 5 % at every feature, the network B figures, the disturbed-mark and GB1 checks, the access table, the route lengths and the production costs.
  • The lab does not validate survey practice. Errors are Gaussian and independent by assumption [A]; real GNSS errors near highwalls are neither. A site validates its accuracy statements with check points not used in any fit.
  • Site validation of the chain. Re-observe check points with the trucks' own receivers parked on them during a quiet shift [A]: if the truck reports the check point where the register has it, map and truck rest on the same control.
  • Procedural validation. Drills: a GB1 change with a deliberately wrong coordinate in a test window (the check must flag it), a survey task that would cross W (the controller must stop it short), and a package with a missing epoch (the map team must reject it).

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Placing control where it is convenient — around the MIA — and transforming the pit from it.
  • Treating GB1's coordinate as installation data rather than as a versioned control item.
  • Quoting instrument specifications as the accuracy of a delivery.
  • Allowing more than one frame or control version into the map pipeline.
  • Judging a transformation by its overall RMS alone.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Surveying the west ramp after R05 has been closed, or crossing W "for the last string".
  • Calling Survey to "check GB1" and accepting "it is transmitting" as the answer.
  • Ranking survey requests by who asked loudest rather than by what waiting costs.
  • Handing over a delivery by e-mail with coordinates but no package.
  • Letting trucks run on a newly installed base before its position check.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — Control error at EX02-L. Network A: n=6, ∑ri2=21548333 m², EX02-L at r=1523.2 m from the centroid. 1/n=0.167; r2/∑ri2=0.108; σT=0.020.167+0.108=0.0105 m, 10.5 mm per axis [A]. Network B: n=4, ∑ri2=555000 m², r=3925.9 m; 1/n=0.25; r2/∑ri2=27.77; σT=0.0228.02=0.106 m. Monte Carlo gives 10.5 mm and 105.0 mm. The marks are equally good; the second network is four kilometres away.

Example 2 — The GB1 check limit. Four check points, 0.02 m per observation [A]. The standard deviation of the mean is 0.02/4=0.01 m; with K=3 the limit is 0.03 m [A]. The observed mean of 0.0621 m east and −0.0844 m north exceeds it on both axes; the base is suspect. With three check points the limit would be 0.035 m; more points make a smaller offset detectable.

Example 3 — Ranking requests. EX02 idle: 3960 t per hour. R05 closed with the detour open: 311.7 t per hour. An hour of Survey spent on EX02's new loading area instead of the R05 as-built saves, in this model, 3960 − 311.7 = 3648.3 t — provided nothing threatens R24. A GB1 check after repeated alarms goes before both.

15. Key takeaways

  • The AHS map and the truck's position agree only while both rest on the same control and the same GB1 coordinate.
  • REFMINE-GRID is local and fictional by design; every delivery is in it, with its control, transformation and GB1 versions.
  • Control that surrounds the work contributes about a centimetre (8.2–14.9 mm σ in the lab); clustered control gives 105.0 mm at EX02-L. On lower R05 the observation error dominates.
  • Per-mark residuals and leave-one-out find a disturbed mark that the overall RMS only hints at.
  • A wrong GB1 coordinate moves everything and alarms nothing; check it with check points after every change.
  • Survey the detour before an R05 closure; during it, any survey of R24, P3 or V isolates AA-North.
  • Rank survey requests by what waiting costs: GB1 first, then a stopped loading unit, then a lengthened cycle.
