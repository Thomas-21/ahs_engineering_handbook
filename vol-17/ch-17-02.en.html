1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

Wednesday, 06:30, the start-of-shift meeting next to CR1. The weekly plan has a shot in AA-North today: the bench next to EX02's face, fired at 10:25 [A]. The Pit Supervisor reads out the window: no new assignments to the four loading units from 10:00, firing at T+1500 s, the staged reopening from T+3000 s. The AHS Controller asks the first practical question — which way do the trucks leave the pit, R05 only or R05 and the west ramp R24? The Shovel Operator of EX02 asks the second: after the shot the face will not be where the AHS map says it is, so when can trucks spot at EX02 again? Survey asks the third: which surfaces are wanted first. And the planner asks the question that the production report will ask tomorrow — what will the window cost?

Nobody at the table doubts that the area must be clear before the shot. The questions are about the interfaces: how the plan's blast becomes a lock in the AMS, how the AMS knows the area is clear of trucks, why that is not the same as "people clear", what has to happen before the first truck is allowed back to EX02, and how the post-blast dig blocks reach the FMS so that ore still goes to C01 and waste to D01.

The questions:

  • What do the FMS and the AMS do during a blast window, and what do people decide?
  • How long does it take to clear AA-North of trucks, through one exit or two, and does it matter?
  • What does the window cost in tonnes, and which choices change the cost?
  • What must be true before a loading area is reopened after a blast, and who confirms it?
  • How do post-blast changes reach loading spots, the AHS map and the FMS assignments?

This chapter covers:

  • the chain of hand-offs from the mine plan through drilling, blasting, survey and the AHS map to loading and hauling;
  • blast clearance of AA-North in the FMS and the AMS, walked through on SCN-BLAST-AANORTH;
  • clearance time and production loss for four clearance strategies (LAB-17-02-01);
  • the staged reopening, the post-blast survey and the EX02 face change, and the DZ03 clean-up;
  • how material type and destination of post-blast dig blocks are carried into FMS assignments.

Blasting itself — the design of the shot, the exclusion distances, fume clearance, the shot firer's duties and the re-entry rules — belongs to site procedures and regulation and is not described here [TBV] (SRC-REG-AU-BLASTING-SHOTFIRING, SRC-PUB-POST-BLAST-REENTRY). The blast crew and its shot firer are an external function [A], not one of the reference mine's roles. Every time, distance and tonnage in this chapter is an illustrative assumption [A] computed with ahs_sim; the procedures and requirements are handbook conventions [H].

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader can:

  1. Explain how blast clearance is implemented in the AMS — area lock, trucks parked outside the blast area, permissions withheld — and how the area is reopened after the post-blast inspection.
  2. Analyse how post-blast changes (muck-pile shape, face position, floor condition) propagate into loading-spot definitions and map updates for EX02.
  3. Explain how material type and destination for post-blast dig blocks are carried into FMS assignments so that ore goes to C01 and waste to D01.
  4. Calculate the time to clear AA-North of trucks for a blast using both pit exits (R05 to P1 and R24 to P3) and compare it with clearance through R05 alone.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader can:

  1. Plan a blast-clearance sequence for AA-North with the Pit Supervisor and CR1, including which pit exits trucks use, where they are parked and who confirms the area clear and re-open.
  2. Coordinate post-blast clean-up by DZ03 at EX02 and the hand-back of the loading area to autonomous trucks.
  3. Estimate the production loss of a blast window for different clearance strategies and schedule blasts to reduce it.
  4. Confirm with Survey and the Shovel Operator that loading spots and the AHS map reflect the new face before autonomous loading resumes at EX02.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

All four loading units of the reference mine are in AA-North: EX01 and EX02 load ore for crusher C01, EX03 and EX04 load waste for dump D01. The pit has two exits for loaded trucks (ch-12-08): the main ramp R05 from P2 up to the crest P1, and the longer west ramp R24 from W up to the crest P3. A blast anywhere near the faces therefore stops the whole production of the mine for the length of the window. At the baseline rate of 15 931.7 t per hour (ch-14-07, ch-16-04) every ten minutes of window are worth more than 2600 t.

Three features of autonomous haulage make the blast window an interface problem rather than a simple stop.

The trucks do not leave by themselves. A manned fleet hears the radio call and drives out. Autonomous trucks follow their missions; they leave only when the FMS stops assigning them to the pit and the AMS routes them out. Trucks already loaded finish their haul; trucks under a shovel finish loading first. How long that takes depends on where the 22 trucks in AA-North are at the moment the window starts, on which exits they may use and on how closely the AMS lets them follow each other up a ramp.

"Clear of trucks" and "clear of people" are different facts. The AMS knows every autonomous truck's position and every granted path. It does not know where a person on foot is, and it knows a manned vehicle only through its position reporting unit (ch-14-02). The AMS can state the first fact; people must establish the second, by the site's blast procedure [TBV].

After the shot, the map is wrong. The face, the muck pile and the floor at EX02 are no longer where the AHS map and the loading-area definition put them. Trucks may not spot on a surface the AMS has never seen. Reopening the area is therefore not "lift the lock"; it is a staged hand-back that runs through the post-blast inspection, Survey's capture (ch-12-04), the map change gates (ch-12-09) and the new loading area (ch-13-05).

The planner's question — what does it cost — has a precise answer in the model, and the answer contains a surprise: the choice of exits that occupies most of the meeting usually does not change the lost tonnes at all.

3. Terminology and foundations

Blast window [H]. The planned interval from the moment the FMS stops assigning trucks to the loading units in the blast area (T+0 s) to the planned start of the staged reopening (T+3000 s in SCN-BLAST-AANORTH). It contains the planned firing time (T+1500 s). The window is a plan item owned by the Pit Supervisor and recorded in the shift plan.

Blast clearance [H]. The removal of autonomous trucks and loading-unit traffic from the blast area before firing: assignments stopped, trucks routed out and parked, area locked by the AMS, permissions withheld, followed by the people check of the site procedure.

Clearance strategy [H]. The combination of exits (R05 only, or R05 and R24) and hold location (park at PK1 in AA-Central, or hold on the pit floor outside the zone) chosen for a window. The four strategies of ahs_sim are named R05-park, both-park, R05-floor and both-floor.

Clearance time [H]. The time from the window start until no autonomous truck is left in the locked area (park strategies: all trucks past the crest P1 or P3) or in the zone (floor strategies).

Zone radius [A]. In the model, the radius around the shot (default the EX02 loading node) within which segments are treated as closed for the floor strategies (ahs_sim.safety.exclusion_zone). It is a model input with no default. The 300 m used in this chapter is arbitrary and is not an exclusion distance; real distances are set by the site's blast procedure [TBV].

Post-blast inspection [TBV]. The inspection after firing that decides when people and machines may re-enter. Its content and timing are site procedure; the scenario only places its completion at T+2400 s [A].

Staged reopening [H]. Release of the ramps first, then of the loading areas one by one, each after its surface and map version are confirmed. It applies the existing glossary term staged restart to an area.

Dig block [H]. A planned volume of blasted material with an outline, a material class (ore or waste in the reference mine) and a destination. The FMS assigns trucks at a loading unit according to the dig block the shovel is digging.

Muck pile — the broken rock thrown by the blast in front of the face. Its shape decides where the shovel can stand and where trucks can spot.

Existing terms used: area lock (ch-10-06), exclusion zone (ch-04-07), pit exit (ch-11-07), park-up, loading area, loading spot, loading-unit relocation (ch-13-05), map version and as-built (ch-12-09), work zone (ch-14-03), material tracking, production plan (shift), staged restart.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The chain of hand-offs

FIG-17-02-01 shows the drill–blast–load–haul chain as a sequence of hand-offs. Each arrow is a moment at which responsibility for the next step passes from one owner to another, and each needs evidence.

  1. Mine plan → shift plan. The weekly plan (ch-17-01) carries the blast, its dig-block outlines and their material classes. The Pit Supervisor turns it into a window in the shift plan, with the clearance strategy and the reopening order.
  2. Shift plan → FMS. At T+0 s the Pit Supervisor starts the window; the FMS stops assigning trucks to the AA-North loading units.
  3. Drill → blast crew. Drilling and charging happen on the bench under the blast crew's control [A]. Drill rigs and the blast crew's vehicles are manned and enter AA-North under permissions (ch-16-04).
  4. FMS → AMS. The AMS routes trucks out and reports when none is left inside.
  5. AMS → people. The area lock is the AMS's statement; the people check is the Pit Supervisor's with the blast crew.
  6. Blast crew → Survey. After the inspection, the Pit Supervisor asks Survey for surfaces (ch-12-04, procedure B).
  7. Survey → AMS. The surface becomes a map change through the gates of ch-12-09.
  8. Clean-up → AMS. DZ03 works at EX02 under a work zone (ch-14-03, ch-14-04) and releases it.
  9. AMS → loading unit. The new loading area is activated; spots are designated on the new face (ch-13-02, ch-13-05).
  10. Mine plan → FMS. The post-blast dig blocks bring material class and destination.
  11. FMS → trucks. Assignments resume.

The rest of section 4 follows this chain: clearance (arrows 2–5), the cost of the window, reopening (6–9) and the material hand-off (10–11).

4.2 Blast clearance in the FMS and the AMS

The clearance uses mechanisms that already exist in the handbook's architecture. No new safety function is needed, and none of them overrides a vehicle-side decision.

FMS: stop assigning. At T+0 s the four loading units go to a planned state blast window [H], as a relocating unit does in ch-13-05. The FMS issues no new loading assignment to them. Loaded trucks keep their haul missions and dump at C01 or D01. Every other truck receives a park mission to PK1, or, in a floor strategy, a hold mission to an agreed point outside the zone. Empty trucks already travelling towards the pit from AA-Central are redirected before they cross the crest.

AMS: route out. The AMS plans the exits with the allowed ramps only. In a both-exit strategy it chooses for each truck the ramp with the earliest arrival at a crest, given the trucks ahead of it. Entry onto a ramp is spaced by a minimum headway (30 s [A], RAMP_HEADWAY_S, the same value ch-17-01 uses for ramp capacity).

AMS: lock when clear. The AMS applies the area lock of ch-10-06 to AA-North: the segments are removed from the network used for routes and granted paths. It confirms clear in the same way as for an access permission (ch-16-04 section 4.2): from position reports, no autonomous truck inside; from the reservation table, no granted path leading in. Only then does it report "AA-North clear of autonomous trucks, locked" with a time stamp.

AMS: withhold permissions. While the lock is in force the AMS grants no path and no access permission into the area. Requests are refused with the reason "blast window". This is the machine side of the rule that nobody enters a blast area without the shot firer's agreement [TBV].

People: confirm people clear. The AMS's statement covers autonomous trucks, and manned vehicles only as far as their position reporting units are working. The Pit Supervisor confirms with the blast crew that people and manned equipment are clear, by the site procedure [TBV]. The AMS records the confirmation and its author but never infers it.

Shovels. The registry has the shovels move back from the shot at T+300 s. A shovel move is a Shovel Operator's action; the FMS keeps the units in blast window state, so no truck is sent to a shovel that is tracking backwards.

What the vehicles do not do is just as important. The ADS drives each truck along its granted path and stops for any obstacle as usual. A truck that stops on R24 during the clearance (a fault, a nuisance stop) is not driven through its stop by any central command; it is recovered as in ch-16-05, and the clearance waits or the other exit takes the queue.

4.3 Clearance time: positions, exits and ramp headway

LAB-17-02-01 uses ahs_sim.planning_ops.clearance. It places the 37 producing trucks of the baseline DAY allocation evenly around their cycles at the window start (10:00 [A]). The 22 trucks that are in AA-North at that moment must leave. Each first finishes its current phase: a truck under a shovel finishes loading, a truck on a road drives to the end of its segment. Then, in the park strategies, it drives to a crest by a route that uses one allowed exit ramp only; with both exits, the AMS picks for each truck the ramp with the earliest crest arrival given the trucks ahead (section 4.2). A truck enters the ramp at least 30 s after the truck ahead.

Strategy Trucks via R05 Trucks via R24 Already climbing to P1 Clearance time
R05 only, park at PK1 13 0 9 851.8 s
R05 and R24, park at PK1 2 11 9 757.9 s
Hold on the floor (either), zone radius 300 m [A] — — — 575.0 s

Nine trucks are already climbing R05 loaded at T+0 s and are out by themselves. With R05 the only exit, the remaining 13 queue for the ramp at 30-s spacing, and the last one, DT20 loaded at EX03, passes P1 at T+851.8 s. With both exits, 11 trucks take R24, which is longer but climbs at a gentler grade, and the queue splits; the last truck, DT01 loaded at EX01, passes P3 at T+757.9 s. The two-exit strategy saves 93.9 s. The binding element in both cases is not distance but the trucks that were under the shovels at T+0 s: they cannot start before loading ends, and they join the end of a ramp queue.

The floor strategies keep trucks inside AA-North at points outside the modelled zone. With a 300 m radius around EX02-L the zone covers R08 and R18 (the EX02 approach and loading link); trucks hold at EX01-Q, EX03-Q, EX04-Q and P2, and the last arrives at T+575.0 s. Whether trucks may stay in the pit at all during a blast, and where, is decided by the site's blast procedure [TBV]; the model only shows what such a choice would mean for production.

Why ch-16-04 gives a shorter number. ch-16-04 (section 4.4) clears a whole AA-North access lock in 444–649 s. That model lets trucks drive out along their own routes and counts trucks at a shovel as stopped inside; it has no ramp headway. Blast clearance must empty the loading areas too, and the queue on the ramp is what sets the end. The two numbers answer different questions and do not contradict each other.

4.4 What a blast window costs

The production loss is computed with ahs_sim.fms.simulate_fleet: one deterministic DAY shift with an Outage on each loading unit from the window start until its first truck is back, against the undisturbed shift. The loss is counted in whole 220-t loads over the shift. With a window of 2700 s and firing at T+1500 s:

Strategy Clearance Firing Full production Restart (reopening → full) Lost (ore / waste) DLY-OD-04 trucks / units
R05 only, park 851.8 s T+1500 s T+4341.8 s 1641.8 s 16 060 t (9900 / 6160) 30.09 h / 4.29 h
Both exits, park 757.9 s T+1500 s T+4341.8 s 1641.8 s 16 060 t (9900 / 6160) 30.09 h / 4.29 h
R05 only, floor 575.0 s T+1500 s T+4056.8 s 1356.8 s 14 740 t (9020 / 5720) 26.63 h / 3.92 h
Both exits, floor 575.0 s T+1500 s T+4056.8 s 1356.8 s 14 740 t (9020 / 5720) 26.63 h / 3.92 h

Three readings matter.

The exits do not change the lost tonnes here. Firing waits for the plan, not for the trucks. The earliest firing the model allows is the clearance time plus the people check (300 s [A]): T+1151.8 s with R05 only, T+1057.9 s with both exits. Both are earlier than T+1500 s, so the window, and with it the loss, is the same. Two exits matter only when the planned firing time is earlier than clearance plus people check would allow (FIG-17-02-03 b). With firing planned at T+1000 s, R05 alone delays the shot by 151.8 s and both exits by 57.9 s; the difference is one load, 14 740 t against 14 520 t. From T+1200 s on, neither strategy delays the shot. Two exits are still the better plan: they give 442.1 s rather than 348.2 s of margin before the planned firing, which absorbs a stopped truck or a slow people check.

The hold location changes the restart, and the restart is where the cost is. Trucks parked at PK1 need 388.0 s from PK1 to EX03-L before the first load; trucks on the floor are already in the pit. The floor strategies restart 285.0 s faster and save 1320 t. They are only available if the site's procedure permits trucks in the pit during the shot and there is room outside the zone; with a 5000 m radius [A] the model finds no hold point and refuses the strategy.

The window length is the main lever. With both exits and parking at PK1, the loss grows with the window:

Window 2400 s 2700 s 3000 s 3300 s 3600 s
Lost, park at PK1 14 740 t 16 060 t 17 600 t 18 920 t 20 680 t
Lost, hold on floor 13 640 t 14 740 t 15 840 t 17 160 t 18 920 t

Each further 300 s of window costs 1320–1760 t. Shortening the post-blast part — by having the inspection crew, Survey and the map change ready at the firing time — is worth more than any choice of exits.

4.5 Reopening: inspection, surfaces, map versions and loading areas

The reopening is the dangerous direction, as the handback is for a closed road (ch-14-07 section 4.5). Before the shot everyone knows the area is locked; after it everyone wants it open. The model, and the procedure of section 8.3, release in this order [H]:

  1. Post-blast inspection complete (site procedure [TBV]), recorded by the Pit Supervisor with the blast crew's confirmation. Until then the lock stays and no permission is granted, not even to Survey.
  2. Ramps released. R05 and R24 are reopened for routing once the inspection has covered them. Trucks can then leave PK1, but they have no assignment in AA-North yet.
  3. Loading areas, one by one. Each loading area is reopened only when its surface after the blast is published and the AHS map version in use matches it. In the model each confirmation takes 300 s [A] (BLAST_MAP_CONFIRM_S) when nothing has moved, and the order is EX03, EX04, EX01, EX02 — waste units away from the shot first, EX02 last because it is nearest the shot.
  4. First truck back. A loading unit produces again when its first truck arrives from PK1.

The order of the loading areas changes which material is lost, not how much. With the registered order the window costs 9900 t of ore; with the ore units first (EX01, EX02, EX03, EX04) the same 16 060 t are lost, but only 8360 t of ore and 7700 t of waste, and full production comes at T+4272.4 s instead of T+4341.8 s. Which material matters more on the day is a planning decision; the plan should say it, not the order in which surfaces happen to arrive [R].

When the EX02 face has moved. The 300 s per area assumes that the surface confirms the existing map. At EX02, next to the shot, the face, the muck pile and the floor have changed. Then the loading area needs a new map version through the full gate of ch-12-09: as-built submitted, validated, approved, distributed and activated, 2400 s [A] (MAP_GATE_S). In the model, EX02 then reopens at T+6000 s instead of T+3900 s, the loss rises from 16 060 t to 18 260 t (12 100 t of ore), and full production comes at T+6441.8 s. That is the price of a face change after a blast, and it is why the EX02 face change should be planned with the blast as one item (SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE) rather than discovered at the reopening.

4.6 Post-blast changes at EX02: face, muck pile, floor and spots

A blast changes three things that the AHS uses at a loading area.

  • Face position. The face advances. The shovel's working position moves, and with it the loading spots and the Cusp points that ch-13-02 and ch-13-05 derive from the shovel's position.
  • Muck-pile shape. The pile decides where the shovel can dig first and from which side trucks can be presented. A pile that spreads over the old Queue point or the approach changes the loading area's boundary.
  • Floor condition. Rock thrown onto the floor, an uneven floor or a soft patch must be cleaned before trucks spot. That is DZ03's job under a work zone (SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02).

The propagation follows the chain set out in ch-12-04, ch-12-09, ch-13-02, ch-13-05 and ch-13-08: the Pit Supervisor asks Survey for a surface (ch-12-04 procedure B); Survey publishes it with epoch and limits (ch-12-04 procedure A); the change set and new map version pass the gates (ch-12-09); the loading-area definition is activated with the new map version (ch-13-05, as at T+3600 s in SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE); the Shovel Operator designates spots on the new face (ch-13-02, ch-13-08). Two rules from these chapters carry the weight here. The AHS Controller does not activate a loading-area change whose surface is older than the change (ch-12-04 procedure B, step 4): a surface captured before the shot is older than the face it should describe. And the old spots become invalid when the unit leaves loading (ch-13-05): no spot designated before the blast may be accepted after it.

The DZ03 clean-up. If the floor needs work, DZ03 enters EX02's loading area under a work zone reserved inside the locked area (ch-14-03). The registry times of SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02 — zone reserved at T+120 s, DZ03 out and the zone released at T+900 s of its own clock — fit between the inspection and the EX02 reopening [A]. The clean-up changes the floor again, so Survey's capture of the loading area is taken after DZ03 has left, not before [R].

4.7 Material and destination: dig blocks into FMS assignments

The shot breaks a volume that the plan has divided into dig blocks. A block near the ore–waste boundary can be waste inside an ore unit's area. The FMS must know, for each load, which block the shovel is digging and where that material goes: ore to C01, waste to D01. A wrong destination is expensive in both directions — ore to the dump is lost metal; waste to the crusher dilutes the feed.

The handbook's rule is fail closed [H]: an assignment is made only from a dig block with a material class and a destination that accepts it, and only on a surface taken after the blast. LAB-17-02-01 part G applies the rule to four illustrative EX02 blocks [A]:

Dig block Material Surface after blast Result
EX02-B07 ore yes assign to C01
EX02-B08 waste yes assign to D01, destination change flagged for the Pit Supervisor
EX02-B09 not classified yes hold: no material class
EX02-B10 ore no hold: no post-blast surface

The destination change for EX02-B08 is legal — D01 accepts waste — but it changes EX02's circuit from C01 to D01 for the time the block is dug. It changes the queue at D01 and the ore plan, so it is the Pit Supervisor's decision, not an automatic consequence. Material tracking (ch-15-05) records each load against the block; it never moves a truck.

4.8 Model limits and trade-offs

The model is a teaching instrument, and its limits shape what the numbers can be used for [A].

  • Positions are idealised. Trucks are spread evenly over their cycles; a real window starts with bunching, queues and a truck or two in the workshop. A seeded spread (seed) shows the range, not the answer.
  • One headway on the ramps, no other interaction. Intersections, passing on the ramps and the empty trucks coming down are ignored during the clearance.
  • No reassignment during the reopening. EX02's trucks wait for EX02; in practice the FMS could send them to the open units for the extra 2400 s (ch-17-03).
  • People check and map confirmation are fixed inputs. Both are site processes with their own variance.
  • The zone is a circle of an arbitrary radius. It is not a blast exclusion and says nothing about one.
  • Losses are counted in whole loads of 220 t, so differences of one load (220 t) are within the model's resolution.

The main trade-off is between margin and restart. Parking at PK1 gives the clearest picture — the pit is empty — at the price of a longer restart. Holding on the floor restarts faster but depends on a site rule the model cannot supply. Two exits cost nothing in tonnes in the normal case and buy margin; they also put R24 traffic past W, which the plan must know if anything else is planned on R24 that day.

6.2 Abnormal operation — the EX02 face has moved, and the people check is slow

EX02 needs a new map version. At T+2400 s the inspection reports that the shot has moved EX02's face and thrown rock onto its floor. EX02 cannot be reopened on a 300 s confirmation. The Pit Supervisor calls DZ03 for the floor (SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02) and asks Survey for the EX02 surface after DZ03 has left; the change runs through the full map gate of 2400 s [A]. The other three areas reopen as planned. EX02 reopens at T+6300 s, its first truck arrives at T+6741.8 s, and the window costs 20 020 t (12 980 t of ore) and 38.43 truck-hours of DLY-OD-04 instead of 17 600 t. The AHS Controller does not shorten this by reopening EX02 on the old version: the AMS refuses it, because the surface is newer than the map in use.

The people check takes longer. If confirming people clear takes 900 s after the lock instead of 300 s [A], the shot fires at T+1657.9 s rather than T+1500 s; everything after it moves by 157.9 s, full production comes at T+4799.8 s, and the window costs 18 480 t, 880 t more. The lock stays in force throughout; the AMS waits for the confirmation and never times it out into an assumption.

6.3 Degraded operation — R24 is not available

On the morning of the blast a truck is stopped on R24 for a fault, or R24 has a speed restriction for a soft patch. The AHS Controller and the Pit Supervisor switch the window to R05 only. The model gives a clearance of 851.8 s instead of 757.9 s; with the people check the earliest firing is T+1151.8 s, still before T+1500 s, so the firing time and the loss do not change. The margin before the planned firing shrinks from 442.1 s to 348.2 s. The decision rule for the degraded case is therefore simple: if clearance plus people check through the remaining exit still ends before the planned firing with a margin the Pit Supervisor accepts, keep the firing time; if not, move the firing time, never the people check [H].

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

  • Use existing objects. The blast window is an FMS state of the loading units plus an AMS area lock; no blast-specific safety function is introduced. The advantage is that every element already has tests and procedures (ch-10-06, ch-13-05, ch-16-04); the cost is that the window is visible on the HMI only as a combination, which section 8.1 addresses with a window banner [R].
  • Lock on evidence, not on the clock. The lock is applied when positions and reservations show the area empty, not at a planned time. The registry's T+900 s is a planned upper bound; the model reaches the state at T+757.9 s.
  • Separate statements. Clear of autonomous trucks is the AMS's; people clear is the Pit Supervisor's. Neither stands in for the other (REQ-17-02-003).
  • Two exits by default. Both exits shorten the clearance by 93.9 s and the earliest firing from T+1151.8 s to T+1057.9 s at no cost in tonnes; they are a margin, not a saving [R].
  • Reopening per loading area with a map gate. The gate costs time only where something moved; where nothing moved it costs the confirmation (300 s [A]).
  • No default zone radius. The model and the requirement refuse a missing radius; the radius comes from the site procedure with an owner (REQ-17-02-004).

7.3 Interface and timing considerations along the walkthrough

  • T+0 s. FMS state change first, then missions. A park mission issued before the state change could be overtaken by a loading assignment issued in the same dispatch cycle.
  • T+0 to T+757.9 s. The AMS plans each exit with the ramp headway of 30 s [A]. The trucks that bind are the ones under the shovels at T+0 s: they finish loading first and join the ramp queues last.
  • T+757.9 s (model) / T+900 s (registry). The AMS's clear needs both position reports and an empty reservation table; a stale position report (ch-14-02) keeps the area not clear (fail closed).
  • T+1200 s. The people confirmation is a recorded input with author and time. The AMS displays it next to its own statement and does not merge the two.
  • T+1500 s. The firing is recorded by the Pit Supervisor from the blast crew. The AMS uses it as the reference epoch: every surface and map version used for reopening must be newer.
  • T+2400 s. Survey's request carries the order of areas and the decision each surface feeds (ch-12-04 procedure A).
  • T+3000 s onward. For each loading area the AMS compares the surface epoch with the firing time and the map version in use with the validated version; it reopens only when both match. Trucks that run an older map version are refused per truck, as after a road rework (ch-12-09, SCN-MAP-MISMATCH-DT17).

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

During a window the AHS Controller's HMI should show [R]:

  • a window banner: window ID, strategy, planned firing time, state (clearing, clear and locked, people confirmed, fired, inspection complete, reopening), and the next expected event;
  • the clearance list: trucks still in AA-North with their exit ramp and expected crest time, and the predicted clear time against the planned firing time minus the people check;
  • the lock state of AA-North with refused requests listed with their reason;
  • during the reopening, a reopening table: per loading area the surface epoch, the map version in use and the validated version, open or not, and the first truck's expected arrival;
  • the running loss of the window against the plan's estimate.

8.3 Procedure excerpt — blast window in AA-North

A. Planning (day before).

  1. Pit Supervisor: enter the window in the shift plan with firing time, strategy, park or hold points, reopening order and the surfaces Survey will be asked for.
  2. AHS Controller: check the exits for planned work (R24, R25, R05) and run the clearance estimate; report the earliest firing time (clearance + people check) and the margin.
  3. Pit Supervisor: if the EX02 face will move, plan the face change with the blast (SCN-SHOVEL-EX02-MOVE) and book Survey and the map gate for EX02.

B. Clearance (T+0 s to the lock).

  1. Pit Supervisor → AHS Controller: "Start blast window AA-North." The controller sets the window in the FMS and confirms that no new assignment is issued to EX01–EX04.
  2. AHS Controller: apply the exit plan; watch the clearance list; report a stopped truck at once.
  3. Shovel Operators: move back from the shot as agreed; report when in position.
  4. AHS Controller: when the AMS reports clear, confirm the lock and report "AA-North clear of autonomous trucks, locked at [time]" to the Pit Supervisor.

C. People check and firing.

  1. Pit Supervisor: confirm people clear with the blast crew by the site procedure [TBV]; record it in the AMS.
  2. Blast crew: fire; Pit Supervisor: record the firing time.

D. Reopening.

  1. Pit Supervisor: record the inspection complete [TBV]; request surfaces in the agreed order.
  2. AHS Controller: release R05 and R24.
  3. Survey: publish each surface with epoch; for EX02, after DZ03's clean-up if one is needed.
  4. AHS Controller: reopen each loading area only when the surface is newer than the firing and the map version in use matches; never on the pre-blast version.
  5. Shovel Operator: check the face and designate new spots; report the first load.
  6. Pit Supervisor: confirm the dig-block destinations; decide any destination change.
  7. AHS Controller: close the window; record actual times and the loss against the estimate.

8.4 KPIs and delay codes affected

Codes follow delay-codes.yml and its coding_rules [H]; time categories and KPI names follow the GMG Time Usage Model (SRC-PUB-GMG-TUM-2020) [F]. Blast windows have their own coding rule: trucks held for a planned blast window are DLY-OD-04, including the time the shovels move back from the shot. The rule extends the planned-work rules for drills and network work; ahs_sim codes the window the same way.

In SCN-BLAST-AANORTH the window is 33.18 truck-hours and 4.62 loading-unit-hours of DLY-OD-04 and 17 600 t of lost production. A window does not reduce Physical Availability: the trucks are healthy and waiting. It shows up in Operating Efficiency and in the plan attainment of the shift. The window record should carry its estimated and actual cost in tonnes [R], so that the planner can see whether blasts are getting cheaper.

8.5 Training and competency implications

AHS Controllers practise the window on the simulator: setting the FMS state before missions, reading the clearance list, recognising a stopped truck on the exit ramp, refusing requests into the locked area and reopening loading areas against the map-version check, including the abnormal case where EX02's face has moved. Pit Supervisors learn to plan the window with the earliest-firing estimate and to choose the reopening order by plan priority. Shovel Operators learn that no spot from before the blast is valid. Blast-related competencies of the blast crew and the Pit Supervisor are set by the site's scheme and regulation [TBV] (SRC-REG-AU-BLASTING-SHOTFIRING).

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-17-02-001 [H] The FMS shall support a planned blast window state for a set of loading units, recorded with window ID, planned firing time and owner; on entering it the FMS shall issue no new loading assignment to those units and shall give every truck that is not on a loaded haul a park or hold mission to the points named in the window. Rationale: autonomous trucks leave only by assignment; the window must be a recorded plan item (sections 4.1, 4.2). Verification: test (LAB-17-02-01 part A); inspection of the window record

REQ-17-02-002 [H] The AMS shall lock a blast area only when position reports show no autonomous truck inside and the reservation table shows no granted path leading in; while the lock is in force it shall grant no path and no access permission into the area, and shall release the lock only on a recorded action of an authorised role. A stale position report shall keep the area not clear. Rationale: the lock is the machine side of the blast clearance and must fail closed (sections 4.2, 7.3). Verification: test (LAB-17-02-01 part B); fault-injection test with a stale report; inspection

REQ-17-02-003 [H] The AMS shall present clear of autonomous trucks and people clear as two separate recorded statements; it shall record people clear only as an input from the Pit Supervisor and shall never derive it from its own data. Rationale: the AMS cannot see people on foot and sees manned vehicles only through working PRUs (section 2). Verification: inspection of the HMI and the event log; test with a PRU not reporting

REQ-17-02-004 [H] Any distance used to define a blast zone or hold points shall be a site input with an owner and a reference to the site procedure; the system shall provide no default and shall refuse to plan a floor-hold strategy without it or when no hold point lies outside the zone. Rationale: exclusion distances are site rules [TBV], not system parameters (section 3). Verification: test (LAB-17-02-01 part F, ahs_sim refuses a missing radius); inspection

REQ-17-02-005 [H] After a blast the AMS shall reopen a loading area only when a recorded post-blast inspection exists, a surface of the area with an epoch later than the firing time is published, the AHS map version in use for the area equals the version validated against that surface, and every work zone in the area is released; spots designated before the firing shall not be accepted. Rationale: the pre-blast map no longer describes the face, the muck pile or the floor (sections 4.5, 4.6). Verification: test (LAB-17-02-01 part E); inspection of the reopening record

REQ-17-02-006 [H] The FMS shall assign trucks for a post-blast dig block only when the block has a material class, a destination that accepts that class and a post-blast surface; otherwise the loading unit shall be held. A destination that differs from the loading unit's default shall require the Pit Supervisor's confirmation. Rationale: ore to D01 or waste to C01 is expensive and cannot be undone (section 4.7). Verification: test (LAB-17-02-01 part G); inspection

REQ-17-02-007 [H] The blast-window plan shall state the clearance strategy, the estimated clearance time, the earliest firing time (clearance plus people check), the reopening order and the estimated lost tonnes; if the earliest firing time is later than the planned firing time, the firing time shall be moved. Rationale: the cost of a window is set by its length and restart, and the margin to the firing time must be known (section 4.4). Verification: inspection of shift plans; test (LAB-17-02-01 part C)

REQ-17-02-008 [H] Truck and loading-unit time held by a blast window shall be recorded with the window ID under the delay code that the coding rules assign to a planned blast window (DLY-OD-04), separately from downtime of trucks that fail during the window. Rationale: the cost of blasting must be visible in the delay report (section 8.4). Verification: inspection of the delay report for a window; test (LAB-17-02-01 part A)

10. Verification and validation

  • Model tests. make test CH=ch-17-02 runs nine tests of LAB-17-02-01: the four strategies (clearance, restart, loss, delay hours), the walkthrough against the registry times, the firing slip, the window sweep, the reopening order and the EX02 gate, the zone radius and the people check, and the dig-block check. ahs_sim's own tests cover the module (two exits faster, required radius, loss growing with the window).
  • Simulator exercise [R]. Before the first blast under autonomous operation, the window is run on the simulator with CR1's real HMI: clearance list, lock, refused requests, reopening per area.
  • Dry run on site [R]. A window without firing: assignments stopped, trucks parked, lock applied, people check rehearsed, reopening with the map check. The measured clearance time is compared with the estimate.
  • Validation of the estimate. After each blast, actual clearance, firing and reopening times and the loss are compared with the plan. A systematic gap (for example trucks bunched at T+0 s) is fed back into the estimate.
  • Fault injection. A stale PRU report, a truck stopped on R24, a surface older than the firing, an unclassified dig block: each must keep the area locked or the unit held.
  • Regulatory topics [TBV]. Whether a site's blast procedure or regulation requires specific evidence for the clearance of autonomous equipment is not verified (SRC-REG-AU-BLASTING-SHOTFIRING).

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • A "blast mode" that stops all trucks where they are instead of routing them out: trucks stay in the pit, inside the zone.
  • Locking the area on a timer rather than on evidence of clear.
  • Letting the AMS show "area clear" in a way operators read as "safe to fire".
  • A default exclusion radius in the configuration.
  • Reopening the whole area with one action, without per-area map checks.
  • Keeping the loading unit's default destination when the dig block changes.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Planning the firing time without the clearance estimate; finding on the day that the last truck is under a shovel.
  • Spending the meeting on exits and none of it on the post-blast part of the window, where the cost is.
  • Asking Survey for the EX02 surface before DZ03 has finished.
  • Reopening EX02 because "the face only moved a little".
  • Assigning trucks to a dig block "probably ore" while the classification is pending.
  • Leaving the window open in the delay report as unexplained Operating Delay.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — Ramp queue. With R05 only, 13 trucks must enter R05 at 30 s spacing. The last, DT20, passes P1 at T+851.8 s. With both exits, 2 trucks take R05 and 11 take R24; the last, DT01, passes P3 at T+757.9 s, 93.9 s earlier.

Example 2 — Earliest firing. 757.9 + 300 = 1057.9 s with both exits; 851.8 + 300 = 1151.8 s with R05 only. Planned firing at T+1500 s leaves 442.1 s and 348.2 s of margin, so the exits do not change the loss (16 060 t for a 2700 s window).

Example 3 — When two exits pay. Firing planned at T+1000 s: R05 only delays it by 1151.8 − 1000 = 151.8 s, both exits by 57.9 s. Loss 14 740 t against 14 520 t: one load of 220 t.

Example 4 — Window length. From 2700 s to 3000 s (the registry's reopening time): 17 600 − 16 060 = 1540 t more. From 3000 s to 3600 s: 20 680 − 17 600 = 3080 t more.

Example 5 — The EX02 map gate. On the scenario clock, EX02 reopens at 3900 + 2400 = 6300 s instead of T+4200 s. The loss rises from 17 600 t to 20 020 t. The extra 2420 t are all ore (12 980 − 10 560 = 2420 t): only EX02 waits longer, and waste stays at 7040 t.

Example 6 — Reopening order. Ore units first: the loss stays 16 060 t, but ore falls from 9900 t to 8360 t (1540 t saved) and waste rises from 6160 t to 7700 t.

15. Key takeaways

  • A blast window in AA-North stops the whole mine's production; at the baseline rate every ten minutes are worth more than 2600 t.
  • Clearance is assignment, routing and locking: FMS stops assigning, AMS routes out and locks on evidence. People clear is a separate statement made by people.
  • Clearing 22 trucks takes 851.8 s through R05 and 757.9 s through both exits; the trucks under the shovels at T+0 s set the end.
  • With firing at T+1500 s the exits do not change the loss (16 060 t for a 2700 s window); they buy margin. The window length and the restart are the levers.
  • Reopening is per loading area, on a surface newer than the shot and a matching map version; a moved EX02 face costs 2420 t of ore on the scenario clock.
  • Dig blocks bring material class and destination into the FMS; without them the unit is held.
