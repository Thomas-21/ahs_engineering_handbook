1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

It is a workshop in the training room at the MIA, during the project that brings autonomous haulage into AA-North. On the screen is the supplier's draft concept of operations. Its access section has one sentence: "Light vehicles request access via the field terminal; the AMS grants access when the area is clear." The pit patrol driver who takes LV07 down R05 most mornings reads it twice and asks three questions. Who do I talk to — the terminal or a person? What do I do if the radio says "enter" and the terminal says nothing? And when I am down on R06 and my time runs out, does the system decide I have left?

The day-shift dispatcher, who will become one of the first AHS Controllers, has a different worry. At 18:00 they hand over to the night shift with a truck down on R03 and a light vehicle in the pit, and today that happens in five minutes at the whiteboard. Nothing in the draft mentions handover. The captain of the Emergency Response Team asks how the team reaches a burning truck on the ramp if the system has locked the ramp, and who tells them it is safe to walk up to it. The Pit Supervisor asks what it costs in tonnes every time LV07 goes into the pit.

None of these questions is answered by a better algorithm. They are needs of people who will live with the system, and the draft has not recorded them. Left unrecorded, each becomes a late requirement or a workaround on site. This chapter is about catching them early and writing them so that both sides can check they were met:

  • who the stakeholders of the reference-mine AHS are, and how their needs are elicited and recorded (FIG-03-01-01);
  • the difference between a stakeholder need, a system requirement and a design solution, using LV07's entry to AA-North as the example (FIG-03-01-02);
  • the structure of a concept of operations (ConOps) for autonomous haulage in AA-North, including operating modes and transitions in AA-North and at the MIA boundary;
  • which manned practices the ConOps must preserve, change or replace, and which situations it must cover before go-live — among them a fire on DT17 and an R05 closure with traffic diverted over the west ramp R24.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Identify the stakeholders of the reference-mine AHS (operator, vendor, workforce, regulator, plant, maintenance, survey) and record their needs in a form that can be traced.
  2. Structure a concept of operations for introducing autonomous haulage into AA-North: operating modes, users, scenarios, interfaces and constraints.
  3. Distinguish a stakeholder need from a system requirement and from a design solution, using the LV07 access request as the example.
  4. Specify the operating modes and mode transitions (autonomous, manual, maintenance, stopped) of trucks in AA-North and at the MIA boundary as part of the ConOps.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Express an operational need of the AHS Controller or Pit Supervisor (for example at shift handover) as a clear, testable statement for the engineering team.
  2. Review a ConOps section on access to AA-North and identify missing roles, situations or procedures.
  3. Describe the current manned-operation practices (radio calls, area entry, shift handover) that the ConOps must preserve, change or replace.
  4. Identify the operational situations the ConOps must cover before autonomous haulage starts in AA-North, including a fire on DT17 and an R05 closure with traffic diverted to the west ramp R24.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

In the manned North Pit, many needs were met without anyone writing them down, because a person sat in every haul truck. When LV07 drove down R05, every truck driver on the ramp saw it and gave way. When a truck broke down, its driver called the dispatcher and stayed with the machine. When a fire alarm sounded, the driver chose where to stop, got out and met the fire crew. At shift change the drivers handed over the machines, and the dispatcher told the next dispatcher which trucks were down. These were not features of a system; they were the behaviour of people, and the needs they served — be seen, get help, stop somewhere sensible, know what is still open — were satisfied implicitly [H].

Autonomy removes the driver, and every need the driver met implicitly is left without anyone to meet it. Each must now be met explicitly by a system function, a procedure, a device or a role. The danger is that nobody notices that a need existed until the system is on site. Four patterns recur [H]:

  • The need is never stated. Nobody asked the night shift, the ERT or the plant, so the design has no answer for them. The gap shows up as a workaround: a controller writes permissions on a notepad because the HMI does not list them, or the ERT waits at the zone boundary because nobody knows who may confirm that the truck cannot move.
  • The need is stated as a solution. "We need a button on the terminal to enter the pit" fixes the design before anyone has asked what must be true when the driver enters. The real need — no autonomous truck is routed into the place where I am — is lost, and a button that does not guarantee it can satisfy the sentence.
  • The need is stated without a situation or a measure. "Access should be quick" cannot be designed against or tested. Quick compared with what, with how many trucks on R05, at night?
  • Needs conflict and nobody decides. The LV07 driver wants a short wait at AP-02; the Pit Supervisor wants the ore circuits of EX01 and EX02 to keep running. Both are legitimate. Without a recorded decision, the conflict is resolved by whoever writes the code.

The cost of each pattern grows with time: a need found in a workshop changes a sentence; found after go-live it changes a release, a procedure, the training of four crews and the safety case. The concept of operations is where the project writes down, before design, how the system will be used, by whom and in which situations.

3. Terminology and foundations

Stakeholder. A person or organisation with an interest in the system. Some use it every shift (the AHS Controller, the field crew); some are affected by it without using it (the plant at C01, the ERT); some govern or supply it (site management, the integration authority, system suppliers, the regulator). The reference mine's seven operational roles are defined in mine.yaml and ch-01-06; drivers of manned vehicles such as LV07, DZ03 and WT02 are not a separate role and work under the Pit Supervisor (ch-01-06).

Operational need [H]. What a stakeholder must be able to do, or be certain of, in a stated situation, written in the stakeholder's terms and independent of any design. "Before I drive in at AP-02, I need to know that no autonomous truck will be routed into the place where I will be, for as long as I am there" is a need. This handbook records needs with chapter-local IDs NEED-03-01-nn; they are illustrative and not registered handbook IDs.

System requirement. A verifiable statement of what the system — or one of its elements, or people — shall do, allocated to FMS, AMS, ADS, VCS, independent safety functions, onboard systems, manned-vehicle devices or people (ch-02-03). Requirements in this handbook use the format REQ-VV-CC-NNN with an evidence label, a rationale and a verification method (chapter template). Ch-03-02 develops requirements engineering; this chapter stops at the step from need to requirement.

Design solution. One way of meeting a requirement: a state machine, a screen layout, a lock algorithm, a button. Design solutions are chosen by engineers after the requirement is known. A design solution written into a need or a ConOps freezes a choice before anyone has compared alternatives.

Concept of operations (ConOps). A description of how the system will be used, from the stakeholders' viewpoint: who uses it, in which modes and situations, through which interfaces, under which constraints, and what changes for the people involved. It is written before the requirements are fixed and is kept as the reference against which they are validated. It is not a requirements specification and not a design document.

Operational scenario. A defined sequence of events used to analyse system behaviour and human actions together. The handbook's scenarios are registered once in scenario-registry.yml with a trigger, an initial state, key events on a timeline, and the elements and roles involved. A ConOps for AA-North refers to registered scenarios rather than inventing its own.

Operating mode. How a truck is driven: autonomous (the AMS assigns paths, the ADS drives), manual (a person in the cab drives; the truck is then a manned vehicle that needs a permission) or maintenance (under the workshop's control in MIA) (ch-16-05). A stopped autonomous truck is in a stop state — fault, safety, remote or comms-lost — that says who owns the stop and what can end it.

Needs elicitation [H]. Finding out what stakeholders need: by observing the current operation, interviewing, running workshops, walking through scenarios and reading records such as shift logs, delay records and incident reports.

Stakeholder map [H]. A diagram of the stakeholders around the system, grouped by how they relate to it and showing the channel through which each one acts.

Validation and verification. Validation asks whether the system meets the stakeholders' needs in its operational context ("the right system"); verification asks whether it meets its specified requirements ("the system right"). Needs and the ConOps are validated; requirements are verified. Both are developed in ch-03-07 and Volume 18.

4.2 Eliciting needs

Elicitation at a brownfield mine has a large advantage over a new site: the operation already exists. Five sources are used together [H]:

  1. Observation of the current operation. Ride with the pit patrol driver; sit with the dispatcher through a shift change; listen to the pit radio channel for an hour. Most implicit needs are visible only in practice: the driver's "LV07 at the crest, going down to R06" is a need for awareness that nobody would mention in an interview.
  2. Interviews and workshops with each stakeholder group, including the night shift and contractors. A workshop held only on day shift elicits day-shift needs.
  3. Scenario walkthroughs. Take a registered scenario and, at each key event, ask every stakeholder involved what they need to know, to do or to be sure of at that moment. Section 6 applies this to SCN-POSCOM-LV07.
  4. Records. Shift logs, delay records coded under delay-codes.yml, incident reports and maintenance records show where the current operation struggles. A delay that recurs is a need that is not met.
  5. Constraints from other parts of the system. Other chapters of this handbook already contain requirements that any ConOps for AA-North must respect — for example that a permission fails closed (ch-10-06) or that an exclusion zone is cleared only on the Pit Supervisor's authorisation (ch-04-07). They are constraints on the ConOps, not needs to be renegotiated.

Two cautions apply. A supplier's product description is a source of design solutions, not of needs. And a manager's account of what the controller needs is second-hand; ask the controller.

4.3 Recording needs: the need statement

A need that cannot be traced cannot be shown to be met. The handbook records each need as a short structured statement [H]:

  • ID (NEED-03-01-nn, chapter-local) and stakeholder (a role from the map);
  • situation — the registered scenario in which the need arises, or a described situation if none is registered;
  • need — what the stakeholder must be able to do or be certain of, in their words, without a design;
  • measure — an observable condition by which a reviewer can judge that the need is met;
  • source — observation, interview, workshop, record or constraint;
  • trace — the requirements, procedures or training items that respond to it, once they exist.

4.4 Need, requirement and design solution: LV07 at AP-02

FIG-03-01-02 follows NEED-03-01-01 from the driver's words to the requirements that answer it and to the design solutions that lie beyond the ConOps.

The need is in the driver's terms and says nothing about how. It names a situation (entry at AP-02 in SCN-POSCOM-LV07) and implies a measure (no granted path enters the area while the driver may be inside).

The ConOps places the need in the operation. It states the scenario steps (request at T+0 s, area submitted at T+20 s, lock at T+25 s, grant at T+90 s, read-back at T+95 s, entry at T+100 s), the operating rule that the digital state is authoritative for the machines and the voice exchange for the people (ch-01-06), and the boundary rule that people enter AA-North only at AP-02 or AP-04 (mine.yaml). It still says nothing about how the AMS computes a lock.

The system requirements are verifiable statements, each allocated. From the existing requirements of other chapters:

  • REQ-10-06-001 (AMS): the AMS shall allow entry under a digital access permission only in the active state, reached for the first time only on a valid acknowledgement of its own grant within the acknowledgement deadline;
  • REQ-10-06-002 (AMS): while a permission is in a locked state, the AMS shall close to routing every segment of the permitted area, including the requester's route from the access point, and every segment that meets an intersection the requester crosses;
  • the acknowledgement is sent from the field terminal (manned-vehicle devices), and the read-back and the "enter" are people's actions under the radio protocol of ch-16-03.

The design solutions — the ten-state permission machine of ahs_sim.ams, the derivation of the lock set from the road network, the screen that shows granted — pending acknowledgement — belong to ch-10-06 and ch-16-03. They are not in the ConOps, because a ConOps that specified them would close off alternatives before the requirements were fixed.

Three confusions are common [H]:

Written as a need What it really is Better need
"A button on the terminal to request entry" Design solution "I can ask for entry from AP-02 without leaving the vehicle, and get an answer I can trust"
"The AMS shall lock R05 and R06 when LV07 enters" System requirement for one case, written too early NEED-03-01-01
"Access shall be quick" Wish without situation or measure "With normal traffic on R05, I wait at AP-02 no longer than the time the site has agreed [A]" — and see section 12, example 3

One principle constrains every step of the chain: central permissions never override vehicle-side safety decisions. A need such as the ERT's "make the truck safe so we can approach" can never become a central command that moves a truck or clears its onboard safe state. NEED-03-01-08 is traced to REQ-04-07-004 — after a fire safe-state request the truck rejects every movement command from the AMS and FMS, and no AMS or FMS command clears the onboard safe state — and to REQ-04-07-008, which puts the decision to approach in people's hands at the zone boundary. NEED-03-01-18 is traced to REQ-10-06-008: no permission state delays or cancels a remote stop. A ConOps that describes a control-room "release" of a vehicle-side stop has made an error that no later design can repair.

4.5 The structure of a ConOps for AA-North

A ConOps is short compared with a requirements specification, but it must be complete in coverage. The handbook proposes the following outline [H]. Published guidance on ConOps content exists in systems-engineering standards and handbooks; its designations, editions and content are not verified here [TBV] (SRC-PUB-CONOPS-PRACTICE).

Section Content for AA-North Main sources
1 Purpose and scope AA-North: R05 main ramp, R24 / R25 west corridor, pit floor, loading areas EX01–EX04, the RL 330 bench; access points AP-02 (P1) and AP-04 (P3); what is out of scope mine.yaml
2 The current operation Manned haulage today: how dispatch, entry, breakdowns, fires and handovers work, with their radio calls Observation, shift logs
3 Stakeholders and users The map of FIG-03-01-01 and the need records of section 4.3 Section 4.1–4.3
4 Operating modes and transitions Truck modes and stop states; who changes them and where; area-level hand-back to manual Section 4.6, ch-16-05
5 Operational scenarios Normal, abnormal, degraded and emergency situations, each by registered scenario ID scenario-registry.yml, section 4.8

7 Constraints ODD; marginal coverage on R24; single control room CR1; two 12 h shifts [A]; existing requirements that apply mine.yaml, other chapters
8 Organisation and support Roles, staffing assumptions, competency and training, support from suppliers ch-01-06, ch-16-10

10 Open issues and assumptions Untraced needs, unresolved conflicts, [TBV] items Section 4.3

Two properties make this outline usable. Every operational section points to registered scenarios rather than describing its own, and every section names the needs it answers, so a reviewer can check both directions: no need without a section, no section without a need.

4.6 Operating modes and transitions in AA-North and at the MIA boundary

A ConOps must say how trucks change mode, because each change is a moment at which responsibility passes between a system and a person. The handbook's modes and transitions are defined in ch-16-05; the ConOps restates them as operating rules and adds where each one may happen. Nothing here changes ch-16-05.

From → to Initiated by Where Condition (ch-16-05)
Autonomous → stop state (fault, safety, remote, comms-lost) ADS, VCS, onboard system, independent safety function or loss of AMS link Anywhere in AA-North The cause; the truck decides or the ISF acts, never the control room alone
Stop state → autonomous AHS Controller (resume, reset) after the vehicle reports the cause cleared In place Per stop state: remote reset of a listed fault; authorised resume with checks; staged restart after a remote stop; reconciliation after comms loss; never after fire or an emergency stop at the machine
Stop state → isolated Field Technician with the AHS Controller At the truck, under a permission Permission active; isolation central and at the truck; for fire, the ERT first
Isolated → autonomous AHS Controller In place Return-to-autonomous checks
Isolated → manual Person in the cab At the truck only, truck at rest Mission withdrawn, no reservations, permission covering the route out
Manual → maintenance Maintenance Planner MIA workshop MIA-WS, reached through AP-01 Handed to maintenance
Maintenance → manual Maintenance Planner releases MIA Released by maintenance; driven out of MIA under a permission
Manual → autonomous AHS Controller Park-up PK1 in AA-Central Return-to-autonomous checks

Four rules follow for the ConOps [H], each taken from ch-16-05 or mine.yaml:

  • Autonomous trucks enter MIA only in manual or maintenance mode. The boundary at AP-01 is a mode boundary: no autonomous route ends inside MIA.
  • No stop state leads to manual without passing isolated. Driving a truck needs a person at it, and the person needs a permission and an isolated truck.
  • Maintenance has no direct way back to autonomous. A truck repaired in the workshop is driven to PK1 and accepted there; it does not drive out of MIA in autonomous mode (NEED-03-01-15).
  • A truck in manual mode is a manned vehicle. It needs a permission like LV07 and is protected by the same lock.

The ConOps also needs the area-level view: AA-North as a whole may be handed back to manual operation for a period (hand-back-to-manual, ch-04-02), for example when its ODD conditions cannot be met. The ConOps states who decides it and how manned trucks then enter, but leaves the criteria to the operational boundary documents of Volume 04.

4.7 Practices to preserve, change or replace

The reference mine is brownfield: people already know how to work in North Pit. A ConOps that ignores current practice will be met with practice that ignores the ConOps. For each practice the ConOps states whether autonomous operation preserves it, changes it or replaces it, and names the procedure that implements the result [H].

Current manned practice Decision In autonomous operation Procedure
Radio call before entering the pit ("LV07 at the crest, going down") Change Positive communication with a digital access permission and read-back; the radio call stays, the digital permission is added ch-16-03, ch-10-06
Truck drivers see the light vehicle and give way Replace Area lock removes the area from routing; PRU reports the light vehicle's position ch-10-06, ch-14-02
Driver of a broken-down truck calls the dispatcher Replace The truck reports its stop state; the controller triages; the Field Technician approaches under permission and isolation ch-16-05
Fire: driver stops, gets out, meets the fire crew Replace Onboard detection and safe state; AMS exclusion zone; ERT under positive communication; zone cleared on the Pit Supervisor's authorisation ch-04-07, ch-16-06
Dispatcher assigns trucks by radio Replace FMS assignments; AMS mission execution ch-10-01
Shift handover at the whiteboard Change Generated list of open items, acknowledged item by item, ownership transferred with read-back ch-01-06, ch-16-02
Pre-start toolbox meeting Preserve Same meeting; adds the open items and restrictions of the AHS ch-16-02
Pit radio channel shared by all Preserve Same channel; read-back rules apply to permissions ch-16-03

The two "change" rows are where most friction is found. A driver used to saying "going down" on the radio now also waits for a terminal confirmation; a dispatcher used to a five-minute handover now acknowledges each item. The ConOps must explain why — what need the change serves — or the change will be worked around.

4.8 Situations the ConOps must cover before go-live

A ConOps covers situations, not features. For AA-North the registry already lists the situations: of the 64 registered scenarios, 40 are located in AA-North (section 12, example 1). Before go-live, every one of them must either have a ConOps section that covers it or a recorded reason why it does not apply (REQ-03-01-003). The operations objectives of this chapter name the minimum set [H]:

  • Normal: production on M-20481 (SCN-BASELINE-SHIFT); a light vehicle entering at AP-02 (SCN-POSCOM-LV07); shift handover with open items (SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER).
  • Abnormal and degraded: a stale or duplicated grant (SCN-POSCOM-STALE); a permission expiring while LV07 is inside (SCN-POSCOM-EXPIRY-LV07); a PRU failure (SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07); loss of coverage on lower R05 (SCN-COMMS-R05); an AMS restart (SCN-AMS-RESTART).
  • Emergency: fire on DT17 on R05 (SCN-FIRE-DT17); a remote stop of AA-North (SCN-RSTOP-AANORTH); a person detected near EX01 (SCN-PERSON-EX01).
  • Planned change: the R05 rework with the map update (SCN-MAP-R05) and its closure seen from the road (SCN-CLOSURE-R05). While R05 is closed, pit traffic reroutes over the west ramp — R09, R24, R25, R12 — and mission M-20481 grows from about 4.50 km to about 5.87 km (registry). The ConOps must state that the pit stays connected through R24 when R05 is closed, that both ramps closed means trucks are held (REQ-18-04-005), and that R05 reopens only for trucks on the validated map version (REQ-16-07-008).

The fire is the hardest test of a draft. It involves seven architecture elements and four roles (registry), and the ERT — a stakeholder that never sits in front of an HMI — needs a route into a locked area, a confirmation that the truck cannot move, and a single owner for the decision to clear the zone. A draft that cannot be walked through SCN-FIRE-DT17 with the ERT captain in the room is not ready.

4.9 Trade-offs and limits

  • Depth versus timeliness. A ConOps that tries to answer every question becomes a requirements specification and arrives too late to shape the design. The outline of section 4.5 keeps it at the level of situations, rules and responsibilities.
  • Supplier ConOps versus site ConOps. A supplier's ConOps describes their product in a generic mine. The site's ConOps describes AA-North with its people, its roads and its practices. Both are useful; only the site's can be validated against the site's needs, and the integration authority owns it (ch-02-01).
  • Stated needs versus constraints. Some answers are not negotiable in a ConOps workshop: fail-closed permissions, vehicle-side safety decisions, the exclusion-zone rules. Presenting them as constraints, with their reasons, avoids re-debating approved safety requirements as if they were preferences.
  • A living document. The ConOps is baselined before design and kept under change control afterwards (ch-03-08); each change marks the requirements traced to it for review (REQ-03-01-008).

5. Responsibilities and interface boundaries

The ConOps assigns each situation to elements and people. The table states what the ConOps for AA-North says each element does in the situations of this chapter, and what it never does [H].

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation — eliciting needs along SCN-POSCOM-LV07

This timeline is shared by sections 6, 7 and 8. The times are the registry's key events of SCN-POSCOM-LV07; the release rows at T+1700 s and T+1715 s are ch-10-06's [A] extension. For each step the table shows whose needs are at stake and what the ConOps must say.

T+0 s LV07 radios a request at AP-02 with location, purpose and area LV07's driver, AHS Controller NEED-03-01-02 Entry to AA-North only at AP-02 or AP-04, by radio and field terminal together

Walked through this way, a 100-second exchange yields needs from five stakeholders, including the Shovel Operator's, which the draft did not mention and no existing requirement answers. Each row asks "who else is affected now?".

6.2 Abnormal operation — the stale grant (SCN-POSCOM-STALE)

In the registered variant the grant is issued (T+0 s), arrives at LV07's terminal after its expiry window and is rejected as stale (T+30 s); the area remains locked and the controller is alerted to re-issue (T+31 s). For the ConOps the question is not the message handling — ch-10-06 and ch-02-07 settle that — but what the people do. The driver has heard nothing that matches the terminal; NEED-03-01-02 says they need one clear "enter". The ConOps therefore states the operating rule plainly: when the radio and the terminal disagree, the driver waits at AP-02 and the permission is treated as not granted; the controller re-issues, and the driver enters only when the terminal shows the permission confirmed and the controller has said "enter". The rule protects the person whichever channel failed.

6.3 Degraded operation — LV07 inside with no position reports from its PRU (SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07)

While LV07 is inside, its PRU stops reporting (T+0 s); the AMS marks the position stale and applies the configured protective action (T+10 s); the controller calls LV07 and confirms its location by voice (T+15 s). The need exposed is the controller's: I know where the person is. The ConOps keeps the voice channel as the fallback for people and states that a stale position never ends the permission or unlocks the area (REQ-10-06-006). Voice alone is not enough either: SCN-POSCOM-RADIO-ONLY is the near miss in which the radio exchange alone was taken as a permission.

6.4 A need that is easy to miss — the 18:00 handover (SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER)

At 18:00 the day controller hands over three open items: the speed restriction on R05, LV07's permission and DT17 down on R03. In ch-01-06's timeline the summary is generated at T−600 s, LV07's permission is transferred with a read-back at T+360 s, the outgoing controller still answers the Field Technician at T+600 s because item 3 is still theirs, and the handover record closes at T+900 s. None of this is visible in a ConOps written from the fleet's point of view, because the trucks do not stop for the handover. The needs come from the controllers: NEED-03-01-05 (the list comes from the system), NEED-03-01-06 (I take over a permission only after speaking to its holder) and, from site management, NEED-03-01-16 (a delay running across 18:00 is split, not closed). The ConOps section on the control room names the handover as a situation in its own right, with SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER as its scenario.

6.5 Emergency — the ERT's needs in SCN-FIRE-DT17

DT17, loaded on R05, raises a fire alarm (T+0 s), begins a controlled stop (T+5 s); the AMS creates an exclusion zone and holds R05 traffic (T+10 s); the controller acknowledges and calls the ERT and the Pit Supervisor (T+15 s); the FMS replans the EX02 trucks (T+60 s); the ERT arrives at the zone boundary under positive communication (T+600 s). Between T+15 s and T+600 s the ERT is driving from the MIA through AA-Central into AA-North. The ConOps must state the route (the ERT's access routes through AP-02 and AP-04 are drilled before go-live, REQ-18-05-005), who confirms at the boundary that the truck is in a latched safe state and isolated in the AMS (REQ-04-07-008), and who may clear the zone afterwards (the AHS Controller on the Pit Supervisor's authorisation, REQ-04-07-009). These are NEED-03-01-08, -09 and -10. None of them is a feature of the fire system; all of them decide whether the ERT can do its job.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

  • Needs before requirements, but not long before. Needs are recorded during elicitation and traced as soon as requirements exist; a need table that is never traced becomes a wish list. The table of section 4.3 carries its trace column from the start, and an empty trace is an open issue, not a blank.
  • Reuse the existing requirements as constraints. Tracing new needs to existing requirements avoids a second, inconsistent set and shows where nothing exists yet (NEED-03-01-14).
  • Scenarios from the registry, not from the ConOps. One definition of each situation serves the ConOps, the requirements, the tests and training; the price is that a new situation must first be proposed for the registry.
  • Allocation is part of the step from need to requirement. Each requirement in the trace names an element. Section 12 shows the spread: 14 of the 19 needs reach the AMS, 8 reach people. A need allocated only to people (a procedure or training) is a valid answer, and the ConOps says so explicitly rather than leaving it to be discovered as "not implemented".
  • Modes from the vehicle-side view. The mode table of section 4.6 is taken from ch-16-05, where it is derived from what the vehicle can confirm. A ConOps that defined modes from the control room's view would tend to add transitions the control room can trigger but the vehicle cannot confirm.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

Needs about time must be stated with their conditions, because the same step takes very different times in different situations. In the registry timeline of SCN-POSCOM-LV07 the area is confirmed clear 65 s after the lock, and LV07 enters at T+100 s; the registry notes that this assumes no truck is left to clear, and that with baseline traffic on R05 and R06 clearing takes 346–492 s (LAB-16-04-01). A need "I wait no longer than two minutes [A]" written against the nominal timeline cannot be met in normal production (section 12, example 3). The ConOps resolves such conflicts in one of three ways [H]: state the expected wait with its conditions so that the driver plans for it; reduce the area (ch-10-06 section 4.6 discusses the area and the access point); or accept the production cost of holding traffic. It must not resolve them by shortening the clearing, because the clearing is what makes the area safe.

The ConOps also fixes, for each people-to-system interface (radio and terminal at T+95 s, HMI at T+20 s and T+90 s, plant and FMS), the channel and which side is authoritative; ch-02-06 turns system interfaces into ICDs.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

The ConOps describes what controllers need to see; the requirements traced in section 4.3 specify it. Along the shared timeline:

  • T+20 s: the area the controller has selected, the derived lock set, the loading units that will be held and the estimated production cost, with the Pit Supervisor's approval requested when a circuit is held (REQ-10-06-011).
  • T+90 s: LV07's permission shown as granted — pending acknowledgement, not as active.
  • T+95 s onwards: the permission list with holder, owner, area, access point, state and validity remaining, and separately the holder's awareness state (REQ-10-06-009).
  • At 18:00: the generated handover list with every open object and its owner (REQ-01-06-004).

A ConOps review asks of each screen: which need does this serve? A screen element without a need is a candidate for removal; a need without one is a gap.

8.3 Procedure excerpt — reviewing the AA-North access section of a draft ConOps

The steps follow the timeline of section 6.1 [H]. They are carried out in a review session with the draft, the need table and the registry entry of SCN-POSCOM-LV07 on the table.

  1. Pit Supervisor (chair): confirm that the reviewers include an AHS Controller from each shift, a light-vehicle driver who enters AA-North, a Shovel Operator from EX01 or EX02 and an ERT member.
  2. AHS Controller: read the registry's key events of SCN-POSCOM-LV07 aloud, one row at a time.
  3. All reviewers, at each row (T+0 s to T+100 s): say who is affected at that moment and what they need to know, do or be sure of. Mark each need as present in the draft, present but written as a solution, or missing.
  4. Light-vehicle driver: check that the draft says what to do when the radio and the terminal disagree (section 6.2) and when the time runs out inside (SCN-POSCOM-EXPIRY-LV07).
  5. AHS Controller: check that the draft names the access points (AP-02, AP-04), the read-back, the release confirmation and the handover of an active permission (SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER).
  6. ERT member: check that the draft gives the ERT a route into AA-North and a named person who confirms at the zone boundary that the truck cannot move.
  7. Shovel Operator: check what the draft says about trucks held by the lock and how the shovel learns of it.
  8. Pit Supervisor: list each missing role, situation or procedure with an owner; send the list to the integration authority; record the review with names, shifts and date (REQ-03-01-007).

8.4 KPIs and delay codes affected

The ConOps states how the events it describes will be coded, so that the KPIs agreed with site management measure the operation it describes. In the situations of this chapter [H]: trucks held by LV07's area lock, and a loading unit left without trucks by a held circuit, are DLY-OD-04; trucks held outside a fire exclusion zone are DLY-OD-14; a latched remote stop of AA-North is DLY-OD-13; trucks held for a map-version reload after the R05 rework are DLY-OD-04; longer cycles over the west ramp while R05 is closed are not a delay. All the coded delays are Operating Delay in the GMG Time Usage Model [F] (SRC-PUB-GMG-TUM-2020) and reduce Operating Efficiency; a running delay crosses the shift boundary and is split (REQ-16-02-007). If the ConOps says nothing about coding, each crew codes differently and the KPI comparison between manned and autonomous operation, which site management will ask for, means nothing.

8.5 Training and competency implications

Operations people need two new skills. Stating a need — what they must be able to do or be sure of, in a situation, with a measure — rather than proposing a screen; Pit Supervisors and senior controllers practise it on their own shift logs. Walking a scenario — reading a registered timeline and asking at each step who is affected; it is reused in ch-03-04 and ch-16-10. Engineers need time in the operation they design for: a shift in CR1 and a ride with the pit patrol [R].

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-03-01-001 [H] Each operational need recorded for the AHS shall state its stakeholder role, the situation in which it arises (a registered scenario ID or a described situation), what the stakeholder must be able to do or be certain of, and an observable condition by which a reviewer can judge whether it is met. Rationale: a need without a situation or an observable condition cannot be traced, verified or validated (sections 4.3, 7.2). Verification: inspection of the need records; review of a sample against the need-statement format of section 4.3.

REQ-03-01-002 [H] The integration authority shall keep, for every system requirement derived from an operational need, a trace link to that need and to the ConOps section that describes the need's situation. Rationale: needs and requirements must be checkable in both directions; an untraced need is an open issue (sections 4.3, 7.1). Verification: inspection of the trace records; analysis listing needs without requirements and requirements without needs.

REQ-03-01-003 [H] The ConOps for an autonomous area shall name, for every registered scenario located in that area, the ConOps section that covers it or the recorded reason why it does not apply. Rationale: the registry already lists the situations of the area; a scenario that the ConOps does not cover is unprepared at go-live (section 4.8; 40 scenarios in AA-North, section 12). Verification: inspection of the ConOps scenario index; analysis of the registry against the index.

REQ-03-01-004 [H] The ConOps shall define the truck operating modes and stop states used in the area and, for every permitted transition between them, the role or element that initiates it, the place where it may happen and the condition that must hold. Rationale: each mode change passes responsibility between a system and a person; the transitions must match ch-16-05 (section 4.6). Verification: inspection against the transition table of ch-16-05; review with AHS Controllers and Field Technicians.

REQ-03-01-005 [H] No action described in the ConOps shall clear, delay or override a vehicle-side stop, an onboard safe state or a remote stop; every control-room action on a truck is described as a request that the vehicle may refuse. Rationale: central permissions never override vehicle-side safety decisions (sections 4.4, 5; REQ-04-07-004, REQ-10-06-008). Verification: inspection of the ConOps; review against REQ-02-03-003 and REQ-10-06-008.

REQ-03-01-006 [H] For every current manned-operation practice listed in the ConOps, the ConOps shall state whether autonomous operation preserves, changes or replaces it and name the procedure that implements the result. Rationale: practices that are changed without explanation are worked around (section 4.7). Verification: inspection; review by Pit Supervisors of both shifts.

REQ-03-01-007 [H] The integration authority shall obtain a recorded review of the operations sections of the ConOps by AHS Controllers and Pit Supervisors of both the DAY and the NIGHT shift, and of its emergency situations by the Emergency Response Team, before the ConOps is baselined. Rationale: needs of the night shift, the handover and the ERT are the easiest to miss (sections 2, 6.4, 6.5). Verification: inspection of the review record (names, shifts, date, findings and their owners).

REQ-03-01-008 [H] The integration authority shall record every change to a baselined ConOps with the needs, scenarios and derived requirements it affects, and mark the trace links to those requirements for review. Rationale: requirements validated against an outdated concept are not validated (sections 4.9, 7.2; suspect links in ch-03-03). Verification: inspection of the ConOps change records and of the marked trace links.

Acceptance criteria for a ConOps of AA-North [H]: every need in the table has a section and every section a need; every one of the 40 AA-North scenarios is indexed or excluded with a reason; the mode table matches ch-16-05; the review record names reviewers from both shifts and the ERT; and no section describes a central override of a vehicle-side stop.

10. Verification and validation

Verification of the needs and the ConOps checks that they are complete and well formed:

  • Inspection of need records against the format of section 4.3. The same checks that ch-03-02 applies to requirements can be applied to needs — no vague terms such as "quick" or "user-friendly" without a measure, a stakeholder and a situation present.
  • Coverage analysis against the registry. The figure script of this chapter counts the scenarios located in AA-North; a project script compares them with the ConOps index (REQ-03-01-003).
  • Trace analysis. Every need has a trace or an open-issue owner; every traced requirement exists (the script checks the IDs against the handbook corpus). Ch-03-03 develops trace graphs and coverage.
  • Consistency with existing requirements (constraints). The mode table is compared with ch-16-05; the access rules with ch-10-06; the fire response with ch-04-07.

Validation of the ConOps asks whether the described operation is the one the stakeholders need. It is done with people [H]: table-top walkthroughs of the registered scenarios (section 8.3), ride-alongs that compare the "current operation" section with what drivers actually do, and later operational trials and site acceptance (ch-03-07, ch-18-04, ch-18-05). A need validated only in a workshop is a hypothesis until the first shifts confirm it.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Writing the ConOps from the supplier's product description. It describes a product, not AA-North.
  • Recording solutions as needs ("a button", "a pop-up"), which closes off alternatives and loses the real need.
  • Defining modes from the control room's view and adding transitions the vehicle cannot confirm.
  • A ConOps without a scenario index, so nobody can tell which registered situations it covers.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Sending managers to the needs workshop instead of the controllers, drivers and ERT members who do the work.
  • Holding every workshop on day shift. Night-shift handover and staffing needs are then missing.
  • Asking for a screen instead of stating a need. "We want a red button" gets a red button, not the certainty behind the request.
  • Leaving conflicts unspoken. A driver who accepts a wait in the workshop and calls the controller impatient on site has not had the conflict decided.

12. Worked examples

Registry and need statistics are printed by figures/make_figures.py (it reads scenario-registry.yml through ahs_sim.scenarios and the handbook's requirements through ahs_sim.traceability). Scenario times are the registry's [A] values.

Example 1 — the situations of AA-North. The registry holds 64 scenarios; 40 of them are located in AA-North. By category: network 6, mixed-fleet 5, access-control 4, production 4, lifecycle 3, loading 3, road 3, vehicle 3, mapping 2, safety-function 2, safety-onboard 2, and one each of architecture, control-room and localisation. Of the 40, all involve the AHS Controller, 38 involve people as an architecture element, 3 involve the ERT and 10 are variants of another scenario. A ConOps index for AA-North therefore has 40 rows; if each row takes 20 minutes [A] to review in a workshop, the index alone needs 40 × 20 = 800 minutes — more than 13 hours of workshop time. That is the honest size of "covering the registered situations".

Example 2 — the need table. The 19 records of section 4.3 come from 12 of the 15 stakeholders on the map. Sixteen are traced to 20 distinct requirements of 8 chapters (ch-01-06, ch-02-03, ch-04-07, ch-10-06, ch-16-02, ch-16-05, ch-16-07, ch-18-05); one is met by training only, one is open and one is a [TBV] placeholder. Counted per element over the allocations of the table: AMS 14, people 8, FMS 4, manned-vehicle devices 2, and ADS, VCS, onboard systems and independent safety functions 1 each. The AMS dominates because most of the needs elicited here concern access, handover and holds; a different elicitation (Survey, maintenance) would shift the spread. The open need (NEED-03-01-14) is 1 of 19, about 5 %: small, but it is the one that would otherwise surface on site.

Example 3 — a need about time. In the registry timeline LV07 enters at T+100 s, 65 s of which is clearing after the lock at T+25 s. With baseline traffic on R05 and R06, clearing takes 346–492 s (registry note, LAB-16-04-01). Keeping the 10 s from grant to entry of the registry timeline, entry happens at

tentry=25+tclear+10 s

that is 25 + 346 + 10 = 381 s to 25 + 492 + 10 = 527 s, or 6.4 to 8.8 minutes. A need stated as "no longer than two minutes [A]" (120 s) cannot be met in normal production; it can be met only when no truck is inside the area. The ConOps must either state the expected wait with its conditions, reduce the area, or accept the production cost — and record which.

Example 4 — a need about the handover. In SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER the handover runs from T+0 s to T+900 s for three open items: 900 / 3 = 300 s per item. A need written as "handover within 15 minutes" holds for three items; with six items at the same pace it would take 6 × 300 = 1800 s, 30 minutes. The controllers' real need is not a duration but completeness — every item transferred explicitly (NEED-03-01-05, -06) — and the duration is a consequence of the number of items. Written that way, the need leads to REQ-16-02-001 (a generated list) rather than to a timer.

15. Key takeaways

  • Autonomy removes the driver, but not the needs the driver met implicitly; each must now be met explicitly by a system function, a procedure, a device or a role.
  • A need states what a stakeholder must be able to do or be certain of, in a situation, with a measure — not a design.
  • Need, ConOps, requirement and design are four different things; FIG-03-01-02 follows LV07's entry through them.
  • The ConOps covers situations by registered scenario: 40 for AA-North. A scenario without a section is unprepared.
  • Modes and transitions are part of the ConOps and follow ch-16-05: no stop state leads to manual without isolation, MIA is entered only in manual or maintenance mode, and the way back to autonomous goes through PK1.
  • No ConOps action clears, delays or overrides a vehicle-side stop, an onboard safe state or a remote stop (REQ-03-01-005).
  • Elicit from the people who do the work, on both shifts, with the ERT in the room.
