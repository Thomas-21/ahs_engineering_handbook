1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

At 17:50 the handover view in CR1 refreshes. The day AHS Controller expects three lines — the R05 speed restriction, LV07's permission on R06, DT17 down on R03 — because those are the three situations they have been talking about on the radio all afternoon. The view shows five. Below DT17 sit two more: the Field Technician's own access permission for the work area around the truck, and DT17's drivetrain-fault alarm, acknowledged at 17:31 and still active. Neither was in the controller's head as a separate thing; both are separate things in the AMS. The technician's permission is also the most expensive item on the list: while it locks R03, the dump D01 cannot be reached, and the eighteen trucks of EX03 and EX04 have been held since 17:45.

The night controller logs in at the second workstation at 17:58. In the next fifteen minutes, while the fleet keeps running, responsibility for all five objects must move to them; two involve people in autonomous areas.

The scene raises questions that this chapter answers:

  • What exactly must cross 18:00, and how can both controllers know that nothing was left behind?
  • How does LV07's permission change owner without being cancelled, re-issued or silently extended?
  • What happens if the Field Technician calls with news halfway through, or if a fire alarm goes off?
  • What does the night controller need to check at the start of the shift that is not an open item at all?
  • What must be in the records at 06:00 so that the next shift, the Pit Supervisor and the Maintenance Planner can act?

ch-01-06 introduced the handover as a transfer of responsibility for running states and set the 18:00 timeline of SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER. This chapter works it out as a procedure and as a system function. It covers:

  • the shift as a cycle of checkpoints, from handover in to handover out;
  • the handover snapshot: which system objects it contains, how they are generated, grouped and checked;
  • ownership transfer of an active digital access permission, and why an unowned permission fails closed;
  • shift-start checks the system can run itself and those that need a person;
  • deferring a handover while an event is not yet stabilised;
  • end-of-shift records: shift log, delay coding across the shift boundary and the open-item list.

All procedures, data fields and check rules are handbook conventions [H]; every time, expiry, identifier and production figure is an illustrative assumption [A] and none is a recommended value. Fatigue management and shift-length regulation are outside the chapter's scope and are named only as topics [TBV].

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Specify the system-state snapshot AMS and FMS must provide at handover (active permissions, stopped trucks, speed restrictions, closed segments, pending map versions, open alarms) so that no open item exists only in a controller's memory.
  2. Explain how ownership of an active digital access permission transfers between controller sessions without revoking it unintentionally or extending it silently, and why an un-owned permission must fail closed.
  3. Design a handover view in which open items are generated from system state rather than typed, each with owner, start time, expiry or review condition, and acknowledgement by the incoming controller.
  4. Specify which shift-start checks the system can perform automatically (map version consistency across trucks, comms sites CT1–CT3, base station GB1, mode of every truck) and which need human confirmation.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Conduct the 18:00 handover in SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER so that each of the three open items (LV07 permission, DT17 down on R03, R05 speed restriction) is stated, confirmed and signed for by the incoming controller.
  2. Perform shift-start checks for CR1: fleet and mode status, AHS map version, comms sites CT1–CT3 and GB1 status, active permissions and restrictions, and planned work for the shift.
  3. Complete end-of-shift records — shift log, delay coding and open-item list — that the next shift, the Pit Supervisor and the Maintenance Planner can act on.
  4. Identify handover failure modes (implicit items, verbal-only transfer, handover during an active event) and apply the rule for deferring a handover until an event is stabilised.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

The reference mine runs two 12 h shifts, DAY from 06:00 to 18:00 and NIGHT from 18:00 to 06:00 (mine.yaml, [A]). The trucks do not stop for the change of controller. Twice a day, therefore, the one person in CR1 who answers for the autonomous fleet in AA-North, AA-Central and AA-South is replaced by another person while the fleet is moving, people are in the pit, and restrictions are in force. The handover is the most predictable hazard in the control room: it happens every twelve hours, at a known time, and the hazard log of ch-04-01 records it as HZ-08, open item lost at the controller shift handover, with controls CTL-31 to CTL-34 (generated summary, item-by-item acknowledgement, ownership accepted after read-back, permissions that persist until released by their owner).

ch-01-06 showed three ways a handover goes wrong at this mine: a restriction carried into the night with no recorded reason, a light vehicle whose permission nobody in CR1 knows about, and a decision that cannot be traced to a person. Three further problems motivate this chapter.

People think in situations; systems hold objects. "DT17 on R03" is, in the AMS, a stopped truck, a Field Technician's permission, a standing alarm and a running delay record. Transferring the situation can leave one object without an owner (section 1.1).

The handover falls at the busiest hour. In ch-01-06 example 1 the controller's occupancy over the whole DAY shift is 0.42, but 0.75 between 17:00 and 18:00, because a stopped truck, a permission and the handover preparation all fall in the last hour. A handover that depends on the outgoing controller assembling a list at that moment will be incomplete on exactly the days when it matters.

Open items carry cost and records across the boundary. The technician's permission on R03 holds every waste circuit (section 4.3); DT17's breakdown starts on one shift and ends on the next. If the night controller does not know why each object exists and when it should end, the mine pays until someone notices, and the shift reports are wrong.

The chapter follows SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER from 17:50 (T−600 s) to the R05 review at 20:00 (T+7200 s) and on to 06:00.

3. Terminology and foundations

Shift and shift handover. A shift is a scheduled working period of a crew (glossary shift); the shift handover is the transfer of responsibility from the outgoing to the incoming person in a role (glossary shift-handover). This chapter is about the AHS Controller's handover; the Pit Supervisors and Shovel Operators have their own (ch-01-06 section 6.1).

Open item and system object. An open item is anything still in progress at a shift change that the incoming shift must carry on (glossary open-item). The handbook distinguishes the system objects that hold an open item in AMS or FMS — a permission, a stopped truck, a restriction, a closure, a pending map version, a standing alarm — from the checklist item a person discusses. One checklist item can contain several linked objects [H].

Handover snapshot [H]. The list of system objects that AMS and FMS generate from their own state at a defined time, each with its owner, start time and review condition. It is a starting point: the incoming controller acknowledges the live state, not the snapshot.

Owner and owner session. Every object has exactly one owning controller (REQ-01-06-003); for a permission the owner is the permission owner (glossary permission-owner), distinct from the holder, the person or vehicle in the area. Ownership is attached to a person's session at a workstation, not to the workstation.

Review condition [H]. The time or event at which an open item must be looked at again: an expiry for a permission, a review time for a restriction (R05 at 20:00), a decision for a stopped truck ("recovery decision by the Pit Supervisor after diagnosis"), a clearing condition for a standing alarm. An object without a review condition has no defined end.

Acknowledgement (at handover). The incoming controller's record that they found the object in the live system and understood it. For a permission the transfer also needs the holder's read-back (section 4.5). It refers to one version of the object [H].

Handover record, shift log and event log. The handover record shows that each object was acknowledged and transferred, by whom and when; the shift log holds decisions, reasons and context; the event log is the machine record of commands and state changes (glossary handover-record, shift-log, event-log; ch-01-06 section 4.7).

Shift-start check [H]. A check made at the start of a shift on the state of the system as a whole — fleet modes, map versions, comms sites, the GNSS base station — as opposed to the open items. Some checks the system can make and report; others need a person.

Handover deferral [H]. The rule that a handover is not started, or is stopped, while an event is in its first response phase, and resumes once the Pit Supervisor declares the event stabilised (section 4.8).

Situational awareness. Knowing what is happening across one's span of control, what it means and what will happen next (glossary situational-awareness). A handover works only if it transfers enough of it for the first call.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The shift as a cycle of checkpoints

A controller's shift does not begin at 18:00 and end at 06:00 as a single block of supervision. It runs through a sequence of checkpoints, each with a purpose and a record [H]. FIG-16-02-01 shows them for the NIGHT shift.

Checkpoint When (NIGHT shift) [A] Purpose Record
Handover in 17:50–18:15 Take over every open object with its context Handover record
Shift-start checks 18:15–18:30 Confirm that the system as a whole is fit to run the shift Check record in the shift log
Review points carried over 18:40 (LV07 expiry), 20:00 (R05 review) Act on the review conditions inherited at handover Shift log; object updated
Structured scans every 2 h Areas, alarms, active permissions and open objects scanned in a fixed order Scan entry
Mid-shift check 00:00 Open objects, delay records, staffing for the second half Shift log
End-of-shift records 05:20–05:50 Close what can be closed; complete shift log and delay coding Shift report
Handover out 05:50–06:15 Generate the snapshot, add context, hand over Handover record

The table is a teaching convention, not a recommended timetable. Handover in and handover out are the two ends of one cycle: what the night controller does at 05:20 decides how long and how complete the 06:00 handover is.

Whether twelve-hour shifts, the number of consecutive shifts, breaks or fatigue limits are appropriate for control-room work is a question for the site's fatigue-management arrangements and the applicable legislation. This handbook does not state any limit or requirement on them [TBV] (SRC-REG-AU-FATIGUE-SHIFT).

4.2 What the snapshot must contain

ch-01-06 divided what crosses the boundary into system state, context and ownership. The snapshot carries the first and third; the outgoing controller adds the second. ahs_sim.control_room models the snapshot with six kinds of system object [H]:

Kind Source Created when Minimum fields Typical review condition
PERMISSION AMS A digital access permission is in any non-terminal state Identifier, holder, area, access point, owner, state, validity remaining Expiry; holder reports out
STOPPED_TRUCK AMS, FMS A truck is stopped for a fault or held for recovery Truck, location, reason, protective reservation, isolation state, owner Recovery decision; return to service
RESTRICTION AMS A speed restriction is in force Segment, value, reason, authorising Pit Supervisor, owner Review time with the role that can judge it
CLOSURE AMS A segment is closed Segment, reason, authorisation, rerouting in force Reopening condition
MAP_PENDING AMS An AHS map version is approved but not deployed to every truck Version, trucks on the old and new version Deployment complete
ALARM AMS An alarm is acknowledged but still active (standing) Source, code, priority, since Clearing condition

Each kind comes from the system that holds the state, so a permission is on the snapshot exactly while the AMS holds it. Each object carries a review condition, because an object that cannot end becomes a permanent cost. Running delay records are attached to the object that causes them (DT17's breakdown to STOPPED_TRUCK-01, the held waste circuits to PERMISSION-02), not listed separately (section 4.9).

4.3 Generating the 18:00 snapshot

At 17:50 (T−600 s) handover_items turns the AMS and FMS state of SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER into five objects, all owned by the day controller's session CTRL-DAY [A]:

Object Subject Since Review condition or expiry
PERMISSION-01 PERM-LV07-0001: LV07, R05 + R06 (windrow on R06), AP-02 17:40 expires 18:40 (T+2400 s)
PERMISSION-02 PERM-FT-0001: Field Technician at DT17, R03, AP-01 17:45 expires 19:45 (T+6300 s)
STOPPED_TRUCK-01 DT17 down on R03 17:30 recovery decision by the Pit Supervisor after diagnosis
RESTRICTION-01 R05 speed restriction 20 km/h, both directions 14:21 review 20:00 with Survey (T+7200 s)
ALARM-01 DT17 drivetrain-fault alarm, acknowledged 17:31 17:30 clears when DT17 is recovered or returned to service

The scenario registry and ch-01-06 speak of three open items, and the handover view still shows three: the lab groups an object whose detail names another object as linked under that object's item. Item 1 is RESTRICTION-01; item 2 is PERMISSION-01; item 3 is STOPPED_TRUCK-01 with PERMISSION-02 and ALARM-01 linked to it. FIG-16-02-03 shows the result.

Grouping is for people; acknowledgement is per object [H]. The technician's permission has its own holder, area, window and cost, and ends at a different time from DT17's recovery. In the lab's case C1 a typed list of the three situations, acknowledged item by item, leaves PERMISSION-02 and ALARM-01 unacknowledged — visible only because the list is generated.

The cost the item carries. The technician's permission was cleared under the area rules of ch-10-06 (section 6.5 there): the lock includes R03 and the technician's route from AP-01, and D01 can be reached only over R03 and R04. ahs_sim.fms.fleet_times confirms that with R03 locked only EX01 and EX02 have a route to their destination. With DT17 out of the allocation, simulate_fleet gives 101 420 t of ore and 88 000 t of waste per shift on the open network, and 101 420 t of ore and no waste with R03 locked. The permission therefore costs 88 000 / 12 = 7333.3 t of waste per hour and holds 18 trucks (DLY-OD-04). The model locks whole segments, so this is an upper bound for a lock on R03; whether one lane can stay open past a stopped truck is a work-zone design question (ch-14-03, ch-16-05). For the handover the lesson is that the smallest-looking line can be the most expensive, so the snapshot must show the held circuits beside the object (REQ-16-02-009).

The fleet overview at T+60 s comes from the same state: 39 trucks scheduled (DT40 not scheduled), 36 available, DT17, DT29 and DT39 down (equipment.yaml baseline allocation; ch-01-06 section 6.2).

4.4 Checking the snapshot: four lists that must be empty

handover_check compares the objects with the incoming controller's acknowledgements and returns four lists [H]:

List Meaning Lab case that fills it
no_owner An object nobody answers for C3: the outgoing session was lost; PERMISSION-01 has no owner
no_review An object with no expiry and no review condition C2: the R05 restriction was entered without a review time
not_acknowledged An object the incoming controller has not (validly) acknowledged C1: typed list; C4: acknowledgement before an update
acknowledged_unknown An acknowledgement of an object that does not exist C5: a paper list adds a closure of R07 reopened at 15:00

The handover is complete only when all four are empty. At T+900 s in the scenario they are.

The fourth list is easily overlooked. It means the incoming controller is working from another list — a paper sheet, a note, memory — and believes in a state the AMS no longer holds: here a harmless closure of R07, elsewhere a road believed closed to trucks that is open.

Acknowledgements refer to a version. The snapshot is taken at T−600 s; the state goes on changing. At T+600 s the Field Technician reports that the fault cannot be cleared, and at T+660 s the night Pit Supervisor approves manual recovery to the MIA workshop. The lab's diff between the 17:50 list and the live list at 18:10 returns exactly one changed object, STOPPED_TRUCK-01. In the scenario item 3 is acknowledged at T+780 s, after the change, so the acknowledgement is valid. In case C4 the incoming controller acknowledged item 3 early, at T+300 s; the update at T+600 s voids the acknowledgement of STOPPED_TRUCK-01 — but not of PERMISSION-02 or ALARM-01, which did not change — and the object returns to not_acknowledged. The rule [H]: an acknowledgement given before the object last changed does not count, and the handover view marks changed objects since the snapshot (REQ-16-02-003).

4.5 Transferring ownership of an active permission

Ownership of an ordinary object moves when the incoming controller acknowledges it. A permission needs one step more, because a person in the field depends on it: the holder must know who in CR1 now answers for them, and the incoming controller must have heard from the holder where they are and what they are doing. ch-10-06 (REQ-10-06-010) set the rule at the machine side: every non-terminal permission is listed and passed to the incoming controller by explicit acknowledgement, without cancelling or re-issuing it. This section adds what the transfer must and must not do.

What changes: the owner, nothing else. transfer_permission(owner, incoming, acknowledged) returns the incoming controller as owner only when the acknowledgement has been given; otherwise the outgoing controller remains owner and an alarm is raised. The lab follows PERM-LV07-0001 as an ahs_sim.ams.Permission object through the handover: its state is ACTIVE and its area locked at every scenario time from T+0 s to T+900 s. Ownership is a property of the permission record, not a state of the permission machine. The area, the validity window and the lock are untouched.

Why not cancel and re-issue. Re-issuing to the new owner looks tidy, but between cancellation and new grant LV07 would be inside AA-North with no permission: the area is either unlocked (unsafe) or locked under nothing. A transfer ends nothing, so there is no gap.

No silent extension. The transfer must not reset the validity window: LV07's permission expires at 18:40 whoever owns it. More time is an extension — agreed by radio, entered by the owner, read back (ch-10-06 section 4.8) — recorded as such. A quiet new window would leave a person working beyond what anyone decided.

The read-back belongs to the transfer. The incoming controller calls the holder, names themselves as the controller now responsible, and accepts ownership only after the holder has confirmed position and task and read back the permission (ch-01-06 section 8.3). At T+366 s in ch-04-10 the controller misheard the expiry, asked for a repeat and only then accepted, so the lab moves PERMISSION-01 at T+366 s. The wording follows the rules of ch-16-03 section 4.4 (call sign first, one permission one identifier, no new information in a read-back) [H]: the incoming controller "LV07, CR1 night controller now holds PERM-LV07-0001, AA-North R05 and R06, expiry 18:40, confirm position and task"; the driver "LV07 at R06 windrow, inspecting, PERM-LV07-0001, AA-North R05 and R06, expiry 18:40"; the controller "correct" — and only then acknowledges the object on the HMI.

No owner fails closed. If the outgoing controller's session is lost before the transfer — a workstation fails, the controller is called away — the permission must not lapse. In case C3 the permission has no recorded owner: handover_check lists it under no_owner, transfer_permission(None, …) returns no owner and an alarm, and the permission object stays ACTIVE with its area locked; the session loss is not an event of the permission machine and changes nothing. Another authorised controller takes the permission over through the same read-back with the holder. A permission with no owner keeps the trucks out; it never lets them in (REQ-01-06-003, REQ-16-02-004).

4.6 Sessions, log-out and the transfer as one transaction

FIG-16-02-02 shows the lifecycle of an open object across a handover: raised and owned with its state, listed in the snapshot, acknowledged against the live system, transferred, and closed — with the two paths that must not end the object: a change, which sends it back to listed, and a lost session, which leaves it without owner but in force.

Ownership is attached to the person's session, not the workstation (ch-01-06 section 7.1). Two consequences follow.

The log-out rule (REQ-01-06-005) is evaluated per object. In the lab the outgoing controller owns five objects at T+0 s, four after the restriction at T+180 s, three after LV07's permission at T+366 s and none after item 3 at T+780 s (18:13). Log-out is refused at T+600 s, when the Field Technician calls, because the outgoing controller still owns item 3 — which is exactly why they are the right person to answer the call. The handover record closes at T+900 s.

Each transfer is atomic: at no instant does an object belong to nobody or to both. For a permission the transaction is: incoming controller selects the object; the AMS shows the holder, area, validity remaining and held circuits; the holder's read-back is confirmed; the AMS writes the new owner, the old owner, the time and "read-back confirmed" in one record. If any step fails, the old owner remains.

4.7 Shift-start checks

The open items say what is unfinished. They do not say whether the system as a whole is fit to run the next twelve hours. That is the job of the shift-start checks, made after the handover (FIG-16-02-01, 18:15 to 18:30). Some the system can make and report; others need a person [H].

Check Automatic? Finding in the lab's synthetic 18:00 state [A] Blocking?
Every truck in autonomous mode carries the active AHS map version yes DT08 still on the previous version yes: DT08 is not dispatched until updated
Trucks not in autonomous mode on an old version yes (information) DT29, DT39 (planned maintenance) no: checked before they return
Mode of every truck agrees with its FMS status yes none —
Comms sites CT1–CT3 up yes all up —
GNSS base station GB1 up, corrections current yes up —
Approved map version not yet deployed yes none —
Every open object has an owner and a review condition yes none —

The principle: the system checks the consistency of its own data — versions, modes, site status, ownership — because it can do so completely every time; people check what the data cannot show — plans, road conditions, who can be reached, where the stop transmitters are. A map-version mismatch is blocking on a truck in autonomous mode, because the truck may follow geometry that no longer matches the road (Volume 12); on a truck in maintenance mode it is information to resolve before the truck returns.

4.8 Handover during an active event: the deferral rule

During an emergency, moving responsibility object by object is wrong: the controller who started the response holds the picture, and splitting it at the most uncertain moment creates the gap the handover is meant to prevent. ch-01-06 section 6.3 set the convention for a fire; this chapter states it as a rule [H].

Defer the handover — do not start it, or stop it — while any event is in its first-response phase: fire or suppression activation, a person or unauthorised vehicle in an autonomous area, a collision or injury, a remote stop before every truck is confirmed stopped, an area-wide loss of communication before its common cause has been identified. The outgoing controller keeps everything not yet transferred and runs the event; the incoming controller, directed by the Pit Supervisor, takes the rest of the fleet as a second controller.

Resume when the Pit Supervisor declares the event stabilised: the protective state is in place (trucks stopped or held, exclusion zone or work zone set), the people needed have been called (ERT, Field Technician), and the Pit Supervisor has decided who runs the event from now on. The event then becomes an open object with an owner and a review condition, and is handed over like any other — usually last, so that its owner can answer questions until the end.

Deferral keeps a tired person on duty longer. How long that may be is a fatigue-management question this handbook does not answer [TBV]; the procedure only requires the Pit Supervisor to decide it explicitly.

4.9 End-of-shift records

The handover out at 06:00 is only as good as the records the night controller completes before it. Three records matter [H].

The shift log holds decisions and reasons: why R05 was kept or lifted at 20:00 and on whose authority, what the technician found, when DT17 left R03, who approved the extension of a permission. The event log already has the commands; the shift log explains them.

Delay coding applies the codes of delay-codes.yml (defined in ch-01-05, analysed in ch-22-07; this chapter does not redefine them). Two rules are specific to the shift boundary. A running delay event is split at the boundary, not closed: DT17's breakdown (DLY-DN-01) is 0.5 h on the DAY shift and 5.5 h on the NIGHT shift if DT17 returns at 23:30 (ch-01-06 example 3). And each open object carries its delay codes with it: while PERMISSION-02 locks R03, the eighteen waste trucks are DLY-OD-04 and so are EX03 and EX04 as loading units left without trucks (coding rules in delay-codes.yml). The lab computes the effect of getting the first rule wrong with ahs_sim.kpi: on the NIGHT shift, with 39 trucks scheduled and DT29 and DT39 in planned maintenance, Physical Availability is 0.9370 with the breakdown coded and 0.9487 if the event is closed at 18:00 and not reopened — overstated by 1.2 percentage points.

The open-item list is not written by the night controller: the system generates it at 05:50. The controller writes the context for each object and closes what can be closed. The best preparation for a short handover is fewer objects open at 05:50.

4.10 Trade-offs and model limits

  • Generated versus typed. A generated snapshot cannot omit an object the system holds, but it can only list what is a system object. A conversation with the Shovel Operator about a wet floor at EX02 is context, not an object; it belongs in the shift log and in the context of the nearest object, or it is lost.
  • Grouping versus completeness. Grouping linked objects makes the list readable; acknowledging per object keeps it complete. A view that only lets the controller acknowledge the group would trade the second for the first.
  • Strict versus usable acknowledgement. The lab voids an acknowledgement on a change of owner, review condition or detail; a site may choose fewer fields [A], but not none.
  • Model limits. The lab's snapshot is a fixed list built from ch-01-06's scenario, not a running AMS; the grouping rule reads a linked: marker in the object detail; the shift-start state is synthetic, with one injected mismatch; the production cost is a steady-state upper bound from a segment-level lock.

5. Responsibilities and interface boundaries

Element Responsibility at shift start, handover and end of shift
FMS Supplies stopped and down trucks, equipment status and running delay records to the snapshot; shows the production cost of held circuits; splits delay records at the shift boundary
AMS Holds every permission, restriction, closure, standing alarm and pending map version with its owner; generates the snapshot; runs the four checks; records acknowledgements, ownership transfers and read-back confirmations; refuses log-out while a session owns objects; keeps a permission and its lock when its owner session is lost; runs the automatic shift-start checks
ADS Not involved in the handover; keeps its own map version and mode, which the shift-start checks read
VCS Reports faults (DT17) that become stopped-truck objects
Independent safety functions Unaffected by the handover; the remote-stop console and portable transmitters are part of the human shift-start check
Onboard safety-related systems Their alarms can be standing alarms carried across the handover
Manned-vehicle devices PRU and radio let the incoming controller confirm each holder's position and read-back
People Outgoing AHS Controller: adds context, remains owner until transfer, runs any event that defers the handover. Incoming AHS Controller: acknowledges each object live, calls each holder, accepts ownership, runs the shift-start checks. Pit Supervisor: accountable for the handover, decides deferral and resumption, confirms decisions that need authority. Holders (LV07, Field Technician): confirm position and task, read back. Maintenance Planner: reads the end-of-shift records

The key boundary runs between what the system lists and what people explain: the AMS must never need a person to remember that an object exists, and nobody should expect the AMS to explain why.

Two details are deliberate: the fleet is reviewed before the first item (T+60 s), so each item is read against the whole picture; and item 3, the one most likely to change, comes last, so its owner stays owner while it does.

6.2 Abnormal operation — a fire during the handover (deferral)

Suppose that on another evening, at T+300 s, DT11 reports fire detection and suppression activation while climbing R05 loaded (SCN-FIRE-DT17 with a different truck, [A]). Item 1 has been transferred; items 2 and 3 have not.

Time [A] Event Who
T+300 s Fire alarm (P1); handover stopped at once Outgoing controller
T+305 s to T+400 s First response: alarm acknowledged, exclusion zone confirmed, traffic held or rerouted via R24, ERT and Pit Supervisor called (ch-04-07, ch-16-06) Outgoing controller
T+320 s Pit Supervisor directs the incoming controller to take AA-Central and AA-South as second controller Pit Supervisor, incoming controller
T+1500 s ERT in attendance; zone stable; Pit Supervisor declares the event stabilised and names the night controller as its owner from the next transfer Pit Supervisor
T+1500 s onward Handover resumes: items 2 and 3, then the fire as a new object with owner and review condition ("ERT scene safe report") Both controllers

During the deferral the outgoing controller still owns LV07's permission; if the fire takes all their attention, the Pit Supervisor tells the driver to hold position under the permission. Nobody transfers it during the fire.

6.3 Abnormal operation — the outgoing session is lost before item 2

At T+300 s workstation 1 freezes and the outgoing session ends ([A]). The AMS raises a "no owner" alarm for each untransferred object; PERM-LV07-0001 stays ACTIVE with R05 and R06 locked, PERMISSION-02 keeps R03 locked. The incoming controller takes over each permission by read-back with its holder (section 4.5). Nothing in the field changes.

6.4 Degraded operation — handover without a generated snapshot, and a failed shift-start check

If the snapshot cannot be generated, the controllers fall back to the manual handover of ch-01-06 section 6.4 (live AMS lists, every holder called, ownership transferred in the AMS as soon as possible). Completeness depends on people again and the four checks cannot run; the degraded handover is recorded and reported to the Pit Supervisor.

A failed shift-start check is different: the handover is complete, but not every truck is fit. In the lab DT08 carries the previous map version; the night controller holds it out of autonomous dispatch, has it updated and records the finding. Had CT3 been down at 18:15, the check would have pointed to SCN-COMMS-R05 and its playbook (ch-16-07).

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

Objects, not notes. Every state that can outlive a shift is a first-class object with an owner and a review condition (ch-01-06 section 7.1); linked objects are recognisable as linked for grouping but stay separate for ownership and acknowledgement.

A snapshot plus a live diff. A snapshot taken ten minutes early gives the outgoing controller time to add context. The cost is that it ages. The design keeps both: the snapshot for context, the live list for acknowledgement, and a diff that marks every object changed since the snapshot.

Ownership is a field, not a state. Ownership changes are records on the permission, never transitions of the permission state machine of ch-10-06. That keeps the fail-closed invariants of the machine untouched: no handover action, and no handover failure, can move a permission into or out of a locked state.

Read-back gates the transfer. The AMS accepts a new permission owner only with a recorded read-back: slower, but it puts a voice check exactly where ch-04-10 found the human error point.

No timer releases anything at handover either. The handover adds no timeout that could end a permission, release a restriction or close an object. If a handover runs long, objects stay with their owners.

System checks for consistency, people for reality. Map versions, modes, site status and ownership are checked by the system; plans, conditions, reachability and the presence of stop transmitters are checked by people (section 4.7).

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

Walk the shared timeline of section 6.1 and ask what each interface must carry [H]:

  • T−600 s. AMS and FMS → view: each object with kind, subject, owner, since, review condition, links, held circuits and attached delay records; HMI → AMS: context per object with author and time.
  • T+0 s. Session start of the incoming controller (person, role, workstation, time); the live list replaces the snapshot as the basis for acknowledgement.
  • T+180 s and T+780 s. Acknowledgements, each with the object version it refers to.
  • T+360 s to T+366 s. Radio call and read-back; read-back confirmed (with a corrected-read-back flag, ch-04-10); ownership transfer record for PERM-LV07-0001.
  • T+600 s to T+660 s. Owner's update of STOPPED_TRUCK-01, object marked changed, earlier acknowledgements void, log-out refused with the list of owned objects; FMS decision record for the manual recovery.
  • T+900 s onward. Four checks on the live list; record closed with both persons; automatic shift-start results and recorded human checks.

Three timing requirements follow. The clock of the AMS, FMS and voice recorder must agree (ch-01-06 section 7.3), or the order of the update at T+600 s and an acknowledgement near it cannot be established. Transfers are atomic (section 4.6). And objects persist across an AMS failover: owners and acknowledgements must survive a restart in the middle of a handover (ch-10-08), or the handover must restart from the beginning.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

The handover view [H] shows the generated list as in FIG-16-02-03: one block per item, one row per object, with owner, start time, review condition, held circuits, cost and acknowledgement state. Changed objects are marked; void acknowledgements are struck through, not removed. A header shows the four checks — the record closes only when all read zero — and what the outgoing session still owns. Permission rows show the holder's PRU position and age, to compare with what the driver says.

The shift-start view lists the automatic checks with pass, finding or information, and the human checks with a field for who confirmed them and when.

8.3 Procedure excerpts

(a) Handover in — keyed to the timeline of section 6.1 [H]

  1. Outgoing AHS Controller (T−600 s): open the generated list. For each object add reason, approval, next action and review condition. Do not start non-urgent work that would create new objects.
  2. Incoming AHS Controller (T+0 s): log in under your own identity. Review the fleet area by area and the alarm list before taking any item.
  3. Both (T+60 s onward): take the items in the order of the list, item 3 last. For each object, find it in the live system, ask until the context is clear, then acknowledge it.
  4. Incoming AHS Controller (each permission): call the holder; give your name as the controller now responsible; have the holder confirm position and task and read back the permission, including its expiry. Accept ownership only after a correct read-back. Do not change the expiry as part of the transfer; if more time is needed, extend it afterwards as a separate step.
  5. Outgoing AHS Controller: answer every call about an object you still own, update the object and tell the incoming controller. Re-acknowledge any object marked changed.
  6. Pit Supervisor: confirm the decisions that need your authority before the objects are transferred.
  7. Incoming AHS Controller (T+900 s): check that the four lists are empty and close the record. Outgoing AHS Controller: log out.

(b) Deferral — decision tree [H]

  • Is any event in its first-response phase (fire, person or unauthorised vehicle in an area, collision or injury, remote stop not yet confirmed, area-wide comms loss with cause not yet identified)?
    • Yes: stop the handover. Outgoing controller runs the event and keeps all untransferred objects. Pit Supervisor assigns the rest of the fleet to the incoming controller as second controller. Go to the next question when the Pit Supervisor declares the event stabilised.
    • No: continue.
  • Is the event stabilised (protective state in place, people called, owner decided)?
    • Yes: resume; hand over the event as an object with owner and review condition, last.
    • No: keep the handover stopped; the Pit Supervisor decides how long the outgoing controller stays.

(c) Shift-start checks (T+900 s to T+1800 s) [H]

  1. Read the automatic check results. Hold out of autonomous dispatch any truck with a blocking finding; record it.
  2. Call every permission holder not already called during the handover.
  3. Take the Pit Supervisor's briefing: planned work, shovel moves, people expected in the pit.
  4. Confirm road and weather conditions from the field.
  5. Confirm that the Pit Supervisor and a second controller can be reached, and that RST-CR1 and RST-P2 are present.
  6. Enter the inherited review times (LV07 at 18:40, R05 at 20:00) as reminders and act on them when due.

(d) End of shift (05:20 to 05:50) [H]

  1. Close what can be closed: release permissions whose holders are out; lift or confirm restrictions at their review.
  2. Complete the shift log: decisions, reasons, who authorised them.
  3. Check every running delay record: it continues across 06:00; nothing is closed by the shift change.
  4. Add context to every object that will appear in the 05:50 snapshot.

8.4 KPIs and delay codes affected

The handover is not a delay for the autonomous trucks, which keep running (ch-01-06 section 8.4). What moves the KPIs are the open objects and the records [H]:

Handover quality can be measured as well [R]: objects handed over, time taken, acknowledgements made void, corrected read-backs, deferrals, and objects found after a handover that were not on it — the last should be zero, and with a generated snapshot any non-zero value points to a state that is not yet a system object.

8.5 Training and competency implications

Controllers practise the handover as a task on a simulator: a generated list, a change injected at T+600 s, a misheard read-back at T+366 s, a lost session, an emergency that forces a deferral. They learn to read linked objects and held circuits. Pit Supervisors learn the deferral decision; field roles learn to expect an unfamiliar voice at 18:06 and what to read back. Assessment and records are the subject of ch-16-09.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-16-02-001 [H] At each shift handover the AMS and FMS shall generate, from their own state, a list containing one object for every non-terminal access permission, stopped or down truck, speed restriction, closed segment, approved but undeployed map version and acknowledged-but-active alarm, each with its kind, subject, owner, start time, review condition or expiry, links to other objects and attached delay records. No object shall be creatable without an owner and a review condition. Rationale: an open item must not exist only in a controller's memory (sections 4.2 and 4.3). Verification: test (LAB-16-02-01: five objects from the SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER state; case C2 flags a missing review condition); inspection of the object schema.

REQ-16-02-002 [H] The handover view shall group linked objects under one checklist item and shall require a separate acknowledgement, and for permissions a separate ownership transfer, for each object. Rationale: a typed or grouped transfer can carry the situation forward and drop a linked object (section 4.3). Verification: test (LAB-16-02-01 case C1: PERMISSION-02 and ALARM-01 reported not acknowledged); demonstration.

REQ-16-02-003 [H] An acknowledgement shall refer to the version of the object it was given for; when the owner, review condition or detail of an object changes, the AMS shall void earlier acknowledgements and mark the object as changed. The handover record shall be closed only when no object lacks an owner, a review condition or a valid acknowledgement and no acknowledgement refers to an object that does not exist. Rationale: the incoming controller must sign for the live state, not for a snapshot or a paper list (section 4.4). Verification: test (LAB-16-02-01: diff 17:50 → 18:10 marks STOPPED_TRUCK-01; case C4 voids its early acknowledgement; case C5 reports CLOSURE-01; complete handover returns four empty lists).

REQ-16-02-004 [H] Ownership of an active permission shall move to the incoming controller only after their acknowledgement and a recorded read-back by the holder, in one atomic record, and shall not change the permission's state, area, lock or validity window. If the owner's session ends before a transfer, the permission and its area lock shall remain unchanged, the AMS shall alarm that the permission has no owner, and only an authorised controller shall take it over, by the same read-back. Rationale: cancelling, re-issuing or silently extending a permission at handover leaves a person without a valid permission or beyond what was agreed (section 4.5; REQ-10-06-010). Verification: test (LAB-16-02-01: PERM-LV07-0001 ACTIVE and locked throughout; case C3: no owner, alarm, area locked, permission unchanged); exercise.

REQ-16-02-005 [H] Site procedures shall defer or stop a handover while an event is in its first-response phase; the outgoing controller shall keep every untransferred object and run the event; the handover shall resume only when the Pit Supervisor declares the event stabilised, and the event shall then be handed over as an object with owner and review condition. Rationale: splitting a response between two people at its most uncertain moment creates the gap the handover is meant to close (section 4.8). Verification: exercise (fire during handover, section 6.2); inspection of the procedure and the handover record.

REQ-16-02-006 [H] At the start of each shift the AMS shall check automatically and report: the AHS map version of every truck against the active version, the mode of every truck against its FMS status, the status of CT1–CT3 and GB1, undeployed map versions, and objects without owner or review condition. A truck in autonomous mode with a map-version mismatch shall not be dispatched until resolved. The checks that need a person shall be recorded with who confirmed them and when. Rationale: the system can check the consistency of its own data completely; people check what the data cannot show (section 4.7). Verification: test (LAB-16-02-01: DT08 blocking, DT29 and DT39 information); inspection of the shift-start record.

REQ-16-02-007 [H] A running delay event shall continue across the shift boundary and be split between the shifts in reporting; no delay event shall be closed by the shift change itself. Rationale: an event closed at 18:00 overstates the next shift's availability (section 4.9). Verification: test (LAB-16-02-01: DLY-DN-01 0.5 h and 5.5 h; Physical Availability 0.9370 against 0.9487); inspection of shift reports.

REQ-16-02-008 [H] The handover record shall hold, for each object, the acknowledging person and time, the version acknowledged, and for permissions the read-back confirmation, including whether a read-back had to be corrected. Rationale: a corrected read-back is the most informative event of a handover and is lost unless recorded (ch-04-10; section 4.5). Verification: inspection of the record after the SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER exercise (correction at T+366 s present).

REQ-16-02-009 [H] The handover view shall show beside each object the loading units and trucks held because of it and its estimated production cost per hour. Rationale: the cost of an open object does not follow from how it sounds; the technician's permission on R03 holds every waste circuit (section 4.3). Verification: test (LAB-16-02-01: R03 lock holds EX03 and EX04, 18 trucks, 7333.3 t of waste per hour); demonstration.

Simulator drills built on SCN-SHIFT-HANDOVER with injected changes, a lost session and a deferral are the closest substitute. Fatigue and shift-length aspects cannot be validated in this handbook at all [TBV].

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • A free-text handover field as the only record. It gives context and guarantees nothing.
  • Acknowledging a group. One tick for "DT17" loses the technician's permission.
  • Ownership as a permission state. A "transferring" state would touch the fail-closed machine of ch-10-06.
  • Re-issuing the permission to the new owner. It opens a gap with a person inside.
  • Resetting the validity window at transfer. It is an extension nobody agreed.
  • Shift-start checks as a tick list. The system should check its own data; people should not tick that for it.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Handing over situations instead of objects. Read the list, not your memory.
  • Accepting a permission without hearing the holder. The read-back is part of the transfer.
  • Taking the most volatile item first. It will change during the handover; take it last.
  • Handing over in the middle of an event. Defer; finish the first response; hand over the event as an object.
  • Closing delay records at 06:00 "to start clean". They continue across the boundary.

12. Worked examples

All values are illustrative [A]; the numbers come from LAB-16-02-01 unless stated.

Example 1 — how long. ch-01-06 example 4 estimated about 210 s per item. Here five objects took 900 s; item 3, three objects and one radio call, took 120 s once the decision was made. Length follows the radio calls (one per permission) more than the number of objects.

Example 2 — the cost of the technician's permission. With R03 locked, EX03 and EX04 have no route to D01: 18 trucks held (DLY-OD-04) and 7333.3 t of waste lost per hour, upper bound. If the permission runs from 17:45 to its expiry at 19:45, the cost is 2 h × 7333.3 t/h = 14 666.7 t of waste and 18 × 2 = 36 truck-hours of DLY-OD-04, of which 1.75 h × 7333.3 = 12 833.3 t falls on the NIGHT shift. This is why the item needs a review condition and its cost on the view, and why the Pit Supervisor, not only the controller, should know it at 18:00.

Example 3 — the boundary coding error. NIGHT shift: 39 trucks scheduled × 12 h = 468 truck-hours; DT29 and DT39 in planned maintenance (24 h of Downtime). With DT17's 5.5 h coded, Physical Availability = (468 − 24 − 5.5) / 468 = 0.9370; with the event closed at 18:00, (468 − 24) / 468 = 0.9487. The difference, 1.2 percentage points, is the availability that would be reported for a truck that was in the workshop.

Example 4 — an early acknowledgement. The incoming controller acknowledges item 3 at T+300 s to save time. At T+600 s the outgoing controller records the technician's report. The view marks STOPPED_TRUCK-01 changed and voids its acknowledgement; PERMISSION-02 and ALARM-01 are unchanged and stay acknowledged. The handover cannot close until STOPPED_TRUCK-01 is acknowledged again — which costs one more look and prevents the night controller signing for a truck whose recovery plan they have not seen.

Example 5 — the log-out clock. CTRL-DAY owns 5 objects at T+0 s, 4 at T+180 s, 3 at T+366 s and 0 at T+780 s: log-out is refused at 18:10 and possible from 18:13. Taking item 3 first would have left the night controller answering the technician's call at 18:10 about a fault they had known for three minutes.

15. Key takeaways

  • A handover transfers objects, not situations: three items at 18:00 are five objects in the AMS.
  • The system generates the list; people add context; the incoming controller acknowledges each object in the live system; the four checks must be empty before the record closes.
  • An acknowledgement refers to a version; a change voids it.
  • An active permission changes owner, never state, area or validity window; without an owner it keeps the trucks out.
  • Defer the handover during the first response to an event; hand the event over afterwards as an object.
  • Shift-start checks: the system checks its own data, people check what the data cannot show.
  • Delay records cross the boundary; an object's cost belongs on the handover view (7333.3 t of waste per hour for a technician's permission on R03).
