1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

09:40, control room. For the third time in ten minutes the C01 panel turns from READY to C01 not ready – downstream full. Each hold has lasted well under a minute, and each time the plant has come back on its own. But the queue at C01-Q has not come back: DT17, loaded at EX02 on mission M-20481, is third in line, and two more ore trucks are coming down R02. The AHS Controller watches the plant send READY again; ten seconds later the AMS grants the tipping bay to the first truck at C01-Q, which drives off along R21. Forty-five seconds after that the plant says not ready once more. The truck that has just left C01-Q keeps going and tips two minutes later, into a plant that has said it cannot take it.

The Pit Supervisor walks over with three questions, and the plant control room asks a fourth by phone:

  • Why do holds of forty seconds build a queue of five trucks, when the crusher is "fine"?
  • Why does D01, which takes almost as many trucks, never queue like this?
  • Why did the AMS let a truck tip after the plant said not ready — and is the crusher pad safe when that happens?
  • (Plant control room) Can the mine send fewer trucks for a while instead of all of them waiting at C01-Q?

The answers depend on how one tipping position behaves as a service station near its capacity, on what the AMS must know before a truck leaves C01-Q, and on who decides what at the crusher. This chapter covers:

  • C01's single tipping position and D01's three tip points as service stations, and why C01 at a utilisation of 0.977 is the sensitive point (open queue against closed loop), with bunching and short holds;
  • the tipping-bay grant, the ready condition before a truck leaves C01-Q, and the loads committed on R21;
  • crusher-pad exclusion through access permissions, delay coding, and the roles of the plant control room and the AHS Controller;
  • SCN-BINLEVEL-C01 walked through from both sides (sections 6 to 8), with SCN-CRUSHER-C01-DOWN and SCN-MISSION-CANCEL-M20481 as further cases.

The manoeuvre at the tipping position and the sensing on the final approach are ch-15-08's subject; crusher failures and the fleet's response to a long stop are ch-15-06's; the plant interface itself is ch-15-03's. ICD-C01-STATUS 1.0 (ch-02-06) is used unchanged. Every time, rate, capacity and distance in this chapter is an illustrative assumption [A] computed with ahs_sim 0.18.0 (LAB-15-01-01); procedures and conventions are the handbook's [H]; no plant design value is asserted.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Model C01's single tipping position as a service station and calculate truck queue time at C01-Q from arrival rate, spot time and dump time in mine.yaml [A].
  2. Specify the tipping-bay reservation and the "ready to accept" condition that the AMS requires before a truck leaves C01-Q for the crusher pad.
  3. Compare the queueing behaviour of C01 (one tipping position) and D01 (three tip points) and explain why C01 is more sensitive to arrival bunching.
  4. Specify the crusher-pad exclusion rules for manned vehicles and plant personnel during tipping and how the AMS enforces them through access permissions.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Interpret the crusher queue at C01-Q and the crusher state indications on the HMI, and decide when to ask the FMS to rebalance ore and waste haulage.
  2. Explain the roles of the plant control room and the AHS Controller at the crusher pad, including who may stop tipping and how it is communicated.
  3. Code crusher-related delays (queue, not ready, blocked) so that plant and mine delays can be separated in reports.
  4. Apply the procedure for plant personnel who need access to the crusher pad area while autonomous trucks are tipping at C01.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

The reference mine has two truck destinations, and they are built very differently (mine.yaml). Crusher C01 has one tipping position, reached from the Queue point C01-Q over the 430.1 m crusher pad link R21. Each truck occupies it for 40 s of Spotting and 50 s of tipping: 90 s, or 40 trucks per hour. Waste dump D01 has three tip points, each taking a truck for 45 s and 40 s: 85 s per truck and 127.1 trucks per hour in total. In the baseline DAY shift the fleet model brings 39.08 ore trucks per hour to C01 and 33.33 waste trucks per hour to D01. C01 runs at a utilisation of 0.977; each tip point at D01 at 0.262.

D01 has spare capacity everywhere; a truck almost never waits. C01 has almost none: the ore circuit is crusher-limited (ch-10-02), and ore trucks queue at C01-Q on every cycle even when nothing is wrong. That queue is the price of keeping a single tipping position fed, not a fault. But every second the position does not work is ore lost, and any disturbance — a short hold, a group of trucks arriving together, a slow restart — shows up at once as a longer queue and less ore.

Three things make the crusher harder to run than the dump.

The truck must commit early. R21 takes a loaded truck 103.2 s. To keep one tip every 90 s, the AMS has to send the next truck down R21 before the truck ahead has even started Spotting. Once it is on R21 it finishes (ch-10-07 section 4.3): the plant's not ready stops the trucks still at C01-Q, not those already committed.

Two control rooms run one interface. The plant control room runs the crusher and decides its state; the AHS Controller runs the trucks. They share ICD-C01-STATUS 1.0 and a telephone. Neither can do the other's job, and a phone call does not make C01 ready.

Plant and mine delays look alike. Unless the delay records carry the plant's reason, the shift report cannot say how much of the queue at C01-Q was the mine's own doing.

3. Terminology and foundations

Tipping position, tipping bay (glossary tipping-bay). The single place at C01 where a truck tips into the crusher pocket, against a wheel stop. The AMS grants it to one truck at a time. At D01 the corresponding places are the three tip points (ch-15-02).

Crusher pad (glossary crusher-pad) [H]. The area around the C01 tipping position in which trucks manoeuvre and tip: the end of R21, the turning and reversing area and the tipping position. Plant personnel reach it from the plant side; the AMS treats it as one area for access permissions.

Queue point (glossary queue-point). Where a truck waits for the tipping bay: C01-Q for C01, D01-Q for D01. Cusp point (glossary cusp-point): where the truck stops travelling forward and starts reversing towards the tipping position.

Spotting (glossary spotting). The manoeuvre into the tipping position. mine.yaml gives C01 a spot time of 40 s and D01 45 s; this volume reads both as Cusp point → spotted (reverse and position) [A], the same reading as for the shovel's spot time (Volume 13). The approach over R21 is travel, not Spotting.

Service time and utilisation (glossary server-utilisation). Service time S is how long one truck occupies a tipping position: spot plus dump. Utilisation is the fraction of time a position is busy: arrival rate × service time ÷ number of positions.

Open queue (glossary open-queue) [H]. A queueing model in which arrivals come from outside at a fixed rate whatever happens in the queue. The textbook formulas used here are open-queue results. The fleet is a closed queueing network (glossary closed-queueing-network): the same trucks come back, and a truck that waits arrives later on its next cycle.

Bunching (glossary bunching). Trucks arriving in groups rather than spread out.

Bay grant and release [H]. The AMS's decision to give a truck at C01-Q the tipping bay and to send it down R21. The grant includes the path over R21 to the Cusp point and the tipping position.

Committed truck (glossary committed-truck) [H]. A truck that has been granted the tipping bay and has left C01-Q: on R21, Spotting or tipping. It completes Spotting and tipping and moves off whatever C01's state becomes (ch-10-07 section 4.3); only vehicle-side causes stop it (ch-15-08).

C01 status (ICD-C01-STATUS 1.0, ch-02-06). READY, STOPPING with stop_at_s, NOT_READY with a reason, repeated every 5 s; older than 15 s is lost; invalid, stale or absent means NOT_READY with its own alarm. Only the plant sets it. The AMS gateway (glossary gateway, debounce) makes a change towards a more restrictive state effective at once and a change towards a less restrictive state only after the plant has sent it for 10 s [H]/[A] (ch-15-03).

Plant control room (glossary plant-control-room) [H]. The processing plant's own operators who run C01 and the plant beyond it. They are an external party to the mine's AHS, not one of the seven roles of mine.yaml; "Plant" in this chapter's timelines is the crusher control system.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The tipping cycle at C01

FIG-15-01-01 shows the crusher area to scale and a schematic of the crusher pad.

One truck's tipping cycle has seven steps [H]:

  1. Wait at C01-Q. The truck stops at the Queue point. The AMS holds it until it can grant the tipping bay.
  2. Grant and release. The AMS grants the bay and the path over R21. This is the only point at which C01's state matters to the truck.
  3. R21. 430.1 m at up to 15 km/h, 103.2 s loaded (haul_cycle, LAB-15-01-01).
  4. Cusp point and Spotting. The truck stops at its Cusp point and reverses to the wheel stop: 40 s in mine.yaml (Cusp point → spotted). The AMS builds the Cusp point and reversing path; the ADS follows them, executes the manoeuvre and stops on its own sensing (ch-15-08, ch-13-02).
  5. Tip. Hoist up, dump, body down: 50 s in mine.yaml. While the body is up the VCS refuses any motion (ch-08-03).
  6. Move off. The truck drives forward out of the tipping position and back towards R21.
  7. Report. The AMS records the tip; the FMS dispatches the truck to its next load.

The tipping position is occupied in steps 4 and 5: 90 s per truck [A]. The model overlaps one truck's move-off with the next truck's approach, which the pad layout must allow (ch-15-08).

4.2 Two destinations as service stations

A destination is a service station with c identical positions, each serving one truck at a time. With arrival rate λ (trucks per second) and service time S=tspot+tdump the utilisation per position is

ρ=λSc

At the baseline (LAB-15-01-01):

C01 D01
Positions c 1 3
Service time S 40 + 50 = 90 s 45 + 40 = 85 s
Capacity 40.0 trucks/h 127.1 trucks/h
Arrivals (fms, DAY shift) 39.08 trucks/h 33.33 trucks/h
Utilisation per position 0.977 0.262

For an open single-server queue with Poisson arrivals and a general service time with coefficient of variation cs, the mean wait before service is given by the Pollaczek–Khinchine formula [TBV] (source register SRC-PUB-QUEUEING-FORMULAS; implemented as ahs_sim.plant.mg1_wait_s):

Wq=ρS(1+cs2)2(1−ρ)

For several positions the lab uses the Allen–Cunneen approximation [TBV] (same source; mgc_wait_s):

Wq≈C(c,a)Sc(1−ρ)·ca2+cs22,a=λS

where C(c,a) is the Erlang C probability of waiting and ca the coefficient of variation of the gaps between arrivals. With spot and dump times each varying with a coefficient of variation of 0.2 [A], cs = 0.142. The formulas give a mean wait of 1957.5 s at C01 and 0.98 s at D01.

The factor 1/(1−ρ) is the whole story of the single tipping position. At D01, 1−ρ = 0.738 and the factor is about 1.4. At C01, 1−ρ = 0.023 and the factor is about 44. The same trucks with the same variability wait two thousand times longer at C01 than at D01 in this model, almost entirely because C01 has no spare capacity.

4.3 Why 0.977 is the sensitive point: the open queue and the closed loop

The figure of 1957.5 s is not a forecast. It is what an open queue would settle to over an infinitely long time if 39.08 trucks per hour kept arriving whatever happened at C01-Q. Three runs of LAB-15-01-01 show what the number means and what it does not.

The open queue over one shift. simulate_tipping lets Poisson arrivals at 39.08 per hour queue for one tipping position over a 12 h shift after a one-hour warm-up, for seeds 0 to 9. The mean wait is 609.4 s, the 90th percentile 1230.5 s, and the longest queue 20 trucks. The shift is too short for the queue to reach the steady state of the formula: near ρ = 1 the queue drifts up and down for hours before it settles. With perfectly regular arrivals the wait is zero — at 0.977 a regular stream fits into the 90 s slots with 2 s to spare. The wait is caused by variability, and the single position has almost no slack to absorb it.

The closed loop. In the mine, arrivals are not independent of the queue. The 19 ore trucks cycle EX01 or EX02 → C01 → back. A truck that waits at C01-Q leaves later, is loaded later and comes back later; the arrival rate falls exactly when the queue grows. fms.simulate_fleet runs this loop. Deterministic, the ore trucks wait 127.6 s per tip at C01 and C01 tips 40.00 trucks per hour — the queue is steady and the crusher never idles. With seeded variability (fms coefficients of variation 0.15 and 0.10 [A], seeds 0 to 9) they wait 113.5 s per tip and C01 tips 38.67 trucks per hour. ch-10-02 measured the same loop per EX02 cycle and found the same pattern: 145.6 s deterministic, 110.3 s seeded, C01 busy 96.7 % of the time. The two measures differ in what they average; the order of magnitude is the point.

What the difference means. The closed loop cannot explode: there are only 19 trucks. It pays for variability in throughput instead — C01 tips 1.33 fewer trucks per hour than its capacity because the queue is sometimes empty — and in waiting spread over C01-Q and the shovels. Neither shows in the open-queue formula.

The open queue still answers one question: how close to the edge is C01? The factor of 44 says that lost tipping time cannot be made up later. In the closed loop that shows up as lost ore rather than as a long queue: a minute in which C01 does not tip is 220 t × 60 / 90 = 146.7 t of ore the circuit never delivers (ch-10-07 section 4.4). At D01 the same minute is absorbed by the other tip points.

Panel (a) of FIG-15-01-02 shows both formulas and both open-queue simulations against the arrival rate, with the two closed-loop results at C01 as single points. The C01 curves rise steeply from about 30 trucks per hour; the D01 curves stay below 20 s up to 80 trucks per hour, over twice the baseline. The closed-loop points sit low and to the left: the loop keeps its queue short by bringing fewer trucks.

4.4 Bunching hits a single position harder

Trucks do not arrive at C01-Q evenly. They merge at Intersection X from two loading units, follow each other down R02, leave a restarted queue together, and catch up behind a slower truck. Panel (b) of FIG-15-01-02 and the table below compare C01 and D01 at the same utilisation per position, 0.7 [A] — 28.0 trucks per hour at C01 and 88.9 at D01 — with trucks arriving alone, in pairs or in threes (LAB-15-01-01, seeds 0 to 9).

Arrivals C01, regular groups C01, random groups D01, regular groups D01, random groups
Single trucks 0.0 s 106.0 s 0.0 s 27.9 s
Groups of 2 45.0 s 255.9 s 2.0 s 69.7 s
Groups of 3 90.0 s 366.1 s 0.0 s 104.6 s

Mean wait at the Queue point. Regular groups are perfectly periodic; random groups arrive as a Poisson stream of groups.

The regular columns isolate the mechanism. At C01 the second truck of a pair always waits a full service, 90 s, and the third of a group of three 180 s: the means are 45 s and 90 s whatever the average rate. At D01 the second and third trucks of a group go to the other tip points; they hardly wait. With randomness on top, C01's waits are three to four times D01's at every group size, and grow by 260 s from single trucks to groups of three. The single position has nowhere to put the second truck.

The AMS must therefore not create bunches itself: after a hold it releases the queue one truck at a time at the bay's pace (section 4.5, ch-15-06). Spreading ore trucks out by dispatch is a design question for ch-21-02.

4.5 The bay grant and the ready condition before a truck leaves C01-Q

The AMS grants the tipping bay at C01-Q, not at the pad. The grant is the only place where C01's state acts on a truck. Once granted, the truck is committed. That simple rule of ch-10-07 has a consequence that is easy to miss because R21 is long.

The R21 pipeline. To tip one truck every 90 s the next truck must reach its Cusp point as the previous one starts to leave. R21 takes 103.2 s, more than one service. The AMS therefore releases each truck from C01-Q a lead time

tlead=tR21−S=103.2−90=13.2 s

before the truck ahead starts Spotting. If instead the AMS waited until the tipping position was empty before releasing the next truck, every truck would add its R21 travel to the cycle and C01 would tip only 3600 / (103.2 + 90) = 18.6 trucks per hour — less than half its capacity. Pipelining is not optional at C01; it is what makes the 40 trucks per hour of mine.yaml possible.

Committed loads. With the pipeline running, the AMS has always committed more than one truck. LAB-15-01-01 checks every instant of a busy cycle: when the plant sends NOT_READY, two or three trucks (2.15 on average) are already on R21 or in the tipping position, and the last of them finishes tipping up to 193.2 s later. The plant therefore receives up to three more loads after it says it cannot take any. Under the rule of ch-10-07 this is by design: the AMS does not stop a truck on R21 or a raised body half-way.

The release condition [H]. The AMS releases a truck from C01-Q only when all of the following hold:

  • the gateway's effective C01 state is READY — a valid message, not older than 15 s, and READY sent continuously for 10 s — or STOPPING with stop_at_s no earlier than the release time plus R21 travel, Spotting and dump (103.2 + 40 + 50 = 193.2 s [A]). A STOPPING check that counts only the dump, or only Spotting and dump, admits trucks that cannot finish in time;
  • the bay slot is free: the truck ahead will have left the tipping position by the time this truck reaches its Cusp point;
  • no access permission and no work zone in a locked state covers R21, the pad or the tipping position (ch-10-06, ch-14-03);
  • the truck itself is fit to go (no active stop cause, localisation and its own checks in order — the ADS's decision).

Anything missing, stale or contradictory counts as not satisfied. Nothing in the condition can be set by the AHS Controller or the FMS on the plant's behalf.

What this means for the plant. NOT_READY stops the trucks at C01-Q within one message (the AMS acts at once on a more restrictive state). It does not stop the two or three committed trucks. A plant that needs to stop accepting loads must therefore send NOT_READY early enough to take up to three more — about 193.2 s of committed tipping in this model — or announce the stop with STOPPING and a stop_at_s, which lets the AMS stop releasing trucks that would not finish. How the plant sizes its triggers (bin level, pocket level) is a plant design matter [A], treated generically in ch-15-04. The interface agreement must state the number, so that the plant designs for it (REQ-15-01-003).

What it does not mean. Committed tipping is not a safety function for the pad. Pad safety rests on the plant's own isolation and on the access permission (section 4.7), never on a NOT_READY stopping a truck. ch-15-08 discusses why the rule of ch-10-07 is written this way.

4.6 Short holds: SCN-BINLEVEL-C01

In SCN-BINLEVEL-C01 the surge bin below C01 [A] runs near its high limit because the plant downstream is slow. The plant switches C01 between READY and NOT_READY (reason DOWNSTREAM_FULL) for short periods. Through the gateway (LAB-15-01-01, run_gateway) the registry times become: effective NOT_READY at T+0 s; the plant's READY at T+40 s confirmed at T+50 s; NOT_READY again at T+95 s, at once.

The cost of one hold. A hold of 40 s plus the 10 s confirmation blocks new Spotting starts for 50 s. If it begins while a truck is in the tipping position, that truck finishes and only the rest of the 50 s is lost. Averaged over every instant at which a hold could begin, a continuously fed position loses 13.9 s of tipping time per hold (deterministic service). The R21 pipeline shifts this by the travel time but does not change the loss: the trucks committed before the hold keep the position busy while the hold lasts, and the gap appears when they have finished and the first truck released after the hold is still on R21.

Recurring holds. LAB-15-01-01 repeats 40 s holds every 600, 300, 190 or 95 s over a whole shift, in the open queue at the baseline rate and in the closed loop (seeds 0 to 9):

Hold every Open queue: mean wait Open queue: longest queue Closed loop: wait per tip Closed loop: C01 tips per hour
no holds 609.4 s 20.0 113.5 s 38.67
600 s 811.2 s 23.3 139.3 s 38.28
300 s 1337.1 s 30.7 181.4 s 37.52
190 s 1348.2 s 30.3 187.9 s 37.47
95 s 1430.5 s 31.2 192.8 s 37.45

The two models tell the same story in different currencies. The open queue shows a queue that more than doubles, because the holds take C01 below the arrival rate. The closed loop shows waits up by 79.3 s per tip and output down by 1.22 tips per hour — about 270 t of ore per hour (8508 against 8238 t) — at the most frequent holds. Neither the AMS nor the controller can win that ore back by sending trucks faster: the bottleneck is downstream of C01, in the plant. What the mine can do is stop feeding a queue that the plant cannot work off — bring fewer ore trucks for a while, at the reduced rate the plant can accept, and send the spare truck time to waste. That is the agreement of the scenario at T+120 s and the FMS rebalancing proposal at T+300 s.

The gateway's 10 s confirmation is a deliberate cost inside these numbers. It never delays a NOT_READY; it delays every READY by 10 s, so that a flicker shorter than 10 s never releases a truck (ch-15-03).

4.7 Crusher-pad exclusion for manned vehicles and plant personnel

Two kinds of people come to the crusher pad: plant personnel walking in from the plant side to clear a chute, inspect the pocket or fix a wheel stop, and manned vehicles — a light vehicle, a service truck, a crane. The rule [H] is simple: no person and no manned vehicle is on the crusher pad while an autonomous truck is granted the tipping bay, and the only way onto the pad is a digital access permission (ch-10-06) whose area covers the pad, the tipping position and R21 beyond C01-Q.

The AMS enforces it in the order of ch-16-04 section 4.2:

  1. Request. The plant person or driver asks the AHS Controller, by radio, for access to the crusher pad, stating the task and the expected time (positive communication, ch-16-03). The plant control room knows of the task.
  2. Area lock. The controller submits the pad area. The AMS stops new grants at once; the trucks at C01-Q are held.
  3. Clearing. The AMS waits until no truck is inside the area and no granted path leads into it (REQ-16-04-003). With the pipeline running that means waiting for the committed trucks to finish and leave — up to 193.2 s for the last tip in the model, plus the move-off. Clearing is never shortened by stopping a committed truck or by granting early.
  4. Grant. Only then does the AMS grant the permission. The requester acknowledges by read-back and enters.
  5. Release. When the requester has reported out and is confirmed outside, the controller releases the permission and the AMS may grant the tipping bay again — on a current, confirmed READY.

Three points need care at C01. NOT_READY is not an entry permission. A crusher that says it is not ready still has up to three committed trucks coming; a plant person who walks onto the pad on the strength of NOT_READY walks into the path of a reversing truck. The plant's isolation is a separate layer. Whether the crusher and its feeder are isolated before someone works in the pocket is the plant's own isolation procedure [TBV] (SRC-REG-AU-ISOLATION-LOCKOUT, ch-16-04 section 4.6); the access permission keeps trucks away, the isolation keeps the plant still, and neither replaces the other. The plant side has no access point of its own in the reference mine: AP-01 to AP-04 are on mine roads. Plant personnel enter the pad from the plant without passing a mine access point. The handbook therefore treats the plant-side entry as an access point without an ID [A].

4.8 Delay coding at C01

The delay codes of delay-codes.yml and their coding rules separate what the plant did from what the mine did, provided the reason travels with the code (ch-02-06 section 8.4).

Situation at C01 Truck code Recorded with
Waiting at C01-Q behind other trucks, C01 READY DLY-NP-02 (wait at a working destination) —
Held at C01-Q while C01 is NOT_READY DLY-OD-10 (destination down) the plant's reason: DOWNSTREAM_FULL, CHUTE_BLOCKED, CRUSHER_TRIP, PLANNED_INSPECTION, UNSPECIFIED
Held at C01-Q because the C01 status is lost or invalid DLY-OD-10 (ch-02-06 section 8.4) "interface" in the exception record

In SCN-BINLEVEL-C01 the record alternates: each hold is DLY-OD-10 with DOWNSTREAM_FULL, and between holds the same trucks are DLY-NP-02. One interval, one code: the codes change with the effective state, not with a person's judgement. Summed per reason over the shift, DLY-OD-10 tells the plant how much truck time its downstream cost; DLY-NP-02 tells the mine how much queue its own fleet size and bunching cost. Holding trucks at a stopped crusher is operating delay in the GMG Time Usage Model [F] (SRC-PUB-GMG-TUM-2020); the codes themselves are the handbook's [H].

Should the 10 s gateway confirmation, during which the plant already says READY, be DLY-OD-10 or DLY-NP-02? The handbook codes by the effective state [A]: the AMS still treats C01 as not ready, so the interval is DLY-OD-10.

The two talk by telephone or radio with positive communication (ch-16-03). The phone carries what the ICD does not — estimates, reasons beyond the five codes, pad access requests, an agreed lower ore rate — and never the C01 state: "you can send them" while the interface says NOT_READY points to an interface fault, not to a release.

4.10 Trade-offs and model limits

  • Pipelining against commitment. A second holding point near the pad would reduce the committed loads but needs a mine change.
  • Model limits. The open queue ignores the circuit; the closed loop uses fms, which places the queue at the tipping position and ignores R21 as a separate stage; spot and dump times are independent gamma draws; holds are regular; pad layout, wheel stop and pocket are illustrative; no bin is modelled here (ch-15-04).

Onboard safety-related systems Payload, fire, tyre monitoring as elsewhere; a safe-state request stands

People Plant control room: runs C01, announces stops, requests pad access for plant personnel, isolates the plant; AHS Controller: watches C01-Q and the holds, phones the plant, grants pad access, proposes rebalancing; Pit Supervisor: accountable for the ore–waste balance and for approving rebalancing; Survey and Field Technician: pad work under a permission

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation

The plant sends READY every 5 s. The AMS releases a truck from C01-Q 13.2 s before the truck ahead starts Spotting, so that one truck tips every 90 s. Ore trucks wait at C01-Q on every cycle: 113.5 s per tip in the seeded closed loop, DLY-NP-02. The HMI shows two or three committed trucks at any time. D01 takes its waste trucks with almost no wait.

6.2 Abnormal operation — SCN-BINLEVEL-C01 (shared timeline for sections 7 and 8)

T+0 s is the plant's third NOT_READY within ten minutes, the trigger of the registry. Times from the registry are marked R; times from LAB-15-01-01 are marked L; other intermediate times are illustrative [A] and chosen for the walk-through. FIG-15-01-03 shows the same sequence.

The scenario has no single moment of failure; its cost is the accumulation of small holds at a position without slack (section 4.6). The decision that matters is at T+120 s: stop feeding the queue rather than wait for the plant.

6.3 Abnormal operation — SCN-CRUSHER-C01-DOWN

A chute blockage starts the same way — NOT_READY at once, bay withheld at T+5 s, committed trucks finish — but lasts longer: a 45 min stop holds all 19 ore trucks (ch-10-07) and raises the questions of ch-15-06 (hold, park, divert or reassign, spill-back towards X, staged restart).

6.4 Planned stop with notice — SCN-MISSION-CANCEL-M20481

When the plant announces STOPPING with a stop_at_s, the AMS releases from C01-Q only trucks that can finish R21, Spotting and the dump before stop_at_s: 193.2 s in this model. A truck further back — DT17 on R01 with M-20481 — cannot, and the FMS cancels its mission (registry T+0 s; the AMS acknowledges at T+1 s and issues a hold at T+40 s; ch-10-08). The notice is worth the most when it exceeds the commitment horizon: a stop_at_s less than 193.2 s ahead still leaves committed trucks tipping after the stop.

6.5 Degraded operation — C01 status lost

If the plant's messages stop (SCN-PLCLINK-C01, ch-15-03), the last state is lost after 15 s and the AMS treats C01 as NOT_READY: no new grant, trucks held at C01-Q, committed trucks finish. The controller phones the plant; if the crusher is running, the trucks stay held until valid READY messages return and are confirmed. The time is DLY-OD-10 with the interface as the cause in the exception record.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

Readiness acts at the grant. The AMS checks C01's state when it releases a truck from C01-Q, and nowhere else; every stop on R21 or in the bay is left to the truck's own sensing and interlocks.

Pipeline at the bay's pace, no further. The release lead is computed from R21 travel and service time; every extra second of lead adds committed load.

Count the whole commitment in a STOPPING check — R21 travel, Spotting and dump, not the dump alone (the default tip_end_s of ahs_sim.plant.TippingSequence, written for a truck already at the pad).

One-way confirmation, separate exclusion from isolation. A flicker never releases a truck and a hold is never delayed; the AMS locks the pad for trucks, the plant isolates its machinery, and the HMI must not suggest that one implies the other.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

Walk the shared timeline of section 6.2:

  • Before T+0 s. Plant → AMS: READY every 5 s. AMS: truck B released 13.2 s before truck A started Spotting.
  • T+0 s. Plant → AMS, FMS: NOT_READY, DOWNSTREAM_FULL. Gateway: more restrictive, effective at once; bin_level_pct, if sent, is shown and never used. AMS: no release from C01-Q.
  • T+5 s. AMS → trucks at C01-Q: hold; AMS → FMS: trucks held, DLY-OD-10 with the reason. Truck A and truck B: no change — committed.
  • T+30 s. AMS → HMI: alarm with the reason, the trucks held, the committed trucks and the count of holds in the last 10 min [A].
  • T+40 s → T+50 s. Plant: READY at T+40 s, T+45 s, T+50 s. Gateway: effective READY at T+50 s. AMS: releases the first truck at C01-Q; with READY no time check is needed.
  • T+95 s. Plant: NOT_READY. Gateway: at once. AMS: no release. The truck released at T+50 s is on R21; it is committed and reaches its Cusp point at T+153.2 s.
  • T+120 s and T+300 s. People and FMS: the reduced ore rate and the rebalancing proposal; FMS → AMS: new assignments for the trucks it reassigns at their next dispatch point.

Two timing figures bound the interface. The gateway can hold a stale READY for at most 15 s before it counts as lost; and the AMS has always committed up to 193.2 s of tipping. A plant that wants a stop to be effective at a given moment must announce it at least that long before.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

The C01 panel shows the effective state with its age, the plant's last message (marked awaiting confirmation during the 10 s), the reason and bin_level_pct if sent, the number of NOT_READY periods in the last 10 min [A], the trucks at C01-Q with the queue time of the first, and the committed trucks on R21 and in the tipping position with their expected tip times. A pad access permission appears on the same panel, with its state and the trucks it holds. The D01 panel shows its tip points and queue (ch-15-02). The FMS panel shows the ore rate per hour against C01's capacity and any rebalancing proposal.

Reading it: a queue with C01 READY and no holds is the normal price of the bottleneck; a queue growing with repeated holds is a plant constraint, signalled by the hold counter; a queue with status lost is an interface problem.

8.3 Procedure excerpts

A. Repeated short holds at C01 — keyed to the shared timeline of section 6.2.

  1. AHS Controller (T+30 s): acknowledge the alarm. Check that the AMS holds the trucks at C01-Q and that the committed trucks are finishing normally. Do not try to release a truck.
  2. AHS Controller: read the hold counter. One hold: watch. Third hold within 10 min [A] (the scenario trigger): plan a call.
  3. AHS Controller (T+120 s): phone the plant control room. Ask: the reason behind DOWNSTREAM_FULL, how long the plant expects the constraint to last, and the ore rate the plant can accept. Read back the agreed rate. Record name, time and figures in the exception record.
  4. AHS Controller: inform the Pit Supervisor of the agreed rate and ask for a rebalancing decision.
  5. AHS Controller (T+300 s): review the FMS rebalancing proposal: which trucks move from ore to waste, at which dispatch point, and the expected ore and waste tonnes. Approve with the Pit Supervisor's agreement.
  6. AHS Controller: inform the Shovel Operators at EX01, EX02 and the waste units.
  7. AHS Controller: when the plant reports the constraint cleared, agree the return to the full ore rate by phone and approve the FMS proposal that restores the assignments. Check the delay codes and close the exception.

B. Plant personnel on the crusher pad.

  1. Plant person: at the plant-side entry, stop; call the AHS Controller by radio: name, task, expected time.
  2. AHS Controller: confirm with the plant control room that the task is known and whether the plant is isolating the crusher; record it.
  3. AHS Controller: submit the crusher-pad permission (area: pad, tipping position, R21 beyond C01-Q). Watch the AMS hold C01-Q and wait for the committed trucks to finish and leave.
  4. AHS Controller: when the AMS shows the area clear and grants the permission, tell the plant person "access granted to the crusher pad"; they read back. Only now may they enter.
  5. Plant person: report out by radio when clear of the pad. AHS Controller: confirm by position report or radio read-back, release the permission; tipping resumes on the next confirmed READY.

A light vehicle or a crane follows the same steps through a mine access point, with its position reporting unit.

Report DLY-OD-10 by reason and DLY-NP-02 separately; the first goes to the plant's review, the second to the mine's. A shift with many short DOWNSTREAM_FULL holds and a normal-looking DLY-NP-02 is a plant capacity problem; a shift with no holds and a long DLY-NP-02 is a fleet sizing or bunching problem (ch-10-02).

8.5 Training and competency implications

Controllers learn to read C01's panel in three layers — state, holds, committed trucks — that a truck released before a NOT_READY will tip, and that NOT_READY never lets anyone onto the pad. Plant control room operators learn the committed-load figure and that STOPPING with a time stops the fleet precisely. Pit Supervisors learn that at a 0.977 bottleneck the answer to a slow plant is fewer ore trucks, not more patience.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-15-01-001 [H] The AMS shall release a truck from C01-Q onto R21 only while the gateway's effective C01 state is READY, or STOPPING with stop_at_s no earlier than the release time plus the truck's expected R21 travel, Spotting and dump time; the tipping position will be free when the truck reaches its Cusp point; and no access permission or work zone in a locked state covers R21, the crusher pad or the tipping position. Rationale: readiness acts only at the grant (ch-10-07 section 4.3); a STOPPING check without R21 travel and Spotting admits trucks that tip after the stop (section 4.5). Verification: test (stale and unconfirmed READY refused; STOPPING with 193.2 s and 192 s remaining [A]); inspection.

REQ-15-01-002 [H] The AMS shall release trucks from C01-Q one at a time with a release lead computed from the R21 travel time and the bay service time, no longer than needed to keep the tipping position fed, and shall never release a group of trucks at once. Rationale: without the pipeline C01 tips 18.6 instead of 40 trucks per hour; a longer lead adds committed loads; group releases cause bunching (sections 4.4, 4.5). Verification: analysis (LAB-15-01-01: lead 13.2 s, 2–3 committed loads); test.

REQ-15-01-003 [H] The C01 interface agreement shall state the maximum number of loads and the time over which committed trucks may still tip after the plant sends NOT_READY, and the plant shall size its NOT_READY triggers to accept them. Rationale: up to three loads within 193.2 s in the reference mine (section 4.5). Verification: inspection of the interface agreement; analysis.

REQ-15-01-004 [H] The AMS shall allow persons and manned vehicles onto the crusher pad only under a digital access permission whose area includes the pad, the tipping position and R21 beyond C01-Q, granted only after no truck is inside the area and no granted path leads into it. A C01 NOT_READY shall not be presented as, or used as, a condition for entry. Rationale: committed trucks still reach the tipping position after NOT_READY (sections 4.5, 4.7; REQ-16-04-003). Verification: test (permission not granted while a committed truck is on R21); inspection of the HMI and procedure.

REQ-15-01-005 [H] The FMS shall record truck time held at C01-Q during NOT_READY as DLY-OD-10 with the plant's reason, time with a lost or invalid status as DLY-OD-10 marked as an interface cause, and queue time at a ready C01 as DLY-NP-02, so that shift reports can sum plant-caused and fleet-caused delay separately. Rationale: plant and mine delays look alike at C01-Q (sections 2, 4.8). Verification: test (SCN-BINLEVEL-C01 replay: alternating codes with reason); inspection of the report.

REQ-15-01-006 [H] The AMS HMI shall show for C01 the effective state and its age, the plant's last message with any pending confirmation, the reason, the number of NOT_READY periods in a configurable window, the trucks held at C01-Q with the queue time of the first, and the committed trucks with their expected tip times. Rationale: the hold counter and the committed trucks are what the controller acts on (section 8.1). Verification: inspection; demonstration with the SCN-BINLEVEL-C01 replay.

REQ-15-01-007 [H] When the number of C01 NOT_READY periods in a configurable window exceeds a configurable limit, the FMS shall offer a rebalancing proposal that reduces the ore trucks to the rate agreed with the plant and shows the expected ore and waste tonnes; the proposal shall take effect only on the AHS Controller's approval. Rationale: at a utilisation of 0.977 short holds cost ore that cannot be recovered by waiting (section 4.6). Verification: test (SCN-BINLEVEL-C01: proposal at the third hold in 10 min [A]); demonstration.

REQ-15-01-008 [R] The site should agree with the plant that planned stops of C01 are announced with STOPPING and a stop_at_s at least the commitment horizon ahead (193.2 s in the reference mine [A]). Rationale: a shorter notice leaves committed trucks tipping after the stop (section 6.4). Verification: inspection of the plant–mine interface agreement.

10. Verification and validation

Verification (make test CH=ch-15-01, 9 tests): every number of sections 4.2 to 4.6 — capacities, utilisations, closed forms, open-queue and closed-loop waits, the bunching table, the R21 pipeline and hold cost, the SCN-BINLEVEL-C01 gateway times and the recurring-hold table — with the qualitative checks that the closed loop grows less than the open queue and that bunching hurts C01 more than D01.

Validation needs data the fictional mine does not have: the real R21 travel time and pad geometry, the real variability of Spotting and tipping at C01, the plant's hold pattern and its triggers, the number of trucks that physically fit at C01-Q. The model shows mechanisms and orders of magnitude; it does not predict a site's waits or tonnes. Field checks for a site: compare the HMI's committed-truck count with the plant's record of loads received after each NOT_READY; compare DLY-NP-02 per tip with the closed-loop estimate for the site's own fleet.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Using the open-queue formula as a forecast.
  • A STOPPING check that counts only the dump.
  • Releasing only into an empty bay. It halves C01's capacity at the reference mine.
  • A release lead longer than needed.
  • Treating bin_level_pct as a permission. It is information only (ch-02-06).
  • No access point for the plant side of the pad.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Reading the queue length instead of the hold counter.
  • Waiting out a slow plant. At 0.977 the lost ore does not come back.
  • Letting someone onto the pad because C01 says not ready.
  • Asking the plant to "just say ready". Only a valid message makes C01 ready; a phone call never does.
  • Coding every C01 wait as DLY-NP-02 — or every one as DLY-OD-10.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — utilisation and the release lead. C01: 39.08 × 90 ÷ 3600 = 0.977; D01: 33.33 × 85 ÷ 3600 ÷ 3 = 0.262. R21 103.2 s, service 90 s: release 13.2 s before the truck ahead starts Spotting. Without pipelining: 3600 / 193.2 = 18.6 trucks per hour. Committed tipping after a NOT_READY: up to 103.2 + 90 = 193.2 s, two or three loads.

Example 2 — a STOPPING decision. The plant sends STOPPING with stop_at_s 150 s ahead. The first truck at C01-Q needs 193.2 s to finish: it is not released, and the FMS holds or reassigns it. A truck already committed 60 s ago finishes at about 193.2 − 60 = 133.2 s: before the stop.

Example 3 — what short holds cost. 40 s holds every 95 s: 38.67 → 37.45 tips per hour in the seeded closed loop, 1.22 tips per hour; 8508 − 8238 = 270 t of ore per hour; over a 12 h shift about 3240 t. A reduced ore rate agreed early moves the spare truck time to waste instead of to C01-Q.

15. Key takeaways

  • C01 runs at a utilisation of 0.977, D01 at 0.262 per tip point: one position near capacity, three with slack.
  • The open-queue formula (1957.5 s) is not a forecast; the closed loop waits 113.5 s per tip and pays in lost throughput.
  • At C01 every second of lost tipping is lost ore; at D01 it is absorbed.
  • Bunching costs C01 three to four times what it costs D01 at the same utilisation.
  • Trucks leave C01-Q 13.2 s before the truck ahead starts Spotting; two or three loads are always committed and finish.
  • NOT_READY stops releases, not committed trucks, and is never a pad-entry permission.
  • Code holds as DLY-OD-10 with the plant's reason and the queue as DLY-NP-02; the hold counter, not the queue length, is the signal to agree a lower ore rate.
