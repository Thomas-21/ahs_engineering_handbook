1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

14:20, waste dump D01. The Pit Supervisor drives LV07 along the edge on the afternoon pit-patrol round. At tip point 2 the edge windrow has slumped after the morning's tipping: lower than the site standard over a few metres, with a crack behind it. They call the AHS Controller: "LV07, D01, tip point 2, edge windrow low over about five metres, immediate, request tip point 2 closed." The controller reads it back and closes tip point 2 in the AMS. DT24, already on R04 with a load of waste for that point, is allocated tip point 3 instead. The D01 panel on the HMI now reads: two tip points active, 14 loads left, about 25 minutes.

DZ03 can rebuild the windrow, but that takes a work zone, a survey pick-up and a new map version — more than an hour. "What do we tip on in 25 minutes?" asks the Pit Supervisor. The controller wonders whether a truck on R22, where coverage is poor, could still be sent to the closed point. The questions of this chapter:

  • How are D01's tip points, its edge and its edge protection represented in the AHS map, and who may change them?
  • What is the life cycle of a tip point, and what keeps a closed, full or out-of-date point from being allocated?
  • How should the AMS allocate trucks among three tip points, and what does a dozer work zone at the dump take away?
  • How many tip points does a shift consume, and what dozer and Survey cadence keeps new points ready in time?
  • What fails at a dump — the edge, the map, the radio link on R22 — and how does each failure fail closed?
  • Why are windrow heights, edge setbacks and dump geometry not AHS parameters?

This chapter covers:

  • D01 in the AHS map, the tip-point life cycle (FIG-15-02-02) and its fail-closed allocation, with LAB-15-02-01 parts E and F;
  • allocation, consumption and the cadence of dozer and Survey work at D01, paddock against edge dumping, and the reserve of tip points prepared ahead (FIG-15-02-03), with LAB-15-02-01 parts A to D;
  • SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE walked through from both sides (FIG-15-02-01), with SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02, SCN-MAP-R05 and SCN-EDGE-COLLAPSE-R05 as patterns.

The final approach to a tip point — sensing the windrow, the Cusp point and the reversing manoeuvre — belongs to ch-15-08 and is only referred to here. The queueing of a single tipping position is ch-15-01's. Every capacity, time, rate and layout distance is an illustrative assumption [A] computed with ahs_sim 0.18.1; the life cycle, roles and procedures are handbook conventions [H] and describe no vendor's dump-management function.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Specify how D01 tip points, the dump edge and edge protection are represented in the AHS map and how a tip point is retired after use.
  2. Design the tip-point allocation logic for three active tip points at D01 (paddock versus edge dumping) and its interaction with dozer work zones.
  3. Analyse the failure modes at the dump (edge degradation, map out of date, poor coverage on R22) and specify the fail-closed reactions.
  4. Calculate the tip-point consumption rate at D01 per shift from waste tonnage and an [A] tip-point capacity, and derive the required dozer and survey cadence.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Apply the procedure to rebuild an edge windrow and relocate tip points at D01 with DZ03, Survey and the AHS Controller.
  2. Inspect and report dump-edge condition from a pit-patrol round and decide when a tip point must be closed.
  3. Explain why edge-protection dimensions and dump geometry are set by the site's own geotechnical and regulatory process, not by the AHS.
  4. Plan survey pick-ups and map updates at D01 with Survey so that new tip points are ready before the active ones are full.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

D01 is the only waste destination of the reference mine (mine.yaml). EX03 and EX04 load waste; their trucks (DT20 to DT28 and DT30 to DT38 in the baseline allocation, equipment.yaml) haul over R03 and R04 to the Queue point D01-Q and then over R22, 403.1 m at 15 km/h, into the tip area. The FMS baseline sends 33.33 trucks per hour there in the DAY shift (plant.fleet_arrival_rates_per_h), 7,333.3 t of waste per hour at 220 t a load. D01 has a single access: ch-14-03 showed that closing R03 costs every waste tonne. The tip area is a single point of failure in the same way, only less visible: when no tip point can be used, the waste circuit stops just as surely as if the road were closed.

A crusher's tipping position is a fixed structure (ch-15-01); a dump is built by the trucks that use it. After a number of loads [A] a tip point is full and must be replaced. For paddock dumping a dozer pushes the piles out and a new paddock point is designated inside the dump boundary. For edge dumping the dozer must rebuild the edge windrow further out, Survey must pick up the new edge line, and the AHS map must change before a truck may reverse towards it. At the model's defaults a DAY shift consumes about 40 tip points (section 4.5): the dump lives on a production line of its own — dozer, Survey and map change control.

Three things go wrong at the reference mine's dump:

  • The edge degrades. Windrows slump, cracks open behind the crest, rain softens the tip head. A truck's own sensing on the final approach (ch-15-08) is the last line, not the first: the windrow and the edge must be right before a point is allocated.
  • The map falls behind the ground. The edge moves every time the dozer works. If the AMS allocates a point whose edge line is no longer where the map says, only the truck's perception stands between it and the edge (ch-14-03 REQ-14-03-009, ch-06-05).
  • The radio link is poor where precision matters most. R22 is the only poor segment in mine.yaml. ch-11-01 found that on poor road a request needing a confirmation succeeds at the first attempt with probability 0.638, and ch-11-03 measured a mean round trip of 641.0 ms on R22 (99.9 percentile: 1,470.6 ms).

SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE combines the first two with the cadence problem: one tip point is closed, the rebuild takes 80 minutes on the registry clock, and LAB-15-02-01 shows that at the model's capacity the remaining points do not last that long unless the site prepared points ahead.

3. Terminology and foundations

  • Tip point — a position at a dump where one truck at a time tips: a pose (rear-axle centre and heading, front away from the edge or pile), a kind (paddock or edge), a capacity in loads [A], a life-cycle state and the map version it belongs to. D01 tip points carry plain labels ("tip point 1", "tip point 2", …), not registered IDs.
  • Tip area — the part of D01 inside the published dump boundary where tip points may lie; in the base map it is the dump node D01 and the zone Z-D01 (ch-12-08).
  • Paddock dumping — tipping on the dump surface in a grid of piles that a dozer later pushes out; the truck does not reverse to an edge.
  • Edge dumping — tipping over the dump edge, reversing towards the edge windrow; the windrow is the reference the truck senses (ch-15-08).
  • Dump edge (crest) — the line where the dump surface meets the dump face. The edge line in the map is its surveyed position.
  • Edge windrow — the windrow (bund) along the dump edge. It marks the edge, is the reference the truck senses on its final approach (ch-15-08) and forms part of the site's edge protection; its dimensions are set by the site's process (section 4.2). The truck stops at the configured windrow stand-off by its own braking (REQ-15-08-003); the handbook does not assume that a windrow can stop a reversing truck, and the model does not represent that capacity.
  • Dump boundary — the published polygon of the tip area in the map version in force.
  • Tip-point capacity — the number of loads a point takes before it is full [A]; 10 loads by default in ahs_sim.
  • Prepared reserve — tip points ready ahead of need: dozed, surveyed where needed, ACTIVE in the version in force, not yet in use.
  • Carry-over — the explicit statement in a new map version that an unchanged tip point keeps its state and loads.
  • Survey cadence — how often Survey picks up new edge sections, and so how many map versions a shift creates.

The states of the life cycle are SURVEYED, ACTIVE, FULL, CLOSED and RETIRED (section 4.3). mine.yaml gives D01 three tipping positions, a spot time of 45 s and a dump time of 40 s [A]. The spot time is read [A] as Cusp point → spotted (reverse and position), the same reading as for the shovel's spot time (truck_spot_time_s in equipment.yaml). The final approach and its manoeuvres are ch-15-08's; this chapter uses only the service time of 85 s.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 D01 in the AHS map

The base map of the reference mine (AHS-MAP-0001, ch-12-08) represents D01 by a dump node and a zone, with no tip points inside. ch-12-08 named "no tipping bays within D01's paddock" as one of the simplifications of its model. A map that lets an AHS dump needs more, and each item has its own owner and change route [H]:

Map item at D01 Content Owner Change route
Queue point D01-Q and link R22 node, segment, speed, coverage class Survey (map owner) map version (ch-12-09)
Dump boundary polygon of the tip area Survey map version
Edge line surveyed crest position Survey map version
Edge windrow presence and condition along the edge line; site standard by reference Survey (geometry), Pit Supervisor (condition) geometry: map version; condition: field report → closure
Edge tip point pose next to the windrow, capacity, state Survey map version
Paddock tip point pose inside the boundary, capacity, state Survey or dozer operator, via the AMS designation on the version in force
Work zone, closure overlay of segments or area AHS Controller, Pit Supervisor overlay (ch-12-08, ch-14-03)

The table separates two kinds of change. A change to the boundary or the edge line changes geometry that the ADS uses as its map prior, so it goes through the workflow of ch-12-09 that SCN-MAP-R05 walks through: request, survey, change set, validation, approval, distribution, activation, per-truck confirmation. A new paddock point inside an unchanged boundary is a designation, like a loading spot (ch-13-08): the AMS checks that it lies inside the published boundary and activates it on the version in force. ahs_sim.plant encodes this: a SURVEYED point becomes ACTIVE on a map version not older than its own, while a CLOSED point needs a newer version to reopen. simulate_dump makes the same assumption for paddock points (no new survey inside the published boundary) [A].

Two map rules from earlier volumes apply at D01 unchanged. The grant rule of ch-12-09: a truck that has not confirmed the version in force gets no reservation on a changed part of the map — after the change of SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE, on the new edge section. And the switch rule: a truck switches to a new version only when its granted path contains nothing the change touched.

4.2 Edge protection is set by the site, not by the AHS

The windrow height at the edge, the setback of tip points from the crest, the spacing between points, the dump's height, slope and lift sequence and the conditions under which edge dumping is allowed at all are decisions of the site's geotechnical and regulatory process. They depend on the material, the dump's foundation, the weather, the trucks' tyre size and the jurisdiction's rules. Australian mining work-health-and-safety legislation and codes of practice deal with dumps and edge protection as a topic; which instrument applies at a given site, and what exactly it requires, is not verified for this handbook [TBV] (SRC-REG-AU-DUMP-EDGE-PROTECTION). The Western Australian Work Health and Safety (Mines) Regulations 2022 are verified as an instrument (title and status, SRC-REG-WA-WHS-MINES-REGS-2022) [F]; their provisions are not. Published haul-road design guidance that relates windrow height to tyre size is likewise not verified (SRC-PUB-HAUL-ROAD-DESIGN-GUIDES) [TBV].

For the AHS this has three consequences [H]:

  1. The AHS consumes edge-protection values; it does not own them. The site's standard enters the configuration as named values with a reference to the site document that sets them, and the AMS refuses to publish an edge tip point without them (REQ-15-02-004). There is no vendor or handbook default.
  2. A tip point is only as good as the ground under it. The AMS checks what the map says; the patrol and Survey check the ground; the truck checks the windrow at the last metres. None replaces the others.
  3. Model values are only inputs. plant.d01_layout places the edge line 120 m beyond the D01 node, edge points 15 m inside it and 20 m apart, and a paddock grid of three rows of four from 40 m [A]. These are drawing and simulation inputs, not a setback, spacing or dump geometry of any site.

4.3 The tip-point life cycle

FIG-15-02-02 shows the life cycle of ahs_sim.plant.TipPoint (TIP_TRANSITIONS) [H].

From Event To Guards
SURVEYED map published ACTIVE the version is not older than the point's
ACTIVE capacity reached FULL loads at capacity
ACTIVE close CLOSED Pit Supervisor or AHS Controller
CLOSED reopen ACTIVE rebuilt, surveyed and a newer map version
FULL retire RETIRED Pit Supervisor or AHS Controller
CLOSED retire RETIRED Pit Supervisor or AHS Controller

The life cycle is fail closed at every step [H]:

  • Allocation only to ACTIVE points on the version in force. plant.allocate considers no other point. In LAB-15-02-01 part E, with tip point 2 closed, every policy and every starting point gives tip point 1 or tip point 3, never tip point 2.
  • Closing is immediate and narrow. Two roles may close a point; the Shovel Operator, for example, may not (guard failed: authorised_role). Closing changes nothing else: other points stay ACTIVE.
  • Reopening is hard. A rebuilt point is not reopened without a survey (guard failed: surveyed) or on the same map version (guard failed: map_version_newer). No timer reopens a point.
  • A full point takes no further load. record_load on a FULL point is refused (tip point 1 is FULL).
  • A point cannot be published on an older version (guard failed: map_version_newer_or_equal).

Retiring is the normal end: FULL → RETIRED when the point has been replaced, CLOSED → RETIRED when it will not be rebuilt in place. In SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE tip point 2 is retired and tip point 4 is a new point on the rebuilt section, not tip point 2 reopened: the edge line changed, so the old pose and its windrow reference no longer exist.

A gap the lab found: carry-over. After version 6 is published, allocate with map_version=6 refuses every point published in version 5, even one the change did not touch: part E gives None for tip points 1 and 3. That is the right fail-closed default, but every new version must then state, point by point, what is carried over. ahs_sim 0.18.1 adds plant.carry_over: ACTIVE → ACTIVE on a newer version, guarded by "unchanged in the new map"; a point listed as changed stays behind (part E: tip point 1 carried, tip point 3 refused) (REQ-15-02-005, section 6.3).

4.4 Allocation among three tip points

mine.yaml gives D01 three tipping positions [A]: at most three trucks tip at the same time. plant.allocate offers three policies [A]:

  • round_robin — the next candidate after the last one used; all points fill together and replacements bunch.
  • nearest — the candidate nearest to the truck; short travel, but blind to the dozer plan.
  • fill_first — the candidate with the most loads; one point fills at a time, replacements fall due one by one, roughly every interval τ of section 4.5, which suits a single dozer.

simulate_dump uses fill-first among the free points, and so does the scenario replay. At D01's load of 33.33 trucks per hour and 85 s of service, utilisation over three positions is only 0.262: most of the time one truck tips while the others are idle. That is why fill-first works — it rarely has to send a truck to a second point — and why D01, with three positions, "never binds" in the closed-loop study of ch-10-02.

What a work zone takes away. A dozer working next to a tip point needs a work zone (section 4.7). If the zone also covers a neighbouring point, that point is lost for the duration. LAB-15-02-01 part D gives the wait at D01-Q for 3, 2 and 1 usable points at the D01 rate, with the open-queue closed form (Allen–Cunneen, plant.mgc_wait_s) and the seeded simulation (plant.simulate_tipping, seeds 0–9):

Usable tip points Utilisation Mean wait, closed form (s) Mean wait, simulation (s) p90 wait, simulation (s) Largest queue
3 0.262 1.0 1.3 0.0 4
2 0.394 7.9 8.9 39.0 6
1 0.787 160.2 151.4 383.9 11

With one point closed for a rebuild, D01 still runs almost without waiting. With a second point inside the work zone, D01 becomes a single-position station at a utilisation of 0.787 — the regime of C01 in ch-15-01 — and the mean wait rises twentyfold. The design rule is simple: draw the work zone so that it takes the point being rebuilt and nothing else in use (REQ-15-02-008). The closed form is an open-queue approximation; at these utilisations it agrees with the simulation within 10 s, which it does not do for C01 near saturation (ch-15-01).

4.5 Consumption and cadence

The number of tip points a shift consumes follows from tonnage, payload and capacity:

N=WmC

with W the waste tonnes of the shift, m the payload and C the capacity in loads. LAB-15-02-01 part A at the model's defaults: W = 7,333.3 t/h × 12 h = 88,000 t, m = 220 t, C = 10 loads [A], so 400 loads and N = 40.0 tip points per DAY shift — 3.33 per hour, one every

τ=TshiftN=1,080 s=18.0 min.

Each consumed point creates work [A]:

Work per consumed point Model value Per DAY shift (40 points)
Dozer, edge: rebuild the windrow segment 600 s 6.67 h
Dozer, paddock: push 10 piles at 60 s 600 s 6.67 h
Survey, edge: pick up the new edge, publish a version 900 s 10.00 h
Map versions, edge, one per point 1 40

Capacity drives everything: at 5 loads per point the dump consumes 80 points a shift (one every 9.0 min), at 20 loads 20 (36.0 min), at 40 loads 10 (72.0 min). It is a site matter; the model's 10 is an assumption.

The last row of the table is the uncomfortable one. Publishing every new edge point as its own map version would mean 40 activations a shift, each with its validation, approval and per-truck confirmation (ch-12-09). Running change control at that rate is impractical, so this chapter assumes that edge points are prepared and published in batches. Section 4.6 shows what batching costs.

What simulate_dump shows. The module's discrete-event model replaces a full point only when it is full: one dozer rebuilds (edge) or pushes (paddock), first come first served, and for edge dumping Survey then picks up the segment and a version is published. There is no prepared point. Part B of the lab:

Policy Run Mean wait (s) p90 wait (s) Points consumed Dozer (h) Time with fewer than 3 active (h)
paddock regular arrivals 0.0 0.0 40 6.51 6.51
edge regular arrivals 0.0 0.0 40 6.51 11.71
paddock seeds 0–9 (mean) 6.9 19.7 38.5 6.36 6.36
edge seeds 0–9 (mean) 73.1 230.0 38.5 6.38 11.03

Dozer hours are equal at these inputs; the Survey step makes the difference. With edge dumping and no prepared point D01 has fewer than three active points for 11.71 of 12 hours: as soon as one point is replaced the next fills. With regular arrivals trucks hardly notice; with random arrivals the mean wait is ten times that of paddock dumping (p90 230.0 s). The large time below three is the model's main result, not an artefact to tune away: it has no spare points.

4.6 Preparing tip points ahead

A site does not wait for a point to fill before preparing the next one. It keeps a reserve of prepared points — the dozer has pushed or rebuilt, Survey has picked up where needed, the map version is in force — and the AMS draws on it as points fill. The question is how many.

Inventory theory gives the first answer: the reserve must cover the lead time L of a replacement at the consumption rate,

R=⌈Lτ⌉.

For a paddock point L = 600 s (the push), so R = 1; for an edge point L = 600 + 900 = 1,500 s (rebuild and survey), so R = 2. This is the deterministic minimum. LAB-15-02-01 part C extends simulate_dump lab-locally: k points are prepared at the start of the shift, a full point is replaced at once from the reserve, and the replacement work refills it. With k = 0 and Survey per point the extension reproduces simulate_dump exactly for every seed tested (a test checks it). Means over seeds 0–9:

Policy, Survey Reserve 0 Reserve 1 Reserve 2 Reserve 3 Map versions per shift
paddock, — 6.36 h 0.21 h 0.00 h 0.00 h 0
edge, per point 11.03 h 4.49 h 0.22 h 0.00 h 37.9
edge, every 1 h 9.26 h 8.72 h 6.83 h 3.72 h 11.0
edge, every 2 h 10.36 h 10.08 h 9.67 h 7.53 h 5.0

(time with fewer than three active tip points per DAY shift). With random arrivals one point more than the deterministic minimum is needed: two for paddock dumping, three for edge dumping with Survey per point. Batching the survey lengthens the lead time by up to one cadence interval: with Survey every hour L is up to 600 + 3,600 + 900 = 5,100 s and R = ⌈5,100/1,080⌉ = 5. The sweep agrees: with five prepared points D01 is never without a ready point (0.00 h) and is below three for 0.16 h; with four it is without one for 0.02 h. With Survey every two hours even six prepared points leave D01 without a ready point for 0.38 h a shift, and with none it cannot accept trucks for 9.90 h — only 180 of 400 loads are tipped. FIG-15-02-03 plots both measures.

The trade-off is between change control and ground: per-point publishing needs few prepared points but 38 versions a shift; hourly batches need five prepared edge points and 11 versions. Paddock dumping inside a published boundary avoids the problem, since a pushed paddock point needs no new version. Paddock or edge is a geotechnical and production decision; the AHS makes its cost visible (REQ-15-02-007).

4.7 Dozer work zones at D01

Dozer work at the dump follows the pattern of SCN-DOZER-DZ03-EX02 (ch-14-04): a work zone is requested, accepted when no truck holds a path inside it, ACTIVE while the dozer works, and released only after a handback and an inspection (REQ-14-03-002, -004). LAB-15-02-01 part E runs the mixed_fleet.WorkZone life cycle for the zone around tip point 2: past its planned end the zone is overdue but stays locked; it cannot be released without a handback; an inspection that finds a defect returns it to ACTIVE.

Two points need care at a dump [H]:

  • Zone and points. The zone must contain the point being rebuilt and the section that will become the new edge — in the scenario tip point 2 and the position of tip point 4, 2.0 m further in [A] — and no point still in use. In the lab layout the zone contains exactly tip points 2 and 4; tip point 1 in the paddock and tip point 3, the next edge point, stay outside (part E).
  • Zone release is not reopening. Rebuilding a windrow changes edge geometry. ch-14-03 REQ-14-03-009 keeps a zone that changes road geometry locked until the change is surveyed and a validated version is active, and ch-14-04 REQ-14-04-007 runs edge work under a closure. At D01 the same intent is met by two separate objects: the work zone protects DZ03 and is released after handback and inspection; the rebuilt edge section stays closed to trucks — tip point 2 CLOSED, no ACTIVE point inside it, an exclusion overlay on the section — until the new version is active. The registry's T+2400 s ("the Pit Supervisor releases the work zone") is read this way [A].

There is one dozer in the model, as in the scenario. While DZ03 is in the zone it pushes no paddock pile. That dependency — the same machine rebuilds the edge and keeps the paddock going — is what turns a closed tip point into a stopped dump in section 6.2.

The table restates Volume 15's architecture rule: Survey and the dozer operator, with the Pit Supervisor, own D01's edges and tip points; the AMS owns reservations, map versions in use and permissions; the ADS stops on its own sensing; no central permission overrides a vehicle-side stop; a lost, stale or contradictory state never lets a truck tip or move closer to an edge.

R22. A truck whose link drops stops within its granted path (ch-11-07). Two dump-specific rules follow [H]. The AMS grants from D01-Q only to the Cusp point of the allocated tip point until the truck reports itself there (the manoeuvre is ch-15-08's). And a closure issued while a truck's link is down cannot reach it, so the closure withdraws grants not yet in use and the AHS Controller reads it back against the truck list; a truck already granted the reverse into a now-closed point is aborted and drives forward out (TippingSequence; ch-15-08), and one already tipping finishes with no motion while the body is up (ch-08-03).

4.9 Model limits

LAB-15-02-01 ignores travel inside D01, interference between neighbouring points, dozer travel and Survey's time on foot; it has one dozer and one capacity; the queue at D01-Q is unbounded, whereas the FMS would stop sending trucks. None of this changes the order of magnitude: 40 points a shift, one every 18 minutes, and two to five prepared points depending on how the edge is surveyed.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation

Paddock and edge dumping run side by side, fill-first, with a reserve of prepared points ACTIVE in the version in force. A full point is retired on the Pit Supervisor's confirmation and replaced from the reserve; DZ03 pushes the paddock area or rebuilds the next edge segment; Survey picks up rebuilt sections at the agreed cadence, each batch one map version that carries over every unchanged point. Trucks queue at D01-Q only for the seconds of section 4.4 (DLY-NP-02).

6.2 Abnormal operation — SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE (shared timeline for sections 7 and 8)

The registry fixes six times: T+0, 10, 300, 2400, 3000 and 4800 s. LAB-15-02-01 part F replays the scenario on that clock with regular arrivals every 108 s (33.33 trucks per hour), 85 s per truck at a point and fill-first allocation, and adds the rows marked [A]. Its assumptions [A]: tip point 1 is a paddock point, tip points 2 and 3 neighbouring edge points (as in ch-15-08); at T+0 they hold 2, 8 and 4 loads of 10; DT24 reaches D01-Q at T+40 s; there is no prepared point (the registry's "trucks tip at tip points 1 and 3"); DZ03 is in the zone from T+300 to T+2400 s and only then pushes full paddock areas, 600 s each; a full edge point needs its own rebuild, survey and version and is not replaced within the scenario; the version in force is 7 and the new one 8, as in ch-15-08 (on site they carry ch-12-09 labels).

The forecast at T+10 s (14 loads at 108 s, empty at about T+1552 s) was right: D01 had no ACTIVE point from T+1529 to T+3000 s, 1,471 s. It did not recover after that. One paddock point at a time lasts 1,080 s, and its push takes 600 s with nothing else ready — the paddock reorder point of section 4.6 (R = 1) missing. Over the two hours replayed (arrivals to T+7200 s) D01 had no ACTIVE point for 2,771 s (further spans T+3850 to T+4450, T+5650 to T+5900 and T+6750 s to the end); 53 of 67 trucks were held, 1,161.7 truck-minutes, the longest 1,848 s, at most 17 at once, and the backlog cleared only at T+8970 s. 2,771 s at one truck per 108 s is 25.7 loads, about 5,645 t. A real fleet would not queue 17 trucks at D01-Q — the FMS would stop loading waste — but the waste is lost either way.

6.3 What prepared points would have changed

The same replay with prepared paddock points, ACTIVE in version 7 before T+0 (lab labels from tip point 5 on, [A]):

Two prepared points — 20 loads of space, dozed before the shift — would have turned the scenario into a non-event for the haulage. The reorder point of section 4.6 agrees: the registry's lead time from closure to the new version is 4,790 s, which at one point every 1,080 s would need ⌈4,790/1,080⌉ = 5 points if nothing else were replaced meanwhile; because DZ03 resumes pushing paddock areas at T+2400 s, two are enough.

The replay also shows the cost of forgetting carry-over. Without it, tip point 6, ACTIVE on version 7, drops out of allocation at T+4800 s; it never fills, so it is never pushed, and once tip point 4 is full at T+5650 s nothing is left to activate: 28 trucks of the run are never served. With two prepared points the same omission costs 2 held trucks.

6.4 Abnormal operation — an edge failure found by the trucks

As in SCN-EDGE-COLLAPSE-R05, a recurring finding at one place points to the ground, not to the trucks or the map. If two trucks abort their final approach at the same edge point (ch-15-08), the AHS Controller closes it and calls the Pit Supervisor before a third tries; if findings recur along a section, every point on it is closed. Reopening follows section 4.3, after the site's geotechnical function has assessed any sign of edge movement [TBV].

6.5 Degraded operation — poor coverage on R22

During a comms-site degradation (pattern of SCN-COMMS-R05) R22 may lose coverage altogether. Trucks inside the tip area stop within their grants (DLY-OD-05); trucks at D01-Q are held short of the dead span (DLY-OD-04, ch-11-07 REQ-11-07-003). A truck that was reversing towards an edge point stops on link loss like any other; it does not tip blind. When the link returns, the AMS reconciles before it resumes anyone (REQ-11-07-004) and resumes trucks in stages (REQ-11-07-006). A tip point closed during the outage is re-checked against every reconciled grant before the first truck moves.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

  • Edge points versioned, paddock points designated. Every point a map change would mean 40 versions a shift; edge points as designations would let the edge line drift away from the map.
  • Fill-first allocation staggers replacements for one dozer; round robin bunches them, nearest ignores the dozer plan.
  • A reserve rather than faster replacement decouples haulage from the lead time of dozer, Survey and change control, at the cost of prepared ground.
  • Explicit carry-over is safer than "unchanged unless stated" and cheap if the change set lists every point.
  • A crude forecast (loads left over arrival rate) predicted the empty dump at T+10 s within 23 s.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations along SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE

  • T+0 → T+10 s. A finding reported by radio becomes a closure in 10 s. The AMS must make the closure effective for allocation at once and must withdraw any grant to tip point 2 not yet in use; DT24 had only been allocated, so it is simply re-allocated.
  • T+10 s. The forecast is computed from loads left in ACTIVE points (14) and the arrival rate: about 25.2 minutes. The next prepared point — none — and the lead time of the rebuild (4,790 s on the registry clock) are shown next to it. The mismatch is visible 25 minutes before it bites.
  • T+300 s. The work-zone request carries its geometry on version 7 (REQ-14-03-001); acceptance checks that no ACTIVE point in use lies inside it.
  • T+1529 s. Allocation returns none. The AMS holds trucks at D01-Q with the reason no active tip point, and the FMS receives D01's state so that it can stop loading waste rather than queue trucks.
  • T+2400 s. Zone release (handback and inspection) and section closure are two records; releasing one does not lift the other.
  • T+3000 s. Two independent events coincide: Survey submits the change, and the first new paddock point becomes ACTIVE on version 7. Only the second resumes haulage.
  • T+4800 s. Activation of version 8 needs the activation gate of ch-12-09 (validation, approval, no reservation on a changed part, no locked permission on it) plus the carry-over list. The switch rule lets a truck reversing to a paddock point finish on version 7.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

The D01 panel [H]:

  • each tip point with label, kind, state, loads / capacity and map version; closed points with reason, reporter and time;
  • the count of ACTIVE points in use and in reserve, and the loads left;
  • the forecast: time until fewer than three, and until none, at the current arrival rate, next to the lead time of the next replacement;
  • work zones and closures at D01 with their state, owner and planned end; overdue zones flagged;
  • trucks at D01-Q and on R22 with their allocated point, link state and map version;
  • pending map changes for D01 and their gate status.

Alarms: no active tip point (priority), forecast below lead time, closure not acknowledged, truck without the version in force allocated to the tip area, communication lost on R22.

8.3 Procedure excerpts

A. Closing a tip point on a patrol finding [H]

  1. Patrol driver: report in the order location, type, severity, action requested (ch-14-06 REQ-14-06-007): "D01, tip point 2, edge windrow low over about five metres, immediate, request tip point 2 closed."
  2. AHS Controller: read back; close the point in the AMS with the reason; check that no truck holds a grant to it, and abort any grant not yet in use.
  3. AHS Controller: read the forecast aloud to the Pit Supervisor: loads left, time to none, next prepared point.
  4. Pit Supervisor: decide the rebuild (zone, dozer, Survey) and, if the forecast is shorter than the lead time, what keeps D01 open — a prepared point, a pushed paddock area first, or reduced waste haulage.
  5. Patrol driver: stay in the vehicle unless inside a locked area; leave only when the controller confirms the closure.

B. Rebuilding an edge windrow and relocating a tip point (SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE) [H]

  1. Pit Supervisor: request the work zone for DZ03 around the point and the rebuilt section, drawn on the version in force; confirm that it contains no point in use.
  2. AHS Controller: check the cost display (usable points, forecast); accept; wait for ACTIVE (no truck inside).
  3. DZ03 operator: enter on permission, rebuild the windrow to the site standard, report out with read-back.
  4. Pit Supervisor: handback inspection; release the zone. The section stays closed.
  5. Survey: pick up the new edge line and windrow; prepare the change set with the new point, the retired point and the carry-over list; validate.
  6. Pit Supervisor: approve. AHS Controller: activate when the gate passes; watch the rollout; confirm the new point ACTIVE and the old one RETIRED.

C. Shift-start dump plan [H]

  1. Pit Supervisor and Survey: agree the mix of paddock and edge dumping and the Survey cadence.
  2. Compute the reserve: lead time divided by the interval between full points, rounded up, plus one for random arrivals [R]; check it against the prepared points on the HMI.
  3. Plan DZ03's pushes and rebuilds so that the reserve is refilled before it is used; plan the work zones so that each takes one point.
  4. Brief the AHS Controller on the forecast thresholds and the throttling plan if D01 runs out.

The coding rules say that map and survey work that hold trucks are DLY-OD-04. When D01 has no ACTIVE point, the replacements are still with the dozer or Survey: the hold is caused by that work, so trucks held at D01-Q for lack of an ACTIVE tip point are DLY-OD-04 [H], like any other hold for map and survey work, and not DLY-OD-10, which codes a destination that is down. EX03 and EX04 left without trucks for the same reason take the same code [A], as a loading unit left without trucks by planned work does. The time categories follow GMG [F] (SRC-PUB-GMG-TUM-2020).

KPIs to watch: tip points consumed per shift; reserve at shift start and minimum during the shift; time with fewer than three and with no ACTIVE point; map versions for D01 per shift; dozer hours at D01; DLY-OD-04 hours caused by D01 having no ACTIVE tip point.

8.5 Training and competency implications

  • AHS Controllers: closing points, reading the forecast, grant withdrawal on R22, carry-over checks at activation.
  • Pit Supervisors: computing the reserve and planning dozer and Survey together.
  • Survey: change sets that list every tip point; the effect of batching on the reserve.
  • Patrol drivers and dozer operators: dump-edge findings in the fixed order; why a rebuilt section is not open after handback.

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-15-02-001 [H] The AMS shall allocate a truck at D01 only to a tip point that is ACTIVE in the map version in force, and shall never allocate or grant a SURVEYED, FULL, CLOSED or RETIRED point. Rationale: the life cycle is fail closed (section 4.3). Verification: test (LAB-15-02-01 part E: every policy and starting point with tip point 2 CLOSED; part F: no truck at tip point 2 after T+10 s, none at tip point 4 before T+4800 s).

REQ-15-02-002 [H] The Pit Supervisor and the AHS Controller shall be able to close a tip point at once, with reason, reporter and time recorded; on closure the AMS shall withdraw every grant to that point not yet in use and show the trucks affected. Rationale: a patrol finding classed as immediate is acted on before the patrol leaves (REQ-14-06-007). Verification: test (closure by both roles accepted, by any other role refused; grant withdrawal); exercise (T+0 → T+10 s).

REQ-15-02-003 [H] A CLOSED tip point shall return to ACTIVE only after rebuild, survey and a map version newer than its own; a point whose edge line changed shall be retired and replaced by a new point rather than reopened. Rationale: section 4.3. Verification: test (reopen refused without survey and on the same version); inspection of change sets.

REQ-15-02-004 [H] Edge-protection values used by the AHS (windrow height, setback, spacing) shall be site-configured named values with a reference to the site document that sets them; the AHS shall have no default for them and the AMS shall refuse to publish an edge tip point without them. Rationale: they are set by the site's geotechnical and regulatory process (section 4.2). Verification: inspection of configuration; test (publication refused with a missing value).

REQ-15-02-005 [H] A map version that changes D01 shall list every existing tip point as carried over, changed or retired; the AMS shall not activate it otherwise, and shall allocate after activation only points carried over or published in it. Rationale: without carry-over every old point drops out (section 4.3, 6.3). Verification: test (LAB-15-02-01 part F with and without carry-over); inspection of the activation gate.

REQ-15-02-006 [H] The AMS shall show for D01 the loads left in ACTIVE points, the time until fewer than three and until none at the current arrival rate, and the lead time of the next replacement, and shall alarm when the time until none is shorter than that lead time. Rationale: at T+10 s the forecast showed the empty dump 25 minutes ahead (sections 6.2, 7.3). Verification: test (forecast 14 loads, about 25.2 min, empty at T+1552 s in the replay); HMI inspection.

REQ-15-02-007 [H] The shift plan shall state the dumping mix, the Survey cadence and the target reserve of prepared tip points at D01, derived from the lead time and the interval between full points. Rationale: the reserve needed depends on the cadence (section 4.6). Verification: inspection of shift plans; LAB-15-02-01 part C.

REQ-15-02-008 [H] Before accepting a work zone at D01 the AMS shall show the tip points inside it and the usable points left, and shall refuse a zone containing an ACTIVE point in use unless that point is closed first. Rationale: a second point inside the zone turns D01 into a single-position station (section 4.4). Verification: test (zone geometry check; LAB-15-02-01 parts D and E).

REQ-15-02-009 [H] Dozer work that changes D01's edge shall run under a closure of the affected edge section in addition to the work zone; the section shall return to use only with a new map version active, the trucks using it holding that version, and the zone handed back and inspected. Rationale: REQ-14-03-009 and REQ-14-04-007 at the dump (section 4.7). Verification: exercise (SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE T+2400 → T+4800 s); inspection of closure records.

REQ-15-02-010 [H] On R22 the AMS shall grant a truck no further than the Cusp point of its allocated tip point until the truck has reported itself there, and a closure issued while a truck's link is lost shall be reconciled against its last grant before it moves again. Rationale: R22 is the only poor-coverage segment (sections 2, 4.8). Verification: test (link loss injected on R22); exercise.

REQ-15-02-011 [H] Trucks held at D01-Q because no tip point is ACTIVE, or because a work zone or closure blocks their path, shall be coded DLY-OD-04; waits for a busy point while points are ACTIVE remain DLY-NP-02. Rationale: a missing ACTIVE tip point is caused by dozer or Survey work, and holds for map and survey work are DLY-OD-04 under the coding_rules of delay-codes.yml (section 8.4). Verification: inspection of the shift's coded intervals.

REQ-15-02-012 [H] Dump-edge condition shall be inspected on each active edge section at the frequency set by the site's process and after any event the site defines (for example rain), and every finding shall be recorded against the tip points it affects. Rationale: the patrol is the first line; the truck's sensing is the last (section 2). Verification: inspection of patrol records.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Default windrow heights or setbacks in the AHS "until the site sets them".
  • A dump node with infinite capacity, so the AMS never knows when the dump runs out.
  • Reopening a rebuilt edge point instead of retiring it; assuming a new version keeps every old point.
  • A work zone drawn generously "for safety" that takes a second tip point; a closed point that times out to ACTIVE.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Closing a point without checking for a truck already granted the reverse into it over a poor link.
  • Taking the work-zone release for the reopening of the edge section.
  • Sending the only dozer to the edge, without looking at the forecast, while the paddock fills.
  • Batching edge surveys without increasing the reserve; coding a stopped dump as an ordinary queue (DLY-NP-02).

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — consumption and reserve. 7,333.3 t/h × 12 h = 88,000 t; / 220 t = 400 loads; / 10 = 40 points; τ = 43,200 / 40 = 1,080 s. Edge points with Survey per point: L = 1,500 s, R = 2 (deterministic), three with random arrivals (lab). With hourly Survey: L ≤ 5,100 s, R = 5.

Example 2 — a second point in the work zone. With one usable point, ρ = 33.33 / 3600 × 85 = 0.787 and the Allen–Cunneen wait is 160.2 s against 1.0 s with three points — a factor of 160 for losing two points to closures and zones.

Example 3 — time to empty at T+10 s. Tip point 1 holds 2 loads and tip point 3 holds 4: 8 + 6 = 14 loads left. At 108 s a truck, starting with DT24 at T+40 s: 40 + 14 × 108 = T+1552 s, against a new edge point at T+4800 s on the registry clock and DZ03 free at T+2400 s. The replay's first new point is at T+3000 s: 1,471 s without a point, 13.6 loads.

15. Key takeaways

  • A dump is built by its trucks: at the model's defaults D01 consumes 40 tip points a DAY shift, one every 18 minutes.
  • Edge points change the map and go through change control; paddock points inside an unchanged boundary are designations.
  • The life cycle is fail closed: only ACTIVE points on the version in force are allocated; reopening needs rebuild, survey and a newer version; a new version must carry unchanged points over.
  • Without prepared points, D01 has fewer than three active points almost all shift with edge dumping; the reserve needed is lead time over interval, rounded up, plus one for random arrivals — two to five here, depending on the Survey cadence.
  • In SCN-DUMP-D01-EDGE the dump stopped at T+1529 s because one dozer was busy at the edge and nothing was prepared, and it kept stopping; two prepared points would have avoided it, and the forecast showed it 25 minutes ahead.
  • A work zone must take the point it rebuilds and no other: one usable point makes D01 behave like C01.
  • Windrow heights, setbacks and dump geometry belong to the site's geotechnical and regulatory process; the AHS consumes them, never sets them.
