1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

Volume 19 follows the conversion of the reference mine itself from manned to autonomous haulage [A]; this chapter is the part of that project history in which the site was made ready. The investment decision has been taken (ch-19-01, G2) and manned production continues at full rate. The readiness team [A] — Survey, a Field Technician, the site network team and the Pit Supervisor — have been asked one question for every road, area and device: "Is it ready for the first autonomous mission, and what is the evidence?"

On a Tuesday morning [A] a light vehicle with a receiver mounted at truck-antenna height drives down the main ramp R05 from the crest P1, under the manned pit's radio procedure. The only pit comms site planned at that time is the crest tower CT2. For the first kilometre the log shows a usable signal. At about 1,000 m from P1 the ramp turns along the wall below the crest, and the AHS-like test traffic stops getting through. On the pit floor CT2 is visible again; on the RL 330 bench — R09 up to Intersection W, R10 and R11 to the waste shovels EX03 and EX04, and their loading links R19 and R20 — it never is. The feasibility study had recorded "marginal" on these roads (ch-19-01, section 6.3). The drive test says something stronger: 1.98 km of road with no serving site at all.

At the debrief the Pit Supervisor asks the question that turns a coverage finding into a production finding: "If we close R05 for windrow rework, the trucks go out over the west ramp. Does that route have coverage?" The answer is no: from the pit floor the west ramp is reached over R09, which is on the dead bench. Without a pit-floor site, neither of the two pit exits would keep AMS authority.

The scene raises the questions of this chapter:

  • What does "ready" mean for a haul road, a coverage span, a GNSS correction, a map, an access point, a light vehicle — and what measurement turns each criterion into evidence?
  • Which roads carry the most readiness risk at this mine, and why?
  • Why must both ramps be ready, when only one carries traffic on a normal day?
  • How is the infrastructure built while manned production continues, who signs off each item, and what keeps the roads within their criteria after go-live?

This chapter covers:

  • the readiness workstreams and their dependencies (FIG-19-02-01) and the readiness criteria RC-01 to RC-09 [A];
  • a gap register over every segment and area of mine.yaml (LAB-19-02-01), ranked by risk (FIG-19-02-02), and the readiness finding that made the project add the pit-floor trailer CT3 — the one the mine has today;
  • both ramps: the R05-closure route over R24–R25 and why a single comms site still removes both pit exits;
  • the survey → map → as-built loop that must close before the first mission on a segment;
  • the infrastructure works, the device fitment of every manned vehicle, the register life cycle (FIG-19-02-03) and the routine that keeps the site ready after go-live.

Network design detail stays in Volume 11, survey and map detail in Volume 12; this chapter uses their methods.

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Specify site-readiness acceptance criteria for haul roads, wireless coverage and GNSS correction availability for AA-North, AA-Central and AA-South, labelling every numerical criterion [A].
  2. Rank the reference mine's segments by readiness risk from their recorded attributes and propose a mitigation for each high-risk segment.
  3. Explain how survey data, the baseline AHS map and the as-built comparison must agree before the AMS is allowed to issue the first mission on a segment.
  4. Analyse why both pit ramps must be ready: R05 as the primary route and the west ramp R24–R25 as the route used when R05 is closed.
  5. Specify the verification measurements that turn a readiness criterion into evidence — drive test, per-segment latency and loss sampling, GNSS correction availability from GB1 — with acceptance values [A].

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Sequence infrastructure works (autonomous fuel bay FB1, park-up PK1, access points AP-01 to AP-04, gates and signage) so that manned production continues during construction.
  2. Assign a sign-off role to each readiness item and track open items to closure in a readiness register.
  3. Describe the road-maintenance and inspection routine the site must sustain after go-live to keep both ramps and all autonomous roads within the accepted criteria.
  4. Organise fitment and acceptance testing of position reporting units, radios and field terminals on all 12 light vehicles, 4 dozers and 3 water trucks before any of them is permitted into an autonomous area.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

A manned mine and an autonomous mine can use the same roads, but they do not use them in the same way. A driver steers round a rock, slows where the windrow is low, waits for the radio to clear and trusts the eyes when the GNSS display is wrong. An autonomous truck does none of these: it follows a map, needs a valid permission over the network to keep moving, and stops when its localisation integrity, its perception or its link says it must. Every road that was "good enough" for drivers has to be shown to be good enough for the system. That is site readiness.

At the reference mine three facts make readiness a project in its own right.

  1. The site is brownfield and keeps producing. Thirty-seven trucks haul a DAY shift while the readiness work is done. Survey crews, test vehicles and construction plant enter the haul roads under the manned site's rules, and the road closures needed for rework (SCN-MAP-R05) cost production. Readiness work is scheduled against production, not beside it.
  2. The main ramp concentrates every risk. R05 is the shortest route from every shovel. It is the only road at the mine with a high GNSS multipath risk, its coverage is marginal, its lower 340 m run below the crest wall, and its grade of 8.99 % is above the illustrative wet-weather ODD limit of 8 % [A] (ch-04-02, ch-12-05). Whatever goes wrong with readiness is likely to go wrong there first.
  3. The second ramp is a readiness item, not a spare. R24–R25 exists so that closing R05 reroutes traffic instead of isolating the pit (added in mine.yaml 0.2.0). A contingency route that has not been surveyed, drive-tested, mapped and signed off is not a contingency: the day R05 closes, it is a closed pit.

Readiness is also where the project meets evidence discipline. The operational-readiness review of ch-18-05 counts a status only with evidence, and a blocking criterion cannot be averaged away. Every readiness item of this chapter is written so that, when it is closed, the review can count it.

3. Terminology and foundations

  • Site readiness: the state in which roads, network, GNSS corrections, survey and map, infrastructure and manned vehicle devices meet the criteria for autonomous operation in an area, with evidence.
  • Readiness criterion: a condition an item must meet, with a measurable acceptance value [A] and a method. This chapter numbers its criteria RC-01 to RC-09 (chapter-local [H]).
  • Readiness register [H]: the single list of readiness items — finding, criterion, owner, sign-off role, mitigation, evidence and state — kept from the first survey to the operational-readiness review.
  • Readiness item: one line of the register: one finding against one criterion for one target (a segment, a span, an area, a node, a comms site or a vehicle).
  • Blocking criterion (ch-18-05): a criterion that must be met for a go; it cannot be compensated by others. In the register an item is blocking when its target is on a production circuit, an exit or the R05-closure route.
  • Evidence record: the record that makes a status count — a drive-test record, an as-built acceptance record, a device acceptance test, an inspection sheet — filed with an ID and date.
  • Drive test (ch-11-04): a measured run along a road with a receiver at truck-antenna height, recording level, delivered AHS-like messages, latency and handover gaps per chainage bin; the only acceptable evidence for a coverage attribute (REQ-11-04-002).
  • Serving site: the comms site that serves a span of a segment (network.yaml); a backhaul links a site towards the network core. CT3 is a mobile comms trailer with a radio backhaul to CT2.
  • Pit exit: a route by which a loaded truck leaves the pit with AMS authority on every leg — R05 to P1, or R24 to P3 (network.yaml pit_exits).
  • R05-closure route [H]: the route of mission M-20481 with R05 closed, over R09, R24, R25 and R12 (SCN-MAP-R05).
  • Wet-weather rule [H]: the operating rule for roads whose grade exceeds the wet ODD limit — at the reference mine, R05 is closed to autonomous traffic in rain and traffic uses the R05-closure route.
  • As-built and AHS map (Volume 12): the surveyed geometry of a road as built, and the map version the AMS and ADS use. A map built from an unaccepted survey is not a ready map.
  • Correction link and correction age (ch-05-02): the delivery of RTK corrections from the base station GB1 to the trucks — over the AHS network at this mine — and how old the latest correction is.

4. Technical explanation

4.1 Readiness workstreams and their dependencies

FIG-19-02-01 shows the five workstreams and the dependencies that matter. Roads, network, survey and map, infrastructure, people and devices run in parallel, but three dependencies decide the order:

  • Survey before map before drive test. A coverage attribute records the map version in force during its drive test (REQ-11-04-004); a drive test on a road that is still being reworked is evidence for a road that will not exist. The as-built survey of each road is accepted first, the map is built from it, and the final drive test records that map version.
  • Comms site before drive test. The drive test that matters is the one with the final sites in place. On the RL 330 bench that means CT3 on its pad, its backhaul to CT2 working and its generator commissioned.
  • Everything before the register is closed, and the register before activation. Area activation (ch-18-04) and the operational-readiness review (ch-18-05) take the register as input. An item that is "nearly done" is open.

4.2 Readiness criteria and the measurements behind them

The handbook gives the structure; the site fills the values. LAB-19-02-01 uses chapter-local criteria with illustrative values [A]:

The acceptance values behind RC-04 and RC-06 are where engineering judgement enters, and the chapter labels every one of them [A]:

  • Coverage (ch-11-04 format): level thresholds per class (the lab of ch-11-04 uses −65 / −80 dBm on the 10th percentile of 50 m bins, at least two samples per bin [A]); the required class by use — pit exits, queue points, loading areas and access points at least marginal with a measured delivery, the dump tip area accepted at poor only with a degraded-link profile [A].
  • Delivery and latency per bin: the share of AHS-like messages delivered and the latency percentile, sampled at haul speed in both directions and at production times. The illustrative link classes of network.yaml (median one-way latency 25 / 60 / 150 ms for good / marginal / poor [A]) are the reference against which the measured values are compared, not acceptance values.
  • Handover gaps: the longest measured gap at each hard handover against the detection time (ch-11-02); the model's assumed gap is 1.5 s [A].
  • GNSS: correction age below the limit the ADS uses on every span [A], and an integrity test at poor satellite geometry on every segment with medium or high multipath risk. Because GB1's corrections reach the trucks over CT1–CT3 (network.yaml), a span without coverage is also a span without corrections; RC-06 is never met where RC-04 or RC-05 fails.

4.3 Roads: grade, edges and the wet-weather rule

Every segment of mine.yaml is 30 m wide, two-way with one lane per direction [A], and the AHS map gives 2 × 12.0 m of lanes (ch-12-05). Width is therefore not where the reference mine's roads fail readiness. Grade and edges are.

Grade. Every road at the mine is within the dry ODD limit of 10 % [A]. One road is not within the wet limit: R05 at 8.99 %. LAB-19-02-01 finds it alone (wet_weather), and finds that with R05 closed the pit still has an exit over R24 (6.33 %) — provided that exit is ready. The register therefore carries R05 as RC-02 with the mitigation of a wet-weather rule: in rain R05 is closed to autonomous traffic and the R05-closure route carries the pit. A rule like this is a readiness item, not an operating detail: it is written in the ConOps, configured in the AMS as a closure the AHS Controller can apply, and tested before go-live. Whether to regrade R05 to 8 % — ch-12-05 computes what it would cost M-20481 — is a mine-planning decision outside the project.

Edges and windrows. No windrow attribute is recorded in mine.yaml; the register therefore carries an RC-03 item for every autonomous road: as-built edge and windrow surveyed and accepted. Twenty-four segments get it (all except the manned MIA road R16). On roads used by a production circuit or by the R05-closure route the item is blocking; on the park-up, fuel-bay, water-fill and MIA-gate roads it is not, because a delay there does not stop production. The rework known from the operations volumes — the R05 windrow and edge (SCN-MAP-R05) and the R24 windrow gap and rut found in the detour inspection of ch-12-05 — are the first entries.

4.4 Network: the gap that became CT3, and what is left

The readiness drive test of section 1.1 found that lower R05 and the RL 330 bench had no serving site from the crest. LAB-19-02-01 reproduces the finding without changing mine.yaml: the network with CT3 removed is the network the project started from.

Without a pit-floor site (network without CT3) Result (LAB-19-02-01 part A)
Spans with no serving site R05 from 1000.0 m to 1339.6 m; all of R09, R10, R11, R19, R20
Length unserved 1.98 km
Pit exits with AMS authority none (R05-P1 and R24-P3 both pass the gap)

The register entry was raised as blocking and high: no autonomous traffic could reach EX03 or EX04 or climb out of the pit. The project considered the options of Volume 11 [A]: re-aiming or adding sectors on CT2 could not see round the wall; a fixed site on the bench would be cut off as the pit deepened; a mobile trailer on the pit floor could serve the lower ramp and the bench now and be moved with the faces (ch-11-02, REQ-11-04-007). The project chose the trailer. That is CT3: a 15 m mast on the pit floor at RL 305, a radio backhaul to CT2 and its own generator (network.yaml). With CT3, lower R05 hands over from CT2 to CT3 at 1,000 m from P1, the pit floor is served by both, and the bench by CT3 alone.

CT3 closed the gap. It did not remove the dependency. The register keeps three network items after CT3 (part B):

Comms site out alone Segments losing service Pit exits left
CT1 (MIA tower, the root of the backhaul chain) 25 none
CT2 (crest tower; CT3's backhaul runs through it) 20 none
CT3 (pit-floor trailer) 6 none
GB1 (RTK base station; corrections are carried by the comms sites) 0 both

Every comms site at the mine is a single point for the pit exits: CT3 because it alone serves lower R05 and R09; CT2 because CT3's backhaul runs through it and R24 is served by CT2 only; CT1 because the whole chain runs back through it to the core. The register records each as a high item with the mitigation "document the outage consequence; temporary cover or a second serving site as an option" [H]. The options are discussed, not added: temporary cover of lower R05 while CT3 is moved (ch-11-02); a second serving site for R24, independent of CT3's backhaul and power, before R24 is relied on as the only exit (REQ-11-04-005); a second trailer [A]. Whichever is chosen is recorded in the reference mine's shared data (mine.yaml and network.yaml) with a changelog entry. Until one is chosen, the mitigation is operational: power and backhaul checks on CT3, a CT3 outage drill before AA-North is activated (section 6.2), and the coding and recovery rules of ch-11-07.

4.5 GNSS: multipath, corrections and integrity

mine.yaml records one high multipath risk (R05) and five medium ones (R07, R08, R17, R18, R24). Multipath is a property of the wall beside the road and of the satellites in view at the time; it cannot be accepted from a map. The register carries an RC-06 item for each of these segments, with an integrity test as evidence: trucks or a test vehicle running the segment at times of poor satellite geometry [A], the integrity indicators logged, and the speed-derating behaviour of SCN-GNSS-R05 observed — integrity degraded on DT17 at T+0 s, a second truck stopping at the same place at T+40 s, the controller applying a ramp restriction at T+60 s and calling Survey at T+300 s. The readiness test asks whether the system behaves that way, and how often it would.

Corrections add a network dependency. GB1 at the MIA computes RTK corrections and the comms sites carry them (network.yaml). On every span the correction age must stay within the ADS limit [A]; where a span loses its link, it loses its corrections too. GB1 itself is not a single point for the exits in the model (part B: both exits usable when GB1 is down, because the trucks keep authority), but trucks without corrections lose RTK precision and will derate or stop on localisation; that is a degraded mode to be tested, not an outage the exits analysis can see. Survey checks GB1's position against the site control and its uptime before any integrity test counts.

4.6 Survey → map → as-built: the loop that must close before the first mission

A segment is ready for its first mission only when three records agree [H]:

  1. The as-built survey of the road as it is, accepted against the design within the as-built tolerances (ch-12-07).
  2. The AHS map built from that survey: lanes, edges, speed limits, zones and attributes, with a version number (ch-12-08). LAB-19-02-01 runs the handbook's map checks on the base map AHS-MAP-0001: no issue, two usable pit exits.
  3. The comparison of the map with the as-built, and the version in use on every truck equal to the validated version (the map-version gate of ch-12-09).

SCN-MAP-R05 is the loop in operation, and the register uses its timeline as the template for every rework done during readiness: R05 closed and traffic over the west ramp from T−3600 s; as-built submitted at T+0 s; map change validated against the survey at T+1800 s; new version deployed and the road reopened at T+2400 s; routes back on R05 at T+2700 s. Before go-live the same steps are done without traffic, but with the same evidence: the as-built acceptance record, the validated map version and the version report from each commissioned truck. A coverage attribute measured before a rework is set back to "to be re-verified" by the map change (REQ-11-04-004); in the register the coverage item of that segment returns from signed off to raised.

4.7 Both ramps: why the contingency must be ready

With R05 open, every circuit uses it. With R05 closed — for rework, in rain under the wet-weather rule, or after an incident on the ramp — mission M-20481 runs over the west ramp. LAB-19-02-01 part D computes the route: 4.50 km grows to 5.87 km over R18, R08, R09, R24, R25, R12, R02 and R21, the same detour SCN-MAP-R05 describes. For that route to exist in practice, every one of those segments and AP-04 at P3 must be accepted, and the R24 exit must keep AMS authority.

R05-closure contingency Result
Nothing accepted yet does not exist; nine items missing (eight segments and AP-04)
All eight segments and AP-04 accepted exists
All accepted except R24 does not exist; R24 alone is missing

Two readiness consequences follow. The west ramp is on the critical path for AA-North, not after it: if AA-North is activated with R05 ready and R24 not, the first closure of R05 isolates every autonomous truck in the pit. And the two ramps share CT3: the contingency route climbs from the pit floor over R09, which only CT3 serves. A CT3 outage removes lower R05 and R09 together, so the west ramp is no contingency for a CT3 outage. The register records this as part of the CT3 item, and section 4.4's options are the only structural answer.

4.8 Ranking the register

LAB-19-02-01 evaluates every segment and area against RC-01 to RC-09 and scores each finding: likelihood [A] from the recorded attribute (coverage good 1, marginal 2, poor 3, plus one for a span served only by CT3; multipath low 1, medium 2, high 3; wet grade 2; an unaccepted as-built 1) times consequence [A] from the road's role (pit exit 3; production circuit or R05-closure route 2; other 1). Scores of 6 and above are high, 3–5 medium, 1–2 low.

The register has 121 items: 25 road, 19 coverage and comms-site, 6 GNSS, 1 map, 10 infrastructure and 60 device items. Sixteen are high, seventeen medium and eighty-eight low; 117 are blocking. The top of the ranking:

A ranking is a work order, not a verdict. A high item with a verified mitigation is ready; a low item with no evidence is not. The ranking tells the team where to start and where the operational-readiness review will look first.

Areas in activation order: AA-South items first, then AA-Central, then AA-North (ch-18-04), so that each area's register is complete when its activation is planned.

4.10 Devices on every manned vehicle that may enter

Every manned vehicle that enters an autonomous area carries a position reporting unit (PRU), a two-way radio and a field terminal (FT) (equipment.yaml, ch-14-01). The register carries one item per vehicle and device: 12 light vehicles, 4 dozers, 3 water trucks and the grader GR01 (added later to the fleet; equipment.yaml fleet_additions), 20 vehicles and 60 items. Each is closed only by a device acceptance test [H]:

  1. Fitment: device installed, powered from the vehicle, antenna positions recorded; identity registered against the vehicle ID in the device registry (ch-02-05).
  2. PRU: position reported at the expected rate and within the expected accuracy at a surveyed check point [A]; the AMS shows the vehicle at the right place with a fresh report age.
  3. Radio: call on the site channel and on the area channel; read-back test.
  4. Field terminal: a test access request at a training access point; grant, acknowledgement, release; a deliberately stale grant refused (fail closed, ch-02-07).
  5. Record: test ID, date, tester, AMS software and map version.

A vehicle without all three items signed off is not permitted into an autonomous area, whatever its driver's competence [H]. The register makes that visible per vehicle, and the access-point procedure checks it.

4.11 The register and its life cycle

The register is the project's memory of readiness. Each item moves through four states (FIG-19-02-03):

  • Raised: a finding is recorded against a criterion, with a target, an owner and a sign-off role.
  • Mitigated: the owner has put the mitigation in place (the rework is done, the site is built, the rule is written).
  • Verified: the owner has filed an evidence record (drive test, as-built acceptance, integrity test, inspection, device test). Without a record there is no verification; "done" without a record is open.
  • Signed off: the sign-off role — never the owner — accepts the evidence. No sign-off without evidence is the handbook's convention for the register [H].

A change re-opens an item with a stated reason: a map change re-opens the coverage and as-built items of the segment; a CT3 move re-opens every item served by CT3; a road rework re-opens the road, map and coverage items. LAB-19-02-01 implements the life cycle and refuses the four shortcuts the operational-readiness review would also refuse: sign-off without verification, verification without evidence, sign-off by the wrong role, re-opening without a reason.

The register's blocking items feed the operational-readiness review of ch-18-05, which owns the go / conditional go / no-go gate. The register does not decide go-live; it makes the review possible.

4.12 After go-live: keeping the site ready

Readiness is not a state reached once. Roads wear, windrows are knocked down, walls are cut back, the trailer moves with the faces. The routine the site must sustain [H]:

  • Pit patrol of both ramps and the bench at least once per shift [A]: windrow condition, spillage, ruts, drainage, dust; findings to the AHS Controller and the road-maintenance plan (ch-14-06).
  • Road maintenance under work zones or closures (ch-14-03, ch-14-07): grading and watering planned against the shift plan; the R24 route maintained to the same standard as R05, because it must be usable the day R05 closes.
  • Re-verification triggers: a map change, a road rework, a CT3 relocation, a new face or bench, a change to GB1 — each re-opens the affected register items, which are closed again with evidence.
  • Periodic checks [A]: a drive test of both ramps and the bench after each CT3 move and at a fixed interval; an integrity review of R05 from the trucks' own logs; the R05-closure route exercised in production at a planned time, so that the contingency is proven, not assumed.

4.13 Trade-offs and model limits

  • Attributes, not measurements. The lab ranks recorded classes. A real register ranks drive-test and survey results; the structure stays the same.
  • Consequence by role, not by traffic. Every circuit segment counts the same; a site would weight by trucks per hour.
  • Scores are a work order. The product of two small integers sorts the list; it does not measure risk in the safety-case sense (ch-04-10).
  • Single-site dependencies are recorded, not solved. The chapter keeps mine.yaml unchanged; the options of section 4.4 are for the project to choose and for the shared data to record if chosen.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation — walking R05's register

In normal readiness work the team walks the register segment by segment. For R05 it means four items, in the order the dependencies allow: the windrow and edge rework in a planned window, the as-built accepted, the map rebuilt and checked; then the final drive test with CT3 in place, both directions, at haul speed and at production times, with the CT2 → CT3 handover at 1,000 m measured; then the integrity test at poor satellite geometry; then the wet-weather rule written, configured and rehearsed with the west ramp. Each step files its record; each item moves raised → mitigated → verified → signed off. Normal readiness work is slow, repetitive and visible in the register; that is its point.

6.2 Abnormal operation — SCN-COMMS-R05 as a readiness test (shared timeline for sections 7 and 8)

Before AA-North is activated, the project tests what the register says about CT3: that its loss removes lower R05 and the bench, and that the system fails safe. The test reproduces the registered scenario with the commissioned trial trucks then in AA-North [A] (eight in the registry's initial state), under commissioning controls: the area is not yet activated, the remote stop of AA-North is available, and manned production runs on its own circuits. CT3's power is removed at a planned time [A]. The registry gives T+0, T+3, T+10 and T+900 s.

It also produces the numbers the project needs for the operational-readiness review: how long a CT3 outage costs, and how many trucks it strands. The register item RR-NET-CT3 moves to verified with this test as its evidence; the structural options of section 4.4 remain open.

6.3 Degraded operation — GNSS on the ramp, and a map that changes

SCN-GNSS-R05. The integrity test of RC-06 on R05 is done at a time of poor satellite geometry [A]. The registered sequence is what the test must show: DT17's integrity degrades and it derates at T+0 s; a second truck at the same place stops at T+40 s; at T+60 s the AHS Controller recognises the pattern and applies a ramp speed restriction through the AMS; at T+300 s Survey is called to check GB1 and satellite availability. A readiness test that never produces a derating has tested the wrong time of day.

SCN-MAP-R05. The R05 rework is the readiness project's first use of the map-change loop of section 4.6: R05 closed and traffic on the west ramp (T−3600 s), as-built submitted (T+0 s), validated (T+1800 s), deployed and reopened (T+2400 s), routes back on R05 (T+2700 s). Its degraded case is the one the register guards against: the rework completes, the survey is late, and someone proposes to reopen R05 "because it looks fine". The register answers: the road item is mitigated, not verified; the coverage item has been re-opened by the map change; the segment is not ready.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

  • A trailer, not a tower, for the pit floor. A fixed site on the bench would be cut off as the pit deepens; a trailer can follow the faces but each move is an AHS change (ch-11-02). The project accepted the moves for the coverage. The cost is a single point that the register keeps visible.
  • Criteria by use, not one class for all. Pit exits and loading areas need more than a water-fill spur. A uniform "good everywhere" criterion would fail the dump tip-area link (poor) and either block AA-South or be quietly waived; a criterion by use with a stated mitigation does neither.
  • Consequence from the network, not from opinion. The lab derives a segment's consequence from the circuits' routes and the R05-closure route. When the mine plan changes, the consequences change with it.
  • The west ramp on the AA-North critical path. Treating R24–R25 as "after go-live" would save weeks and create a pit that cannot survive its first R05 closure.
  • Evidence, not status. The register refuses a status without a record, as the readiness review does. That costs paperwork and saves the review.

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

The SCN-COMMS-R05 readiness test is a timing test of four interfaces. Between the network and the ADS: link loss must be detected and the truck stopped within its granted path by T+3 s; the test records each truck's stop position against the lost span. Between the ADS and the AMS: the AMS must mark the trucks communication-lost by T+10 s and freeze their reservations, so that no other truck is granted into their path. Between the AMS and the controller: the HMI must show which trucks and which spans, from the same coverage attributes the AMS uses (REQ-11-04-008), so that the controller's hold points are right. Between the network and the AMS on restoration: reconciliation must rebuild truck states and reservations before any restart (ch-02-08, ch-16-07). The CT2 → CT3 handover at 1,000 m on R05 adds a fifth: the drive test must show the handover gap below the detection time (the model assumes 1.5 s [A]), or trucks will stop at the handover in normal operation. Corrections ride on the same links: a truck that stops at T+3 s for link loss also has an ageing correction, and its localisation state after restoration is part of the reconciliation check.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

During readiness the AHS Controller's HMI is already running for commissioning. Its coverage layer shows each segment span with its class, evidence date and status, drawn from the AMS attribute records (REQ-11-04-008): before the final drive test the bench shows "to be verified"; after it, "marginal, CT3". The register itself is a project tool [A]; the controller sees its effect as the segments and areas the AMS allows. In the SCN-COMMS-R05 test the HMI shows at T+3 s the stops on lower R05 and the bench, at T+10 s the trucks marked communication-lost and their frozen reservations, and from T+900 s the reconciliation and the staged restart.

8.3 Procedure excerpt — the CT3 outage readiness test (SCN-COMMS-R05)

  1. Commissioning team [A] (day before): confirm the test window with the Pit Supervisor; list the trial trucks in AA-North and their planned positions; confirm the remote stop of AA-North is available and tested.
  2. Pit Supervisor: approve the window; confirm no manned vehicle will be in the CT3-only spans; inform the crews.
  3. Field Technician: at the CT3 pad (served by CT2), confirm the radio check with the AHS Controller; prepare the planned power removal.
  4. AHS Controller (T−60 s [A]): confirm truck positions on the HMI; start the test log.
  5. Field Technician (T+0 s): remove CT3's power on the controller's call; report the time.
  6. AHS Controller (T+3 to T+10 s): record which trucks stopped and where; confirm the communication-lost marks and frozen reservations; confirm no mission is issued into the dead zone.
  7. AHS Controller (T+10 to T+900 s): hold trucks bound for the bench short of the dead zone; do not move any truck in the dead zone; report to the Pit Supervisor.
  8. Field Technician (T+900 s): restore CT3's power; report the time and the backhaul state.
  9. AHS Controller: supervise reconciliation and the staged restart; record any truck needing manual recovery.
  10. Commissioning team [A]: file the test record as evidence for RR-NET-CT3 and the CT3-only items; raise any deviation as a new register item.

8.4 KPIs and delay codes affected

  • During readiness tests before area activation, trucks under test are in Unscheduled Time: DLY-UN-01, also while a remote stop is latched during the test (handbook coding rule).
  • Readiness works on manned circuits (closures for rework, CT3 installation) are manned delay in the baseline; the project records them separately so that the cost of readiness is visible (ch-19-01).
  • After go-live, a CT3 outage codes each stopped truck DLY-OD-05; trucks held short of the dead zone DLY-OD-04; a planned CT3 relocation or power-down DLY-OD-04 (handbook coding rules). A site-wide code (DLY-SE-02) applies only when no segment keeps a serving site.
  • Map work that holds trucks — survey, map reloads, waits at a temporary single lane — is DLY-OD-04 (handbook coding rule).
  • Readiness KPIs [H]: blocking items open per area; items verified without sign-off for more than a week [A]; share of segments with a drive-test record dated after the last map change.

8.5 Training and competency implications

Survey and the network team need the drive-test method of ch-11-04 and the as-built acceptance of ch-12-07, applied together. Field Technicians need device fitment and acceptance testing and the CT3 power and backhaul checks. AHS Controllers in training should take part in the CT3 outage readiness test: it is the first time they see a comms-loss pattern on their own mine, with time to think. Pit Supervisors need to plan closures against production and to sign off items only on evidence. Every manned-vehicle driver needs the device and access briefing before their vehicle's items are signed off (ch-19-05 owns the workforce side).

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-19-02-001 [H] The project shall keep one readiness register covering every road segment, area, access point, infrastructure node, comms site and manned-vehicle device in the autonomous areas, with for each item a criterion, an owner, a sign-off role, a mitigation, an evidence record and a state. Rationale: readiness that is not listed cannot be reviewed (sections 4.2, 4.11). Verification: inspection of the register against mine.yaml, network.yaml and equipment.yaml; analysis (LAB-19-02-01).

REQ-19-02-002 [H] A segment span shall be signed off for coverage only on a drive-test record made in both directions, with the final serving sites in place and the validated map version in force, classed against the site's acceptance criteria. Rationale: an estimate or an early test describes a network or road that is not the one trucks will use (sections 4.1, 4.2; REQ-11-04-002). Verification: inspection of drive-test records.

REQ-19-02-003 [H] For each comms site the project shall record the segments that lose service and the pit exits left when the site alone is out, and shall record for each outage that leaves no pit exit either a structural mitigation or an operational mitigation tested before the affected area is activated. Rationale: every comms site at the reference mine is a single point for the pit exits (section 4.4). Verification: analysis (LAB-19-02-01 part B); test (CT3 outage readiness test, section 6.2).

REQ-19-02-004 [H] AA-North shall not be activated until every segment of the R05-closure route and AP-04 are signed off and the R24 exit keeps AMS authority with all comms sites up. Rationale: without the contingency, the first closure of R05 isolates the pit (section 4.7). Verification: inspection of the register; analysis (LAB-19-02-01, ramp_readiness).

REQ-19-02-005 [H] A map change, a road rework or a comms-site move shall re-open every register item of the affected segments, and the items shall be signed off again only on evidence dated after the change. Rationale: evidence for a road or network that has changed is not evidence (sections 4.6, 4.11; REQ-11-04-004). Verification: test of the register tool; inspection.

REQ-19-02-006 [H] A register item shall be verified only with an evidence record and signed off only by its sign-off role, which shall not be the item's owner. Rationale: independent acceptance on evidence, as the operational-readiness review requires (section 4.11; ch-18-05). Verification: test (LAB-19-02-01 life cycle); inspection.

REQ-19-02-007 [H] Readiness works shall never close both pit ramps at the same time, and the R24 route shall be accepted before R05 is closed for its own rework. Rationale: production continues during readiness and the pit must keep an exit (section 4.9). Verification: inspection of the closure plan.

REQ-19-02-008 [H] No manned vehicle shall be permitted into an autonomous area until its PRU, radio and field terminal items are signed off on a device acceptance test that includes a refused stale grant. Rationale: a vehicle the AMS cannot see or cannot grant correctly defeats the access-permission design (section 4.10; ch-14-01). Verification: inspection of the register at the access point; test.

REQ-19-02-009 [H] Every segment whose grade exceeds the wet ODD limit shall have a wet-weather rule in the ConOps, configured in the AMS as a closure the AHS Controller can apply, and rehearsed with the alternative route before go-live. Rationale: R05 at 8.99 % is outside the wet ODD [A]; a rule that exists only on paper fails in the first rain (section 4.3). Verification: inspection; test (rehearsal).

10. Verification and validation

  • Verification of the register model: LAB-19-02-01 evaluates every segment and area of mine.yaml, reproduces the serving and exits analysis of ahs_sim.network, runs the map checks of ahs_sim.mapping, and enforces the life cycle (10 automated tests).
  • Verification of readiness itself is by the evidence records of RC-01 to RC-09: drive tests, as-built acceptance, integrity tests, map checks, inspections and device tests — each filed against its item.
  • Validation comes from operation: the first months of autonomous production (ch-19-07) show whether the register's high items were the ones that caused stops (DLY-OD-05 on R05 and the bench, DLY-OD-06 on R05, nuisance stops on rough roads). A register whose top items never appear in the delay data was ranked wrongly, or mitigated well; the review must say which.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Accepting coverage from a terrain model or from the feasibility estimate.
  • Drive-testing before CT3 is commissioned or before the map is final.
  • Treating every comms site as independent when the backhaul chain runs CT3 → CT2 → CT1.
  • Leaving R24 out of the AA-North scope because "R05 is the route".
  • Forgetting that RTK corrections ride on the AHS links.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • Signing off an item because the work is done, without the evidence record.
  • Letting the owner sign off their own item.
  • Scheduling the R05 rework while R24 is still being repaired.
  • Fitting PRUs to light vehicles without testing them against the AMS.
  • Reopening a reworked road before the map version is validated.
  • Running the R05-closure route for the first time on the day R05 has to close.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — The pre-project gap. Without CT3, lost_spans returns R05 from 1000.0 m to 1339.6 m (339.6 m), R09 (462.0 m), R10 (427.2 m), R11 (360.6 m), R19 (212.1 m) and R20 (180.3 m): 1,981.8 m, the 1.98 km of the lab. Both pit exits pass through it — R05-P1 directly, R24-P3 via R09 — so usable_exits is false for both.

Example 2 — Scoring R05. R05 is a pit exit: consequence 3. Its lower span is served only by CT3: likelihood 2 (marginal) + 1 = 3, score 9. Its multipath risk is high: 3 × 3 = 9. Its grade, 8.99 %, exceeds the wet limit: 2 × 3 = 6. Its coverage is marginal: 2 × 3 = 6. Its as-built item scores 1 × 3 = 3. R05 holds the top four places of the register.

Example 3 — R24 and R09. R24 is a pit exit (consequence 3), marginal (2 × 3 = 6), served by CT2 only (2 × 3 = 6), with medium multipath (2 × 3 = 6): three high items. R09 is not an exit, but it is on the EX03 and EX04 circuits and on the R05-closure route (consequence 2); served only by CT3 (3 × 2 = 6): high. The contingency route's weakest link is not R24 but R09.

Example 4 — Device items. 12 light vehicles + 4 dozers + 3 water trucks + 1 grader = 20 vehicles; × 3 devices = 60 items, each with a fitment and an acceptance test. At an illustrative two hours per vehicle for the three tests [A], that is 40 hours of Field Technician time, planned in the weeks before AA-South is activated — water trucks and the grader first, because they work the roads the first area uses.

15. Key takeaways

  • Site readiness is evidence, item by item, in one register; a status without a record is open.
  • The reference mine's readiness risk concentrates on the main ramp R05: CT3-only lower span, high multipath, a grade above the wet ODD, marginal coverage.
  • The readiness drive test found 1.98 km of road with no serving site from the crest; that gap is why the project added the pit-floor trailer CT3 — the one the mine has today. mine.yaml is unchanged.
  • CT3 closed the gap but every comms site remains a single point for the pit exits; the options are discussed and recorded, not added.
  • Both ramps must be ready: the R05-closure route (4.50 → 5.87 km) needs eight segments and AP-04 accepted, and it shares CT3 with lower R05 through R09.
  • Survey, map and as-built must agree before the first mission; a change re-opens the items it affects.
  • Infrastructure is built against production: never both ramps closed, areas in activation order.
  • Every manned vehicle's PRU, radio and field terminal is tested before it may enter an autonomous area.
  • After go-live, patrol, maintenance and re-verification keep the site ready; the R05-closure route is exercised, not assumed.
