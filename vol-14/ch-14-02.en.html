1. Overview

1.1 Why this chapter matters

09:20, CR1. LV07 has been inside AA-North for twenty minutes, inspecting the windrow on R06 under a permission that locks R05 and R06. On the HMI its icon sits 300 m short of Intersection Z, with a small grey number beside it: age 0.8 s. The number changes every second and nobody looks at it. At 09:20:10 the icon turns into an outline with a circle around it and the text LV07 — position unknown, last seen R06, age 10 s. Nineteen ore trucks bound for Z stop. At 09:20:15 the controller calls LV07; the driver answers at once and says they are parked at the windrow marker. The circle on the HMI keeps growing.

The Pit Supervisor arrives in CR1 a minute later and asks three questions. Why did it take ten seconds, and why not two? If the driver says they are parked, why is the circle still growing and why are the trucks still stopped? And yesterday afternoon the same icon went grey for half a minute on lower R05 and came back by itself — was that the same thing?

The answers depend on what a position report is, how it travels, how old it is when the AMS uses it and what the AMS is allowed to conclude from silence. This chapter covers:

  • the PRU reporting chain from LV07's antenna to the AMS HMI, with the latency, rate and loss at each step (FIG-14-02-01);
  • report age, position uncertainty and the awareness envelope they define along the roads (FIG-14-02-02);
  • staleness detection and its two costs: the time to notice a real failure and the false alarms on marginal road;
  • the awareness states the AMS shows and the actions behind them (FIG-14-02-03), including a vehicle shown outside its permitted area;
  • the device side of SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07, aligned with the protective action of ch-04-08 and the substitute release confirmation of ch-16-04.

Report periods, latencies, speed bounds, staleness limits and lost limits are illustrative assumptions [A] (LAB-14-02-01). No staleness value is recommended. Procedures, states and requirements are handbook conventions [H].

1.2 Learning objectives — Engineering

After this chapter an engineering reader will be able to:

  1. Explain the PRU reporting chain (positioning, field terminal, network, AMS) and the latency, rate and loss at each step.
  2. Calculate the position uncertainty of LV07 as a function of report age and speed and use it to size an awareness envelope [A].
  3. Specify staleness detection and the protective action the AMS takes when a manned vehicle's report becomes stale, consistent with fail-closed permissions.
  4. Analyse PRU report delivery on the marginal-coverage segments (R05, R09–R11, R24) and its effect on false staleness alarms and on detection time.

1.3 Learning objectives — Operations

After this chapter an operations reader will be able to:

  1. Interpret the manned-vehicle layer on the control room HMI (fresh, stale, unknown, outside permitted area) and state the action for each indication.
  2. Apply the pre-entry device check at AP-02 for LV07 (PRU, radio, field terminal) and refuse entry when a device is not working.
  3. Explain to light-vehicle drivers why PRU reports supplement but do not replace positive communication by radio.
  4. Respond to LV07 shown outside its permitted area on the HMI, distinguishing a report error from a real excursion by radio before acting.

2. Operational motivation: the problem at the reference mine

Every manned vehicle that enters an autonomous area at the reference mine carries a position reporting unit (PRU), a two-way radio and a field terminal (equipment.yaml). The PRU is the only device that tells the AMS, without a person speaking, where the vehicle is. Three things about it make it harder to use than it looks.

A report is always old. The position was measured on the vehicle, sent over the site network and processed by the AMS. By the time the controller sees it, the vehicle has moved on. On a working link the age is small; when reports are lost it grows by a full report period for each missing one.

Silence is ambiguous. A missing report may mean a failed PRU, a burst of radio loss on marginal road, a hard handover between comms sites, an outage of a whole comms site, or a vehicle that has driven out of coverage. The AMS cannot tell which from the silence itself; it can only measure how long the silence has lasted.

The network is not uniform. The lower part of the main ramp R05, the RL 330 bench (R09–R11) and the west ramp R24 are marginal-coverage segments in mine.yaml; the dump road R22 is poor. R05 has a hard handover from CT2 to CT3 at 1000 m below P1, and CT3, the trailer on the pit floor, can fail on its own (SCN-COMMS-R05). A staleness rule that is quick on good road raises false alarms on marginal road; one that is quiet on marginal road is slow everywhere (ch-11-01).

ch-02-04 and ch-04-08 define what the AMS does with a stale position: it shows the position as unknown with its age, keeps the permission and the area lock, and applies a protective action (ch-02-04 REQ-02-04-007, ch-04-08 REQ-04-08-003). ch-16-04 defines how such a permission ends when the PRU never recovers: the substitute release confirmation. This chapter supplies what sits under those rules: the chain, the numbers, the states and the device-side checks.

3. Terminology and foundations

Position reporting unit (PRU). The device on a manned vehicle that sends its identity, position and time to the AMS (glossary position-reporting-unit). A position report carries the vehicle's identity, the position in the local grid REFMINE-GRID, the time the position was measured, a sequence number and the device health (ch-02-05, ch-11-03).

Report period [H]. The interval between consecutive reports from one PRU, a configuration value [A] (1 s in this chapter's reference case, as in ch-04-08 and ch-11-01).

Report age. Time since the position in the newest received report was measured, computed from the PRU's time stamp against a synchronised clock (glossary report-age). The age at use is never zero: at best it is the chain latency, just before the next report it is one period plus the latency.

Position uncertainty. How far the vehicle could be from its last reported position: the fresh-fix accuracy plus a speed bound times the age, r=a+vbound·age (glossary position-uncertainty). The handbook takes a=0 for comparisons with ch-04-08; ahs_sim.mixed_fleet.uncertainty_m adds it as a parameter.

Awareness envelope [H]. The set of road positions within the uncertainty radius of the last reported position, measured along the roads, not as a circle in plan. It is the region where the vehicle could be now, and the region the protective action must keep trucks out of.

Staleness limit and lost limit [H]. Two configured ages [A]. Beyond the staleness limit the position is stale — shown as unknown, with the protective action applied. Beyond the lost limit it is lost — the envelope has grown past what the configured protective action was sized for, and the controller must act. Neither limit is recommended here.

Awareness state. What the AMS knows about where a permission holder is, kept separate from the permission state (glossary awareness-state). In ahs_sim.mixed_fleet the states are FRESH, STALE and LOST; the HMI adds two indications that do not depend on age: no report yet and outside permitted area (section 4.6).

Voice position [H]. A location stated by the driver over the radio and read back, recorded by the controller with its time. It is evidence for a person's decision; it is not a position report and does not reset the report age.

Protective action. The automatic AMS response to a stale position — in the reference design, holding the trucks whose paths could bring them to where the vehicle may be (glossary protective-action; ch-04-08).

4. Technical explanation

4.1 The reporting chain

FIG-14-02-01 shows the six steps from LV07's antenna to the HMI in CR1, with a latency budget for each part and the way each step can fail.

  1. Positioning. The PRU's receiver computes a fix and stamps it with the measurement time. Without a fix there is no report; with a wrong fix there is a wrong report that looks fresh (ch-14-08).
  2. PRU. Builds the report — identity, position, measured time, sequence number, device health — and sends it at the report period. A PRU that is off sends nothing.
  3. Site network. Carries the report over the serving comms site (CT1, CT2 or CT3) to the core. Latency and loss depend on the coverage class of the segment the vehicle is on (network.yaml), and a hard handover stops traffic for the handover gap.
  4. Device gateway. Checks the device against the registry and the message version; rejects what it cannot accept and says so (ch-02-05, REQ-02-05-006). A gateway that discards silently turns a format change into an apparent radio fault.
  5. AMS. Computes the age from the measured time, never from the arrival time (REQ-04-08-002), updates the awareness state and checks the position against the permitted area.
  6. HMI. Shows the icon, its age, the envelope when stale, the alarm and the protective hold. An HMI that hides the age turns an old position into apparent knowledge.

The field terminal is not in this chain: it carries requests, grants and acknowledgements over the same network, and the radio carries voice over a separate trunked system that does not use CT1–CT3 (network.yaml). That separation is why the radio is the fallback when position reports stop (section 4.7).

LAB-14-02-01 puts illustrative numbers on the chain [A]: 0.2 s on the vehicle from fix to transmission, the mean one-way latency of the coverage class (median plus the mean of the tail in network.yaml: 0.035 s good, 0.1 s marginal, 0.27 s poor), and 0.2 s for the gateway, the AMS and the HMI update. The totals are 0.435 s, 0.5 s and 0.67 s. With one report per second, the oldest age at use with no report lost is the period plus the latency:

Coverage class Latency Period 1 s: worst age / uncertainty at 40 km/h One report lost Period 2 s Period 5 s
good 0.435 s 1.435 s / 15.9 m 2.435 s / 27.1 m 2.435 s / 27.1 m 5.435 s / 60.4 m
marginal 0.5 s 1.5 s / 16.7 m 2.5 s / 27.8 m 2.5 s / 27.8 m 5.5 s / 61.1 m
poor 0.67 s 1.67 s / 18.6 m 2.67 s / 29.7 m 2.67 s / 29.7 m 5.67 s / 63.0 m

The marginal row reproduces ch-04-08's 1.5 s and 16.7 m. The lesson of the table is about proportions: the whole network latency, from good to poor coverage, changes the uncertainty by 2.7 m; one lost report adds 11.1 m; a five-second period adds 44.4 m. The report period and lost reports dominate; network latency hardly matters.

4.2 From age to uncertainty and the awareness envelope

The uncertainty grows linearly with age at the speed bound. For a light vehicle the AMS knows of no speed limiter; the bound available is the speed limit of the road — 40 km/h on R05 and R06 (mine.yaml) — and nothing guarantees that a driver keeps to it [A]. The 40 km/h row gives the values of SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07 in ch-04-08: 111.1 m when the AMS marks the position stale at T+10 s, 166.7 m when the controller calls at T+15 s.

A radius is not yet an envelope. LV07 cannot leave the roads, so the envelope is the set of road positions within r of the last report, measured along the roads. LAB-14-02-01 computes, for LV07's last report on R06 300 m short of Z (the ch-04-08 case [A]), the age at which the envelope reaches each node, with each segment's speed limit as the bound:

With a flat 40 km/h bound everywhere instead of the 30 km/h and 15 km/h limits of the pit-floor roads, EX02-Q is reached at 67.2 s, W at 68.6 s and EX02-L at 85.8 s: the choice of bound matters as soon as the envelope leaves the road it started on.

Two engineering consequences follow [H]. First, the envelope crosses Z at 27.0 s in this case; a protective action that holds the trucks bound for Z covers the envelope until about then, and longer only because a voice position stops its actual growth — the AMS's envelope keeps growing (section 4.7). Second, adding the trucks' stopping distance turns these ages into the zero-margin ages of ch-04-08 (19.4 s for the bounding case); the separation, the staleness limit and the protective action must be chosen together (REQ-04-08-002). This chapter sizes the envelope; it does not choose the limit.

4.3 Staleness detection: time to notice a real failure

When a PRU stops, the AMS notices only when the age passes the staleness limit. The detection delay after the moment of failure is the limit minus the time since the last report was measured — between the limit minus one period and the limit (ahs_sim.mixed_fleet.detect_fault, mode dropout). With one report per second and 0.5 s latency [A]:

Staleness limit [A] Detection delay after the failure Uncertainty when marked stale (40 km/h)
2 s 1.1 to 2.0 s 22.2 m
3 s 2.1 to 3.0 s 33.3 m
5 s 4.1 to 5.0 s 55.6 m
10 s 9.1 to 10.0 s 111.1 m
20 s 19.1 to 20.0 s 222.2 m

The 10 s row is SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07: last report T+0 s, stale at T+10 s. Every second of limit is 11.1 m of envelope the protective action must already cover when it starts.

4.4 Staleness detection: false alarms on marginal road

The other side of the trade-off is that working links lose reports in bursts. LAB-14-02-01 runs the two-state burst model of each coverage class (network.GilbertElliott, parameters in network.yaml) for 200 device-hours per class and counts how often the age passes each limit on a link that works. In the representation of ch-11-01 — "stale after N consecutive missing reports" — it reproduces LAB-11-01-01 exactly (good 13.29, 1.88, 0.59 and 0.04 false events per device-hour for N = 1, 2, 3, 5; marginal 131.74, 57.03, 31.49, 9.9; poor 329.62, 181.28, 129.28, 67.77). Three readings matter:

  • The same limit means different things on different roads. At 10 s a light vehicle on good road almost never goes falsely stale; on marginal road about once a device-hour; on poor road 18.66 times. A single limit for the whole mine is either slow on good road or noisy on marginal road (ch-11-01 REQ-11-01-005: limits per coverage class, and a display that distinguishes late from stale).
  • A slower report period costs on both sides. At 5 s per report, a 10 s limit raises 39.91 false events per device-hour on marginal road instead of 1.01, and the envelope at detection is just as large. Fewer reports do not buy quiet; they buy bigger gaps.
  • False alarms are not free. Each one triggers a protective hold — on R06 in SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07 the hold of the trucks bound for Z costs 8103.3 t per hour (ch-16-04) — and each one teaches people that a grey icon comes back by itself. The second cost is the dangerous one (section 11.2).

4.5 LV07's entry route and the common-mode case

LV07 enters at AP-02 (P1), drives down R05 and along R06 to the task: 1820.6 m, 163.9 s at 40 km/h [A]. R05 is marginal and has a hard handover from CT2 to CT3 at 1000 m below P1 with a 1.5 s gap (network.yaml); R06 is good and is served by both CT2 and CT3. Over 2000 seeded drives the probability of at least one false stale event during the entry is 1.0 for a 3 s limit, 0.429 for 5 s and 0.03 for 10 s. With a 3 s limit the handover alone does it: the gap swallows two consecutive reports, and the age reaches 3.5 s on every drive.

This is the explanation of the Pit Supervisor's third question. A grey icon on lower R05 that recovers by itself is the expected behaviour of a short limit on marginal road with a hard handover — and it is the reason why the HMI must show late differently from stale, and why the controller still treats each stale event as real until a fresh report or a voice position says otherwise.

Common mode: CT3 down. In SCN-COMMS-R05 the pit-floor trailer CT3 loses power. With CT3 down, network.lost_spans leaves no serving site on lower R05 from 1000 m to 1339.6 m and on R09, R10, R11, R19 and R20; R06 stays served by CT2. For LV07 two consequences follow [A]:

  • Parked on R06 at its task, LV07 keeps reporting: its awareness is unaffected even though the trucks on the bench have lost their links.
  • Driving down lower R05 at 40 km/h, LV07 crosses 339.6 m without coverage in 30.6 s; 31 reports are lost and the age reaches 32.5 s before the first report on R06 arrives. Any staleness limit below that marks LV07 stale, and possibly lost, during an ordinary drive.

A vehicle on the RL 330 bench during the outage stays stale for the whole outage — 900 s in the registry — however healthy its PRU is. Here the PRU reports and the trucks' links fail together: the AMS should say so — a site alarm for CT3 with every affected vehicle listed — and must not treat the stale positions as fewer people to protect (REQ-14-02-005). Voice radio is unaffected, because it does not use CT1–CT3; the roll call by radio of ch-14-08 is the fallback.

4.6 Awareness states and what the HMI shows

FIG-14-02-03 shows the awareness states and their transitions.

The manned-vehicle layer of the HMI combines the age-based state with two checks that do not depend on age [H]:

HMI indication Condition What the AMS does Controller's action
Fresh age within the staleness limit icon at the position, age shown; position checked against the permitted area none
Late (optional display of a fresh state) some reports missing, age within the class's normal bursts age highlighted watch; no action
Stale — position unknown, last seen …, age age beyond the staleness limit permission ACTIVE, area locked; envelope drawn; protective action applied (ch-04-08) radio call; record the voice position; inform the Pit Supervisor
Lost / unknown age beyond the lost limit, or no report since registration or entry as stale, higher-priority alarm; the vehicle is never assumed gone voice contact or escalation: no voice contact where trucks move means an area remote stop (ch-04-05)
Outside permitted area fresh position outside the permitted area treated as a real excursion: protective hold around the reported position, alarm radio call before any other conclusion (section 4.8)

The table's unknown is the handbook's lost: a vehicle for which the AMS has no usable report. The word "unknown" also appears in the stale indication (position unknown, last seen …), the wording of ch-04-08 and ch-16-04, unchanged here; the difference between lost and stale is the second limit and the escalation it triggers.

Three rules hold in every state [H]: the awareness state never changes the permission state (ch-10-06); no state other than a confirmed release ends the protection; and a transition back to fresh needs reports from the registered device that are consistent with the last voice position (REQ-14-02-003). A PRU that recovers after a reboot may come back with a wrong offset; a fresh report 400 m from where the driver said they are parked is a question, not an answer.

4.7 What the radio adds and what it does not

Operations objective 3 is about the radio. Position reports and positive communication answer different questions:

  • The PRU says where the vehicle is, continuously, without anybody speaking. It cannot say what the driver intends, whether they understood the permission or whether they are about to move.
  • The radio says what the person knows and intends — the request, the read-back, the voice position, "I'm parked at the marker, staying here". It is intermittent and depends on someone speaking.

A release needs both: the position report outside the area and the read-back (REQ-01-02-002). When the PRU fails, the radio supplies the voice position, which supports the Pit Supervisor's decision to continue on voice reports or to end the work (ch-04-08); it does not replace the missing half of the release. When the radio fails too, nothing supplies either half, and the response is the area remote stop. A driver who thinks "the PRU tells them where I am, so I don't need to call" has removed the half that knows intentions; a driver who thinks "I told them where I am, so the PRU doesn't matter" has removed the half that works when nobody is speaking.

In the AMS the voice position is recorded beside the stale PRU position, with its time and the controller's name; it does not reset the report age and does not stop the envelope from growing on the HMI (REQ-14-02-006). The envelope shows what the device knows; the voice position shows what the people know; the decision uses both.

4.8 A vehicle shown outside its permitted area

A fresh position outside the permitted area has two explanations: LV07 has left the area (a real excursion), or the report is wrong (an offset fix, a wrong reference point, a swapped device). The AMS cannot tell them apart, so it treats the first as true [H]: it applies a protective hold around the reported position as well as keeping the area locked, and raises an alarm. The controller calls LV07 before drawing any other conclusion. If the driver confirms they are where the report says, it is an excursion: the driver stops, and the area is extended or the driver returns under the controller's instruction. If the driver gives a location inside the area that the controller can check — a marker, a landmark, a sighting by the Pit Supervisor — the report is in doubt: the vehicle is treated as position-uncertain, the protective hold stays until the device is checked, and the PRU is tagged for the Maintenance Planner. A report error is never "cleared" by deleting the alarm.

A wrong but fresh position is the dangerous case because staleness cannot see it: the age is normal. Map matching sees an offset larger than the road corridor at the next report; a smaller offset is seen only by an independent cross-check such as a periodic radio position check (ahs_sim.mixed_fleet.detect_fault, mode offset; SCN-PRU-OFFSET-WT02). ch-14-08 treats that failure mode.

4.9 Trade-offs and model limits

  • Short limit against long limit. A short staleness limit protects with a smaller envelope and raises more false holds on marginal road; a long one is quiet and starts the protective action with a larger envelope. Limits per coverage class and a late display soften the choice; they do not remove it.
  • Fast reports against network load. One report per second per vehicle is small traffic (ch-11-02, ch-11-03); a faster rate shortens the gaps only while the link delivers.
  • Model limits. Constant latency per class; burst loss per 1 s slot with the class of the segment; a hard handover modelled as a fixed gap; speed bound = speed limit; envelope along the roads without vehicle length; no positioning error except as an offset (ch-14-08). The lab shows structure, not values.

6. Normal, abnormal and degraded operation

6.1 Normal operation — the healthy picture (SCN-POSCOM-LV07)

Before LV07 enters at T+100 s of SCN-POSCOM-LV07, the access check at AP-02 shows LV07's PRU fresh, at P1, with the registered identity; the radio check is answered; the FT is logged in (ch-16-04 section 6.1). During the drive down R05 the age stays near 1.5 s except at the handover 1000 m below P1, where it briefly exceeds 3 s; on R06 at the task it stays near 1.5 s. The icon is fresh, inside the permitted area {R05, R06}, and the envelope is not drawn. This is the picture every other state is compared with.

6.2 Abnormal operation — LV07's PRU fails (SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07, shared timeline)

Three points of the device side stand out. The awareness state never improves: from the AMS's point of view LV07 is lost until the end, and its envelope at T+495 s is 5500 m, larger than the pit. What ends the protection is not awareness but evidence: the observer's fresh PRU at the access point and the read-back (ch-16-04 section 4.8). And the protective hold from T+10 s to T+495 s costs 1091.7 t at 8103.3 t per hour (ch-16-04); every minute the observer needs to reach AP-02 costs about 135 t.

6.3 Abnormal operation — a delayed grant (SCN-POSCOM-STALE)

The same logic applies to permission messages. In SCN-POSCOM-STALE a grant issued at T+0 s reaches LV07's terminal at T+30 s, after its validity window; the terminal rejects it as stale at T+30 s, the area stays locked and the controller is alerted to re-issue at T+31 s (registry). A permission message, like a position report, is judged by its age from the issuer's time stamp, not by when it arrived (ch-02-07, ch-11-03). Both fail closed: an old grant is not a grant; an old position is not a position.

6.4 Degraded operation — CT3 down with vehicles in AA-North

Repeat SCN-COMMS-R05 with LV07 driving down lower R05 and a Survey vehicle on R10 [A]. At T+0 s CT3 fails. LV07's reports stop at 1000 m below P1 and resume on R06; its age peaks at 32.5 s. The Survey vehicle on R10 has no serving site and goes stale and then lost for the whole outage. At the same time the trucks on lower R05 and R09–R11 stop on link loss. The AMS shows a site alarm for CT3 with every affected truck and manned vehicle, keeps every permission and area lock, and applies the protective actions. The controller does a roll call by radio — unaffected by CT3 — and records a voice position for each manned vehicle (ch-14-08). Nobody is released until reports return or the substitute confirmation is completed; reconciliation of the trucks follows ch-11-07.

7. Engineering view

7.1 Design decisions and trade-offs

Age from the measured time, everywhere. Every awareness decision uses the PRU's measurement time against the AMS's synchronised clock (REQ-04-08-002). An AMS that used arrival time would understate the age by the latency and, with a clock offset, could treat a stale report as fresh.

Two limits, both configuration. A staleness limit starts the protective action; a lost limit escalates to the controller. Both are set per coverage class [A] (REQ-11-01-005), recorded with the separation analysis of REQ-04-08-002, and justified with false-stale rates like those of section 4.4 (REQ-14-02-001).

An envelope along the roads. A circle in plan would cover benches and roads the vehicle cannot reach and miss the fact that it can reach Z before P2. The envelope uses the network and each segment's speed bound.

Awareness separate from permission. The awareness state drives the HMI and the protective action; the permission state drives entry and release. Keeping them apart keeps the permission state machine of ch-10-06 unchanged.

Return to fresh is checked. Reports that resume after a stale or lost period must come from the registered device and agree with the voice position before the protective action is lifted (REQ-14-02-003).

7.2 Failure modes and mitigations

Failure mode Effect if unmitigated Mitigation
Age computed from arrival time or with a clock offset stale report shown as fresh measured-time age, synchronised clock (REQ-04-08-002)
Stale position drawn as a position controller believes the vehicle is where it was stale indication with age and envelope (REQ-02-04-007, REQ-14-02-002)
One staleness limit for all coverage classes slow on good road or noisy on marginal road limits per class, late display (REQ-11-01-005, REQ-14-02-001)

Common-mode loss shown as many separate stale vehicles controller misses the cause; slow roll call site alarm with affected vehicles (REQ-14-02-005)

7.3 Interface and timing considerations

On the shared timeline of section 6.2:

  • Up to T+0 s. PRU → network → gateway → AMS every 1 s [A]; 0.5 s latency; age 0.5 to 1.5 s; HMI fresh.
  • T+1 s to T+10 s. No report arrives. The AMS's age grows from the T+0 s measurement; the HMI may show late within the class's normal bursts.
  • T+10 s. AMS: age > staleness limit → STALE; envelope drawn; protective action → trucks bound for Z held; AMS → FMS: held trucks, DLY-OD-04; alarm to the HMI.
  • T+15 s. Voice radio (separate system): call, voice position, read-back. HMI: voice position recorded beside the stale position.
  • T+30 s. AMS: age > lost limit → LOST; alarm escalated; no change to permission, lock or hold.
  • T+300 s. Observer's PRU → AMS: fresh, at P1, outside the area; HMI: observer selectable for a substitute confirmation.
  • T+480 s to T+495 s. FT → AMS: report out; AMS: position_lost returns the permission to ACTIVE; radio: sighting and read-back; HMI: release with evidence type substitute, observer, position, time, approver; AMS → trucks: hold lifted, new granted paths.

8. Operations view

8.1 What the control room sees

Each manned vehicle in or at an autonomous area appears as an icon with its call sign and its age [H]. A fresh icon is solid; a late one shows the age highlighted; a stale one becomes an outline at the last position with the envelope drawn along the roads, the text position unknown, last seen …, age …, and the protective hold with the trucks it holds; a lost one adds a higher-priority alarm; outside permitted area shows the reported position, the permitted area and the protective hold around the reported position. Voice positions appear as a separate marker with the time and the controller's name. A comms-site outage appears as one site alarm listing every affected vehicle. At the access point the requester's icon shows fresh, the registered identity and the device health before any request is accepted.

8.3 Procedure excerpts

Pre-entry device check at AP-02 (LV07).

  1. Driver: stop at AP-02, outside the area. Look at the PRU status on the dashboard or terminal: reporting.
  2. Driver: radio check with CR1: "CR1, LV07 at AP-02, radio check." Wait for the answer.
  3. AHS Controller: confirm on the HMI that LV07 is fresh, at P1, with the registered identity, and that its health is reported. Tell the driver: "LV07, CR1, you show fresh at AP-02."
  4. Driver: confirm the FT is logged in.
  5. If any check fails: no request. The controller records the failure; the driver withdraws (REQ-16-04-001, REQ-14-01-006).

LV07 stale inside AA-North (keyed to section 6.2).

  1. T+10 s — AHS Controller: read the indication as position unknown. Confirm permission and lock active and the trucks bound for Z held. Do not assume LV07 has left.
  2. T+15 s — AHS Controller: "LV07, CR1, your position is not reporting. Confirm your location." Read back the driver's answer. Record it as a voice position.
  3. T+20 s — AHS Controller: "LV07, stay where you are until I call you." Inform the Pit Supervisor.
  4. T+30 s: the lost indication needs no new action while voice contact exists. If the call at T+15 s was not answered, call once more [A] and, if there is still no answer, initiate the area remote stop (ch-04-08 section 6.3).
  5. From T+60 s: follow the Pit Supervisor's decision and the substitute release confirmation of ch-16-04 section 8.3, steps 4 to 10. In the release dialog select the observer only if their PRU shows fresh at the named access point.

LV07 shown outside its permitted area.

  1. AHS Controller: confirm the protective hold around the reported position; do not dismiss the alarm.
  2. AHS Controller: "LV07, CR1, I show you on [segment]. Confirm your location." Read back.
  3. If the driver confirms the reported position: "LV07, stop where you are." Agree with the Pit Supervisor whether to extend the area or bring LV07 back.
  4. If the driver gives a location inside the area: treat LV07 as position-uncertain; keep the hold; seek an independent check (a landmark the driver reads, a sighting); have the PRU checked before the next entry.
  5. Record the event as an excursion or a report error, with the evidence.

Situation Machine Code Time category KPI that moves

Time categories and KPI names follow the GMG Time Usage Model (SRC-PUB-GMG-TUM-2020) [F]; codes are [H]. The delay codes say that trucks waited; they do not say why awareness was lost. Three indicators per shift and per coverage class are worth reporting [R]: stale events split into those that recovered by themselves and those that did not, time from stale to voice position, and time from device failure to release. A rising count of self-recovering stale events on one segment is a network finding; a falling time to voice position is a training finding.

8.5 Training and competency implications

Drivers learn the device check and that a failed device means no entry; that when the controller says their position is not reporting they answer, give a location the controller can check and stay put; and that the PRU and the radio are two halves, neither optional. Controllers learn to read the age, not just the icon; to treat every stale event as real, including the third self-recovering one on lower R05 that afternoon; to record voice positions; and to use the substitute release confirmation without shortcuts. Pit Supervisors practise being the observer. Engineers learn to justify limits per coverage class with false-stale rates and the separation analysis of ch-04-08. Any competency requirement from legislation or codes of practice for these roles is not stated here [TBV].

9. Requirements and acceptance criteria

REQ-14-02-001 [H] For each coverage class and each type of manned vehicle, the site shall document the report period, the latency budget of the reporting chain, the resulting worst report age and uncertainty at the speed bound, the staleness limit and the lost limit chosen, and the expected false stale events per device-hour on a working link. The limits shall be configurable per coverage class. Rationale: the same limit is quiet on good road and noisy on marginal road; the period and lost reports dominate the age (sections 4.1, 4.4; REQ-11-01-005). Verification: inspection of the configuration record; analysis (LAB-14-02-01 parts A, C and D).

REQ-14-02-002 [H] The HMI shall show every manned vehicle in or at an autonomous area with its call sign, report age and one of the indications fresh, stale, lost (including no report since registration or entry) and outside permitted area. A stale or lost vehicle shall be shown at its last position with the awareness envelope drawn along the roads at the configured speed bound, and with the protective action and the trucks it holds. Rationale: what the AMS shows must match what it knows (sections 4.2, 4.6; REQ-02-04-007). Verification: demonstration with injected report loss; test of the envelope against LAB-14-02-01 part B.

REQ-14-02-003 [H] After a manned vehicle has been stale or lost, the AMS shall return it to fresh and lift the protective action only when reports again arrive from the device registered to that vehicle and the reported position is consistent with the last recorded voice position; otherwise the vehicle shall remain position-uncertain and the controller shall be alerted. Rationale: a recovered device may report a wrong position (section 4.6; ch-14-08). Verification: test (PRU resumes with an offset after a reboot: protective action stays).

REQ-14-02-004 [H] When a fresh position places a manned vehicle outside its permitted area, the AMS shall raise an alarm and apply a protective hold around the reported position in addition to keeping the permitted area locked, until the controller has established by radio whether it is an excursion or a report error. A report error shall lead to the device being checked before the next entry. Rationale: the AMS cannot distinguish an excursion from a wrong report; the safe assumption is the excursion (section 4.8). Verification: test (injected outside-area position); exercise.

REQ-14-02-005 [H] When the position reports of several manned vehicles stop together with the links of trucks served by the same comms site, the AMS shall show one site alarm listing every affected vehicle, while keeping each vehicle's permission, area lock and protective action as for a single stale vehicle. Rationale: common-mode loss needs the cause to be visible without weakening each vehicle's protection (sections 4.5, 6.4; SCN-COMMS-R05). Verification: test (CT3 outage in SIL with LV07 on lower R05 and a vehicle on R10); exercise of the radio roll call.

REQ-14-02-006 [H] A position obtained by voice shall be recorded by the AMS as a voice position, with the time, the stated location and the controller's identity, shown beside the last PRU position. It shall not reset the report age, change the awareness state or end the protective action. Rationale: the voice position supports a person's decision; it is not a position report (section 4.7; ch-04-08). Verification: inspection of the HMI and the log; test.

REQ-14-02-007 [H] For a permission whose holder's PRU is stale or lost, the AMS shall offer the substitute release confirmation of REQ-16-04-006 only when an observer is selected whose own PRU is registered, fresh and places them at the named access point outside the permitted area; it shall record the evidence type, the observer's identity, position and time and the approver with the release, and shall flag the failed device so that the vehicle cannot request entry until it passes the access check. Rationale: device-side checks of the substitute release confirmation defined in ch-16-04; no new condition is added to it (section 6.2). Verification: test (substitute release refused with no observer, with a stale observer and with an observer away from the access point); inspection of release records.

10. Verification and validation

Verification (make test CH=ch-14-02): the latency budget per coverage class and the worst age and uncertainty table; the uncertainty radius table and the envelope reach times from LV07's last report; the detection delays per staleness limit; the false-stale table, including the exact reproduction of LAB-11-01-01's table and the cross-check with pru_reports; the entry drive (route, handover at 1000 m on R05, false-stale probabilities 1.0, 0.429 and 0.03); the CT3 outage (lost spans, 31 reports lost, 32.5 s maximum age, R06 still served); the awareness states along SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07.

Validation needs real devices and real networks: measured report ages and loss per segment, drills with a PRU powered off inside a permitted area (time to stale indication, to radio call, to protective hold), recorded false-stale events per coverage class against the lab's model, and recovery behaviour of real PRUs after a reboot. Whether the ISO 21815 series (SRC-STD-ISO21815) or ISO 17757:2019 (SRC-STD-ISO17757) address position reporting, staleness or separation for manned vehicles among autonomous trucks is not verified here [TBV] (SRC-PUB-MIXED-FLEET-STD-RELEVANCE); no report rate, staleness limit or separation is taken from any standard.

11. Common mistakes

11.1 Design mistakes

  • Age from arrival time. The latency disappears from the age, and a clock offset can make a stale report look fresh.
  • An icon without an age. The HMI shows a position the AMS stopped knowing about seconds ago.
  • A circle in plan instead of an envelope along the roads. It covers places the vehicle cannot reach and hides the order in which it can reach intersections.
  • A slower report period to "reduce alarms". It lengthens every gap and raises false events on marginal road.
  • Accepting a recovered PRU without a check. A rebooted device with an offset ends the protection on a wrong position.

11.2 Operational mistakes

  • "It always comes back on lower R05." The third grey icon of the afternoon is handled like the first.
  • Treating the voice position as a release. It supports a decision; it is not a position report.
  • Dismissing an outside-area alarm as a glitch. Call first; the vehicle may really be outside.
  • Selecting an observer who is not yet at the access point. The observer's PRU must show them there, fresh.
  • Entering with a failed device because the radio works. One failed device fails the access check.

12. Worked examples

Example 1 — worst age on a working link. Period 1 s, latency 0.5 s (marginal): 1 + 0.5 = 1.5 s; at 40 km/h (11.11 m/s): 1.5 × 11.11 = 16.7 m. One report lost: 2.5 s, 27.8 m. Period 5 s: 5.5 s, 61.1 m.

Example 2 — the envelope in SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07. Stale at T+10 s: 10 × 11.11 = 111.1 m; the controller's call at T+15 s: 166.7 m. The envelope reaches Z, 300 m away, at 300 / 11.11 = 27.0 s, and P2, 481 m away, at 43.3 s.

Example 3 — choosing between 5 s and 10 s on marginal road. At 5 s: stale 4.1 to 5.0 s after a failure, 55.6 m of envelope, 17.45 false events per device-hour. At 10 s: 9.1 to 10.0 s, 111.1 m, 1.01 false events per device-hour. The first protects with half the envelope and raises about seventeen times more false holds; which is acceptable depends on the separation (ch-04-08) and on what each false hold costs. The handbook does not choose.

Example 4 — CT3 down during LV07's entry. Lower R05 without coverage from 1000 m to 1339.6 m: 339.6 m at 11.11 m/s = 30.6 s; reports measured in that time are lost (31), and the age reaches 32.5 s. With a 30 s lost limit LV07 would be shown lost for a moment while driving normally.

Example 5 — the price of the observer's walk. Protective hold from T+10 s to T+495 s, 485 s, at 8103.3 t per hour: 8103.3 × 485 / 3600 = 1091.7 t (ch-16-04).

15. Key takeaways

  • A position report is always old: period plus latency when everything works, plus a period for each lost report. The period and the losses dominate; network latency hardly matters.
  • Uncertainty grows at the speed bound — 11.1 m per second at 40 km/h — and the awareness envelope grows along the roads; in SCN-PRU-FAIL-LV07 it reaches Z at 27.0 s.
  • A staleness limit trades detection time against false alarms, differently on each coverage class; no value is recommended.
  • Stale and lost never mean the vehicle has left; awareness never changes the permission state; a voice position helps a decision but is not a report.
  • A fresh position outside the area is treated as real until the radio shows otherwise.
  • After a PRU failure the release uses the substitute release confirmation of ch-16-04; the device side is a fresh observer PRU at the access point and a flagged failed device.
